One conscious participant · two biological information systems
ME can gradually recognise and use information arising through ESS and CLS. The three are related, but they are not three equivalent systems. ME · conscious participantThe lived person who can notice, reflect, choose, learn and take responsibility. ESS · Emotional Somatic SystemFast, embodied information processing that works largely outside conscious awareness. CLS · Cognitive Logical SystemReflective, symbolic information processing that can compare, explain and plan across time.
Coordinated conscious access
Coordinated conscious access is the relationship through which bodily information, differentiated feeling, situational meaning and direction may become consciously readable. It does not perform the underlying detection or evaluation, and it is not a separate biological system. In Porges’s theoretical framework, neuroception names a proposed form of below-awareness safety, danger and life-threat evaluation; it is not another name for coordinated conscious access. Nervous system organisation may shape the conditions under which information becomes available and usable by ME, but a Position does not set an access band. Model 1 records access separately.

Nervous System Organisation
This card explains the Nervous system organisation row in both tables. Organisation is the whole-organism configuration through which the nervous system reallocates energy, attention, perception, feeling, connection and action in response to present or learned conditions. Each Position names a different organisation—not a personality, identity, emotion or behaviour.

How to read the two tables
Fluid · present-condition organisation

The Fluid table follows how nervous system organisation changes as present conditions change.

Scientific question What is recruited now, and does organisation update and Return when conditions change?

Chronic · learned protective availability

The Chronic table follows protection that may remain easier to recruit, harder to update or slower to release.

Scientific question How can repeated or developmental experience alter recruitment, weighting, felt access, interpretation, action and recovery?

These are reference planes, not two kinds of people. Between them are degrees of recurrent bias, learned restriction and recovery. A pattern may sit at a different depth across processes and contexts.

The conscious choice is not whether the underlying information initially appears. ME may use CLS to turn towards, test and act on what becomes available—or to dismiss or rationalise it. Neither route makes the underlying biological processing disappear; using CLS to dismiss an ESS signal does not stop that signal from forming. Grounding Homeostatic and allostatic recovery, parasympathetic reactivation, sleep, metabolism, digestion, interoception, reward learning, agency, mastery, curiosity, and goal completion ground restorative and self-directed availability. Recovery is multisystem and has no single autonomic marker or fixed clock (McEwen; Roddick et al., 2025).Attachment, social safety, interpersonal emotion regulation, positive affect, exploratory attention, social perception, mentalizing, and empathy ground the reduced cost and wider information field possible with trusted others. Mentalizing, affect sharing, and concern remain separable capacities rather than one “social-engagement system” (Slavich, 2020; Reeck et al., 2016; Kanske et al., 2016).Research on orienting, uncertainty, anticipatory threat, prediction error, attention to change, and safety learning grounds checking before the situation is settled. The strength and direction of the response depend on expected threat, ambiguity, controllability, and prior learning (Grupe & Nitschke, 2013).Acute-stress research grounds rapid sympathetic and HPA recruitment, salience-led attention, altered pain access, and reduced working-memory and cognitive flexibility under immediate threat. Effects on inhibition, mentalizing, and empathy are contextual rather than uniformly absent (Arnsten et al., 2015; Shields et al., 2016; Nitschke & Bartz, 2023).Research on sustained and anticipated threat, stress controllability, prospective control, perseverative cognition, and HPA duration grounds keeping selected variables active across time. “Strategic Management” is a TEG-Blue integration: evidence supports prediction and control processes, not a discrete biological state or inevitable interpersonal control (Herman et al., 2016; Ottaviani et al., 2016).Human and comparative research on the defensive cascade, periaqueductal-gray networks, active defence, pain modulation, and extreme action urgency grounds maximum protective mobilisation. It does not establish violence, domination, loss of empathy, or one universal “rage circuit” from arousal alone (Linnman et al., 2012; Kozlowska et al., 2015; Nitschke & Bartz, 2023).Research on attentive freezing, immobility under attack, tonic immobility, collapsed immobility, dissociation, and post-threat recovery grounds reduced movement or engagement when active response is unavailable or overwhelmed. These are distinguishable patterns with variable heart-rate and autonomic profiles, not one proven “dorsal-vagal shutdown” (Kozlowska et al., 2015; Volchan et al., 2017).

Grounding scope. The Grounding row is an orientation map of relevant research domains and claim boundaries, not the final source trace. It identifies where later cell-by-cell grounding should expand, test, qualify, or separate TEG-Blue integration from established findings. These process rows describe characteristic allocation under each Environmental condition, not an all-or-nothing switch or a required experience. A process may remain available while not being recruited, and an active signal may organise action without becoming differentiated feeling.

The evidence questions and selection boundaries used to ground the Fluid and Chronic tables are explained in the Science Grounding Protocol. Current research anchors for the Fluid organisations remain in the working notes while row-level grounding develops.

Chronic Survival

Learning, not duration alone

How protective organisation becomes easier to reach

Chronic Survival is not defined by duration alone. It describes protective organisation that has become easier to recruit, harder to update or slower to release after repeated or developmental conditions.

Conditions that can contribute

  • Unsafe or unpredictable conditions during development.
  • Repeated threat, neglect, coercion, instability, loss or overwhelm.
  • Prolonged stress with too little rest, safety, support or recovery.
Protection repeatedly required Protective organisation repeatedly recruited Becomes easier to enter or slower to release

Protection explains function; it does not erase impact. Responsibility, repair and whether the pattern changes still matter.

Developmental, stress, learning and autonomic research can ground these mechanisms. The seven Positions remain a working map, not a clinical taxonomy.

Grounding Allostatic-load, impaired stress-recovery, sleep, autonomic-flexibility, and perseverative-cognition research ground cumulative multisystem dysregulation despite apparent rest. Allostatic load is a composite research construct with heterogeneous measures, not a synonym for this Position (Ottaviani et al., 2016; McCrory et al., 2023; Roddick et al., 2025).Attachment insecurity, social-evaluative threat, rejection sensitivity, ostracism, self-silencing, and interpersonal emotion-regulation research ground connection used to protect belonging. Hyperactivating and deactivating strategies are supported; a unitary biological “appeasement state” is not yet established (Williams, 2007; Malik et al., 2015).Uncertainty, deficient safety learning, threat expectancy, fear generalisation, and sustained attention to threat ground checking that does not resolve. PTSD eye-tracking evidence is stronger for difficulty disengaging from threat than for universally faster threat detection, so “hypervigilance” must remain component-specific (Grupe & Nitschke, 2013; Lazarov et al., 2019).Chronic-stress, sensitisation, threat generalisation, impaired safety learning, and defensive-habit research ground protection recruited across situations that no longer differ clearly. Research must keep actual ongoing threat separate from a generalised read of safety as danger; diagnosis does not define the Position (fear-generalisation research, PMID 29282161; ongoing-threat review, PMID 34139653).Perseverative cognition, anticipatory stress, controllability, executive control, and instrumental social-cognition research ground sustained prediction and outcome management. The claim that management “becomes the relationship” is TEG-Blue integration, not an established autonomic category; detailed mentalizing can remain intact while being used instrumentally (Ottaviani et al., 2016; Kanske et al., 2016; Tafet & Ortiz Alonso, 2025).Coercive-control, dominance, repeated aggression, restored-versus-restricted agency, accountability, and recurrence research ground patterned power and its impact. Current evidence describes behaviour, relational power, danger, and outcomes; it does not establish a unique autonomic profile, trauma re-enactment, or absent empathy as the cause (Dichter et al., 2018; Lohmann et al., 2024; Hilton & Radatz, 2025).Burnout, exhaustion, cognitive impairment, social withdrawal, dissociation, learned helplessness, immobility, depression, and autonomic-recovery research ground persistent low-output states. These constructs overlap but are not interchangeable, and chronic collapse is not established as one dorsal-vagal mechanism (Volchan et al., 2017; Parker et al., 2021; Tafet & Ortiz Alonso, 2025).

The current Chronic table is the far reference plane of the depth model. It describes patterns that have become strongly persistent or difficult to update; it does not describe every chronic adaptation or place a whole person in one column. A process may remain highly active while becoming threat-biased, rigid, fused, compulsory or instrumental. Reduced ME access is only one possible form of chronic change, and the Position alone cannot establish which access band is present.

Three processes and their human translation

The scientific rows describe how each nervous system organisation allocates three underlying processes. The participation card for ME translates what those processes may make consciously possible. The optimal Fluid design is not maximum recruitment at all times, but flexible access, self–other distinction and updating in response to what the situation actually requires.

Underlying process What the process does Important boundary
Interoceptive processing (internal-state representation) The nervous system senses, represents and integrates internal bodily information arising from Body Signals and from the embodied components of Situation Signals. Interoception does not by itself identify an experience as love, shame, fear or anger, and internal information can influence action without becoming consciously felt or named.
Affective sharing (experience sharing) Another person’s affective state becomes represented within one’s own organism while remaining identifiable as the other person’s experience. Affective resonance is not accuracy, agreement or obligation. Without self–other distinction it may become emotional contagion rather than empathy.
Mentalizing (cognitive representation of another) Another person’s perspective, intention, feeling and likely action are represented and can be revised through new information. Understanding can remain cognitively detailed while affective sharing or concern is reduced; it can therefore be used reciprocally, defensively or instrumentally.

Empathic concern is shown separately as relational output. It asks whether another person’s welfare can motivate, restrain or redirect action. Concern can draw on affective sharing and mentalizing, but it is not identical to either process.

Return

Return is movement toward non-defensive organisation after the organism registers that its present protective configuration is no longer required, or can be interrupted. Return can become biologically available before physiological recovery is complete.

Return is not simply calming down, ending visible conflict, complying, withdrawing, becoming exhausted, apologising, or reconnecting. It does not establish that impact has been repaired, that the relationship is restored, or that later organisation has changed.

The biological sequence

These are gates, not fixed stages or a universal clock. Different systems can reopen at different times, and movement can reverse if the conditions change again.

01Environmental resolution
Has the danger, rupture, demand, loss, or uncertainty actually changed?
02Defensive completion
Has the required protective action succeeded, stopped, or become unnecessary?
03Updated situation read
Can the organism detect that the present conditions are now different?
04Return availability
Can defensive organisation begin relinquishing control of action?
05Capacity re-entry
Which capacities for feeling oneself, feeling another, understanding another, and concern become usable again?
06Physiological recovery
What activation, depletion, pain, hormonal, metabolic, or sleep cost remains?
07Learning and integration
Does this update change how the organism organises in later situations?

Pathways of completion

The condition domain changes what can complete the configuration. Completion may use more than one pathway, and only some pathways require another nervous system.

Environmental change
Danger ends, distance or shelter becomes available, a resource arrives, or the material obstruction changes.
One’s own action
Protection succeeds, a boundary becomes effective, a task completes, a solution works, or a goal is revised.
Another nervous system
Reception, recognition, reciprocity, clarification, co-regulation, accountability, or repair is genuinely relational.
Physiological recovery
Mobilisation releases and the body receives the rest, nourishment, treatment, sleep, or restoration still required.
Grief and reorganisation
What cannot be restored is registered, expectation changes, and life reorganises around the irreversible condition.

The pathway is determined by what the signal concerns. External praise cannot complete a purely physical escape route, and solitary calming cannot supply mutual recognition when the unresolved condition is genuinely relational.

Repair after rupture

Repair is adjacent to Return but not contained inside it. Enough Return may make accountability biologically possible; it does not guarantee that repair occurs, that recovery is complete, or that a relationship will be rebuilt.

Actor-side reparation
What can the person who caused harm stop, acknowledge, take responsibility for, restore, or change?
Harmed-person recovery
What safety, distance, support, justice, meaning, grief, or other recovery belongs to the person harmed?
Relational rebuilding
Do all involved voluntarily choose renewed contact, trust, or reconciliation?

Actor-side reparation can continue without forgiveness or renewed access. Harmed-person recovery does not require reconciliation, and relational rebuilding may never occur.
