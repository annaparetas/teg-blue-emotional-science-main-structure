Working grounding index · 14 nervous system organisations

Autonomic organisation is coordinated participation, not one pathway switched on.

This file opens the compact Autonomic pathway row. It separates functional task, pathway participation, organ-level change, timing, conscious access and evidence limits.

Page responsibility · Model 2 physiology companion

Scientific-facing scope · coordinated autonomic, central, endocrine, metabolic and organ-level participation across Gradient organisations

Governing questionHow may pathway participation, organ regulation and timing differ as the organism recruits different functional organisations?

This page owns

The expanded reading of the Gradient’s compact autonomic row, including pathway, organ, timing, measurement and evidence limits.

It receives

The seven Gradient Positions and the project’s claim-matched grounding rules.

It does not establish

One pathway–one state identities, a body-wide switch, a vagal hierarchy or a complete organisation from behaviour, heart rate or HRV alone.

Central research questionAs the organism moves from restoration and connection through uncertainty, direct defence, sustained management, life threat and shutdown, how do the coordination, timing and functional role of autonomic pathways change?

The labels in the main table are functional shorthand. Sympathetic and parasympathetic activity can occur together; organs can show different patterns at the same time; neural and hormonal responses unfold on different timescales. No behaviour, feeling, heart-rate value or HRV value can establish a complete autonomic organisation by itself.

How to read autonomic organisation

Not one exclusive branch

An organisation coordinates sympathetic, parasympathetic, enteric, central, endocrine and metabolic participation according to the task.

Not one body-wide setting

Heart, lungs, blood vessels, digestion, pupils, bladder, temperature and muscle can be regulated differently within the same response.

Not a diagnosis from behaviour

Similar behaviour can arise through different physiology, and overlapping physiology can support different actions. Measurement and context must stay together.

Fluid Gradient

Baseline

↩ Main table

Working synthesis · papers pending

Functional autonomic task
Restoration and internal maintenance while no defensive demand requires priority.
Likely pathway participation
Parasympathetic activity can support digestion, cardiac flexibility and recovery while sympathetic tone continues at ordinary background levels.
Organ-level pattern
Heart and breathing can vary flexibly; digestive, thermoregulatory and restorative processes remain available.
Timing
Neural autonomic adjustments occur continuously; endocrine and metabolic restoration unfolds across longer periods.
What may become conscious
Quiet breath, digestive activity, warmth, heaviness, comfort or tiredness may become perceptible without urgency.
What allows updating
A new demand can recruit orienting or defence; continued safety allows restoration to continue.
Measurement and evidence limits
Baseline cannot be diagnosed from a low heart rate or high HRV. Organ systems can differ, and ordinary parasympathetic activity is not a unitary state.

Fluid Gradient

Connection / Belonging

↩ Main table

Working synthesis · papers pending

Functional autonomic task
Support reciprocal social engagement, co-regulation and relational coordination while defensive demand may remain lower.
Likely pathway participation
Flexible parasympathetic and sympathetic coordination may support face, voice, listening, movement and engagement. Vagal cardiac regulation can participate without becoming a single ventral-vagal switch.
Organ-level pattern
Cardiac and respiratory timing can remain responsive to interaction while digestion, warmth and movement stay available.
Timing
Adjustment can track another person moment by moment; social buffering and recovery effects can continue after contact.
What may become conscious
Face, voice, breath, warmth, ease, energy and relational timing may be available together.
What allows updating
Connection updates from direct relational information. Separation, uncertainty or threat can recruit a different organisation.
Measurement and evidence limits
Social engagement cannot be inferred from vagal tone alone. Ventral-vagal language is a functional shorthand, not a complete anatomical measurement.

Fluid Gradient

Safety Checking

↩ Main table

Working synthesis · papers pending

Functional autonomic task
Interrupt ongoing activity long enough to establish what changed and whether safety or belonging remains available.
Likely pathway participation
Brief sympathetic recruitment, parasympathetic adjustment and orienting circuits can combine as attention, posture, breath and sensory gain change.
Organ-level pattern
Heart rate, pupil size, skin conductance, muscle tone, head movement and breathing may shift while information is gathered.
Timing
Orienting is rapid and provisional. It should close or reorganise when enough information arrives.
What may become conscious
A stomach drop, held breath, skin change, stillness, widened attention or urge to look may become conscious.
What allows updating
Clear information can restore connection or baseline; confirmed danger can move organisation towards B.
Measurement and evidence limits
No one autonomic measure separates curiosity, novelty, uncertainty and threat. The defining feature is functional updating, not arousal alone.

B

Fluid Gradient

Protection / Defence

↩ Main table

Working synthesis · papers pending

Functional autonomic task
Make rapid protection, resistance, escape, guarding or appeasement available under direct threat.
Likely pathway participation
Sympathetic neural signalling and possible cardiac vagal withdrawal support rapid mobilisation; parasympathetic processes may still participate by organ and response route.
Organ-level pattern
Heart, circulation, breathing, pupils, sweating, skeletal muscle, digestion and bladder function can be reprioritised around immediate action.
Timing
Autonomic changes begin within seconds; catecholamine and HPA participation unfold on overlapping but different timescales.
What may become conscious
Heartbeat, rapid breath, heat, cold, shaking, bracing, nausea or urgent movement may dominate awareness.
What allows updating
The response can reduce when danger ends, protection succeeds or a viable route becomes available.
Measurement and evidence limits
Fight, flight, fawn and freezing do not have one diagnostic autonomic profile. Behaviour alone cannot reveal pathway activity.

C

Fluid Gradient

Strategic Management

↩ Main table

Working synthesis · papers pending

Functional autonomic task
Sustain prediction, planning and management when threat persists across time.
Likely pathway participation
Continued sympathetic and autonomic-endocrine participation may support vigilance and effort while selected parasympathetic functions continue. Coordination can vary with controllability and task demands.
Organ-level pattern
Cardiovascular and muscular readiness may remain elevated; digestion, pain, fatigue and restorative processes can be backgrounded rather than absent.
Timing
Rapid autonomic adjustments are repeatedly recruited while slower endocrine, metabolic and immune effects accumulate and recover.
What may become conscious
Jaw, shoulder or chest tension, shallow breathing, restlessness or narrowed awareness of fatigue may be perceptible.
What allows updating
Resolution, reduced demand and enough time for recovery allow sustained mobilisation to stand down.
Measurement and evidence limits
C cannot be defined as sustained sympathetic activation alone. Focused work, exercise and ordinary challenge can recruit overlapping physiology.

D

Fluid Gradient

Power Mobilisation

↩ Main table

Working synthesis · papers pending

Functional autonomic task
Make maximum force, movement and survival action available when life is at stake and ordinary protection is insufficient.
Likely pathway participation
Intense sympathetic, catecholaminergic, HPA, opioid, respiratory and metabolic participation may combine. Freezing, bradycardic or mixed responses can also occur depending on route and imminence.
Organ-level pattern
Circulation, breathing, glucose availability, muscle recruitment, pain modulation, perception and visceral function can be radically reprioritised.
Timing
Some defensive changes occur immediately; hormonal, inflammatory, metabolic and recovery consequences continue after the event.
What may become conscious
Force, heat, tunnel vision, altered pain, shaking, breath disruption or an experience of action happening before reflection may occur.
What allows updating
Ending danger, reaching a viable route and substantial physiological recovery allow wider capacity to return.
Measurement and evidence limits
Maximum activation is not a chemical or autonomic signature for rage, violence or dominance. Human life-threat data are difficult to obtain and responses vary.

Fluid Gradient

Shutdown

↩ Main table

Working synthesis · papers pending

Functional autonomic task
Conserve remaining capacity and reduce engagement when active mobilisation is unavailable, unsuccessful or too costly.
Likely pathway participation
Reduced movement and engagement can involve parasympathetic, sympathetic and central inhibitory processes in different combinations. Bradycardia, hypotension or immobility may occur, but are not universal.
Organ-level pattern
Cardiovascular output, muscle tone, breathing, temperature, digestion, attention and responsiveness may reduce or become unstable.
Timing
Shutdown may occur rapidly or follow prolonged mobilisation. Recovery can be slow and uneven across systems.
What may become conscious
Cold, heaviness, numbness, distance, weakness, faintness, fog or immobility may become dominant or difficult to interpret.
What allows updating
Reduced demand, physical safety, warmth, energy and gradual re-engagement can make mobilisation and connection available again.
Measurement and evidence limits
“Dorsal vagal” is a functional shorthand, not a complete diagnosis. Collapse, fainting, freezing, fatigue and dissociation must not be treated as one autonomic event.

Chronic Survival

Chronic Elevated Baseline

↩ Main table

Working synthesis · papers pending

Functional autonomic task
Maintain background protection when the organism does not fully register that restoration is available.
Likely pathway participation
Low-level sympathetic monitoring, reduced cardiac flexibility or incomplete parasympathetic recovery may persist or recur, but patterns vary by person and organ.
Organ-level pattern
Resting heart, breath, muscle tone, sleep, digestion and recovery may remain subtly organised around readiness.
Timing
Activation may be modest at any moment yet repeat across hours and days, interrupting complete recovery.
What may become conscious
Jaw or chest tension, shallow rest, vague unease or difficulty feeling restorative needs may remain in the background.
What allows updating
Repeated evidence of safety, usable rest and completion of unresolved demands may gradually widen autonomic flexibility.
Measurement and evidence limits
A chronic elevated baseline cannot be inferred from one HRV, cortisol or heart-rate value. Longitudinal patterns matter more than a snapshot.

Chronic Survival

Chronic Conditional Belonging

↩ Main table

Working synthesis · papers pending

Functional autonomic task
Preserve connection by monitoring and regulating around another person rather than resting in reciprocal safety.
Likely pathway participation
Autonomic coordination remains contingent on social cues; appeasement, vigilance and compensatory engagement may prevent parasympathetic settling from deepening.
Organ-level pattern
Face, voice, breath, stomach, throat and cardiac responses may organise around another person’s approval, mood or availability.
Timing
Moment-to-moment social adjustment repeats without completing into stable recovery.
What may become conscious
Another person’s state may be highly legible while fatigue, hunger, limits or self-directed preference remain faint.
What allows updating
Connection that tolerates difference, boundaries and direct information can reduce the need for continuous adjustment.
Measurement and evidence limits
People-pleasing has no unique autonomic signature. Similar outward warmth can arise from safety, strategy, fear or learned social behaviour.

Chronic Survival

Chronic Safety Checking

↩ Main table

Working synthesis · papers pending

Functional autonomic task
Keep uncertainty open because no amount of checking produces a trusted conclusion about safety or belonging.
Likely pathway participation
Repeated orienting may combine sympathetic bursts, breath interruption, sensory gain and incomplete parasympathetic recovery.
Organ-level pattern
Pupils, skin conductance, heart, breath, posture and gastrointestinal sensations may shift repeatedly with each possible cue.
Timing
Short orienting responses recur so often that checking becomes the baseline rather than a temporary interruption.
What may become conscious
Skin and breath changes, startle, stomach movement, tension or scanning may remain continuously available.
What allows updating
Reliable information, tolerable uncertainty and experiences in which checking can end may gradually restore closure.
Measurement and evidence limits
Persistent checking cannot be established from arousal alone. Attention, learning, sleep, medication and medical conditions can produce overlapping measures.

Chronic Survival

Chronic Protection / Defence

↩ Main table

Working synthesis · papers pending

Functional autonomic task
Keep protection ready before present threat is established or after the initiating danger has ended.
Likely pathway participation
Sustained or easily re-recruited sympathetic mobilisation and reduced engagement-related flexibility may support guarding, appeasement or rapid defence.
Organ-level pattern
Cardiovascular readiness, breathing, sweating, muscle tone, digestion, pain and sleep may remain organised around protection.
Timing
Protective bursts may be frequent, prolonged or slow to recover rather than continuously maximal.
What may become conscious
Heartbeat, bracing, heat, shaking, irritability, urgency or numbing of competing bodily needs may recur.
What allows updating
Accurate present-moment updating, effective boundaries and repeated recovery can help defence stand down.
Measurement and evidence limits
Chronic defensiveness is not identical to chronic sympathetic activation. Behavioural pattern and physiology must be assessed separately.

Chronic Survival

Chronic Control & Strategic Management

↩ Main table

Working synthesis · papers pending

Functional autonomic task
Use prediction, management and control to prevent threat from becoming unmanageable.
Likely pathway participation
Repeated sympathetic, HPA, metabolic and attentional recruitment may support continuous planning while tension and effort become normalised.
Organ-level pattern
Muscle tone, cardiovascular load, sleep, digestion, immune regulation and awareness of fatigue may be affected across different timescales.
Timing
Fast mobilisation repeats within slower allostatic adaptation and incomplete recovery.
What may become conscious
Tension may feel ordinary or disappear from awareness while output, planning and monitoring remain high.
What allows updating
Reduced demand, relinquished control, tolerable uncertainty and genuine recovery can make internal limits readable again.
Measurement and evidence limits
Control behaviour does not prove a particular autonomic state. Strategic cognition can remain high under several physiological configurations.

Chronic Survival

Chronic Dominance & Repression

↩ Main table

Working synthesis · papers pending

Functional autonomic task
Preserve the required outcome through repeated override when vulnerability, limit or another person’s reality cannot constrain action.
Likely pathway participation
High-effort sympathetic and metabolic recruitment may be repeatedly accessed, while pain, fatigue and interoceptive limits are suppressed, ignored or unavailable.
Organ-level pattern
Circulation, muscle force, breath, glucose regulation, pain modulation and visceral functions may be organised around imposed action.
Timing
Mobilisation may surge rapidly and recur before recovery, accumulating cardiovascular, metabolic and relational cost.
What may become conscious
Force and outcome may dominate while exhaustion, injury, fear and impact become conscious only later or not at all.
What allows updating
Ending override, restoring limits and allowing recovery and accountability are necessary for wider information to return.
Measurement and evidence limits
No autonomic pattern establishes coercion, intent or responsibility. Those require behavioural evidence over time; physiology does not excuse harm.

Chronic Survival

Chronic Collapse / Shutdown

↩ Main table

Working synthesis · papers pending

Functional autonomic task
Conserve capacity through withdrawal and reduced engagement when demand repeatedly exceeds what can be mobilised.
Likely pathway participation
Low or unstable autonomic output, parasympathetic and sympathetic mixtures, central inhibition, inflammatory and metabolic processes may contribute through different routes.
Organ-level pattern
Movement, blood pressure, temperature, digestion, sleep-wake regulation, attention and responsiveness may remain reduced or difficult to coordinate.
Timing
Collapse can persist, recur or lift unevenly; different organ systems recover on different schedules.
What may become conscious
Cold, heaviness, numbness, fog, weakness, faintness or inability to locate needs may become familiar.
What allows updating
Reduced demand, medical assessment where needed, warmth, energy, safety and gradual re-engagement can support return.
Measurement and evidence limits
Burnout, depression, dissociation, fatigue, syncope and medical illness cannot be collapsed into a single dorsal-vagal explanation.
