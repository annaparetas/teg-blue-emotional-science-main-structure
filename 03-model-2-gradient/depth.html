Working architecture · Fluid and Chronic

The Gradient as a three-dimensional field

The Fluid and Chronic tables are not two kinds of people. They are two reference planes. The space between them allows the same organisation or capacity to remain fluid in one territory while becoming increasingly restricted in another.

This model locates a repeated pattern in a defined context. It does not place a whole person at one permanent coordinate. Read the governing model premise →

Page responsibility · Model 2 coordinate view

Scientific-facing scope · contextual organisation, processing layer and degree of updating or learned restriction as separable coordinates

Governing questionHow can one locate a pattern without collapsing organisation, processing layer and Fluid–Chronic depth into one label?

This page owns

The coordinate geometry: across for organisation, down for processing layer and through for context-specific depth between reference planes.

It receives

The Gradient Positions and rows; the intermediate field explains what may vary between the planes.

It does not establish

A severity scale, a whole-person location, one latent “depth” trait or a causal pathway from Fluid to Chronic.

Three questions locate the pattern

Each axis answers something different. Keeping them separate prevents nervous-system organisation, emotional access, behaviour and diagnosis from being collapsed into one label.

The intermediate field

These are provisional depth descriptions, not new diagnoses or fixed stages. They give us language for the territory currently missing between the two tables.

Far Chronic reference

Present information poorly corrects the organisation; competing capacities and reliable Return are severely constrained.

far plane

A pattern may move in either direction. Depth can differ by signal family, relationship, context, processing layer and time.

What the far Chronic plane represents

The far plane is a limit reference. It helps us describe the strongest form of a repeated protective pattern without treating that pattern as a complete account of the person.

It represents

  • protection recruited beyond the conditions that originally required it;
  • signal access, weighting or interpretation that is difficult to correct;
  • action routes that become compulsory, instrumental or severely narrowed;
  • limited participation from competing needs, another person’s reality or later evidence;
  • updating, Return and repair that no longer occur reliably.

It does not establish

  • a diagnosis, personality type or moral identity;
  • that every capacity is absent in every context;
  • one childhood history, intention or biological mechanism;
  • that adversity inevitably produces harm;
  • that empathy is simply present or absent;
  • that understanding a protective function removes responsibility for behaviour and impact.

Locate patterns, not people

A person is not one point in the field.

The useful unit is a repeated process in a defined context. Someone may use reflective thought fluidly at work while shame remains deeply restricted in intimate relationships. Mentalizing may remain detailed while affective sharing becomes unavailable during conflict.

context + organisation + processing layer + depth + time
Example of uneven depth.
Threat strategy may update when a practical problem ends, while Belonging-at-Risk signals continue to organise checking long after reassurance arrives.
Example of capacity separation.
Another person’s intentions may be represented accurately while their feeling does not become affectively shared or does not constrain the chosen action.

What changes with depth

Depth does not mean that ESS stops producing information. It describes how readily an organisation is recruited, what information receives priority, what becomes available to ME, how CLS interprets it, which actions remain possible, and whether the sequence can update when conditions change.

ESS + organisation

Recruitment threshold

How much present evidence is needed before a protective organisation is recruited? With greater depth, a smaller or more ambiguous cue may be enough.

ESS · signal selection

Information weighting

Which Body and Situation Signals gain priority, and which recede? Depth may amplify one signal family while making competing information harder to use.

ESS → ME

Differentiation and felt access

Can bodily change, emotion, need, prediction and another person’s state be distinguished, or do they arrive fused, faint, delayed or inaccessible?

ME ↔ CLS

Interpretive flexibility

Can the first explanation remain provisional and be revised with context, or does it become the only explanation that feels possible?

ME · action

Action flexibility

Can the person pause, choose among several responses and include competing needs, or has one route become automatic, compulsory or instrumental?

Whole sequence

Updating, persistence and Return

When the situation changes, how long does the organisation continue? Can new information enter, pressure fall and the capacities needed for repair return?

Depth does not reduce every capacity equally. It can make one protective route unusually available while another process remains fluid. Each variable therefore needs to be read separately and in context.

Internal restriction and harmful behaviour are not the same scale

The depth axis describes flexibility and updating inside the organism. Harm requires a second assessment of what the person repeatedly does with the capacities, choices and power available to them.

Depth question · internal organisation

How restricted has this process become?

This reading stays with recruitment, signal access, interpretation, action flexibility and Return.

  • Does present evidence still update the pattern?
  • Are alternative capacities available?
  • Is the restriction specific or widespread?
Harm question · behaviour in relationship

What happens to other people?

This reading examines behaviour over time rather than inferring character from nervous-system organisation.

  • What action occurred, and was it repeated?
  • What power, pressure or coercion was used?
  • What was the impact?
  • What happened when limits or consequences appeared?
  • Did accountability, change and repair follow?
Chronic depth ≠ harmfulness ≠ diagnosisA diagnosis requires its own evidence and clinical assessment. Understanding protection does not remove responsibility for behaviour.
