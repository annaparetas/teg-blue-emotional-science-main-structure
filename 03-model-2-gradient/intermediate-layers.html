Nervous System Gradient · layers between two reference tables

From Fluid Response to Chronic Pattern

The Fluid Gradient and Chronic Survival tables describe two reference planes. Between them, the same organisation can become easier to recruit, harder to question and slower to release—while other capacities remain fluid.

Governing questionWhat can change between a present-responsive organisation and a recurrent pattern that current information no longer revises reliably?

This page owns

The variables and evidence questions used to describe uneven intermediate depth within a defined context and processing layer.

It receives

The Fluid and far Chronic reference profiles and the three-coordinate field.

It does not establish

Universal stages, a one-way progression, severity, permanence or one coordinate for the whole person.

Two endpoints, not two kinds of people

The tables become clearer when they are read as limits of a field. Fluid names organisation that remains responsive to present conditions. Far Chronic names a protective pattern that present information no longer corrects reliably.

Near reference plane

Fluid Gradient

The organisation corresponds closely enough to present conditions. Competing information remains usable, several actions remain possible, and the system can update and Return when the condition changes.

Far reference plane

Chronic Survival

The organisation is readily reconstructed or remains active beyond its original conditions. Access, interpretation, action or Return may be substantially narrowed.

The intermediate field is uneven. A pattern can be deep in one relationship, emotion family or processing layer and fluid in another.

The depth lens

Select a reference layer to read what changes. These are working descriptions, not diagnoses, scores or fixed stages. Movement can occur in either direction.

Near plane

Fluid reference

Organisation changes with present conditions closely enough to use available information, choose among responses and Return.

Protective recruitment is proportionate to the situation. Body and Situation Signals can enter participation without one family consistently excluding the others. The first interpretation remains revisable, and changed conditions can change the response.

Look across time: the organisation can stand down without requiring the same protective action to be repeated.

Six variables carry the change

Depth is not simply “more activation.” It describes how the whole sequence changes: what starts it, what information gets through, what can be felt and understood, what actions remain available, and what happens after conditions change.

Read behaviour in three time frames

The same visible behaviour can occur at several depths. Its position becomes clearer through correspondence, flexibility and what happens next—not through appearance alone.

Now · observable

What can another person see?

Action, speech, attention, decision-making, boundaries, response to limits and use of power can be observed. These do not disclose the whole internal process.

Inside · inferred carefully

What may be organising it?

Body state, emotion, felt access, organising pull, inner narrative and views of self or others require report, context or cautious inference.

After · longitudinal

What happens when conditions change?

Watch whether the behaviour can stop, feedback can enter, alternatives return, impact becomes representable and repair follows across time.

Where the behaviour domains belong

The behaviour vocabulary translates the six internal variables into questions that tools and observation can carry. It is evidence for a depth reading, not a separate depth scale.

Depth and harm require separate readings

Internal restriction describes what is available inside the organism. Harm describes what repeatedly happens to other people. Neither scale can substitute for the other.

Internal organisation

How restricted has this process become?

  • Does present evidence still update it?
  • Are alternative capacities available?
  • Is the restriction specific or widespread?
  • Can the organisation Return when conditions change?
Behaviour in relationship

What happens to other people?

  • What action occurred, and was it repeated?
  • What power, pressure or coercion was used?
  • What was the impact?
  • What happened when limits or consequences appeared?
  • Did accountability, change and repair follow?
Chronic depth ≠ harmfulness ≠ diagnosis Understanding a protective function does not authorise behaviour or remove responsibility for impact.
