Fluid and Chronic Organisation

Two responses can look equally intense and still have very different relationships with present conditions. One may change as the situation changes. Another may recur across contexts, remain difficult to revise, or continue because the danger itself has not ended. Intensity does not tell us which we are seeing.

Does not establish
A severity scale, a Chronicity score, a whole-person location, a causal pathway from Fluid to Chronic, or that any visible behaviour reveals which route is operating.

Compare one position under Fluid and Chronic organisation using the same sixteen questions. Depth introduces the differences in persistence and flexibility; intermediate patterns expand that comparison.

Central proposition

A relationship with present conditions, not a type of person.

Fluid and Chronic describe how organisation, information and response relate to present conditions, feedback, time and updating. They do not describe two kinds of person.

The distinction operates across the Emotional Signal Map and all three models. It changes meaning slightly at each layer while preserving one question: does the pattern remain sufficiently responsive to what is happening now, or has it become recurrent, generalised, persistent or difficult to revise?

Approved definitions

Two reference orientations.

Near reference

Fluid organisation

Present-responsive organisation that remains sufficiently coupled to current and predicted conditions, available action, outcome information and updating.

Fluid does not mean calm, comfortable or non-defensive. A strong mobilisation can be Fluid when it corresponds to present danger and changes as conditions and outcomes change. Fluidity may involve recruitment that fits the demand, access to relevant information, action that can alter or test the condition, feedback that can revise weighting, and the possibility of completion, Return and later recovery.

Far reference

Chronic organisation

Recurrent narrowing, generalisation, persistence or difficult revision in recruitment, information access, interpretation, action or recovery.

Chronic does not mean continuously activated, permanently damaged or disconnected from reality. A Chronic pattern may be reconstructed after apparent recovery, remain limited to one context, involve reduced access rather than visible arousal, or continue because adverse conditions remain present. It may involve lowered thresholds, broader generalisation, recurrent weighting of familiar signals, narrowed access, restricted action expectancy, persistence despite changed outcomes, uneven recovery, or continuing danger.

A relationship, not a binary

The formal description is a profile, not a score.

Fluid and Chronic are reference orientations. Real patterns are mixed. A response may update in one domain and remain restricted in another. A person may show wide reflective access while action remains constrained, or recover physiologically while relational expectations remain unchanged.

Persistence and flexibility profile · proposed formal term

A context- and time-specific description of how readily a pattern is recruited, how broadly it generalises, what information and actions remain available, how it responds to changed conditions and how recovery unfolds.

The profile asks separately about
1 · Current demandIs there a continuing condition the pattern corresponds to?
2 · Recruitment thresholdHow much present evidence recruits the pattern?
3 · Response magnitudeHow large is the response relative to the demand?
4 · Generalisation breadthAcross which cues, contexts, relationships or signal families?
5 · Danger–safety discriminationCan relevant differences still be distinguished?
6 · Context sensitivityDoes the pattern vary with where and with whom?
7 · Information and felt accessWhat can still be noticed, differentiated and used?
8 · Action range and controllabilityWhich responses remain available and expected to matter?
9 · Persistence and outcome responsivenessDoes the pattern change when outcomes change?
10 · Recovery profileHow does recovery unfold across channels?

No total

No total is calculated. The same pattern can move differently across these dimensions.

Research timescale

Acute and Fluid are related but not identical.

Acute

In research, acute usually names an event-timescale exposure or response. Use it when the scientific claim concerns duration or event timing.

Fluid

In TEG-Blue, Fluid names the response's relationship with present conditions, feedback and updating. Use it when the architectural claim concerns present responsiveness.

An acute response can be Fluid. It can also leave carryover or contribute to a recurrent route. A long response can still correspond accurately to a continuing condition. Timescale alone does not decide the organisation.

Chronic routes

No one route defines the category.

Chronic organisation can arise or persist through several routes. The route matters because similar visible behaviour can require different explanations and different forms of change.

RouteWhat may be happening
Prolonged persistenceRecruitment remains active or slow to loosen within the same episode.
Interrupted or incomplete recoverySome channels settle while others remain recruited, depleted or unavailable.
Repeated reconstructionA familiar organisation is rebuilt after it had loosened or was no longer visible.
Lowered recruitment thresholdLess, earlier or more ambiguous information recruits the pattern.
Broader generalisationRecruitment spreads across cues, contexts, relationships or signal families.
Reduced discriminationRelevant danger, safety and contextual differences become harder to distinguish.
Context-bound updatingNew learning is available in some conditions but not retrieved or transferred in others.
Restricted action expectancyEscape, refusal, help-seeking or another response is expected not to change the outcome.
Perseverative reconstructionAnticipation, rumination, imagery or repeated representation renews demand.
Ongoing correspondenceThe pattern continues because danger, deprivation, pain, illness or coercion continues.

One route has its own name

Model 3 uses Chronic Cycle for one specific recurrent cognitive-override route. That cycle is one member of this wider family of routes, not the family itself.

Current demand

A pattern cannot be read without asking what is happening now.

Four condition descriptions

Continuing demand present · demand uncertain or mixed · demand has changed · insufficient information. Use one where possible.

Persistence in danger

Persistence in continuing danger is not evidence of failed updating because the response is costly or long-lasting.

End of the event

The end of the external event does not prove that all relevant systems have updated or recovered.

Movement

Chronic organisation can become more Fluid.

Fluid organisation makes updating and Return more available. It does not guarantee completion. Completion, Return and recovery remain different processes; one can begin while another remains open.

What can move a pattern

The condition changes; effective action becomes possible; credible feedback becomes usable; emotional–somatic information becomes more accessible; the CLS account revises; recovery restores capacity; new learning transfers into the relevant context.

Reconciliation · proposed decision, revision required

Depth shows the orientation. The profile explains what it is made of.

The three-dimensional Gradient uses visual depth to show movement between the Fluid and far Chronic reference planes. That visual architecture is kept. What is being clarified is what the distance represents, so that one depth axis does not become a hidden score of how Chronic a person is.

Visual term

Fluid–Chronic visual depth

The spatial metaphor used to display broad movement between present-responsive and recurrently restricted reference configurations. It can stay in titles and interface language when its visual role is explicit.

Formal term

Persistence and flexibility profile

The dimensions described separately. Used in grounding documents, claim records and any future assessment method. Never a depth score.

Why the decision is still open

The bodily-information, affective-sharing and mentalizing controls generate uneven capacity configurations. Those configurations are not points or successive layers on the Fluid–Chronic axis; each can have its own profile. The visual implementation must therefore be revised together with the capacity-configuration work, and no implementation should begin from the earlier profile-axis proposal alone. The depth page and the intermediate-layers page are carried as working pages; the decision record is in the notes.

Boundaries and consistent use

Never describe a person as Fluid or Chronic.

Boundaries

Fluid does not mean healthy, good, calm or fully conscious. Chronic does not mean false, pathological, permanent or caused by trauma. Intensity, emotion, diagnosis or behaviour alone cannot locate a pattern. No visible behaviour reveals which route is operating. Continuing protection may correspond accurately to present conditions. Internal organisation does not determine intention, harmlessness, responsibility or repair.

Consistent use

Use Fluid organisation and Chronic organisation as the primary terms. Retain acute for research timescale. Describe the profile dimensions or the route rather than saying "more Chronic". Keep current demand visible beside persistence. Use visual depth only as diagrammatic orientation. Name the specific pattern, context and time. Keep adaptation, resilience, heterogeneity and null findings visible in grounding.
