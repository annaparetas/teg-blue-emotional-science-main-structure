Approved model territory

What is the organism organised for now?

Central proposition

Nervous-system regulation continuously reorganises the organism's resources and capacities in relation to detected and predicted demands. The resulting organisation changes what information and action become easier, harder, clearer or less available.

The Gradient models present organisation. It does not classify the person.

Working definition

The Nervous System Organisation Gradient is TEG-Blue's model of changing organism-level organisation across restoration, connection, uncertainty, protection, sustained management, maximum mobilisation and conservation.

It owns present organisation, resource allocation, action-readiness, controllability, transition and current Return availability. It does not own emotion identity, behaviour or temporal outcome. Its scientific-facing subtitle is present organism-level resource and capacity organisation across Fluid and Chronic reference positions; its short navigation label is Nervous System Gradient.

Protected coordinates

The same questions apply across seven positions and routes.

Each Fluid Position is a reference organisation within the gradient. The positions make differences visible. They are not sealed states: several biological systems participate in each, and many processes operate across more than one. X and A are non-defensive; B to Z are defensive organisation

X and Z sit off the gradient of defensive demand at either end: X because no present demand requires defence, Z because active mobilisation is no longer available. A↔B is where defensive organisation begins, by checking what changed.

Governing premise

Five rules for reading any position.

These rules govern every Model 2 page and every page that connects to it. The full statement, with a worked example for B and the sixteen lenses, is the governing premise.

01

Regulation does not mean calm

Regulation is the ongoing organisation and allocation of biological resources and capacities in relation to current demands. Mobilisation can be regulatory. Conservation can be regulatory. Arousal alone cannot tell us whether an organisation is adaptive.

02

Finite resources, changing priorities

The nervous system does not support every function at maximum capacity at once. Conditions register, the situation is evaluated, demands change, resources are redistributed, physiology and perception and readiness change, action becomes available, organisation updates. This is recursive, not a one-way sequence.

03

Organisation is not behaviour or emotion

One mobilisation pattern can support many behaviours: run, confront, orient, call for help, protect another, remain still. Fear, anger, urgency and determination can all occur within it. The Gradient models the organisation within which emotional, cognitive and behavioural processes operate.

04

The thinking brain does not switch off

As defensive demand increases, cognition can become increasingly recruited into solving the immediate survival problem, while some broad, reflective, flexible or long-horizon processing may become harder to access. Avoid "the prefrontal cortex goes offline".

05

Graded language, not boxes

Transitions can be gradual, rapid, partial, mixed and nonlinear. Prefer increasingly, more available, prioritised, constrained, recruited, shifted toward. Avoid on/off, completely shuts down, cannot, always, only.

Fit and persistence

Mobilisation is not mismatch

A defensive organisation is not pathological because it is uncomfortable or expensive. Ask about fit, intensity, updating, flexibility and Return. Persistent or mismatched organisation matters for chronic stress and trauma, but must not be projected backwards onto ordinary defensive physiology.

Model boundary

What the Gradient owns, and what it does not.

Owns

Present organism organisation; the reference positions X, A, A↔B, B, C, D and Z; changing resource-allocation emphasis; capacity availability under different demands; the distinction between non-defensive and defensive organisation; fit, proportionality, flexibility, duration, updating and Return at the organisational level.

Does not own

The definition of individual emotions or signals (Signal Map); the original source of detected information and its conscious availability (Model 1); the event sequence through which completion or carryover occurred (Model 3); the complete developmental history of a pattern (F2); behaviour, intention, morality, diagnosis or responsibility (Behaviour and responsibility).

Shared event record

Model 2 supplies the organisation configuration at each moment of an event. A Position is not the event. It is one time-indexed reading of the organism's present priorities, resources and capacity access inside that event. When Model 3 says the nervous system updated, it must name which Gradient variable changed.

Two survival functions

Survival function is kept separate from defensive recruitment.

X and A

At X, organism-protection can operate non-defensively through maintenance and restoration. At A, connection-preservation can operate non-defensively through belonging, reciprocity and participation.

A↔B

Connection-preservation and organism-protection interact while safety and belonging are checked.

B to Z

From B towards D, direct protection receives increasing organisational priority. At Z, organism-protection may continue through conservation when active mobilisation is unavailable or too costly.

Why this matters

A can serve survival without being defensive, and X can protect the organism without representing threat. The two functions are defined on Emotions as Information; the shared biological foundation is in Reference.

Two reference planes

Fluid and Chronic are not additional positions.

The seven positions describe what the organism is organised to do. Fluid and Chronic describe how responsive, recurrent, generalised or difficult to revise that organisation is across time and context. Any position can be examined through a Fluid–Chronic profile. A person is not at one permanent depth, and Chronic organisation is not a later position beyond Z.

Near reference plane

Fluid organisation

Present-responsive organisation that remains sufficiently coupled to current and predicted conditions, available action, outcome information and updating. A strong mobilisation can be Fluid when it fits present danger and changes as conditions change.

Far reference plane

Chronic organisation

Recurrent narrowing, generalisation, persistence or difficult revision in recruitment, information access, interpretation, action or recovery. A pattern, not a kind of person. Not severe, chosen, fake or permanent.

The shared distinction, its ten profile dimensions, the Chronic routes and the reconciliation between visual depth and the persistence and flexibility profile are on Fluid and Chronic Organisation.

Non-inference rule

Protective organisation can make some information urgent and other information faint. It can narrow action without determining the action taken. A protective reading can also be accurate when danger, deprivation, illness or coercion remains present. Felt certainty alone cannot establish that a person has the complete situation.

Never infer a person from a Position. A Gradient Position is not an emotion, behaviour, personality, diagnosis, moral category or biological worth scale. Bodily-information, affective-sharing and mentalizing configurations describe access, not character. How access works belongs to Model 1.

Return and recovery

Return is renewed flexibility, not calm.

Return

Renewed availability of more flexible, less defensively dominated and more restorative organisation when the previous demand no longer requires the same resource allocation. It can become available before physiological recovery is complete. It is not calming down, complying, withdrawing, exhaustion, apologising or reconnecting.

Recovery

The replenishment, repair and settling that may continue across different channels after or alongside Return. Hours or longer. No single autonomic marker and no fixed clock.

Ownership

The Gradient owns Return as reorganisation and current Return availability. Model 3 owns the event sequence through which completion, carryover or changed conditions may make Return possible. Completion, Return, recovery and repair remain different questions; one can begin while another stays open.

The nine terms that are not interchangeable, the biological gates, the pathways of completion and the Fluid route matrix are on the Return page. The Chronic routes there are not yet written.

Whole-Gradient method

Ask the same questions at every position.

Do not document B physiologically while describing X or Z only psychologically. Every position is examined through the same domains rather than defined by one favourite mechanism.

Conditions and physiology

Current and predicted conditions; autonomic, endocrine, metabolic and immune participation; cardiovascular, respiratory, motor and sensory organisation.

Information and participation

Attention, memory, prediction and meaning; emotional information and felt access; connection, affective sharing and mentalizing.

Action and change

Action availability and controllability; non-urgent functions; updating, persistence, recovery and Return.

Many to many

One autonomic branch, hormone, brain region, facial expression or emotion cannot stand in for a Position. The sixteen lenses are listed on the governing premise; the tables apply them row by row.

Scientific and ethical boundaries

The whole person is more than the organisation visible at one moment.

Boundaries

A Position is not a diagnosis, personality, developmental stage or moral category. Organisation does not determine one emotion, behaviour or intention. Low arousal does not establish safety; high arousal does not establish pathology. An expensive protective response can be proportionate. Chronic organisation does not prove present danger is absent. Nervous-system explanation does not excuse harm or establish innocence.

Consistent use

Use organisation, not on/off states. Use Fluid Position for X to Z. Name the present condition and survival function separately from the Position. Distinguish resource allocation from a literal pool of energy. Use Return for renewed flexibility and recovery for rebuilding. Describe the pattern in a context and time, not the person as a type.

Behaviour, power, impact, viable alternatives, response to feedback, recurrence and repair are assessed on their own evidence plane. See Behaviour and responsibility.
