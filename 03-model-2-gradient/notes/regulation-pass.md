## Dual-name working rule

**The Regulation Pass** is the accessible working name. The scientific-facing subtitle states the distinctions being tested. Both should remain visible while the work develops.

## Why this pass is necessary

The word **regulation** currently appears across TEG-Blue at several levels:

- biological systems maintain viable conditions;
- nervous-system organisation changes under demand;
- people try to alter emotion, attention, bodily activation or action;
- another person may provide support, reassurance, coordination or constraint;
- a behaviour may reduce immediate pressure;
- control may secure quiet or compliance by transferring cost;
- institutions maintain rules, roles, incentives and social order;
- recovery, completion, updating and Return may follow—or may not.

These processes can affect one another. They are not the same process.

When one word carries all of them, several errors become easy:

- relief starts to look like resolution;
- calm starts to look like health or safety;
- compliance starts to look like co-regulation;
- control starts to look adaptive because it reduced someone's uncertainty;
- a social pattern starts to look like a nervous-system mechanism;
- a proposed function starts to define a behaviour;
- explanation starts to displace responsibility;
- Return, recovery, repair and reconciliation collapse into one outcome.

The pass begins by separating the questions.

## Central question

> **When TEG-Blue uses the word regulation, what is changing, through which process, at what level and timescale, with what observable outcome, and who carries the cost?**

## Governing boundaries

1. **Regulation does not mean calm.** Biological regulation continues during mobilisation, protection, conservation, connection and restoration.
2. **Regulation does not identify a good state or good person.** A process can preserve viability, reduce pressure or stabilise a system while remaining costly, coercive or poorly fitted to the conditions.
3. **A behaviour is not a regulation strategy by appearance.** Its proposed function and actual consequences require evidence.
4. **Immediate pressure reduction is not completion.** The relevant condition may remain unchanged.
5. **Nervous-system organisation is not behaviour.** It changes available fields without authoring the act that follows.
6. **Co-regulation is not compliance.** Another person's successful influence does not establish care, consent or relational safety.
7. **Institutions are not organisms.** Biological and social regulation may share a useful pattern description without sharing one mechanism.
8. **Explanation does not exonerate.** Behaviour, power, impact, responsibility, protection and repair remain their own plane.

## The first definition gate

Before using **regulation**, complete the sentence:

> **The regulation of _what_, by _which process_, towards _which observable change_?**

If those fields cannot be named, the term is too broad for the claim.

| Required field | Question |
| --- | --- |
| **Unit of analysis** | Organ, physiological variable, distributed organism, emotion episode, attention, behaviour, relationship, group or institution? |
| **Target or concern** | What variable, demand, uncertainty, condition, feeling, action or social outcome is changing? |
| **Regulating process** | Feedback, prediction, attentional deployment, reappraisal, action, support, reassurance, rule, threat, force, resource allocation or another process? |
| **Carrier or actor** | Which biological processes, person, relationship, group or authority participate? |
| **Timescale** | Milliseconds, episode, hours, repeated interactions, development, generations or institutional history? |
| **Immediate outcome** | What changed first, and how was it observed? |
| **Longer outcome** | Did the condition resolve, recur, generalise, become more costly or update? |
| **Cost and power** | Who used resources, lost options, carried fear, labour or recovery cost, and was refusal viable? |
| **Evidence status** | Established process, project synthesis, hypothesis, analogy or public shorthand? |

## Working distinctions

### 1. Organism regulation

**Working meaning:** the distributed biological activity through which viable conditions are maintained or restored across changing demands.

Relevant scientific territories may include homeostasis, allostasis, autonomic and endocrine regulation, immune and metabolic coordination, thermoregulation, pain, sleep and energy balance.

**Boundary:** organism regulation does not validate the Gradient Positions, ESS, CLS or Return as discovered biological systems. Each component claim requires its own evidence.

### 2. Nervous-system organisation

**Working meaning:** TEG-Blue's whole-organism description of how energy, attention, perception, feeling, connection and action availability may be configured under present and learned conditions.

**Boundary:** organisation is a field of changing availability. It is not synonymous with regulation, one autonomic branch, an emotion, a coping strategy or a behaviour.

### 3. Emotion regulation

**Working meaning:** processes through which the occurrence, intensity, duration, expression, attention, interpretation or experience of emotion may be influenced.

The person may act before an emotion develops, while it is unfolding or after it has changed. Strategies can be deliberate or less conscious, internal or interpersonal, helpful in one context and costly in another.

**Boundary:** emotion regulation is not automatically emotional health. Suppression, reappraisal, distraction, avoidance, expression, acceptance, action and support need separate definitions and outcome measures.

### 4. Immediate pressure reduction

**Working meaning:** an observed reduction in urgency, distress, uncertainty, conflict, activation or action pressure following a behaviour or changed condition.

**Boundary:** pressure reduction is an outcome, not proof of the behaviour's function, fit, safety or longer-term value.

### 5. Interpersonal regulation and support

**Working meaning:** processes through which one person's presence, communication or action changes another person's emotional experience, biological demand, interpretation, options or access to support.

Possible routes include comfort, information, practical help, shared attention, reassurance, boundary support and coordinated action.

**Boundary:** influence is not automatically co-regulation, care or consent. The model must ask whether the other person retained a viable option to disagree, refuse, pause or leave.

### 6. Imposed stability and transferred regulation cost

**Working meaning:** a provisional TEG-Blue inquiry category for situations in which one person or system reduces its own uncertainty, conflict or burden by restricting another person's behaviour, expression, access or agency.

Examples may include demanded appeasement, enforced silence, surveillance, punishment, deprivation or control. These are not interchangeable and should be named directly whenever evidence permits.

**Boundary:** domination and coercion are established through behaviour, power, imposed cost and restricted agency—not through a claim about the actor's internal regulation.

### 7. Social and institutional regulation

**Working meaning:** the use of norms, incentives, procedures, resource distribution, authority, monitoring and sanctions to shape conduct or maintain an institutional arrangement.

**Boundary:** this is a sociological, political, legal or organisational level of analysis. It must not be described as the institution having an autonomic state, emotional need or neural mechanism.

### 8. Recovery

**Working meaning:** change after demand across physiological, cognitive, experiential, behavioural, sleep, pain, metabolic, immune and relational channels.

**Boundary:** channels may change at different rates. Quiet behaviour, a lower heart rate or reported relief does not establish whole-person recovery.

### 9. Completion

**Working meaning:** a public-facing umbrella for the more specific changes that allow a particular event or demand to change course or finish.

Depending on the event, this may involve changed conditions, effective action, protection, nourishment, grief, information, boundary enforcement, recovery, updating, reorganisation or repair.

**Boundary:** there is no one universal discharge or completion pathway.

### 10. Updating

**Working meaning:** a change in weighting, expectation, interpretation, readiness or future recruitment following new conditions, action, evidence or consequence.

**Boundary:** changed explanation does not establish changed emotional–somatic weighting, behaviour, recovery or repair.

### 11. Return

**Working meaning:** TEG-Blue's whole-system construct for renewed availability of more flexible organisation, restoration and participation as demand changes.

Its exact relationship with Baseline X remains governed by the active Gradient architecture and later reconciliation work.

**Boundary:** Return is not calm, compliance, forgetting, forgiveness, completed physiological recovery or restored relational trust.

### 12. Repair

**Working meaning:** action concerning impact, responsibility, protection, restitution and possible rebuilding after harm or rupture.

**Boundary:** the actor's regulation, insight, recovery or Return does not repair another person's injury or restore access to a relationship.

## Five outcome questions

The legacy work recovered five questions that help prevent relief, recovery and Return from becoming one event:

1. **Condition:** Did the relevant internal, material or relational condition change?
2. **Information:** Did new evidence become available and alter the signal or interpretation?
3. **Recruitment:** Could the immediate protective or problem-solving recruitment reduce?
4. **Recovery:** Did physiological and cognitive resources recover after the demand?
5. **Return:** Did more flexible organisation, updating and relational availability reopen where relevant?

The questions can have different answers. A person may feel relief while the condition remains. A condition may end while physiology is still recovering. Return may begin before all channels have recovered. Repair may still be required after all five.

## Route audit

Every proposed regulation route should be examined through:

```text
condition · information and access · proposed function · actual behaviour
        · immediate outcome · duration · person-level cost
        · transferred cost · power used · response to feedback
        · recurrence · completion, updating or Return
```

### Candidate outcome classes

These are working inquiry classes, not labels for a surface behaviour.

| Candidate class | What may have happened | What remains to be checked |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Condition-changing action** | The relevant demand, danger, deprivation, uncertainty or need changed | Fit, proportionality, longer outcome and cost |
| **Supportive modulation** | Pressure reduced enough to create time, access or capacity | Whether the condition later changed and whether support remained consensual |
| **Pressure reduction without relevant updating** | Immediate experience changed while the recruiting condition or expectation remained | Recurrence, generalisation, cumulative cost and alternatives |
| **Imposed stability** | Conflict or uncertainty reduced through another person's compliance, silence or restricted options | Power, viable refusal, harm, recurrence and accountability |
| **Recovery or restoration** | One or more response channels rebuilt after demand | Which channels, timescales and remaining costs |
| **Unknown or mixed function** | The evidence does not support one classification, or several processes occurred together | Preserve uncertainty rather than choosing a master explanation |

## ESS, CLS and ME within Regulation

ESS and CLS do not divide into a regulating system and a regulated system.

- ESS may make condition-reading, emotional significance, bodily organisation, learned weighting, felt access, readiness and recovery participation visible.
- CLS may make naming, comparison, context, anticipation, narrative, planning and revision visible.
- Both may contribute to pressure reduction, condition-changing action, persistence, updating or harmful control.
- ME may participate with some of what becomes available, without standing outside biology or holding total control.

The key question is not **which system regulates**. It is:

> **What information and capacity were available, what process was recruited, what did the person do, and what changed for whom?**

## Claims from earlier Regulation work requiring re-review

The existing [Regulation and Return · Settled Concepts](regulation-and-return-concepts.md) records important distinctions, but it predates parts of the present grounding architecture. “Settled” describes the decision status in that earlier working session, not permanent scientific authority.

The following need explicit re-review before carrying them into this pass:

- that every Gradient Position should be defined as regulatory in the same sense;
- that Return always travels through Connection A before Baseline X;
- that A requires another nervous system to be physically present;
- that connection-preservation can only complete through another nervous system;
- that isolation necessarily leaves Return incomplete;
- that one route predicts solitary sleep restores less when belonging is unavailable;
- that self-regulation and co-regulation can be assigned categorically by Position;
- that Baseline X is the only or defining location of restoration;
- that any permanent A organisation becomes Chronic by definition.

Some may survive in narrower form. Others may be public teaching propositions, hypotheses or retired architecture. None should be carried forward solely because it was previously labelled settled.

## Evidence architecture for this pass

The Regulation pass will require different evidence for different levels.

| Level | Relevant evidence examples | Cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Physiological** | autonomic, endocrine, immune, metabolic, sleep, pain and recovery measures | personal meaning, ethical fit, whole-person Return or behaviour |
| **Emotional and cognitive** | experience sampling, strategy studies, attention, appraisal, memory, prediction and updating tasks | one hidden function, real-world safety or harm |
| **Behavioural** | observed action, timing, recurrence, alternatives and consequences | private emotional access or motive |
| **Relational** | interaction observation, reports from each person, viable refusal, support, influence and repair | institutional mechanism or one person's entire organisation |
| **Institutional** | rules, incentives, promotion, records, resource flows, sanctions, voice and outcomes | neural state, empathy or emotional awareness of members |
| **Historical and cultural** | archives, material evidence, concepts, practices, institutions and comparative research | one universal psychological mechanism or causal origin |
