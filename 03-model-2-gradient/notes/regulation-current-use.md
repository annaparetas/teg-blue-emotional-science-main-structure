# The Regulation Language Inventory

Scientific-facing subtitle: **Current uses of regulation classified by unit, process, outcome and decision need**

Status: Pass 3A first inventory · 21 August 2026
Scope: current top-level Inner Compass and Gradient pages · F1–F12 reviews · Regulation and Return workbench
Purpose: identify where **regulation** is already precise, where it needs narrowing and where another word would make the process clearer

Companion: [The Regulation Pass](regulation-pass.md)

## How this inventory was made

This first pass searched the current top-level HTML files and the twelve Foundational Framework reviews for **regulate**, **regulated**, **regulating**, **regulation**, **regulatory** and **co-regulation**.

The search found four different kinds of use:

1. a defined biological or psychological process;
2. a TEG-Blue whole-organisation term;
3. a social or institutional process;
4. a broad explanation attached to a behaviour, Position or outcome.

The first three can remain when their level is visible. The fourth usually needs to be decomposed.

## Decision key

| Decision | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Retain** | The unit, process and outcome are sufficiently clear for the present level |
| **Narrow** | The observation is useful, but **regulation** needs a modifier or a named process |
| **Replace** | Another term describes what is happening more accurately |
| **Route** | The claim belongs to a separate evidence or framework question |
| **Re-review** | The wording carries an earlier decision that may not survive the current architecture |

## Uses that can remain

### Specified organism regulation

The governing Gradient premise defines regulation as the ongoing organisation and allocation of biological resources and capacities in relation to current demands. This is the clearest whole-organism use in the current project.

**Decision: retain with a visible level.** Prefer **organism regulation** or **biological regulation** when confusion with emotion regulation, social order or behavioural strategy is possible.

The accompanying statement **regulation does not mean calm** also remains necessary. It prevents activation, mobilisation and protection from being treated as failure merely because they are not relaxed.

### Regulated physiological conditions

The signal and neurochemistry pages use **regulated condition**, **resource regulation**, temperature regulation, cardiorespiratory regulation, food-intake regulation and similar phrases. These uses point to named physiological variables or processes.

**Decision: retain when the variable is named.** “Signalling and regulation” alone may still need a more specific description inside individual entries.

### Within-cell and gene regulation

F10 uses regulation for changes affecting gene activity and possible epigenetic processes. This is a standard molecular-level use.

**Decision: retain.** Keep its level visible so it is never treated as psychological regulation or inherited emotional organisation.

### Emotion-regulation research

The science protocol, F8, F9 and F12 refer to emotion-regulation research or strategies.

**Decision: retain as a research family.** Each applied claim should still name the target, strategy, timing and measured outcome. Emotional granularity, affect labelling or bodily attention must not be called regulation merely because they may sometimes influence emotion.

### Social and institutional regulation as level-specific language

F1 refers to social regulation. F4 examines rules, enforcement and collective organisation. These uses can describe social coordination or conduct-shaping processes when they do not borrow organism-level mechanism.

**Decision: narrow.** Prefer the actual process—caregiving, social buffering, coordination, norm enforcement, sanction, resource distribution or control—whenever it is known.

## Uses that need narrowing or replacement

### “Every Position is regulatory”

The governing premise and Return pages use this sentence to protect the model from a calm-versus-dysregulated hierarchy. That protection matters. The phrase also risks implying that every Gradient Position performs one comparable regulatory task or is fitted to present conditions.

**Decision: re-review.** Preserve **regulation does not mean calm**. Test whether the stronger claim should become:

> Biological regulation continues across every Position, while the organisation's fit, flexibility, cost and responsiveness to current conditions may differ.

This keeps regulation continuous without treating each Position as one scientifically established regulatory programme.

### “Regulatory task” for every Position

The Regulation and Return workbench and Return table assign each Position a regulatory task. Some rows describe resource emphasis or present functional demand. Others infer a purpose from the organisation, including management of people, imposed outcome or preservation of connection.

**Decision: replace or decompose.** Possible column names include:

- **resource-allocation emphasis**;
- **present organising demand**;
- **action availability**;
- **observed behavioural route**;
- **proposed function**, visibly marked as a hypothesis.

No one row should combine all five.

### Self-regulation or co-regulation assigned by Position

The Return page classifies Positions as self-regulation, co-regulation or neither and states that Connection A requires another nervous system present.

**Decision: re-review.** The current architecture should distinguish:

- another person being physically present;
- another person's cues being perceived;
- social buffering or practical support;
- reciprocal coordination;
- interpersonal emotion regulation;
- compliance or imposed influence;
- shared quiet in which active coordination is not required.

A Position alone cannot establish which process is occurring.

### “Another person's regulation has become linked to safety”

The Chronic table uses this phrase to describe connection-preserving patterns. It may point to dependency, expected support, another person's availability, appeasement or learned monitoring.

**Decision: narrow.** Name the observable or proposed route. Another person's “regulation” is not one variable and cannot be inferred from the person's strategy.

### “Substitute regulation”

The Chronic table uses **substitute regulation** when functioning or monitoring continues while an original feeling does not guide updating.

**Decision: replace pending route evidence.** Ask what action occurred, which pressure or condition changed, for how long and at whose cost. **Pressure-reducing route** is safer when the immediate outcome is known; **proposed substitute** should remain a hypothesis when it is not.

### “Regulation + updating” as one stage

The Inner Compass sequence groups regulation and updating after action.

**Decision: replace with named questions.** The stage already asks: Did the action work? Did the condition change? Is the need met? Those questions are clearer than the heading. A later revision might use **Action, feedback and updating**.

### “Dysregulation” as a cumulative state

The Chronic science row uses **multisystem dysregulation** in relation to allostatic-load research. This is recognisable scientific language but may look like a diagnosis of the whole person or Position.

**Decision: narrow.** Name the measured physiological systems, index and population wherever the evidence is discussed. Do not use an allostatic-load composite as a Gradient depth score.

### “Too much empathy” as insufficient regulation

The relational-capacity page says the popular phrase may be better described as affect sharing without enough self–other distinction, mentalizing or regulation.

**Decision: narrow.** The first two distinctions are specific. Replace the last word with the actual process being proposed—attention shift, emotional modulation, boundary use, action inhibition or recovery—when known.

## First replacement vocabulary

| Broad wording | Prefer when this is what is meant |
| --- | --- |
| regulation | organism regulation · emotion regulation · interpersonal influence · social coordination · institutional enforcement |
| regulatory task | resource-allocation emphasis · present demand · action readiness · proposed function |
| regulating through another | seeking support · reassurance seeking · reciprocal coordination · appeasement · demanded compliance |
| substitute regulation | pressure-reducing route · temporary support · avoidance · imposed stability · function unknown |
| regulation completed | condition changed · recruitment reduced · channel recovered · expectation updated · Return became available |
| dysregulated | name the measured variable, impaired process, mismatch, rigidity, cost or recovery difficulty |

## Pass 3A result

The project does not need to remove **regulation**. It needs to stop asking the word to explain several levels at once.

The strongest current uses already name a biological variable, an emotion-regulation process or a social mechanism. The highest-risk uses attach regulation to a Position, visible behaviour, relationship role or institutional outcome and then infer purpose. Those are the first candidates for revision after the definitions are agreed.
