# Regulation and Return · settled concepts

Every concept currently load-bearing in the Return model, with what it is, what
work it does, and whether it was settled in the 12 August 2026 session or
carried from earlier files.

**Function** answers one question: *what distinction collapses if this concept
goes away?* A concept that cannot answer it is not doing work.

Status is one of **carried** (established before this session), **settled**
(decided in it), or **under review** (written down, not accepted).

**Four are confirmed stable enough to describe and teach from:** Fluid Gradient /
Chronic organisation, Resource allocation emphasis, Non-defensive / Defensive organisation,
and Connection-preservation / Organism-protection.

---

## 1 · The structure the map is built from

**Nervous System Organisation** · *carried*
The whole-organism configuration through which the nervous system allocates
energy, attention, perception, feeling, connection and action, in response to
present or learned conditions.
**Function:** makes the unit of analysis a state of organisation rather than a
person. Without it every column reads as a personality type or a diagnosis.

**Position** · *carried*
One named organisation on the Gradient: X, A, A↔B, B, C, D, Z.
**Function:** gives each organisation a fixed address, so the same column can be
carried between files and compared row by row.

**Environmental conditions** · *carried, clarified this session*
What is happening around the person. Not the organisation, and not a reading of
it.
**Function:** the independent side of the map. Keeping conditions and
organisation in separate rows is what lets the Fluid table show the two tracking
each other, and the Chronic table show them coming apart. Collapse them and the
difference between the two tables disappears.
**Clarification:** *Safe to be alone* names a condition in which nothing
requires a response. It does not assert that nobody is present.

**Non-defensive / Defensive organisation** · *carried*
The axis above the Position row. In Fluid, X and A are non-defensive and A↔B, B,
C, D, Z are defensive. In Chronic, all seven are defensive.
**Function:** names whether defence is recruited, which is the first thing to
know about a column.
**Boundary set this session:** non-defensive does not mean unactivated.

**Fluid Gradient / Chronic Survival** · *carried*
Fluid: organisation changes responsively with present conditions. Chronic: an
organisation persists or is reconstructed when the present environment no longer
corresponds to it.
**Function:** separates a response that is tracking reality from one that is not.

**X and Z · why both are off the gradient** · *settled*
The gradient the Environmental conditions row runs is a scale of threat: safe
with others → uncertain → threat → persistent threat → life threat. X and Z are
the two columns that scale does not place, for related but distinct reasons. X
has a named condition that is not a degree of threat — *safe to be alone* is the
absence of demand. Z has no environmental condition named at all, because what
places it is the organism's capacity being exceeded rather than anything outside
it. The science matrix labels that column *capacity exceeded*, which reads the
organism, not the environment, and it was never merged into the conditions row.
**Function:** explains the off-gradient marks as one structural fact rather than
two exceptions.

**Everything else about X and Z is opposite** · *settled*
X restores; Z does not — conservation reduces spending without rebuilding, so it
buys time while the rebuilding waits for capacity to re-enter. X is where Return
completes; Z is not a route to X at all. X's Inner Compass is quiet and readable;
Z's is difficult or impossible to read. X is non-defensive; Z is defensive.

**The two columns observation cannot separate** · *settled*
A person lying still, quiet and not engaging could be in either. This is why two
guardrails exist and point in opposite directions:
- **Non-defensive is not unactivated** — protects X from being read as inert.
- **Low outward activation does not establish low internal cost** — protects Z
  from being read as rest. Already in the science matrix guardrails.

**Function:** together they say activation is not readable from outside and does
not sort the columns, which is why *non-defensive / defensive* remains the only
categorical split the table carries. The collapse to prevent is reading X and Z
as two degrees of one low-activation state. That misreading is how collapse gets
mistaken for relaxation, and how someone in Shutdown is told to rest —
prescribing X's strategy where the problem is absent capacity, not excess demand.
Shutdown is not the quiet end of a scale that starts at rest.

**Chronic organisation develops through several routes** · *revised 19 August 2026*
A pattern may persist within one episode, recover incompletely, be reconstructed
later, acquire a lower recruitment threshold, generalise, become difficult to
discriminate or update, or remain relevant because adverse conditions continue.
Incomplete or interrupted recovery is one possible route, not the definition of
every Chronic pattern.
**Function:** keeps Return connected to Chronic development without turning one
recovery route into a universal cause. It also distinguishes persistence from
reconstruction, recurrence, generalisation and ongoing correspondence.

## 2 · Five words that are not interchangeable

These five get substituted for each other constantly, and each substitution
breaks something.

| Term | What it is | What breaks if it is merged |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Regulation** *(carried)* | The ongoing organisation of energy, attention, perception, action and available capacity. | Every Position is regulatory. Merge it with calm and the map acquires a good end and a bad end. |
| **Return** *(redefined this session)* | Movement toward Baseline X, where restoration becomes available. | Merge it with recovery and the model loses the fact that Return can begin while the body is still paying. |
| **Recovery** *(carried)* | Physiological rebuilding and remaining cost, across different timescales. | Merge it with Return and visible calm starts counting as completion. |
| **Restoration** *(carried)* | The energy strategy of Baseline X — rebuilding rather than spending. | Merge it with Return and X stops being a destination and becomes a mood. |
| **Repair** *(carried)* | Impact, accountability, agency and possible relational rebuilding after rupture. | Merge it with Return and the person who caused harm reaching baseline starts counting as the harm being addressed. |

## 3 · Energy and activation

**Resource allocation emphasis** · *revised 19 August 2026*
What the organism is primarily using available resources to do in each Position:
Restoration (X), Relational coordination (A), Preparation and checking (A↔B),
Protective mobilisation (B), Strategic mobilisation (C), Power mobilisation (D)
and Protective conservation (Z).
**Function:** distinguishes the functional demand of each organisation without
treating energy as one directly observable pool moving between Positions.

**Relational coordination** · *accepted 19 August 2026*
At A, resources support reciprocal attention, communication, shared activity and
adjustment. Defensive demand may be lower without implying that connection has
zero cost or stores one quantity of energy.
**Function:** preserves A's relational function without assigning it an
unsupported accumulation mechanism.

**Activation** · *settled*
A is an activation. Coordination raises arousal even when nothing defensive is
running.
**Function:** this is why A must end. It resolves what looks like a
contradiction — A accumulates energy *and* has to be left — because full
restoration requires the activation to come down, and that is X.
**Research axis:** arousal, on the circumplex model. Contentment is low-arousal
positive; love and gratitude are high-arousal positive. Same valence, different
activation.

**Coordination, restoration and recovery are different questions** · *revised 19 August 2026*
Supportive connection may reduce some forms of defensive demand and can aid
recovery. Relational coordination also uses attention and physiological resources.
Restoration may become available across supportive conditions and is represented
most explicitly at X; it is not claimed to occur only there.
**Function:** prevents social connection, metabolic storage, restoration and
subjective vitality from being treated as one process.

## 4 · The route to Baseline

**Return, defined by destination** · *settled — replaces the earlier definition*
Return is movement toward Baseline X. It is no longer defined as movement toward
non-defensive organisation.
**Function:** the earlier definition excluded A, which is already non-defensive
and still has to return. Defining Return by where it arrives covers every
organisation that has to get there.

**Two entry classes** · *settled*
From A↔B, B, C, D, Z, protective organisation must first be relinquished. From
A, nothing defensive is relinquished and the coordination demand ends.
**Function:** keeps the true part of the old model — nothing defensive is being
given up at A — while still making A→X a Return.

**A is both gate and departure point** · *settled*
Other organisations reach X through A. A also departs for X on its own account.
**Function:** A is the only Position that appears twice in the route. Treating it
only as a gate is what hid the fact that it has to return as well.

**Z is not a route** · *carried*
Capacity and orientation must re-enter before A is reachable at all.
**Function:** stops the lowest-output column being read as the end of the
journey, or as rest.

**X is the arrival** · *settled*
X answers a different question from every other column: it is where Return
completes, not something that returns.

**A permanent A becomes chronic** · *settled*
**Function:** this is what makes A's return necessary rather than optional. An
organisation that never completes is the definition of chronic.

## 5 · Connection

**A requires another nervous system present** · *settled*
A cannot be entered alone.
**Function:** makes A the one organisation on the map with an external
precondition, and makes co-regulation structural rather than optional.

**Self-regulation / co-regulation** · *carried terminology, applied this session*
Whether another nervous system participates. X, B, C and D are self; A is
co-regulation; A↔B is neither yet — cues are being read, not shared; Z is
neither, for lack of capacity.
**Function:** the commonly used name for the question, so the claim stays
checkable against a literature.

**Belonging is the evidence** · *settled — the mechanism*
*Safe to be alone* is a conclusion the organism draws, and it cannot be drawn
from being alone. A is where belonging is established, so A is the evidence that
solitude is safe. Solitude without established belonging is not baseline; it is
exposure.
**Function:** this is the reason the route runs through A. Without it the A-gate
is an assertion; with it, it is a mechanism that predicts things.
**Root:** a special case of *each survival problem is a question, and the
question fixes who can answer it* — section 6.

**Two restorative mechanisms** · *settled*
X restores by removing demand — sleep, tissue maintenance, digestion. A restores
by lowering cost — social buffering reduces stress-axis output, and
bonding-related opioid and oxytocinergic participation has its own effects.
**Function:** stops A being modelled as pure expenditure, without merging it into
X. Two mechanisms, not one restorative Position and six spenders.

**Shared baseline** · *settled*
Baseline does not require solitude. With deeply trusted people it can be shared —
two people quiet in the same room, or asleep beside each other, with nobody
coordinating.
**Function:** what has to end at A is the coordination demand, not the company.

**Isolation leaves Return incomplete** · *settled, under review as a claim*
Not merely slower. A person with no available connection can end a defensive
configuration without reaching X.
**Function:** converts a correlation everyone already accepts — isolation harms
people — into a mechanism with predictions. Its prediction: solitary sleep
restores less when belonging is unavailable.

## 6 · Survival problem

**Survival problem** · *carried from the emotion tags, elevated to a row this session*
What is at stake and could be lost. Two families: **organism-protection** and
**connection-preservation**, with a tension form,
`connection-preservation ↔ organism-protection`.
**Function:** names *which* problem defence is answering, which is where A↔B and
B differ. The axis row only says whether defence is recruited.

**It names what is at risk, not what is being secured** · *settled*
**Function:** this is why Fluid X and A carry none — at A connection is
available, so nothing is at risk. Read the other way, A becomes
indistinguishable from A↔B, where the tag exists precisely because connection has
become uncertain.

**Each survival problem is a question, and the question fixes who can answer it**
· *settled — the reason under the pathway rule*

- **Organism-protection asks *am I safe?*** The environment answers it. The
  organism can verify it by perceiving: the threat is gone, the exit worked, the
  body is intact. No other nervous system's response is required as evidence.
- **Connection-preservation asks *do I belong?*** Only another nervous system's
  response constitutes an answer. Belonging cannot be verified by looking around,
  and it cannot be reasoned into place alone.

**Function:** this is the reason the pathway rule holds, rather than a
restatement of it. It derives from one principle four things that were settled
separately — why A requires another nervous system present, why isolation leaves
Return incomplete, why A↔B checks, and why solitary calming cannot complete a
relational condition.

**Research anchors.** Baumeister & Leary's belongingness hypothesis requires
*reciprocated* care rather than mere contact: proximity does not answer the
question. Ostracism research (Williams — already cited in the Chronic A grounding
row) finds exclusion resists cognitive reappraisal, so knowing the exclusion is
trivial or computerised does not remove the effect, which is direct evidence that
the question cannot be answered internally. Dickerson & Kemeny's meta-analysis
finds social-evaluative stressors produce larger cortisol responses and slower
recovery than non-social ones. Unverified except Williams.

**The survival problem determines the completion pathway** · *settled*
This is the rule that links three concepts, and it is the same rule the Gradient
table already states for pathways: *the pathway is determined by what the signal
concerns.*

- **Organism-protection** can complete without another nervous system — through
  environmental change, one's own action, or physiological recovery. Another
  person may help or hinder; the problem does not require one.
- **Connection-preservation** can only complete through another nervous system.
  No amount of self-regulation supplies recognition, reciprocity or repair.

**Function:** ties *which problem is active* to *which pathway can resolve it* to
*whose nervous system is involved*. Without the link, co-regulation looks like a
preference or a resource, when for one of the two problems it is the only route
that closes.

**Note the wording.** The contrast is self-regulation against co-regulation, not
regulation against co-regulation. Regulation is the encompassing process, so
co-regulation is regulation too.

**Why A↔B checks** · *settled — derived from the rule above*
At A↔B both survival problems are active at once, and they point to different
pathways: one that requires another nervous system and one that does not. The
checking is the unresolved question of which route applies, which is why A↔B is
neither self-regulation nor co-regulation yet — another person's cues are being
read, not shared.

**Where connection-preservation belongs** · *settled*
Fluid A↔B, as one side of the tension. Chronic A, as the standing organisation.
**Function:** the row makes the difference between the two tables visible in one
line. In Fluid, X and A are free of survival work; in Chronic they are not.

## 7 · Terms deliberately not used

| Term | Why not |
| --- | --- |
| **Regulated** (as a state of a person) | In popular use it means calm. Every Position is regulatory, so the adjective undoes the definition of regulation in one word. |
| **Deactivation** | Sounds like settling. In attachment research it names an avoidant strategy, which collides with Chronic A. |
| **Discharge**, for A | Belongs to B, C and D, where energy was mobilised and may not have been used. At A nothing was mobilised for defence; what ends is spending on coordination. |
| **Window of tolerance** | Widely used, thinly evidenced. If it appears it carries an evidence-status marker rather than structural weight. |
| **Dorsal / ventral vagal**, as mechanism | Stays an *also known as* alias, because the science matrix holds that dorsal vagal cannot define Shutdown. |
| **Baseline point** | Imports a homeostatic set point. X is defined by conditions and a resource-allocation emphasis, not by a value being corrected toward. Baseline is *re-accessed*, not returned to a set point. |
