# Pilot 05 · Mentalizing, Affective Sharing and Instrumental Use

Status: first claim-level evidence pass · PEND-044 cluster 1
Scope: configurations where mentalizing is in an upper band and affective sharing is in a lower band · empathy asymmetry · psychopathy · Machiavellianism · narcissistic functioning · manipulation
Purpose: test when another person can become cognitively readable without becoming affectively shared, and what further conditions are needed before that asymmetry can support manipulation or harm

## What the research lets us see

Several processes are often placed under the single word **empathy**:

1. representing what another person may think, feel, intend or expect;
2. representing those states accurately rather than merely forming an interpretation;
3. sharing or resonating with part of another person's affective state;
4. feeling concern for that person's welfare;
5. allowing what is known or felt to constrain action;
6. using interpersonal information to produce a preferred outcome.

They can interact, but they are not interchangeable. Someone may infer what another person fears without sharing the fear. Someone may share distress without understanding its cause. Someone may understand and feel another person's state but still privilege entitlement, retaliation or personal gain. Someone may also behave safely through principle, restraint or accountability while affective resonance is low.

This gives TEG-Blue a grounded version of its central observation: **another person can become readable without their experience becoming a felt limit on what happens next**. The dangerous configuration does not arise from mentalizing alone. It arises when usable interpersonal information is recruited towards exploitation, control or harm and is not sufficiently constrained by concern, principle, feedback, accountability or consequences.

## Six-part decomposition

| Component | Working definition | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mental-state representation** | forming an account of what another person may think, feel, know, intend or expect | accuracy, affective sharing, concern or ethical use |
| **Interpersonal accuracy** | correspondence between an inference and the other person's actual or subsequently verified state | care, restraint or broad skill across contexts |
| **Affective sharing** | some degree of vicarious or resonant affective response to another person's state | self–other distinction, concern, helping or proportional action |
| **Empathic concern** | other-oriented concern for another person's welfare | accurate interpretation, effective help or fairness across people |
| **Instrumental use** | recruiting information about another person or their option field to obtain a preferred outcome | coercion or harm unless behaviour, power and impact show it |
| **Harmful action** | observable behaviour that restricts, exploits, deceives, injures or repeatedly overrides another person | one diagnosis, one capacity configuration or one developmental route |

The separation between affective sharing and empathic concern is especially important. Feeling with another person and caring what happens to them can overlap, but neither can be inferred safely from the other.

## The correction to “all manipulative people”

The original observation contains something valuable: manipulation cannot work without information about the other person or the conditions shaping their choices.

The scientifically safer formulation is:

> **Manipulative behaviour uses information about another person's likely responses, needs, vulnerabilities or available options to produce an outcome that serves the actor. That information may come from accurate mentalizing, partial social inference, learned scripts, repeated trial and error, role knowledge, surveillance, deception or direct control of the option field.**

This preserves the relational mechanism without implying that every person who manipulates has high cognitive empathy. The Machiavellianism literature is a particularly useful correction: strategic manipulation is part of the construct, yet aggregate evidence does not show superior cognitive-empathic ability.

## When the asymmetry may become dangerous

```text
usable information about another person
        ×
low or selectively disengaged affective sharing / concern
        ×
instrumental, entitled, retaliatory or dominance-oriented goals
        ×
access to power, dependency or restricted alternatives
        ×
repeated harmful action despite feedback and consequence
        =
elevated capacity for calculated interpersonal harm
```

This is an explanatory conjunction, not a validated risk equation. Each line belongs to a different part of the architecture:

- the first two lines describe capacity and capacity quality;
- F2 investigates supported developmental routes;
- Chronic describes recurrence, rigidity and difficult updating;
- F3 owns goals, justification, selective recruitment and response to feedback;
- HPR owns observable action, power, impact, accountability and repair.

The configuration must never carry the behavioural verdict by itself.

## Research-family findings

### Psychopathy and callous-unemotional traits

The strongest aggregate evidence does not support the clean popular picture of intact cognitive empathy beside absent affective empathy. Psychopathic traits show larger average deficits in affective empathy, but smaller cognitive deficits are also present. The size and direction vary across callous-affective, interpersonal, boldness and antisocial dimensions and across measures.

Developmental work on callous-unemotional traits identifies reduced emotional responsivity and affiliative-reward or threat-sensitivity hypotheses. These are candidate pathways, not evidence that a child never learned connection, that adversity is always present or that one adult outcome follows.

### Machiavellianism

Machiavellianism is relevant because its definition includes strategic and manipulative interpersonal orientation. Yet the meta-analysis reviewed here found low affective empathy without evidence of generally superior cognitive-empathic skill. This shows why **instrumental use** and **mentalizing ability** must remain separate components.

A person may manipulate through narrow but effective knowledge: which threat produces compliance, which ambiguity preserves deniability, which dependency limits refusal or which reward keeps someone engaged. Broad understanding of the other person's mind is not required.

### Narcissistic functioning

Clinical and experimental studies offer the clearest evidence for variable recruitment. One clinical study found impaired emotional empathy but no group-level cognitive-empathy deficit in people diagnosed with NPD. Experimental work found that perspective-taking instructions altered self-reported and physiological responses among participants higher in maladaptive narcissistic traits.

This does not mean that all narcissistic people possess accurate mentalizing and choose not to care. It suggests that empathic functioning can be multidimensional, motivationally influenced and context-dependent. F3 can later examine entitlement, self-protection, status threat, justification and selective disengagement without treating them as consequences of one slider position.

### Bullying, coercion and learned interpersonal prediction

Research on ringleader bullying provides a bounded example of harmful conduct accompanied by usable social cognition. It does not establish that all bullies are skilled mind-readers or that social cognition caused the bullying. It does show that interventions based only on teaching perspective-taking can miss cases where the capacity is already available and the problem lies in goals, group reward, concern, power or behavioural consequence.

## F2 developmental hypotheses to test later

The framework may examine whether some asymmetries develop through:

- low affiliative reward or reduced sensitivity to another person's distress;
- caregiving where safety depends on predicting people rather than mutually sharing experience;
- punishment, ridicule or exploitation of vulnerability;
- modelling and reinforcement of dominance, entitlement or emotional dismissal;
- repeated need for strategic monitoring under danger;
- emotional neglect, invalidation or limited opportunities for reciprocal emotional learning;
- neurodevelopmental, sensory, health and disability-related variation;
- selective disengagement that develops later rather than an early learning absence.

None is yet attached causally to configuration `001` or `101`. Similar adult patterns can emerge through different routes, and similar histories can lead to very different adult conduct.

## F3 and HPR handoff

F3 should distinguish:

- what outcome the person is trying to obtain;
- whether another person's state is treated as information, relationship or obstacle;
- whether concern is absent, overridden, compartmentalised or selectively recruited;
- whether entitlement or grievance licenses harmful action;
- whether feedback changes the interpretation or triggers retaliation;
- whether social understanding is used for repair, concealment, leverage or control.

HPR should then record:

- the behaviour that occurred;
- the power and dependency conditions around it;
- whether options, information or reality-testing were restricted;
- impact on the other person;
- recurrence and escalation;
- accountability, cessation, restitution and repair.

Danger becomes visible here, in the conjunction of capacity, use, power and repeated conduct—not in a diagnostic label or capacity configuration alone.

## Terminology decisions

### Keep “mentalizing access and use”

The phrase is more accurate than “cognitive empathy” as the card's governing educational term because cognitive-empathy measures mix self-perceived tendency, emotion recognition, theory of mind and performance. Its boundary should be explicit:

> **Mentalizing access and use** describes how much a person can form, check and revise representations of another person's perspective or mental state in the present context. Availability does not establish accuracy, affective sharing, concern or ethical use.

### Keep “affective-sharing access”

The term makes the configuration visible without claiming that affective sharing is identical to empathy as a whole:

> **Affective-sharing access** describes how much of another person's affective state can arise within ME while self–other distinction is preserved. It does not by itself establish concern, helping, fairness, restraint or correct interpretation.

### Add “empathic concern” as a separate research variable

Empathic concern should not become a fourth slider yet. It belongs first in the capacity-quality and F3/HPR bridge because concern may be present, absent, selective, overridden or behaviourally ineffective. Evidence should determine whether it later requires a distinct visual control.

## What this permits TEG-Blue to say

TEG-Blue can say that understanding another person's mind and affectively sharing their experience are partly separable. It can say that usable social understanding does not guarantee concern, restraint or safe conduct. It can also say that this asymmetry may increase the capacity for calculated harm when it is repeatedly organised around exploitation, entitlement, retaliation or control.

It should not say that mentalizing without affective sharing is inherently dangerous, that all manipulation reflects high cognitive empathy, that psychopathy preserves cognitive empathy uniformly, or that one developmental history creates a harmful adult personality.

## Evidence status and next handoff

This first pass establishes 15 bounded claims and 11 canonical source records. It is sufficient to govern terminology and the research architecture. It is not sufficient to implement a diagnosis-like interpretation, dangerousness output or fixed personality card.

The next PEND-044 cluster should migrate and reassess the old-engine evidence on **entitlement, threatened self-concept, narcissistic antagonism, power and retaliation**. That cluster will clarify the F3 and HPR conditions that can turn interpersonal information into organised domination.
