# Pilot 04 · ESS Condition-Reading and Emotional Significance

Status: first claim-level evidence pass · terminology recommendation ready for review
Scope: ESS-01 · ESS-02 · IC-01 Conditions · IC-02 Detection and transmission · IC-04 ESS · IC-06 Body Signals · IC-07 Situation Signals
Purpose: distinguish how conditions become available and selected from how they acquire organism-relative emotional significance

## The central distinction

An organism does not respond to every feature available at once. Information is detected through limited sensory and interoceptive routes, selected under present demands and shaped by attention, learning, prediction and action possibilities. TEG-Blue groups this first family of processes under **condition-reading**.

Some selected information then matters in relation to the organism: it may concern safety, harm, need, connection, loss, attraction, a boundary or an available response. TEG-Blue groups this second family under **emotional significance**.

The distinction is functional, not anatomical. The processes can overlap, recur and influence each other. “First” and “then” describe an explanatory order, not a universal linear sequence in the nervous system.

## The handoff between ESS-01 and ESS-02

```text
conditions and bodily changes
        ↓
partial availability through sensing and representation
        ↕
selection, attention, prediction and learned weighting
        ↓
organism-relative relevance and valuation
        ↕
bodily organisation, action readiness and felt experience
        ↓
possible conscious recognition, interpretation and naming
```

The arrows are reciprocal. Emotional significance can redirect attention and change what is sampled next. Bodily organisation can alter later perception. Naming can change subsequent appraisal. This is a recurring loop, not a one-way pipeline.

## Where the research traditions agree

- emotion is a process with distinguishable components, not merely a finished category label;
- information is processed selectively and in relation to the organism's state, concerns or possible action;
- bodily regulation, attention, learning, memory and action participate in emotional episodes;
- conscious feeling and verbal naming are not identical to early detection or relevance processing;
- context and prior experience matter;
- a bodily or neural response does not by itself reveal one specific emotion, cause or correct action.

## Where they disagree

| Question | Main disagreement | TEG-Blue decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is appraisal the cause of emotion or one component within a wider construction? | Appraisal and construction accounts assign different causal and conceptual roles. | Use **appraisal** only for bounded evaluative processes; do not make one appraisal theory the ESS mechanism. |
| Is perception direct or predictively constructed? | Ecological and predictive accounts begin from different assumptions about representation. | Condition-reading can include organism–environment information and prediction without claiming that one theory has settled perception. |
| Are emotional stimuli processed automatically? | Findings vary with attention, task load, relevance and individual differences. | Use **rapid**, **prioritised** or **outside current report** when supported; avoid **automatic** as a blanket claim. |
| Do emotion categories have dedicated neural or autonomic signatures? | Locationist, basic-emotion and construction accounts interpret variable evidence differently. | Do not map one category to one region, circuit, chemical or fixed autonomic fingerprint. |
| Does prediction dominate incoming information? | Predictive accounts differ in scope and empirical specification. | Treat prediction as a candidate contributor to selection and meaning, not as a complete explanation. |

## Terminology assessment

### Retain “condition-reading” as an educational term

It captures something that “detection” alone misses: organisms respond to relations among bodily condition, surroundings, prior learning and possible action. Its boundary must remain visible:

> **Condition-reading** is TEG-Blue's educational term for the selective processes through which internal, external, relational, remembered and anticipated conditions become available and relevant to the organism. It is neither a passive recording of reality nor a single biological mechanism.

Scientific-facing companion terms: **sensing**, **perceptual selection**, **interoceptive processing**, **attention**, **relevance processing**, **prediction**, **attunement** and **perception–action coupling**, chosen according to the exact claim.

### Retain “emotional significance” with a narrower definition

The term helps name the part the framework cannot afford to lose: conditions do not only become represented; they come to matter. The safer definition is:

> **Emotional significance** is TEG-Blue's educational term for the organism-relative importance that information may acquire in relation to concerns, needs, viability, connection, boundaries, learned outcomes and possible action. It may contribute to emotion without constituting a complete emotion by itself.

Scientific-facing companion terms: **relevance**, **appraisal**, **affective valuation**, **motivational significance**, **core affect** and **action readiness**, without treating them as interchangeable.

## Recommended component revisions

### ESS-01 · Condition-reading

- **Definition:** the ESS grouping for the selective processes through which internal, external, relational, remembered and anticipated conditions become available and relevant to the organism.
- **Phenomenon:** a person may orient, brace, soften, withdraw or mobilise before they can report which information received priority.
- **Mechanism:** sensory and interoceptive processing, attention, salience, memory, prediction, appraisal and perception–action processes may contribute; no single mechanism is the ESS component.
- **Development:** maturation, health, disability, caregiving, culture, repeated exposure and learning may alter sensitivity, attention, expectation and calibration.
- **Application:** asks what present or represented conditions the person may be responding to before treating the response as irrational or self-explanatory.
- **Boundary:** condition-reading is partial, selective and revisable. It is not passive access to objective reality, proof of danger or evidence of conscious recognition.

### ESS-02 · Emotional significance

- **Definition:** the ESS grouping for how information may come to matter in relation to concerns, needs, viability, safety, harm, connection, loss, attraction, boundaries, repair and possible action.
- **Phenomenon:** something may feel urgent, painful, welcome or important before the person has a complete account of why.
- **Mechanism:** relevance appraisal, affective valuation, bodily regulation, learning, memory, prediction, motivational processes and conceptual knowledge may contribute.
- **Development:** significance may change through maturation, language, culture, attachment, socialisation, repeated outcomes and changing material conditions.
- **Application:** treats emotion as consequential information to examine, neither noise to suppress nor an instruction that must be obeyed.
- **Boundary:** emotional significance is not objective importance, proof of another person's intent or a finished emotion category. It may concern present conditions, earlier learning, prediction or several sources together.

## What this permits TEG-Blue to say

TEG-Blue can say that emotional-somatic processes may organise attention, bodily readiness and behaviour before a person can explain them. It can also say that ignoring these processes does not remove their influence.

It should not say that emotion acts alone, that people lose free will in a literal all-or-none sense, that every bodily response reveals truth, or that conscious reasoning is separate from biology. The stronger scientific formulation is that conscious participation can become narrower when influential emotional-somatic information remains unavailable, unnamed or unexamined.

## Evidence status and next handoff

This pass establishes a bounded conceptual foundation and eight canonical source records. It is not yet implementation-ready for broad public claims because developmental, cultural and trauma-specific evidence has not been critically reviewed here.

The next ESS handoff is **ESS-04 learned weighting**: how repeated outcomes alter what receives priority and how present significance may become generalised, context-bound or difficult to update. That work should connect to the Chronic scientific spine rather than develop as a separate branch.
