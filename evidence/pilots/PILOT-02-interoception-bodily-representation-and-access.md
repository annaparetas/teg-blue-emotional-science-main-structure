# Pilot 02 · Interoception, Bodily Representation and Conscious Access

Status: first claim-level evidence pass
Scope: IC-02 Detection and transmission · IC-04 ESS · IC-06 Body Signals · IC-08 ME · IC-09 Conscious Participation · RC-01 Bodily information access and use · F2 layers 1–4
Purpose: distinguish bodily sensing, nervous-system processing, subjective access, attention, report, accuracy and interpretation

## Why these distinctions matter

“The body sends a signal” can describe several different events. A physiological variable may change. Receptors and afferent pathways may detect some aspect of that change. The nervous system may integrate it with other information. Attention may or may not turn towards it. A person may feel something without identifying it, identify it without interpreting its cause accurately, or report confidence that does not match performance on a task.

F2 and the Inner Compass become more grounded when these steps remain separate.

## Process decomposition

| Process | Question | Example of evidence | What it does not answer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Bodily condition** | What is changing in the organism? | Heart rate, blood gases, gastric distension, inflammation, temperature | Whether the change is detected or consciously felt |
| **Sensing and transmission** | Which receptors and pathways carry information? | Vagal, spinal, humoral and other pathways | What the person experiences or believes |
| **Integration and representation** | How is bodily information combined and modelled? | Neural and non-neural processing across distributed circuits | One-to-one localisation of a feeling or self |
| **Attention** | Is processing directed towards or away from the bodily channel? | Task instructions, attentional effects | Objective accuracy or useful interpretation |
| **Subjective access** | Does some bodily change become felt? | Momentary report under defined conditions | Identification, cause or factual accuracy |
| **Identification** | Can the experience be differentiated and named? | Emotion vocabulary, labelling and discrimination measures | Whether the named interpretation is correct |
| **Metacognitive correspondence** | Does confidence track measured task performance? | Trial-level confidence and performance | General self-trust or truth about life history |
| **Interpretation and action** | What is the sensation taken to mean, and what follows? | Contextual appraisal, help-seeking, avoidance, regulation | That the first meaning is infallible or merely mistaken |

### IBA-S01 · Khalsa et al. 2018 · Interoception and mental health roadmap

- **Citation:** Khalsa, S. S., et al. (2018). *Interoception and mental health: A roadmap*. Biological Psychiatry: Cognitive Neuroscience and Neuroimaging, 3(6), 501–513. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bpsc.2017.12.004
- **Source type:** expert-consensus review and research roadmap.
- **Level:** definitions, measurement, mechanisms and clinical research across interoceptive domains.
- **Useful result:** treats interoception as a broad family of nervous-system processes and identifies major gaps in measurement and causal inference.
- **Claim links:** IBA-01 through IBA-06.
- **Cannot establish:** ESS, Body Signals, a universal interoceptive deficit or one neural location for bodily awareness.
- **Decision:** **supports the decomposition and research boundaries**.

### IBA-S02 · Chen et al. 2021 · Emerging science of interoception

- **Citation:** Chen, W. G., et al. (2021). *The emerging science of interoception: Sensing, integrating, interpreting, and regulating signals within the self*. Trends in Neurosciences, 44(1), 3–16. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tins.2020.10.007
- **Source type:** interdisciplinary scientific review.
- **Level:** neural and non-neural pathways, definitions, function, disease, intervention and computational modelling.
- **Useful result:** separates sensing, integration, interpretation and regulation while emphasising bidirectional brain–body communication.
- **Claim links:** IBA-01, IBA-03, IBA-05 and IBA-06.
- **Cannot establish:** a one-way body-to-brain signal sequence or that all internal regulation becomes conscious.
- **Decision:** **supports with a bidirectionality correction**.

### IBA-S03 · Murphy, Catmur and Bird 2019 · Classifying individual differences

- **Citation:** Murphy, J., Catmur, C., & Bird, G. (2019). *Classifying individual differences in interoception: Implications for the measurement of interoceptive awareness*. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 26(5), 1467–1471. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-019-01632-7
- **Source type:** theoretical and measurement review.
- **Level:** behavioural measurement and individual differences.
- **Useful result:** distinguishes what is measured—accuracy or attention—from how it is measured—performance or self-report—and revises simpler three-part taxonomies.
- **Claim links:** IBA-03 and IBA-04.
- **Cannot establish:** one stable trait called interoceptive awareness or direct correspondence between a questionnaire and biological accuracy.
- **Decision:** **challenges broad use of “interoceptive awareness”**.

### IBA-S04 · Berntson and Khalsa 2021 · Neural circuits of interoception

- **Citation:** Berntson, G. G., & Khalsa, S. S. (2021). *Neural circuits of interoception*. Trends in Neurosciences, 44(1), 17–28. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tins.2020.09.011
- **Source type:** mechanistic neuroscience review.
- **Level:** comparative and mammalian sensory circuits.
- **Useful result:** interoceptive processing often remains outside conscious awareness, and conscious access is selective rather than a complete internal readout.
- **Claim links:** IBA-01 through IBA-03.
- **Cannot establish:** first-person meaning, emotion identity, developmental history or a complete human Inner Compass route.
- **Decision:** **supports non-conscious processing and selective access**.

### IBA-S05 · Leech, Stapleton and Patching 2024 · PTSD and interoception scoping review

- **Citation:** Leech, K., Stapleton, P., & Patching, A. (2024). *A roadmap to understanding interoceptive awareness and post-traumatic stress disorder: a scoping review*. Frontiers in Psychiatry, 15, 1355442. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2024.1355442
- **Source type:** scoping review; search completed January 2022.
- **Level:** clinical PTSD and interoception literature.
- **Useful result:** found extensive inconsistency in terminology and measurement, with several different constructs treated as synonyms.
- **Claim links:** IBA-04 and IBA-07.
- **Cannot establish:** a single PTSD-specific interoceptive mechanism, causal direction, or an individual trauma history from present bodily access.
- **Decision:** **maps an emerging field and limits stronger claims**.

## Claim decisions affecting current language

### Decision 1 · Keep Body Signals broader than interoception

Retain:

> Body Signals are TEG-Blue’s functional grouping for information concerning bodily condition, maintenance, viable limits, need, effort, injury and recovery.

Add when needed:

> Interoception is one major scientific territory contributing to this grouping. Body Signals are not a scientific synonym for interoception.

### Decision 2 · Replace “signal reaches consciousness” with a decomposed route

Prefer:

> Bodily change may be sensed and processed without becoming clearly felt. When some information becomes subjectively available, attention, differentiation, concepts, language and context may affect what the person can identify and report.

This protects the F2 distinction between signal generation, bodily representation, access and identification.

### Decision 3 · Separate access from accuracy

A person may:

- notice a sensation accurately but misinterpret its cause;
- attend strongly to one bodily channel while missing another;
- report low confidence despite good performance on a defined task;
- feel distress clearly without being able to differentiate or name it;
- identify an emotion while still lacking a viable action.

“Connected to the body” is therefore too broad for scientific use unless the relevant dimension is named.

### Decision 4 · Preserve the signal–interpretation distinction in both directions

An interpretation can overgeneralise from earlier learning. It can also recognise real present danger. Grounded language must not assume that bodily activation is either an infallible truth or an error to be corrected.

### Decision 5 · Do not use insula shorthand

Avoid formulations such as “the insula creates bodily awareness” or “trauma dysregulates the insula”. Interoceptive representation depends on distributed, bidirectional neural and bodily processes, and region-level associations do not establish one psychological function or developmental cause.

## What this pilot changes

The current IC-06 Body Signals definition and boundary are already well aligned with the evidence. The most significant improvement belongs in F2 and RC-01: “interoceptive access” should become a family of measurable questions rather than one capacity that is simply high or low.

Recommended dimensions are:

1. bodily channel and condition;
2. detection or discrimination performance;
3. attention;
4. subjective intensity or clarity;
5. confidence and metacognitive correspondence;
6. differentiation and naming;
7. interpretation in context;
8. ability to use the information for action.
