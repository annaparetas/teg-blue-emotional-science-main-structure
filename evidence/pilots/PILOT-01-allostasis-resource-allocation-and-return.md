# Pilot 01 · Allostasis, Resource Allocation and Return

Status: first claim-level evidence pilot
Scope: IC-03 Biological organisation · IC-10 Updating · IC-11 Return · NG-01 Fluid organisation · NG-02 Chronic organisation · NG-04 Fluid–Chronic depth · NG-05 Baseline
Purpose: test the source-record method on one scientific bridge that affects the Inner Compass, Fluid, Chronic and the Return model

Implementation companion: [Signal Recruitment, Persistence and Recovery](../../01-signal-map/grounding/recruitment-persistence-and-recovery.html)

## Why this pilot comes first

Allostasis appears to fit much of the Gradient because it concerns changing regulation under changing demand. That breadth is also the risk. If it is allowed to explain sensing, emotion, action, persistence, recovery and health cost at once, it stops telling us which part of the process has evidence.

This pilot separates five claims:

1. organisms regulate viable conditions through changing activity rather than one fixed setting;
2. present demands can alter coordination across several physiological systems;
3. these systems respond and recover on different timescales;
4. repeated or prolonged demands can carry multisystem cost;
5. TEG-Blue’s Return construct integrates several recovery processes but is not identical to allostasis or allostatic load.

## Linked source records

The [Shared Source Record Index](../sources/shared-source-record-index.md) is canonical. The records below are retained temporarily as pilot snapshots and must not be updated independently.

### ALR-S01 · McEwen 1998 · Stress, adaptation and disease

- **Citation:** McEwen, B. S. (1998). *Stress, adaptation, and disease: Allostasis and allostatic load*. Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences, 840, 33–44. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1749-6632.1998.tb09546.x
- **Source type:** theoretical and narrative review.
- **Population / level:** multisystem physiological and health research; integrates human and animal work.
- **Phenomenon studied:** adaptive activation of neural, neuroendocrine and immune mechanisms; possible cost when mediators are repeatedly activated or poorly regulated.
- **Useful result:** provides the influential distinction between adaptive allostasis and allostatic load as the possible cost of adaptation.
- **Claim links:** ALR-01, ALR-02 and ALR-05.
- **Cannot establish:** TEG-Blue Positions, one energy strategy per Position, a universal sequence of Return, or a measure of Chronic depth.
- **Decision:** **supports and contextualises**.

### ALR-S02 · McEwen 1998 · Protective and damaging effects of stress mediators

- **Citation:** McEwen, B. S. (1998). *Protective and damaging effects of stress mediators*. New England Journal of Medicine, 338, 171–179. https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199801153380307
- **Source type:** clinical-scientific review.
- **Population / level:** human health and multisystem physiology, drawing on broader experimental research.
- **Phenomenon studied:** the same stress mediators can contribute to adaptive response and, under some patterns of activation, physiological damage.
- **Useful result:** supports separating short-term function from longer-term cost.
- **Claim links:** ALR-02 and ALR-05.
- **Cannot establish:** that activation is harmful by itself, that cortisol is a stress meter, or that every prolonged response produces damage.
- **Decision:** **supports with boundary**.

### ALR-S03 · Ramsay and Woods 2014 · Homeostasis and allostasis

- **Citation:** Ramsay, D. S., & Woods, S. C. (2014). *Clarifying the roles of homeostasis and allostasis in physiological regulation*. Psychological Review, 121(2), 225–247. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0035942
- **Source type:** theoretical review and conceptual clarification.
- **Population / level:** physiological regulation across systems and experimental traditions.
- **Phenomenon studied:** differences and overlap between homeostatic and allostatic accounts.
- **Useful result:** shows that “homeostasis reacts; allostasis predicts” is too simple and that definitions vary across authors.
- **Claim links:** ALR-01 and ALR-03.
- **Cannot establish:** one settled regulatory theory or a clean division between reactive Body Signals and predictive Situation Signals.
- **Decision:** **challenges oversimplification**.

### ALR-S04 · Herman et al. 2016 · HPA response regulation

- **Citation:** Herman, J. P., McKlveen, J. M., Ghosal, S., Kopp, B., Wulsin, A., Makinson, R., Scheimann, J., & Myers, B. (2016). *Regulation of the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenocortical stress response*. Comprehensive Physiology, 6(2), 603–621. https://doi.org/10.1002/cphy.c150015
- **Source type:** mechanistic review.
- **Population / level:** primarily animal neuroendocrine evidence with implications for mammalian stress physiology.
- **Phenomenon studied:** initiation, feedback and recovery of HPA-axis responses across stressor types.
- **Useful result:** glucocorticoid responses are delayed and longer-lasting relative to some faster response channels; response time courses depend on active feedback and passive clearance.
- **Claim links:** ALR-02 and ALR-04.
- **Cannot establish:** the subjective completion of a human event, relational Return, or a Gradient Position from cortisol.
- **Decision:** **supports a multi-timescale account**.

### ALR-S05 · Mauss et al. 2015 · Allostatic-load measurement review

- **Citation:** Mauss, D., Li, J., Schmidt, B., Angerer, P., & Jarczok, M. N. (2015). *Measuring allostatic load in the workforce: A systematic review*. Industrial Health, 53(1), 5–20. https://doi.org/10.2486/indhealth.2014-0122
- **Source type:** systematic review.
- **Population / level:** employed adult populations; biomarker-based allostatic-load studies.
- **Phenomenon studied:** how allostatic load was operationalised and measured.
- **Useful result:** studies used heterogeneous biomarkers, thresholds and calculation methods; no agreed gold-standard index was identified.
- **Claim links:** ALR-05.
- **Cannot establish:** a universal allostatic-load score, individual clinical diagnosis, or biological validation of Fluid–Chronic depth.
- **Decision:** **supports the construct’s relevance while limiting measurement claims**.

### ALR-S06 · McCrory et al. 2023 · Towards a consensus definition

- **Citation:** McCrory, C., McLoughlin, S., Layte, R., Ni Cheallaigh, C., O’Halloran, A. M., Barros, H., Berkman, L. F., Bochud, M., Crimmins, E. M., Farrell, M. T., Fraga, S., Grundy, E., Kelly-Irving, M., Petrovic, D., Seeman, T., Stringhini, S., Vollenweider, P., & Kenny, R. A. (2023). *Towards a consensus definition of allostatic load: a multi-cohort, multi-system, multi-biomarker individual participant data meta-analysis*. Psychoneuroendocrinology, 153, 106117. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psyneuen.2023.106117
- **Source type:** multi-cohort individual-participant-data meta-analysis.
- **Population / level:** 67,126 participants aged 40–111 across 13 cohorts; multisystem biomarkers and health outcomes.
- **Phenomenon studied:** candidate biomarker configurations for physiological dysregulation and health prediction.
- **Useful result:** some compact multisystem indices predicted selected outcomes, while the field still lacked a consistent definition and several theoretically central mediators were underrepresented.
- **Claim links:** ALR-05.
- **Cannot establish:** generalisation to children or younger adults, causal direction, stressor-specific mechanisms or a complete measure of adaptive cost.
- **Decision:** **supports with population and measurement limits**.

## Claim decisions affecting current language

### Decision 1 · Keep “stability through change”, but explain its level

Accept as a concise description of the allostatic tradition:

> Physiological viability can be maintained through changing activity across regulatory systems.

Do not translate it into:

> The nervous system moves through the Gradient to maintain stability.

The first is a research-level regulatory principle. The second would treat TEG-Blue’s synthesis as an established biological sequence.

### Decision 2 · Replace one energy quantity with allocation and demand

Current statement under review:

> Energy accumulates at A because it is not being spent on protection.

Why it needs revision:

- energy is not one directly observable pool moving between Gradient columns;
- non-defensive social activity can itself carry metabolic and attentional demand;
- restoration, storage, mobilisation, expenditure and subjective vitality are different phenomena;
- allostasis supports context-sensitive allocation, not the proposed A-specific accumulation mechanism.

Provisional direction:

> In sufficiently supportive connection, defensive demand may be lower while social activity, regulation and access to resources continue. Whether restoration, storage or expenditure changes must be specified rather than represented as one accumulating quantity.

### Decision 3 · Replace universal incomplete Return with several Chronic routes

Current statement under review:

> Chronic Survival is incomplete Return. An organisation persists because it did not complete.

Why it needs revision:

- persistence during one episode and later reconstruction are not the same phenomenon;
- learning can alter future recruitment even after measurable channels have recovered;
- anticipatory cognition can recreate demand in the absence of the original event;
- ongoing adverse conditions may make continued organisation presently relevant;
- different physiological channels may recover at different speeds without forming one Chronic pattern.

Provisional direction:

> Chronic organisation may develop through prolonged persistence, repeated reconstruction, altered recruitment thresholds, generalisation, reduced discrimination or interrupted recovery. Incomplete recovery is one possible route, not the definition of every Chronic pattern.

### Decision 4 · Keep Return as a profile, not an endpoint biomarker

Return remains useful if it names a project-level integration across:

- reduction or completion of immediate demand;
- change in action readiness;
- autonomic and endocrine recovery;
- metabolic restoration and sleep;
- renewed access to attention, emotion and relationship;
- contextual updating;
- behavioural and relational repair where harm occurred.

These channels can diverge. No single measure should be treated as proof that Return has occurred.

## What the pilot says about the method

The claim-level approach works because the same source can support one component while challenging a broader formulation. Allostasis can ground dynamic physiological regulation and cumulative cost. It cannot independently ground the Gradient’s Positions, the proposed energy strategies, emotion meaning, learned persistence or Return as a single sequence.

The source record should therefore remain the reusable evidence unit. Crosswalks should link to records rather than acquire separate summaries of the same paper.

## Next evidence needed before implementation

1. human studies comparing autonomic, endocrine, subjective and behavioural recovery within the same episode;
2. studies of recovery after social-evaluative, physical, cognitive and relational stressors;
3. evidence distinguishing prolonged activation from later reconstruction;
4. work on sleep, metabolic recovery, fatigue and immune timescales;
5. social buffering and co-regulation studies that measure recovery rather than synchrony alone;
6. evidence for anticipatory regulation stated at the level of a specific task or physiological variable;
7. explicit examination of ongoing adversity, where persistence may still correspond to present conditions.
