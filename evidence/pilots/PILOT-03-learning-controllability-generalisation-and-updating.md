# Pilot 03 · Learning, Controllability, Generalisation and Updating

Status: first claim-level evidence pass
Scope: IC-07 Situation Signals · IC-10 Updating · IC-11 Return · NG-01 Fluid organisation · NG-02 Chronic organisation · NG-04 Fluid–Chronic depth · F2 action and emotion-belief layers
Purpose: identify distinct learning routes through which present organisation may remain flexible, become context-bound, generalise or resist updating

## The central distinction

Learning is present in both Fluid and Chronic organisation.

- **Fluid learning** remains sufficiently responsive to current conditions, context and consequences.
- **Chronic learning** is the project’s name for patterns in which recruitment, interpretation, action or recovery has become recurrently narrowed, generalised, persistent or difficult to revise.

The scientific difference is not “learned versus unlearned”. It concerns what was learned, under which conditions, how broadly it transfers, what current evidence retrieves and whether effective action changes the outcome.

## Linked source records

The [Shared Source Record Index](../sources/shared-source-record-index.md) is canonical. The records below are retained temporarily as pilot snapshots and must not be updated independently.

### LCU-S01 · Maier and Seligman 2016 · Learned helplessness at fifty

- **Citation:** Maier, S. F., & Seligman, M. E. P. (2016). *Learned helplessness at fifty: Insights from neuroscience*. Psychological Review, 123(4), 349–367. https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000033
- **Source type:** theoretical reassessment integrating behavioural and neuroscience research.
- **Level:** primarily animal experimental evidence with conceptual implications for humans.
- **Useful result:** reframes the older account: uncontrollable aversive stimulation recruits a default passive/defensive response, while detecting and exercising control engages processes that inhibit or modify its later effects.
- **Claim links:** LCU-06 and LCU-07.
- **Cannot establish:** that a person has “learned helplessness”, lacks agency or will fail to act across contexts.
- **Decision:** **supports controllability; replaces the older shorthand**.

### LCU-S02 · Dunsmoor and Paz 2015 · Fear generalisation and anxiety

- **Citation:** Dunsmoor, J. E., & Paz, R. (2015). *Fear generalization and anxiety: Behavioral and neural mechanisms*. Biological Psychiatry, 78(5), 336–343. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2015.04.010
- **Source type:** translational review.
- **Level:** human and animal conditioning research.
- **Useful result:** threat responding can generalise along perceptual and conceptual dimensions, while discrimination gradients provide a way to measure how selectively responding tracks learned threat.
- **Claim links:** LCU-02 and LCU-03.
- **Cannot establish:** autobiographical meaning, real-world danger accuracy or one Gradient depth from laboratory generalisation.
- **Decision:** **supports generalisation/discrimination as separate variables**.

### LCU-S03 · Bouton et al. 2012 · Relapse after instrumental extinction

- **Citation:** Bouton, M. E., Winterbauer, N. E., & Todd, T. P. (2012). *Relapse processes after the extinction of instrumental learning: Renewal, resurgence, and reacquisition*. Behavioural Processes, 90(1), 130–141. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.beproc.2012.03.004
- **Source type:** experimental programme review.
- **Level:** primarily non-human animal instrumental learning.
- **Useful result:** behaviour reduced through extinction can return with context change, reappearance of prior conditions or renewed reinforcement.
- **Claim links:** LCU-04 and LCU-05.
- **Cannot establish:** that harmful human conduct is an automatic conditioned response or that recurrence removes agency and responsibility.
- **Decision:** **supports context-dependent retrieval with translational restraint**.

### LCU-S04 · Craske et al. 2014 · Inhibitory learning approach

- **Citation:** Craske, M. G., Treanor, M., Conway, C. C., Zbozinek, T., & Vervliet, B. (2014). *Maximizing exposure therapy: An inhibitory learning approach*. Behaviour Research and Therapy, 58, 10–23. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brat.2014.04.006
- **Source type:** clinical translational review.
- **Level:** anxiety treatment informed by experimental extinction research.
- **Useful result:** emphasises expectancy violation and retrieval of new inhibitory learning rather than requiring fear to disappear during an exposure session.
- **Claim links:** LCU-04, LCU-05 and LCU-08.
- **Cannot establish:** that exposure is appropriate while danger remains, that distress reduction equals updating or that one treatment mechanism applies to all Chronic patterns.
- **Decision:** **supports updating without erasure**.

### LCU-S05 · Milad and Quirk 2012 · Fear extinction as translational neuroscience

- **Citation:** Milad, M. R., & Quirk, G. J. (2012). *Fear extinction as a model for translational neuroscience: Ten years of progress*. Annual Review of Psychology, 63, 129–151. https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.psych.121208.131631
- **Source type:** translational neuroscience review.
- **Level:** rodent and human fear-conditioning/extinction research.
- **Useful result:** supports separable acquisition, extinction learning and extinction retrieval processes across distributed circuits.
- **Claim links:** LCU-01, LCU-04, LCU-05 and LCU-08.
- **Cannot establish:** one “fear centre”, a direct map from circuitry to subjective fear, or the whole mechanism of PTSD.
- **Decision:** **supports component processes with circuit-level boundaries**.

## Updating must be decomposed

| Updating target | What may change | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Expected outcome** | What the cue is predicted to bring | “Expression will be punished” becomes less certain after reliable repair |
| **Cue discrimination** | Which stimuli belong to the danger class | One person’s raised voice becomes distinguishable from all disagreement |
| **Context retrieval** | Which learning becomes available here | Safety learning available in therapy becomes retrievable elsewhere |
| **Controllability** | Whether action changes conditions | Refusal, escape or help-seeking begins to produce a reliable effect |
| **Response weighting** | Which available information receives priority | Current evidence gains more influence relative to an earlier prediction |
| **Action repertoire** | Which responses can be selected | Pause, boundary, approach, withdrawal and repair become alternatives to one habitual route |
| **Meaning** | How an event is consciously understood | A bodily response is recognised as protective without being treated as proof of present danger |

## Claim decisions affecting current language

### Decision 1 · Define Chronic through several persistence routes

Chronic organisation may involve:

- lower recruitment thresholds;
- broader cue or context generalisation;
- poor danger–safety discrimination;
- strong retrieval of earlier learning;
- context-bound access to newer learning;
- expectations of low controllability;
- restricted action alternatives;
- prolonged response or interrupted recovery;
- repeated reconstruction through anticipation or rumination;
- actual continuing adversity.

No one route should define every Chronic pattern.

### Decision 2 · Replace “the nervous system thinks” with learned prediction

Prefer:

> Earlier experience may increase the predicted relevance of a cue, alter which context is retrieved or change whether action is expected to work.

This avoids assigning one belief or conscious intention to the whole nervous system.

### Decision 3 · Keep correspondence separate from comfort

A Fluid response may be painful, highly activated or immobilised while still corresponding to present conditions. A low-activation response may be Chronic if it is recurrently reconstructed outside the conditions that require it. Appearance and arousal do not decide the classification.

### Decision 4 · Updating does not require declaring the earlier response false

New learning may concern changed conditions, increased action availability or improved discrimination. The earlier response may have been accurate in its original context.

### Decision 5 · Harmful behaviour requires a separate plane

Learning mechanisms may help explain recurrence. They do not establish necessity, intention, entitlement, coercion, impact, response to feedback or responsibility. Those remain Harm–Power–Repair questions.

## What the three pilots now establish

Together the pilots support a more precise Fluid-to-Chronic bridge:

> Present conditions alter bodily and situational information. Distributed regulatory processes organise action and resource use. Experience can change which information is weighted, what outcomes are predicted, which actions seem viable and what learning is retrieved in a context. A pattern becomes more Chronic as recruitment, access, interpretation, action or Return becomes recurrently narrowed, generalised, persistent or difficult to revise. These dimensions can change separately.

This is an integrative TEG-Blue statement. Its component relations have scientific grounding; the whole sequence has not been validated as one natural system.
