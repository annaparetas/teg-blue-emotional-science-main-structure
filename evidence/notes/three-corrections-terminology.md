Evidence base: Pilots 01–03
Purpose: decide the language for resource allocation, Chronic routes and bodily information access before changing active framework files

Implementation boundary: the current Inner Compass/Gradient, F1 and F2 pages have been updated. Historical diagrams, F1 skeletons and working panels retain earlier language as provenance. Source-level evidence remains first-pass and continues through the Pending Tasks Register.

## Decision summary

| Area | Current language | Decision | Recommended replacement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A and the energy row** | Body energy strategy · Accumulation | **Replace** | Resource allocation emphasis · Relational coordination |
| **Chronic and Return** | Chronic Survival is incomplete Return | **Replace as universal definition** | Incomplete or interrupted recovery is one possible persistence route |

These decisions preserve the framework’s observations while removing claims the evidence cannot presently carry.

## Correction 1 · From “energy accumulation” to resource allocation

### What the old term was trying to protect

The word *Accumulation* distinguished connection at A from mobilisation at B–D and restoration at X. It also tried to explain why supportive connection may leave someone with more capacity than defensive activity does.

That observation remains useful. The proposed mechanism does not.

### Why “accumulation” should leave the model

*Accumulation* implies a single quantity of energy being stored because it is not used for protection. The current grounding does not establish:

- one measurable energy pool corresponding to Gradient Positions;
- that social connection is low-cost or non-expending;
- that resources are stored at A rather than restored, redistributed or used in coordination;
- that restoration waits entirely for X;
- that subjective vitality reveals metabolic storage.

The term therefore combines a real observation—supportive connection can change effort and availability—with an ungrounded physiological explanation.

### Recommended terminology

**Row name**

> **Resource allocation emphasis**

**Teaching question**

> What is the organism primarily using available resources to do here?

**Provisional Position vocabulary**

| Position | Recommended resource-allocation emphasis | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **X** | **Restoration** | Immediate protective and relational demands are sufficiently low for maintenance, replenishment and self-directed activity to become more available. |
| **A** | **Relational coordination** | Resources support reciprocal attention, communication, shared activity and adjustment. Defensive demand may be lower without implying zero cost or metabolic storage. |
| **A↔B** | **Preparation and checking** | Resources support orientation, uncertainty reduction and readiness for possible action. |
| **B** | **Protective mobilisation** | Resources support rapid protective action such as approach, withdrawal, escape or boundary defence. |
| **C** | **Strategic mobilisation** | Resources support sustained monitoring, planning, inhibition and management of uncertain or persistent conditions. |
| **D** | **Power mobilisation** | Resources support forceful, high-impact or controlling action; this does not determine whether the action is justified or harmful. |
| **Z** | **Protective conservation** | Available output narrows when demand, injury, exhaustion or limited capacity constrains sustained action. Conservation is not restoration. |

### Scientific and teaching language

| Use | Preferred wording |
| --- | --- |
| **Scientific grounding** | Dynamic allocation across physiological, attentional, motor and regulatory processes may vary with present demand. |
| **TEG-Blue teaching** | Different conditions place different demands on the resources available to us. |
| **Do not use** | Energy accumulates at A; connection stores energy; restoration only happens at X. |

### Decision state

**Accepted 19 August 2026.**

This is the clearest correction. It removes a literal energy claim while preserving the functional distinction among restoration, relationship, preparation, mobilisation and conservation.

## Correction 2 · From one incomplete Return to several Chronic routes

### What the old statement was trying to protect

> Chronic Survival is incomplete Return.

This statement made an important observation visible: the end of visible behaviour does not guarantee that physiology, felt access, action flexibility or relational availability have recovered.

The problem is the word *is*.

It turns one possible route into the definition of every Chronic pattern.

### Recommended route vocabulary

Use **Chronic route** as the umbrella term.

| Chronic route | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Prolonged persistence** | The recruited organisation remains active or slow to loosen within the same episode. |
| **Interrupted or incomplete recovery** | Some response channels begin to settle while others remain recruited, depleted or unavailable. |
| **Repeated reconstruction** | The organisation is reassembled after a period of apparent or measurable recovery. |
| **Lowered recruitment threshold** | Less, earlier or more ambiguous present evidence begins to recruit the pattern. |
| **Broader generalisation** | Responding spreads beyond the conditions or cues in which it was learned. |
| **Reduced discrimination** | Danger, safety and context become more difficult to distinguish in the relevant domain. |
| **Context-bound updating** | New learning becomes available in some conditions but fails to transfer or retrieve in others. |
| **Restricted action expectancy** | Escape, refusal, help-seeking or another action is expected not to change the outcome. |
| **Perseverative reconstruction** | Anticipation, rumination or repeated representation renews demand after the external event changes. |
| **Ongoing correspondence** | The organisation persists because danger, deprivation, coercion, illness or another demand continues. This is not evidence of mismatch. |

### Recommended replacement statements

**Construct definition**

> Chronic organisation is readily reconstructed, broadly generalised or slow to release because earlier, repeated or continuing conditions have changed recruitment, weighting, access, action or recovery.

**Return relationship**

> Interrupted or incomplete recovery can contribute to Chronic organisation, but it is one possible route. A pattern may also be reconstructed after recovery, generalised across contexts or maintained by continuing conditions.

**Teaching language**

> A protective pattern can stay active, return easily or become available in more situations than the ones that first required it.

### Terms to distinguish

| Term | Use it when |
| --- | --- |
| **Return** | A wider range of bodily, experiential and relational capacities is becoming available again. |
| **Recovery** | Specific physiological or functional channels are settling, rebuilding or regaining capacity. |
| **Persistence** | The same episode or organisation continues over time. |
| **Reconstruction** | A pattern is recruited again after it had loosened or was no longer observable. |
| **Recurrence** | A pattern appears again; the mechanism is not yet specified. |
| **Generalisation** | Recruitment spreads to similar or different cues and contexts. |
| **Updating** | Expectation, weighting, interpretation, retrieval or action changes with new information and consequences. |

### Naming decision: “Chronic Survival” or “Chronic organisation”

Use **Chronic organisation** as the scientific and architectural term.

Retain **Chronic Survival** only when naming the historical table or describing the framework’s protective-survival territory. *Chronic organisation* is more precise because:

- not every persistent route concerns immediate survival;
- ongoing illness, deprivation, relationship and cognition may participate;
- the term describes organisation rather than a person or diagnosis;
- it pairs clearly with Fluid organisation.

### Decision state

**Accepted 19 August 2026.**

The route model should replace the universal incomplete-Return statement. *Incomplete recovery* remains important but loses its status as the only cause.

## Correction 3 · From one “interoceptive access” capacity to bodily information access and use

### What should remain

**Interoception** should remain the scientific research term for nervous-system processing related to internal bodily states.

**Body Signals** should remain TEG-Blue’s broader functional grouping. It includes interoceptive contributions but is not a synonym for interoception.

### What needs to change

*Interoceptive access* currently risks reading as one capacity that a person possesses in a high or low amount. Research instead distinguishes bodily channel, task performance, attention, subjective report, confidence and interpretation.

The framework needs an understandable umbrella without collapsing those dimensions again.

### Recommended terminology

**TEG-Blue umbrella**

> **Bodily information access and use**

**Scientific territory**

> Interoceptive processing and measurement

**Definition**

> The variable ways bodily information may be detected, attended to, felt, differentiated, interpreted, trusted and used for action.

### Component vocabulary

| Dimension | Question | Preferred term |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Channel** | Which bodily process is being examined? | Bodily channel or interoceptive domain |
| **Detection** | Can a defined signal be discriminated under task conditions? | Detection or discrimination performance |
| **Attention** | How strongly or deliberately is attention directed towards bodily information? | Interoceptive attention |
| **Subjective access** | Does the change become consciously felt, and how clearly? | Subjective bodily access or felt clarity |
| **Self-report** | What does the person believe or report about their bodily awareness? | Interoceptive sensibility or self-reported bodily awareness |
| **Metacognition** | Does confidence correspond to performance on the defined task? | Metacognitive correspondence or interoceptive insight |
| **Differentiation** | Can bodily sensations, affect, need and activation be distinguished? | Bodily differentiation |
| **Identification** | Can the experience be named and connected to a category? | Identification |
| **Contextual interpretation** | What is the sensation taken to mean here? | Interpretation or appraisal |
| **Action use** | Can the information contribute to effective choice or help-seeking? | Signal-guided action |

### Terms to avoid unless defined

- connected to the body;
- disconnected from the body;
- good or poor interoception;
- accurate body awareness;
- the body knows;
- the body remembers;
- the signal reaches consciousness;
- trauma dysregulates the insula.

These phrases can sometimes introduce an idea in ordinary language, but they cannot function as scientific constructs without specifying the dimension being discussed.

### Recommended construct change

Rename:

> **RC-01 · Interoceptive access**

to:

> **RC-01 · Bodily information access and use**

Retain *interoceptive access* only in source records where a study uses and defines that term.

### Decision state

**Accepted 19 August 2026 with staged implementation.**

The new umbrella is clearer and broader. Before replacing RC-01 everywhere, each active use should be checked to see whether it actually means felt clarity, attention, differentiation, confidence, interpretation or action.

## Combined terminology architecture

The three decisions fit one shared sequence:

```text
Present and learned conditions
        ↓
Bodily and situational information
        ↓
Variable access, weighting and interpretation
        ↓
Resource allocation and available action
        ↓
Outcome, updating and Return
        ↓
Possible Chronic routes:
persistence · incomplete recovery · reconstruction · generalisation · restricted updating · continuing demand
```

This is a TEG-Blue synthesis. Each arrow remains a claim to ground; the sequence is not presented as one established biological pathway.

## Decisions ready to implement

### Accept now

1. **Resource allocation emphasis** replaces **Body energy strategy**.
2. **Relational coordination** replaces **Accumulation** at A.
3. **Chronic organisation** becomes the main architectural term; **Chronic Survival** remains the historical table name.
4. **Chronic routes** replace **Chronic Survival is incomplete Return** as the explanatory structure.
5. **Incomplete or interrupted recovery** remains one Chronic route.
6. **Persistence**, **reconstruction**, **recurrence**, **generalisation**, **updating** and **ongoing correspondence** remain distinct.
7. **Bodily information access and use** replaces **interoceptive access** as RC-01’s TEG-Blue name.
8. **Interoception** remains the linked scientific territory, not a synonym for Body Signals.

### Keep provisional

1. The exact Position-level wording for C and D resource allocation until the behavioural and ethical rows are checked.
2. Whether *perseverative reconstruction* is understandable enough for public-facing use; *repeated mental reactivation* may be clearer.
3. Whether *metacognitive correspondence* needs a simpler teaching label such as *confidence–performance match*.

### Reject

1. Energy accumulates at A.
2. Connection stores energy.
3. Restoration occurs only at X.
4. Every Chronic pattern exists because Return failed to complete.
5. Interoceptive access is one general high/low capacity.
6. Bodily experience independently establishes the cause or historical meaning of a signal.
