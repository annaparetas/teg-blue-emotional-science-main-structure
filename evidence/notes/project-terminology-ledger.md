# Project Terminology Ledger

Status: first governing ledger · 19 August 2026
Scope: terminology shared across the Inner Compass, F1, F2, Fluid, Chronic and Harm–Power–Repair
Purpose: keep accessible TEG-Blue language connected to precise formal descriptions without treating the two as identical

Companion: [Scientific Architecture Inventory](../reviews/scientific-architecture-inventory.md)

## How to use the ledger

A concise term can remain useful in a diagram even when the scientific account needs narrower language. The ledger therefore records four parts separately:

- **display term** — the language visible in TEG-Blue explanations;
- **formal description** — the more precise wording used in claim and evidence records;
- **scientific territory** — the research fields or constructs that may inform particular components;
- **boundary** — what the term must not be taken to establish.

The formal description is not presented as a standard scientific construct unless the ledger says so. In many cases it is a bounded TEG-Blue translation assembled from several research territories.

## Decision states

| State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Accepted** | The wording decision has been agreed and should govern new work |
| **Implemented** | The accepted decision has been applied to the named active files |
| **Provisional** | The direction is useful, but its wording or construct boundary still needs review |
| **Open** | More than one materially different option remains |
| **Retired** | The wording should not be used as a current explanatory claim; it may remain in historical material |
| **Keep with boundary** | The term remains useful if its limits are stated |

## Terms retained with a formal companion

| Display term | Formal companion | Why both are needed | Boundary or usage rule | State |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Biological organisation** | Whole-organism response organisation | The display term is compact; the formal term makes distributed participation clearer | No central organiser, single marker or hidden state is implied | **Provisional formal companion** |
| **Gradient Position** | Reference organisation or organisation profile | Letters remain useful map references; the formal wording reduces stage and identity readings | Never describe a person as occupying a fixed biological location | **Open between two formal options** |
| **Baseline** | Relative low-protective-demand organisation; candidate display term: Restorative Availability | Baseline is familiar, but can sound like a universal set point | X is where restoration is represented most explicitly and may predominate; restoration is not exclusive to X | **Open display decision** |
| **Safety Checking** | Orienting under uncertainty | The display term is accessible; the companion names the wider organisation rather than one visible behaviour | Checking is not automatically pathological or consciously deliberate | **Provisional dual label** |
| **Organising pull** | Action tendency, action readiness or motivational pull | The project term protects the difference between pressure and action | Select the scientific companion claim by claim; a pull is not a behaviour | **Provisional dual label** |
| **Situation Signals** | Situation-relevant information | The display term is readable; the formal term avoids suggesting that situations send complete meanings | Separate detected features, felt significance, inference, memory and interpretation | **Provisional dual label** |
| **Updating** | Name the updating target and route | The umbrella connects several pages | State whether expectation, discrimination, context retrieval, controllability, weighting, action repertoire or meaning changes | **Keep with decomposition** |
| **Affective sharing** | Affective sharing | The established term usefully differs from mentalizing | Separate shared affect, source attribution, self–other distinction, concern and action | **Keep with boundary** |
| **Mentalizing** | Inferring and revising mental states | The technical term connects to research; the translation supports public explanation | Inference can be wrong and is distinct from affective sharing, agreement, concern and helping | **Keep with boundary** |
| **Cost** | Response costs and consequences, named by recipient and timescale | The short term is useful but presently carries several kinds of burden | Distinguish bodily effort, opportunity cost, relational impact and burden transferred to others | **Provisional clarification** |

## Retired explanatory claims

| Retired wording | Why it was retired | Current replacement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Body energy strategy** | Suggests one energy quantity defines a Position | Resource allocation emphasis |
| **Accumulation** at A | Implies connection stores a single pool of energy | Relational coordination; specify demand, restoration or expenditure separately |
| **Energy accumulates because it is not spent on protection** | Makes an unmeasured mechanism sound established | Supportive conditions may reduce some defensive demands while social and regulatory activity continues |
| **Restoration occurs only at X** | Treats a predominance as exclusivity | Restoration is represented most explicitly and may predominate at X, but restorative processes may occur elsewhere |
| **Chronic Survival is incomplete Return** | Turns one possible persistence route into a universal definition | Chronic routes; interrupted recovery is one route |
| **Interoceptive access** as one high/low capacity | Collapses channel, performance, attention, felt clarity, report, differentiation, interpretation and use | Bodily information access and use, with the relevant dimension named |
| **The nervous system thinks/believes** | Assigns one conscious-like proposition to a distributed organism | Earlier learning may alter predicted relevance, context retrieval, outcome expectation or expected controllability |
| **The amygdala takes over** | Reduces dynamic network participation to one region and implies loss of the cortex | Name the measured network, task, timescale and functional change |
| **One emotion = one chemical** | Neurotransmitters, hormones and immune mediators participate across many functions and timescales | Describe mediator participation in a defined process, measure and context |

## Terms requiring special care

| Term | Distinction that must be named |
| --- | --- |
| **Safety** | Actual danger, felt safety, predicted safety, social safety, relational trust or sufficient capacity |
| **Regulation** | Regulator, target, route, timescale and outcome; reduced visible distress may also reflect suppression or compliance |
| **Trauma** | Exposure, immediate response, lasting consequence, diagnosis or clinical framework |
| **Survival** | Literal survival, bodily integrity, attachment or social threat, symbolic consequence or learned generalisation |
| **Nervous-system state** | Use response pattern or organisation unless a study directly operationalises a state |
| **Dysregulation** | Name the process that is costly, persistent, mismatched or failing and the measure used |
| **Co-regulation** | Distinguish coordination, social buffering, expected support, active help, compliance and suppression |
| **Empathy** | Affective sharing, mentalizing, empathic concern, personal distress or helping behaviour |
| **Resilience** | Outcome, context, resources, burden and timescale rather than a presumed individual trait |
| **Epigenetic inheritance** | Germline inheritance, prenatal exposure, caregiving, social transmission and material continuity |
| **Polyvagal language** | Mark theory-specific claims and do not treat the theory as settled anatomy or a complete hierarchy |

## Concepts that must remain separate

- capacity, access, recruitment, performance and use;
- bodily change, biological sensing, subjective feeling, self-report and interpretation;
- actual conditions, detected conditions, inferred conditions and remembered or imagined conditions;
- activation, danger and correspondence;
- expression, suppression, automatic inhibition, masking and action;
- persistence during an event, later reconstruction, recurrence and continuing demand;
- social safety, relational trust, reciprocal connection and belonging;
- defensive organisation, organising pull, behaviour, coercion and impact;
- physiological recovery, contextual updating, behavioural repair and relational repair;
- explanation, prediction, responsibility and intervention.

## Decision protocol

Before a term changes in an active page:

1. identify the construct and claim IDs affected;
2. decide whether the term is a research construct, bounded translation, TEG-Blue integration or accessible umbrella;
3. record its formal description and boundary;
4. mark the decision accepted;
5. inspect each active occurrence in context rather than replacing it globally;
6. record where the accepted change was implemented;
7. leave historical wording intact when it is clearly labelled as provenance.

The aim is not to make TEG-Blue sound more academic. It is to let each word carry one understandable amount of meaning.
