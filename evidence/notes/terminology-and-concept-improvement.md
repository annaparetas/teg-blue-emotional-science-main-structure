# Terminology and Concept Improvement Register

Status: working audit
Purpose: record terms and ideas that could become clearer, more scientifically traceable or more coherent across the current system

Governing ledger: [Project Terminology Ledger](../../GLOSSARY.md) records the current decision and implementation state. This register remains the place where candidate improvements and unresolved conceptual pressures are developed.

Companion: [Harm, Power and Repair Grounding](../../07-reference/grounding/harm-power-and-repair.md) develops power, agency, coercion, feedback and repair as a separate claim layer.

## A two-language approach

TEG-Blue does not need to replace every accessible term with academic language. It does need to show when a familiar TEG-Blue name and a research construct are not identical.

The proposed approach is:

- **TEG-Blue display term:** the concise language used in diagrams and public explanation;
- **formal description:** the narrower wording used in claim records and scientific grounding;
- **status label:** established research construct, bounded translation, TEG-Blue integration or accessible umbrella.

This lets “Return” remain visible while the grounding record examines recovery, completion, updating and renewed capacity separately.

## Strongest conceptual additions

These concepts are present implicitly but would significantly improve coherence if they became explicit dimensions or recurring questions.

### 1 · Actual conditions, inferred conditions and remembered conditions

The system already distinguishes internal, external and represented conditions. It would become clearer if every claim asked:

- What is materially present?
- What is detected?
- What is inferred or predicted?
- What is remembered or imagined?
- Where do these agree or differ?

This prevents “perception” from erasing actual danger and prevents actual danger from being assumed solely from an internal response.

### 2 · Predictability, controllability and imminence

Threat intensity alone is not enough. Responses may differ depending on:

- whether the event is predictable;
- whether action can change the outcome;
- how near or immediate the threat is;
- whether escape, support or repair is available.

These variables may explain movement across several Gradient references more precisely than a single safety-to-threat line.

### 3 · Context sensitivity and generalisation

The Fluid–Chronic distinction becomes more measurable when it asks:

- Does the response vary when the context varies?
- Can safety and danger be discriminated?
- Does learning generalise too widely or too narrowly?
- Can a response update in one context but remain persistent in another?

### 4 · Recovery as a profile

Return should be recorded across several channels:

- visible behaviour;
- autonomic and endocrine recovery;
- attention and working memory;
- pain and bodily access;
- relational openness;
- sleep, energy and restoration;
- willingness to re-enter the situation.

The channels may recover at different times.

### 5 · Available actions and affordances

The Gradient currently describes action tendencies. It could become stronger by asking what the person experiences as possible:

- Can I leave?
- Can I ask for help?
- Can I pause?
- Can I resist safely?
- Can I communicate?
- Can I repair without surrendering a boundary?

This connects bodily organisation with material and relational reality.

### 6 · Capacity, access, recruitment and use

These should remain separate:

- **Capacity:** can this process occur at all?
- **Access:** does enough of it become available to ME?
- **Recruitment:** is it brought into use in this situation?
- **Performance:** what happens under the specific task or condition?
- **Use:** how does it influence action?

This distinction is especially important for interoception, affective sharing, mentalizing and Conscious Participation.

### 7 · Social safety, relational trust and belonging

These are connected but not interchangeable:

- **Social safety:** sufficiently low interpersonal threat for some forms of contact.
- **Relational trust:** expectation formed through repeated evidence about a particular relationship.
- **Belonging:** lived and socially recognised inclusion.
- **Reciprocal connection:** active mutual coordination in a particular interaction.

A person may feel socially safe without belonging, belong to a group that is unsafe, or coordinate successfully without trusting the relationship.

### 8 · Co-regulation, social buffering and interpersonal influence

“Co-regulation” is often used too broadly. The model would benefit from separating:

- observed interpersonal coordination;
- reduced stress response in another person’s presence;
- learned expectation of support;
- active help with regulation;
- compliance or suppression under relational pressure.

Not every reduction in visible distress is regulation, and not every interpersonal influence is care.

### 9 · Threat, deprivation and unpredictability

Adversity should not become one undifferentiated dose. Threat, lack of expected input, environmental unpredictability, discrimination, illness, poverty and relational loss may affect development through partly different routes.

### 10 · Masking, camouflaging, suppression and override

The emotion-override work should distinguish:

- an emotion arising;
- bodily representation;
- conscious access;
- expression;
- deliberate suppression;
- automatic inhibition;
- social camouflaging or masking;
- action that follows or contradicts the signal.

This will improve both neurodiversity and Chronic-development work.

### 11 · Conflict, coercion and patterned control

Relational difficulty needs at least three levels:

- disagreement or conflict between people with meaningful freedom;
- dysregulated or harmful behaviour that may still permit accountability and change;
- patterned coercive control that restricts another person’s options and reality over time.

A nervous-system account should not flatten these into mutual “activation”.

### 12 · Epistemic access and epistemic harm

Some experiences alter not only emotion but access to knowing:

- whether one’s perception is trusted;
- whether language is available for an experience;
- whether another person repeatedly distorts shared reality;
- whether institutions recognise the person as a credible knower.

This provides a bridge among CLS, ME, gaslighting, power and repair without treating all disagreement as epistemic harm.

### 13 · Adaptation, function and cost

A response can be adaptive in one respect and costly in another. Every pattern should be examined through three separate questions:

- What function might it serve under these conditions?
- What does it cost now and over time?
- Does it remain responsive when conditions change?

### 14 · Explanation, prediction and intervention

These are different scientific tasks. A model may help describe or predict a pattern without identifying its cause. An intervention may help without confirming the theory used to explain why it helps.

### 15 · Measurement level

Every scientific claim should say whether it concerns:

- first-person report;
- observed behaviour;
- task performance;
- peripheral physiology;
- endocrine, immune or metabolic measures;
- neural activity;
- relationship or institutional observation;
- functional outcome over time.

Convergence across levels can strengthen an account, but disagreement among levels is also meaningful data.

## Concepts to handle with special care

| Concept | Why it matters | Working rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regulation | Can mean automatic physiological adjustment, deliberate emotion regulation, interpersonal support or social compliance | Name the regulator, target, route, timescale and outcome |
| Safety | Can mean absence of actual danger, felt safety, predicted safety, social safety or sufficient capacity | State which meaning is intended |
| Trauma | Can mean exposure, response, lasting consequence, diagnosis or clinical framework | Name the exposure or measured outcome; do not use trauma as a universal mechanism |
| Survival | Powerful TEG-Blue language but can overstate the biological stakes of social or symbolic events | Distinguish literal survival threat, bodily integrity, attachment or social threat, and learned generalisation |
| Nervous-system state | Often implies a discrete and measurable natural category | Prefer response pattern or organisation unless a study operationalises a state directly |
| Dysregulation | Can pathologise an organised response without naming conditions, function or measurement | Describe the process that is failing, costly, persistent or mismatched |
| Resilience | Can hide unequal burden or imply an individual trait | Name the outcome, context, resources and timescale |
| Empathy | Used for several separable phenomena | Prefer affective sharing, mentalizing, empathic concern or helping behaviour |
| Polyvagal language | Accessible but often treated as settled anatomy or a complete hierarchy | Use only for claims directly supported by Polyvagal research and mark theory-specific terms |
| Epigenetic inheritance | Commonly used far beyond what human designs can establish | Separate germline inheritance, prenatal exposure, caregiving, social transmission and material continuity |

## Initial research anchors for terminology

These sources help stabilise distinctions; they do not validate the TEG-Blue assembly.

- [Barrett & Simmons (2015) · interoceptive predictions](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn3950) — links interoception, prediction and allostatic regulation while keeping these as distinguishable concepts.
- [Kanske et al. (2016) · affective sharing and mentalizing](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27129794/) — supports separating affect sharing from mentalizing.
- [Stietz et al. (2019) · empathy and perspective-taking](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6428036/) — reviews terminology and evidence for dissociable social-affective and social-cognitive processes.
- [Roelofs (2017) · freezing](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5332864/) — distinguishes freezing as action preparation from tonic immobility and other low-movement responses.
- [Mobbs et al. (2007) · threat imminence](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17717184/) — supports examining defensive organisation in relation to how near a threat is.

## Decision order

1. Decompose ESS and CLS into component claims and evidence territories while preserving their educational architecture.
2. Resolve the historical ESS expansion: **Emotional Somatic System** in the current model and **Embodied Somatic System** in the older foundation folder.
3. Decide whether Position becomes Reference Organisation across active pages.
4. Rename Power Mobilisation and restructure Shutdown before cell-level biological grounding.
5. Convert Fluid–Chronic depth into a multidimensional persistence and flexibility profile.
6. Add the conceptual dimensions above to the claim-record template.

The purpose is not to make the language sound more scientific. It is to reduce the number of different claims each word is being asked to carry.
