# Current Construct Claim Decomposition

Status: working claim architecture
Companions: [Current Construct Grounding Registry](current-construct-grounding-registry.md) · [Scientific Lineage and Integrative Grounding](../reviews/scientific-lineage-and-integrative-grounding.md)

## How to read this file

Each current construct has been separated into six claims. This does not mean that all six claims already have the same level of support.

- **Definition** records what the term means inside TEG-Blue. A project may define a term, but the definition must not be mistaken for an empirical finding.
- **Phenomenon** identifies what can be observed, measured or reported without assuming one mechanism.
- **Mechanism** names processes that may contribute. These usually require the narrowest wording and the strongest source control.
- **Development** asks how the pattern may change through learning, repetition, maturation, relationship and material conditions.
- **Application** states what the construct helps someone notice, compare or do.
- **Boundary** protects against an inference the construct cannot carry.

Where a current name may change, the construct ID remains stable.

## Inner Compass process constructs

### IC-01 · Conditions

- **Definition:** The internal, external, relational, represented and anticipated circumstances that may affect the organism at a particular time.
- **Phenomenon:** Bodily variables, sensory events, other people’s actions, resources, constraints, memories and expectations can all coincide with changes in attention, feeling, physiology and behaviour.
- **Mechanism:** Exteroceptive, interoceptive and proprioceptive processing, memory, appraisal, prediction, social inference and ongoing physiological regulation may make different aspects of conditions available.
- **Development:** Experience can change which conditions are noticed, expected, trusted, ignored or treated as relevant. Social position and material access also change which conditions a person encounters.
- **Application:** Keeps actual context visible before a response is interpreted as irrational, learned or internally generated.
- **Boundary:** Conditions are not merely “cues”, perceptions or beliefs. A danger, exclusion or deprivation may be materially present even when another observer does not recognise it.

### IC-02 · Detection and transmission

- **Definition:** The processes through which change is registered and information becomes available within the organism.
- **Phenomenon:** Sensory and physiological responses can begin before a person can report what changed or why it matters.
- **Mechanism:** Receptors, peripheral nerves, spinal and brainstem pathways, endocrine and immune signalling, and distributed neural processing may participate. No single route carries every kind of information.
- **Development:** Sensory thresholds, attention, learned relevance, health, medication, disability and repeated exposure may change what is detected and how strongly it is weighted.
- **Application:** Separates the arrival of information from later emotion, interpretation and conscious report.
- **Boundary:** Detection does not guarantee accuracy, conscious access, emotion or a complete reading of the situation.

### IC-03 · Biological organisation

- **Definition:** The changing coordination of bodily and neural processes around current demands, needs and available action.
- **Phenomenon:** Attention, posture, movement, cardiovascular activity, breathing, pain, energy use, social availability and recovery can change together or on different timescales.
- **Mechanism:** Autonomic, endocrine, immune, metabolic, sensory, motor, salience, learning and regulatory processes may interact dynamically.
- **Development:** Repetition, maturation, illness, sleep, medication, injury, caregiving and learning may alter thresholds, coupling, persistence and recovery.
- **Application:** Provides a whole-organism view without assigning one chemical, pathway or brain region to a complete response.
- **Boundary:** “Organisation” is a TEG-Blue level of description. It is not a hidden biological object, central controller or unique measurable state.

### IC-04 · ESS

- **Definition:** TEG-Blue's educational system for emotional-somatic condition-reading and organisation through which organismic significance, bodily change, felt experience, attention and action readiness may develop.
- **Phenomenon:** People can feel bodily or emotional change and begin acting before they can explain what has become relevant.
- **Mechanism:** Interoceptive and exteroceptive processing, affective learning, autonomic and endocrine regulation, memory, salience and action-selection processes may contribute.
- **Development:** Repeated conditions may change what becomes salient, how readily responses are recruited and whether bodily and emotional information becomes consciously usable.
- **Application:** Makes emotional-somatic influence visible when a cognitive explanation alone cannot account for a response.
- **Boundary:** ESS is a TEG-Blue educational integration, not a discovered anatomical system, all non-conscious cognition, a single network or an infallible source of truth.

Component decomposition: [ESS-01 to ESS-07](../../02-model-1-ess-cls-me/grounding/ESS-CLS-component-decomposition.md#part-i--ess-components).

### IC-05 · CLS

- **Definition:** TEG-Blue's educational system for cognitive-logical modelling through which available experience may be represented, named, compared, sequenced, contextualised, communicated, planned and deliberately reconsidered.
- **Phenomenon:** People can reconsider an initial response, compare evidence, imagine consequences and communicate an account across time.
- **Mechanism:** Working memory, semantic and autobiographical memory, language, cognitive control, metacognition, counterfactual reasoning and social cognition may contribute.
- **Development:** Language, education, culture, relationship, practice, stress and access needs may shape which reflective capacities are available and trusted.
- **Application:** Makes the explanatory and model-building side of experience visible without granting it automatic authority over emotional-somatic information.
- **Boundary:** CLS is not one neural system, pure logic, all cognition or a conscious executive. Much cognition, prediction and evaluation occurs outside reflective awareness.

Component decomposition: [CLS-01 to CLS-08](../../02-model-1-ess-cls-me/grounding/ESS-CLS-component-decomposition.md#part-ii--cls-components).

### IC-06 · Body Signals

- **Definition:** TEG-Blue’s functional grouping for information concerning bodily condition, maintenance, viable limits, need, effort, injury and recovery.
- **Phenomenon:** Hunger, fatigue, pain, temperature, breath, tension, nausea, comfort and other bodily changes can become noticeable and influence action.
- **Mechanism:** Interoception, somatosensation, nociception, proprioception, metabolic and immune signalling, affective valuation and learned interpretation may contribute.
- **Development:** Repeated dismissal, punishment, illness, caregiving, language and practice may alter attention to, interpretation of and action on bodily information.
- **Application:** Helps people ask what the body may require before translating every signal into an emotion or social meaning.
- **Boundary:** Body Signals are not identical to interoception, objective readouts or commands. A bodily sensation may have several possible sources and meanings.

### IC-07 · Situation Signals

- **Definition:** TEG-Blue’s functional grouping for information concerning relationships, opportunity, obstacle, belonging, danger, goals, norms and context.
- **Phenomenon:** Changes in another person, the environment or an anticipated outcome can alter feeling, attention and action even when no single external cue explains the response.
- **Mechanism:** Multisensory integration, memory, appraisal, prediction, affective learning, mentalizing, cultural knowledge and bodily feedback may contribute.
- **Development:** Experience can alter which patterns are recognised, how ambiguity is resolved and whether a situation is expected to permit safety, dignity or repair.
- **Application:** Keeps situational meaning distinct from bodily maintenance while showing that both can recruit embodied information.
- **Boundary:** Situation Signals are not a separate sensory system, guaranteed facts about other people or purely conscious interpretations.

### IC-08 · ME

- **Definition:** TEG-Blue’s name for the lived first-person participant: the person who experiences, is affected, may reflect and acts within the process.
- **Phenomenon:** People can notice some processes but not others, experience conflict, reconsider meaning, choose among some available actions and take responsibility over time.
- **Mechanism:** No single mechanism is proposed. First-person participation depends on many interacting biological, cognitive, affective, relational and cultural processes.
- **Development:** Agency, self-understanding, access and trust may change through maturation, relationship, learning, disability, practice and material conditions.
- **Application:** Prevents the person from disappearing inside a biological process diagram.
- **Boundary:** ME is not a brain module, soul claim, detached controller or guarantee of unrestricted free choice.

### IC-09 · Conscious Participation

- **Definition:** The variable possibility of turning towards available experience, reflecting, checking, communicating, restraining, choosing and revising action.
- **Phenomenon:** A person may act before understanding, understand later, pause in one condition but not another, or recognise information without being able to use it immediately.
- **Mechanism:** Conscious access, metacognition, attention, working memory, inhibitory control, language, emotion regulation and social support may contribute.
- **Development:** Practice, modelling, safety, education, trauma, chronic stress, disability and repeated consequences may widen or narrow participation.
- **Application:** Locates meaningful human participation without pretending it is permanent top-down control.
- **Boundary:** Conscious participation is not the same as compliance, calmness, verbal fluency or total agency. Reduced access does not make impact disappear.

### IC-10 · Updating

- **Definition:** Change in expectation, interpretation, response weighting or future action as new information and consequences become available.
- **Phenomenon:** Responses can change after new experience, remain unchanged despite contradictory information or update in one context but not another.
- **Mechanism:** Prediction error, reinforcement learning, memory reconsolidation, extinction and safety learning, cognitive reappraisal, social learning and habit change may contribute.
- **Development:** Timing, repetition, controllability, developmental stage, trust, generalisation and the reliability of new conditions may affect updating.
- **Application:** Replaces the vague idea that insight alone should make a protective pattern disappear.
- **Boundary:** Updating is not one mechanism, simple belief correction or proof that the earlier response was mistaken. Repeated safe exposure may fail when the situation remains unsafe or uncontrollable.

### IC-11 · Return

- **Definition:** The process through which protective recruitment can complete, loosen or reorganise and a wider range of bodily, experiential and relational capacities becomes available.
- **Phenomenon:** Behaviour may end before physiology settles; some response channels recover quickly while sleep, pain, attention or relational openness take longer.
- **Mechanism:** Removal or resolution of demand, autonomic and endocrine recovery, metabolic restoration, completion of action, new safety learning, social support, rest and meaning-making may contribute.
- **Development:** Repeated stress, interrupted recovery, learned expectation, health and the availability of repair may alter how readily Return occurs.
- **Application:** Draws attention to recovery dynamics rather than treating the end of visible behaviour as the end of the event.
- **Boundary:** Return is a TEG-Blue integration, not one physiological endpoint, forced calm, suppression, forgiveness or a requirement to reconnect.

## Gradient organisation constructs

### NG-01 · Fluid organisation

- **Definition:** Organisation that remains sufficiently responsive to present conditions to change, use competing information and begin Return when conditions change.
- **Phenomenon:** Within-person responses can shift with threat imminence, uncertainty, controllability, social context and recovery opportunity.
- **Mechanism:** Dynamic allocation across sensory, autonomic, endocrine, motor, attentional, affective and regulatory processes may contribute.
- **Development:** Learning shapes Fluid responses too; Fluid means present responsiveness, not absence of history.
- **Application:** Provides a reference for examining state-dependent change without turning it into personality.
- **Boundary:** Fluid does not mean calm, healthy, conscious, socially engaged or proportionate in every channel. It is a project classification, not a validated natural kind.

### NG-02 · Chronic organisation

- **Definition:** Organisation that is readily reconstructed, generalised or slow to release because prior or repeated conditions have changed thresholds, weighting, access or recovery.
- **Phenomenon:** Responses may recur across contexts, remain after a demand changes or become difficult to revise despite some contradictory information.
- **Mechanism:** Conditioning, sensitisation, habit, perseverative cognition, altered controllability expectations, threat generalisation, impaired safety discrimination and repeated physiological load may contribute through different routes.
- **Development:** Timing, duration, predictability, relationship, deprivation, threat, repair and ongoing material conditions may shape different recurrent patterns.
- **Application:** Helps ask what has become persistently available without treating it as character or permanent damage.
- **Boundary:** Chronic does not mean lifelong, diagnostic, continuously active or disconnected from present reality. Actual ongoing danger must remain visible.

Governing evidence companion: [Chronic Scientific Spine](../../03-model-2-gradient/grounding/chronic-scientific-spine.md).

### NG-03 · Gradient Position

- **Definition:** A named TEG-Blue reference pattern describing characteristic priorities, capacities, action tendencies, costs and Return under a class of conditions.
- **Phenomenon:** Several response channels may change together, although not identically, as conditions and organismic capacity change.
- **Mechanism:** Each Position may involve overlapping processes rather than a unique mechanism. The assembly itself has not been directly validated.
- **Development:** Experience may alter thresholds, routes and persistence without fixing a person permanently in one Position.
- **Application:** Gives people a common comparison language for changing organisation across time and context.
- **Boundary:** A Position is not a stage, type, diagnosis, moral ranking, colour-coded biomarker or complete explanation of behaviour.

### NG-04 · Fluid–Chronic depth

- **Definition:** A multidimensional description of how strongly recruitment, information access, interpretation, action range, context sensitivity and Return have become restricted or persistent.
- **Phenomenon:** A pattern may be flexible in one dimension and persistent in another; depth may vary across contexts and time.
- **Mechanism:** Different learning, stress, developmental, relational and health processes may affect each dimension.
- **Development:** Repetition without repair may deepen some restrictions, while reliable safety, support, practice and changed conditions may widen them.
- **Application:** Prevents Fluid and Chronic from appearing as a binary switch.
- **Boundary:** Depth is not a single severity score, linear distance, disease progression or fixed location inside a person.

### NG-05 · Baseline

- **Definition:** The current Gradient reference for organisation in which immediate protective demand is low enough for restoration, bodily need and self-directed activity to become more available.
- **Phenomenon:** Rest, digestion, sleep pressure, curiosity, ordinary pain or fatigue and spontaneous activity may become more noticeable when urgent demand recedes.
- **Mechanism:** Shifting autonomic, endocrine, metabolic, immune, circadian, attentional and motivational processes may contribute.
- **Development:** Repeated stress, illness, caregiving demands and limited recovery opportunity may alter what low-demand organisation feels like or whether it is accessible.
- **Application:** Makes restoration and being safely alone visible rather than treating social connection as the only form of safety.
- **Boundary:** Baseline is not one physiological set point, inactivity, isolation, “ventral vagal state”, perfect health or a universal personal norm.

### NG-06 · Connection / Belonging

- **Definition:** The Gradient reference for organisation in which reciprocal contact, coordination and shared activity are sufficiently safe and available.
- **Phenomenon:** Voice, gaze, posture, timing, attention, affect sharing and mutual adjustment may coordinate during trusted interaction.
- **Mechanism:** Social perception, reward learning, autonomic regulation, attachment-related expectation, affect sharing, mentalizing and endogenous modulatory systems may contribute.
- **Development:** Caregiving, peer experience, culture, discrimination, accessibility and repair shape where and with whom belonging becomes available.
- **Application:** Distinguishes reciprocal connection from performance, appeasement or mere proximity.
- **Boundary:** Visible sociability, eye contact, conformity or oxytocin-related activity does not prove safety, trust or belonging.

### NG-07 · Safety Checking

- **Definition:** The Gradient reference for orienting, uncertainty monitoring and information gathering when the meaning or safety of conditions is not yet clear.
- **Phenomenon:** Attention may narrow toward novelty or ambiguity; gaze, posture, breath, memory search and checking behaviour may change until sufficient information is available.
- **Mechanism:** Orienting, uncertainty processing, vigilance, prediction error, salience, attention and threat appraisal may contribute.
- **Development:** Prior learning may alter what counts as uncertain, how much evidence is required and whether checking resolves.
- **Application:** Gives uncertainty its own place rather than forcing every ambiguous response into safety or defence.
- **Boundary:** Checking is not automatically anxiety, pathology, indecision or proof that danger exists.

### NG-08 · Protection / Defence

- **Definition:** The Gradient reference for organisation around avoiding, resisting, escaping, guarding against or reducing a meaningful threat.
- **Phenomenon:** Attention, pain, cardiovascular activity, muscle readiness and available behaviour may shift toward context-dependent protective routes.
- **Mechanism:** Defensive-circuit, salience, autonomic, endocrine, sensory-motor, analgesic and action-selection processes may contribute.
- **Development:** Learning and available action shape whether protection tends toward flight, fight, freezing, appeasement, concealment, help-seeking or another route.
- **Application:** Frames protective pressure without reducing it to fight-or-flight.
- **Boundary:** Defence does not identify one physiology or behaviour, establish proportionality, prove danger or justify harm.

### NG-09 · Strategic Management

- **Definition:** The current Gradient reference for sustained organisation around monitoring, anticipating and managing a persistent demand or threat.
- **Phenomenon:** Planning, rehearsal, selective attention, inhibition, role management and continuing bodily effort may become organised around a narrow set of relevant variables.
- **Mechanism:** Prospective control, working memory, vigilance, perseverative cognition, stress physiology, habit and social learning may contribute.
- **Development:** Repeated unpredictability, responsibility without support, coercion or environments requiring constant performance may make management more available and harder to release.
- **Application:** Distinguishes sustained protective management from immediate defence and from deliberate interpersonal manipulation.
- **Boundary:** “Strategic” must not imply conscious calculation. The Position does not establish deception, domination or malicious intent.

### NG-10 · Power Mobilisation

- **Definition:** The current Gradient reference for extreme mobilisation toward decisive action when life, bodily integrity, escape or control of an acute threat may be at stake.
- **Phenomenon:** Rapid motor, cardiovascular, respiratory, analgesic, attentional and sensory changes may support intense escape or active defence.
- **Mechanism:** Threat-imminence, hypothalamic, midbrain, periaqueductal, autonomic, endocrine, opioid, metabolic and sensory-motor processes may contribute.
- **Development:** Experience, physical capacity, training, entrapment and available routes may shape whether and how extreme mobilisation occurs.
- **Application:** Separates survival-level mobilisation from ordinary anger, dominance and the social exercise of power.
- **Boundary:** The name does not establish violence, rage, superiority, control over others or one universal “maximum” state.

### NG-11 · Shutdown

- **Definition:** The current umbrella Position for markedly reduced or altered movement, speech, awareness, energy or engagement when active capacity is constrained or exceeded.
- **Phenomenon:** Freezing, tonic immobility, dissociation, fainting, collapse, exhaustion, reduced speech and reduced voluntary action can look similar while differing in timing and physiology.
- **Mechanism:** Several defensive, autonomic, dissociative, metabolic, pain, fatigue and consciousness-related processes may contribute. They should not be treated as one route.
- **Development:** Repeated inescapability, dissociation, illness, sleep loss, pain and learned expectation may change which low-output responses become available.
- **Application:** Prevents low visible output from being mistaken for safety, consent, indifference or lack of internal activity.
- **Boundary:** Shutdown is a heterogeneous TEG-Blue umbrella, not “dorsal vagal state”, one parasympathetic profile, one diagnosis or evidence that all systems are offline.

## Relational capacity constructs

### RC-01 · Bodily information access and use

- **Definition:** The variable ways bodily information may be detected, attended to, felt, differentiated, interpreted, trusted and used for action.
- **Phenomenon:** Bodily channel, detection or discrimination performance, attention, subjective clarity, self-report, metacognitive correspondence, differentiation, interpretation and action use can vary independently.
- **Mechanism:** Peripheral signalling, central representation, attention, precision weighting, learning, language and metacognition may contribute.
- **Development:** Caregiving, emotion socialisation, illness, pain, neurodivergence, trauma and contemplative or therapeutic practice may alter different dimensions of access.
- **Application:** Separates biological processing from a person’s ability to notice and use it.
- **Boundary:** Low report does not prove absent bodily representation; high attention does not guarantee task accuracy, correct causal interpretation or effective action. Interoception is one scientific territory contributing to this TEG-Blue construct, not a synonym for Body Signals.

### RC-02 · Affective sharing

- **Definition:** The process through which something of another person’s affective state becomes represented in one’s own experience while some self–other distinction remains.
- **Phenomenon:** People may share another person’s distress or joy without necessarily understanding its cause or acting to help.
- **Mechanism:** Perception-action coupling, learning, affective resonance, attention, affiliation and self–other representation may contribute.
- **Development:** Relationship, motivation, group membership, culture, stress and experience may change when affect sharing is recruited.
- **Application:** Keeps “feeling with” separate from perspective-taking, personal distress, concern and moral action.
- **Boundary:** Affective sharing is not accuracy, agreement, compassion, obligation or proof of care. Without self–other distinction the phenomenon may be closer to emotional contagion.

### RC-03 · Mentalizing

- **Definition:** Representing and revising possible beliefs, intentions, feelings and perspectives belonging to oneself or another person.
- **Phenomenon:** People can infer another mind accurately or inaccurately, revise an inference, or understand someone without sharing their affect.
- **Mechanism:** Social perception, perspective-taking, memory, language, executive function and theory-of-mind processes may contribute.
- **Development:** Caregiving, conversation, culture, stress, motivation, neurodevelopment and relationship history may affect use and performance.
- **Application:** Encourages wondering, checking and revision instead of treating inference as fact.
- **Boundary:** Mentalizing is not mind-reading, affect sharing, compassion or moral concern. It can support reciprocal care or instrumental control.

## Behavioural and ethical constructs

### BE-01 · Organising pull

- **Definition:** TEG-Blue’s name for the changing pressure or readiness toward a class of actions before any particular behaviour is assumed.
- **Phenomenon:** A person may feel an urge, inhibition or narrowed action range without acting on it, or may perform the same behaviour under different pressures.
- **Mechanism:** Action preparation, motivational salience, habit, reinforcement history, autonomic mobilisation, affordance perception and inhibitory processes may contribute.
- **Development:** Repeated consequences, modelling, coercion, practice and available alternatives may change which actions feel immediate or possible.
- **Application:** Separates internal pressure from observable action and therefore preserves room for participation and responsibility.
- **Boundary:** An organising pull cannot be read directly from behaviour and does not establish intent, inevitability or innocence.

### BE-02 · Behaviour

- **Definition:** Observable action or omission by a person within particular conditions and capacities.
- **Phenomenon:** Similar actions can arise from different internal processes, while similar internal pressure can lead to different actions.
- **Mechanism:** Multiple biological, cognitive, affective, learned, relational and situational processes may contribute.
- **Development:** Reinforcement, norms, modelling, skill, access, coercion, opportunity and consequences can change behavioural repertoires.
- **Application:** Provides the clearest shared object for discussing impact, boundaries and change without claiming direct access to inner state.
- **Boundary:** Behaviour alone cannot reveal Gradient Position, diagnosis, intention, moral character or developmental history.

### BE-03 · Cost

- **Definition:** What an organisation or behaviour requires from the person, other people, the relationship and the wider environment, immediately and over time.
- **Phenomenon:** A response may achieve protection while producing fatigue, pain, lost information, fear in others, damaged trust or institutional burden.
- **Mechanism:** Physiological load, opportunity cost, attentional narrowing, suppression, relational feedback and material consequence may contribute.
- **Development:** Repeated cost without recovery or repair may accumulate, while support and changed conditions may redistribute or reduce it.
- **Application:** Makes hidden effort and externalised burden visible alongside short-term function.
- **Boundary:** Cost is not proof that a response was wrong, chosen, unavoidable or equally distributed among everyone affected.

### BE-04 · Responsibility and repair

- **Definition:** The linked but distinct work of recognising impact, protecting people, responding within available capacity, changing behaviour and addressing what can be repaired.
- **Phenomenon:** Explanation, intent, impact, available choice, accountability, boundary-setting and repair often diverge within the same event.
- **Mechanism:** No single biological mechanism establishes responsibility. Participation, foresight, control, knowledge, relational power, norms and consequences may all matter.
- **Development:** Modelling, culture, power, repeated reinforcement, reflective capacity and prior repair experiences may shape how responsibility is taken or avoided.
- **Application:** Holds nervous-system understanding and accountability together without using either one to erase the other.
- **Boundary:** Explanation is not exoneration; impact is not complete proof of intent; repair is not forced reconciliation, forgiveness or renewed access.

## Whole-system construct

### TB-01 · TEG-Blue integrative grounding

- **Definition:** The explicit process of connecting findings and insights from different knowledge families to bounded parts of the current system while marking where TEG-Blue makes its own synthesis.
- **Phenomenon:** Human responses cross biological, experiential, developmental, relational, behavioural, material and cultural levels that no single discipline measures completely.
- **Mechanism:** No single mechanism is proposed for the whole system. Component claims require their own evidence and inference boundaries.
- **Development:** The integration should change as constructs are clarified, evidence accumulates, criticism is considered and lived or cultural knowledge reveals missing questions.
- **Application:** Makes scientific lineage traceable without presenting breadth of influence as validation.
- **Boundary:** Coherence does not prove empirical correctness. Alignment with many frameworks does not validate the complete TEG-Blue architecture.

## First cross-construct observations

Several improvements become visible once the claims are separated:

1. **“Signal” currently carries several meanings.** It sometimes means incoming sensory information, a felt bodily change, an interpreted emotional message or a TEG-Blue functional category. Those uses need a visible vocabulary.
2. **ESS and CLS are retained educational systems whose components require separate grounding.** “System” identifies the TEG-Blue architecture rather than a discovered anatomical division. The definitions must keep distributed processing, overlap and the limits of “cognitive” and “logical” visible.
3. **Gradient Positions describe coordinated reference patterns, not mechanisms.** Each Position needs a profile of dimensions and candidate processes rather than a search for one biological signature.
4. **Fluid and Chronic are best distinguished through present responsiveness, persistence, generalisation and recovery.** Activation level alone cannot separate them.
5. **Baseline is a relative low-protective-demand reference, not an individual norm or physiological set point.** The name may need a qualifier.
6. **Power Mobilisation risks confusing biological capacity with social power.** Its intended meaning is extreme defensive mobilisation, not dominance.
7. **Shutdown is too heterogeneous to function as one scientific mechanism.** It can remain as accessible umbrella language only if distinct low-output routes remain visible beneath it.
8. **Conscious Participation is stronger than a simple regulation concept.** It includes noticing, checking, communicating, restraining, choosing and revising, while preserving variable access.
9. **Responsibility requires its own analysis level.** It cannot be inferred from physiology, but it also cannot disappear when physiology is discussed.
10. **The project needs a distinction between construct usefulness and construct validation.** A term may organise observation well before the full assembly is directly tested.
