Companions: [Science Grounding Protocol](../protocol.html) · [Current Construct Grounding Registry](../claims/current-construct-grounding-registry.md) · [Current Construct Claim Decomposition](../claims/current-construct-decomposition.md)

## Source-record format

Every canonical record contains:

- stable source ID and full citation;
- source type and evidence level;
- population, species or measurement level;
- phenomenon actually studied;
- result relevant to a bounded TEG-Blue claim;
- claim IDs the source may inform;
- what the source cannot establish;
- current decision state.

A source may support one component while challenging a broader formulation. Placement in this index does not validate ESS, CLS, Return, Fluid, Chronic, a Gradient Position or TEG-Blue as a whole.

# Allostasis, resource allocation and Return

<a id="src-alr-s01"></a>
## ALR-S01 · McEwen 1998 · Stress, adaptation and disease

- **Citation:** McEwen, B. S. (1998). *Stress, adaptation, and disease: Allostasis and allostatic load*. Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences, 840, 33–44. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1749-6632.1998.tb09546.x
- **Source type:** theoretical and narrative review.
- **Population / level:** multisystem physiological and health research; integrates human and animal work.
- **Phenomenon studied:** adaptive activation of neural, neuroendocrine and immune mechanisms; possible cost when mediators are repeatedly activated or poorly regulated.
- **Useful result:** provides the influential distinction between adaptive allostasis and allostatic load as the possible cost of adaptation.
- **Claim links:** ALR-01, ALR-02 and ALR-05.
- **Cannot establish:** TEG-Blue Positions, one resource state per Position, a universal sequence of Return or a measure of Chronic depth.
- **Decision:** **supports and contextualises**.
- **Review depth:** first-pass.
- **Implementation use:** resource-allocation boundary and adaptive-function/cost distinction.

<a id="src-alr-s02"></a>
## ALR-S02 · McEwen 1998 · Protective and damaging effects of stress mediators

- **Citation:** McEwen, B. S. (1998). *Protective and damaging effects of stress mediators*. New England Journal of Medicine, 338, 171–179. https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199801153380307
- **Source type:** clinical-scientific review.
- **Population / level:** human health and multisystem physiology, drawing on broader experimental research.
- **Phenomenon studied:** the same stress mediators can contribute to adaptive response and, under some patterns of activation, physiological damage.
- **Useful result:** supports separating short-term function from longer-term cost.
- **Claim links:** ALR-02 and ALR-05.
- **Cannot establish:** that activation is harmful by itself, that cortisol is a stress meter or that every prolonged response produces damage.
- **Decision:** **supports with boundary**.
- **Review depth:** first-pass.
- **Implementation use:** cost/function boundary.

<a id="src-alr-s03"></a>
## ALR-S03 · Ramsay and Woods 2014 · Homeostasis and allostasis

- **Citation:** Ramsay, D. S., & Woods, S. C. (2014). *Clarifying the roles of homeostasis and allostasis in physiological regulation*. Psychological Review, 121(2), 225–247. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0035942
- **Source type:** theoretical review and conceptual clarification.
- **Population / level:** physiological regulation across systems and experimental traditions.
- **Phenomenon studied:** differences and overlap between homeostatic and allostatic accounts.
- **Useful result:** shows that “homeostasis reacts; allostasis predicts” is too simple and that definitions vary across authors.
- **Claim links:** ALR-01 and ALR-03.
- **Cannot establish:** one settled regulatory theory or a clean division between reactive Body Signals and predictive Situation Signals.
- **Decision:** **challenges oversimplification**.
- **Review depth:** first-pass.
- **Implementation use:** terminology boundary.

## ALR-S04 · Herman et al. 2016 · HPA response regulation

- **Citation:** Herman, J. P., McKlveen, J. M., Ghosal, S., Kopp, B., Wulsin, A., Makinson, R., Scheimann, J., & Myers, B. (2016). *Regulation of the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenocortical stress response*. Comprehensive Physiology, 6(2), 603–621. https://doi.org/10.1002/cphy.c150015
- **Source type:** mechanistic review.
- **Population / level:** primarily animal neuroendocrine evidence with implications for mammalian stress physiology.
- **Phenomenon studied:** initiation, feedback and recovery of HPA-axis responses across stressor types.
- **Useful result:** glucocorticoid responses are delayed and longer-lasting relative to some faster response channels; response time courses depend on active feedback and passive clearance.
- **Claim links:** ALR-02 and ALR-04.
- **Cannot establish:** subjective completion of a human event, relational Return or a Gradient Position from cortisol.
- **Decision:** **supports a multi-timescale account**.
- **Review depth:** first-pass.
- **Implementation use:** multi-channel and multi-timescale recovery boundary.

- **Citation:** Mauss, D., Li, J., Schmidt, B., Angerer, P., & Jarczok, M. N. (2015). *Measuring allostatic load in the workforce: A systematic review*. Industrial Health, 53(1), 5–20. https://doi.org/10.2486/indhealth.2014-0122

- **Citation:** McCrory, C., McLoughlin, S., Layte, R., Ni Cheallaigh, C., O’Halloran, A. M., Barros, H., Berkman, L. F., Bochud, M., Crimmins, E. M., Farrell, M. # Learning, controllability, generalisation and updating

<a id="src-lcu-s01"></a>
## LCU-S01 · Maier and Seligman 2016 · Learned helplessness at fifty

- **Citation:** Maier, S. F., & Seligman, M. E. P. (2016). *Learned helplessness at fifty: Insights from neuroscience*. Psychological Review, 123(4), 349–367. https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000033
- **Source type:** theoretical reassessment integrating behavioural and neuroscience research.
- **Population / level:** primarily animal experimental evidence with conceptual implications for humans.
- **Phenomenon studied:** effects of uncontrollable aversive stimulation and detection or exercise of control.
- **Useful result:** reframes the older account: uncontrollable aversive stimulation recruits a default passive/defensive response, while detecting and exercising control engages processes that inhibit or modify its later effects.
- **Claim links:** LCU-06 and LCU-07.
- **Cannot establish:** that a person has “learned helplessness”, lacks agency or will fail to act across contexts.
- **Decision:** **supports controllability; replaces the older shorthand**.
- **Review depth:** first-pass.
- **Implementation use:** controllability and action-expectancy boundary.

- **Population / level:** human and animal conditioning research.

# ESS condition-reading and emotional significance

<a id="src-esr-s01"></a>

- **Source type / design:** integrative review emphasising experimental emotion research.
- **Population / level:** human behavioural, experiential and psychophysiological research.

## Migration and maintenance rules

1. New sources receive the next stable ID within their claim family or a new family prefix when necessary.
2. Pilots and crosswalks link to these records and retain only the claim-specific reason for using a source.
3. Citation corrections, population limits and decision changes are made here once.
4. A source can link to several claim families, but each link must state the exact supported relation.
5. Contradictory or critical sources receive full records rather than being placed in an informal caution list.
6. Active-page citations should link to a source only after the relevant claim and boundary have been reviewed for that use.
