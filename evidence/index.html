Does not establish
That one study validates the whole architecture, or that a TEG-Blue synthesis is an established mechanism, diagnosis or clinical instrument. A Level 3 architectural term does not need to appear under the same name in Level 1 research; the test is whether Levels 1 and 2 responsibly ground its territory.

The governing rule

Match the study design to the claim, and the evidential demand to the level.

Acute experiments can ground immediate organisation but not the developmental origin of a recurrent pattern. Cross-sectional group differences can locate an association but cannot show how it formed. A chronic diagnosis cannot stand in for a Gradient Position, and behaviour alone cannot reveal a person's history or internal mechanism. Established findings ground functional territory; convergent findings can support a grounded synthesis; TEG-Blue terminology organises that synthesis.

Question zero

What kind of claim is this, at what level of human life, and what kind of knowledge could responsibly inform it? The same sentence can hold a biological observation, an interpretation of lived experience, a developmental proposal and an ethical conclusion. Separate them before selecting evidence.

Four moves before searching

Locate the level: biological, experiential, developmental, relational, behavioural and ethical, or material, cultural and systemic. Name the timescale: moment, event, repeated, developmental, intergenerational, or institutional and historical. Choose the knowledge family. Protect the boundary: one level may inform another, but it cannot silently replace it.

One Gradient, two scientific questions

Fluid and Chronic can share biological systems; they cannot share an inference.

Search decisionFluid planeChronic plane
Primary questionHow does organisation change as safety, uncertainty, threat, persistence or imminence changes now?How do repeated or developmental conditions change what is expected, prioritised, inhibited, generalised or difficult to release?
Unit of analysisState and within-person transition: reactivity, allocation, action, updating and Return.Learning trajectory and recurrent organisation: threshold, default, persistence, generalisation, recovery and context sensitivity.
Time windowSeconds, minutes, hours and the immediate recovery period.Repeated episodes, developmental periods, months or years, with timing, duration, recency and opportunities for repair.
Priority designsWithin-person experiments, condition manipulation, time-resolved physiology, repeated measures, momentary assessment, recovery paradigms.Prospective longitudinal cohorts, exposure-timing designs, learning and generalisation studies, natural experiments, intervention or recovery follow-up.
Recovery testDoes organisation widen, update or relinquish protection when conditions change?Can the learned organisation discriminate new safety, vary by context and change through lived safety, support, accountability, practice or intervention?
Prohibited leapTurning an acute average into a fixed stage, universal sequence or total loss of a capacity.Turning an association, diagnosis or group mean into a person's origin story, personality, inevitable outcome or unique biological state.

Stop, narrow or relabel when

A single autonomic, hormonal or neural marker is treated as proof of a whole Position. Animal findings are presented as direct evidence of human subjective or relational experience. A clinical diagnosis is treated as one mechanism or one Chronic organisation. Cross-sectional correlation is written as developmental causation. Actual ongoing danger is relabelled as an outdated response. Reduced task performance is called absence of empathy, awareness or moral concern. A protective origin is used to infer intent or erase impact, responsibility and repair. Bridge domains, such as stress neurobiology, learning, predictive processing, interoception, social cognition and autonomic regulation, inform both planes without erasing the distinction.

Two evidence labels for every cell

How close the evidence is, and how strong it is, recorded separately.

Axis 1 · relationship to the claim

DIRECT · BRIDGE · INDIRECT · TEG-BLUE

Direct tests substantially the same process, condition and timescale. Bridge supports a component mechanism, not the complete cell. Indirect uses animal, clinical, trait-level or neighbouring-construct evidence requiring translation. TEG-Blue marks project integration not directly tested.

Axis 2 · strength and consistency

A · B · C · M · U

Convergent, replicated across strong studies or methods. Supported by several studies but heterogeneous. Preliminary, from limited samples or an early literature. Mixed, where credible findings disagree. Untested, where no adequate direct test has been located. BRIDGE–B and TEG-BLUE–U are honest labels.

Minimum record for a selected source

Claim, the exact cell sentence being tested. Population. Condition and comparator. Timescale. Measure. Finding, with its uncertainty. Inference limit. The two labels. Claim level. Knowledge family. Current construct the source is asked to inform. Last reviewed.

Protected research programme

Being taught to override emotion is not one event or one outcome.

This question belongs to the Chronic search and must be investigated before any Chronic process row is rewritten. Emotion socialisation may affect what is expressed, attended to, believed, named, regulated or acted upon. Hidden expression does not prove absent feeling, and absent naming does not prove absent bodily representation.

  1. 1 · Signal generationDid the emotional signal arise?
  2. 2 · Bodily representationWas its internal bodily pattern represented?
  3. 3 · Subjective accessCould the person feel or identify it?
  4. 4 · ExpressionCould it be shown, spoken or made visible?
  5. 5 · RegulationWas it modulated, suppressed, amplified or avoided?
  6. 6 · ActionCould its information influence behaviour or boundaries?
  7. 7 · Emotion beliefWas the emotion treated as acceptable, safe, useful or dangerous?

Required context: who responded, how consistently, under what cultural and material conditions, and whether repair was available. The full protocol, with the evidence families to recruit and the initial anchors, is the carried protocol page.

One record per source

The shared source record index.

Every reusable scientific source is recorded once, in the minimum record format, and linked to the claim identifiers it can inform. Pilots, crosswalks and page decisions cite the record rather than re-citing the paper, so the finding and its boundary travel together. The index records evidence and decision states and a coverage summary; its initial migration completed on 19 August 2026.

Open the shared source record index → Records are grouped by claim family, for example allostasis and Return, interoception and bodily access.

Constructs as separable claims

A term is not a finding.

Current construct claim decomposition

Each current construct is separated into six claims, beginning with definition (what the term means inside TEG-Blue, never mistaken for an empirical finding), phenomenon (what can be observed without assuming one mechanism) and mechanism (processes that may contribute, worded narrowly). The six do not share one level of support.

Current construct grounding registry

A working intake registry that begins with the current system rather than the original external-framework list. It identifies what each construct is trying to explain before sources are selected, and routes to the pilots.

Ten claim-level pilots

The source-record method tested on ten bridges.

PilotWhat it tests
01 · Allostasis, resource allocation and ReturnThe bridge that affects biological organisation, updating, Return, Fluid and Chronic organisation and depth.
02 · Interoception, bodily representation and conscious accessBodily sensing, nervous-system processing, subjective access, attention, report, accuracy and interpretation kept distinct.
03 · Learning, controllability, generalisation and updatingDistinct learning routes through which organisation stays flexible, becomes context-bound, generalises or resists updating.
04 · ESS condition-reading and emotional significanceHow conditions become available and selected, separated from how they acquire organism-relative significance.
05 · Mentalizing, affective sharing and instrumental useWhen another person becomes cognitively readable without being affectively shared, and what more is needed before that asymmetry can support manipulation or harm.
06 · Entitlement, self-threat, power and retaliationHow a challenged self-position may become harmful when it recruits power, without treating shame, narcissism, authority or defensive activation as Domination.
07 · People-pleasing, dependency, appeasement and refusalCheckable components in place of "people-pleasing"; responsibility for coercion is not assigned to the person adapting to it.
08 · Avoidance, detachment and chosen distanceOutward distance decomposed so withdrawal is neither diagnosed nor mistaken, and protection and chosen solitude are not pathology.
09 · Control, over-control and coercive controlControl that organises the self or a task, distinguished from control that intrudes on another person's inner life or restricts their options.
10 · Self–other distinction, identity and attachment stressCheckable components in place of "fusion" and "unstable personality", keeping the fact that affect, self-access and understanding of another can change sharply under relational pressure.

Pilots 05 to 10 are the six clusters behind Model 1's configuration bridge and F3's construct families. All ten are first passes: they test the method on a bridge and record what a narrower claim can carry. None validates a Position.
