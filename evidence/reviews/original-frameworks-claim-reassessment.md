# Original Frameworks → Current Claims Reassessment

Status: first-pass lineage crosswalk; not yet a completed evidence review
Source set: the original “Research Alignment 30+ Models” table and companion framework explanations

## What changed

The original alignment asked which of eleven older TEG-Blue maps each external framework resembled. This reassessment asks a narrower question:

> Which current claim, if any, can this source tradition help us examine, and in what role?

A framework is no longer retained because it aligns broadly with TEG-Blue. It remains only when it helps clarify a present construct, a missing level or a bounded application.

## Decision labels

- **RETAIN** — the source family clearly informs a current claim, subject to source-level review.
- **NARROW** — retain a smaller claim than the original document assigned.
- **SEPARATE** — the original row combines distinct theories, disciplines, authors or evidence types that need independent records.
- **CONTESTED** — preserve in lineage but review major criticism before using it to ground a claim.
- **PRACTICE ONLY** — may inform intervention or language but should not be used as evidence for its full explanatory theory.
- **ARCHIVE LINK** — historically relevant but no current claim has yet been identified.

## What the crosswalk reveals

### The original list contains more than 40 items

Several rows combine distinct bodies of work. Once separated, the research programme includes more than 40 source traditions:

- predictive processing, threat learning and amygdala research;
- critical race, feminist and disability scholarship;
- attachment theory and the Dynamic-Maturational Model;
- executive-function and sensory-processing constructs;
- UDL, differentiated instruction and social-emotional learning;
- several family-systems schools;
- Ubuntu traditions and Māori knowledge.

The number should no longer be used as a measure of depth.

### Most historical frameworks do not ground Gradient mechanisms directly

Their strongest present contributions are often elsewhere:

- describing social and material conditions;
- clarifying lived or relational phenomena;
- contributing a practice;
- identifying power and institutional patterns;
- protecting ethical or epistemic boundaries;
- showing where TEG-Blue’s questions came from.

That is not a lesser role. It is a different one.

### Five entries need early critical review

Before they appear as science anchors, prioritise:

1. Polyvagal Theory;
2. Intense World Theory;
3. human intergenerational epigenetic trauma claims;
4. Somatic Experiencing explanatory claims;
5. broad “trauma bonding” explanations.

These are influential in public language and therefore carry a higher risk of entering the model with more certainty than the evidence allows.

### Five source families recover missing levels

The following should be developed early because the current biological work cannot replace them:

1. coercive control;
2. disability and neurodiversity scholarship;
3. Bourdieu and material/social resources;
4. emotional labour and role pressure;
5. social identity and system justification.

They help distinguish internal organisation from the environment, relationship or institution that continues to shape it.

## Source-level reassessment template

Each row that remains after this pass should be expanded into one or more source records:

1. Exact current claim ID and claim type.
2. Primary source or authoritative formulation.
3. Population, setting and historical context.
4. What was observed or argued.
5. What the method or tradition can support.
6. What it cannot establish.
7. Main criticism, alternative explanation or contested point.
8. TEG-Blue translation sentence.
9. TEG-Blue boundary sentence.
10. Decision: retain, narrow, relabel, practice-only, lineage-only or remove.

The crosswalk is an intake map. Scientific support begins at the source-and-claim level beneath it.
