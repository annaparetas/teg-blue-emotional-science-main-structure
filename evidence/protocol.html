TEG-Blue · evidence selection protocol

One Gradient, two scientific questions

Fluid and Chronic organisation can involve some of the same biological systems. They cannot be grounded by making the same inference from the same study. The difference lies in the time window, the comparison and what the evidence is being asked to explain. Read the governing model premise → Read the architectural-language rule →

Fluid Gradient

What changes while present conditions change?

Follow state-dependent allocation, action, updating and Return while the environmental event and the organism’s response remain coupled.

Same organism
different inference
Chronic Survival

What was learned, and what stays available after conditions change?

Follow development, repetition, altered thresholds, generalisation, persistence and recovery when organisation may no longer match the present environment.

Page responsibility · Evidence protocol

Scientific-facing scope · claim decomposition, study-design matching, evidence classification and inference boundaries

Governing questionWhat kind of claim is being made, and what evidence can support that claim without being asked to prove something else?

This page owns

The grounding workflow: separating claims, matching time window and comparison, recording evidence status and limiting inference.

It receives

Decomposed claims from the Signal Map, the three models, companion pages and F1–F12 reviews, together with the three-level architectural-language rule.

It does not establish

That one study validates the whole architecture or that TEG-Blue synthesis is an established mechanism, diagnosis or clinical instrument.

The governing rule

Match the study design to the claim. Acute experiments can ground immediate organisation but not the developmental origin of a recurrent pattern. Cross-sectional group differences can locate an association but cannot show how it formed. A chronic diagnosis cannot stand in for a Gradient Position, and behaviour alone cannot reveal a person’s history or internal mechanism.

Match the evidential demand to the level. Established findings ground functional territory; convergent findings can support a grounded synthesis; TEG-Blue terminology organises that synthesis. A Level 3 architectural term does not need to appear under the same name in Level 1 research.

Before the two searches: identify the kind of claim

The same sentence can contain a biological observation, an interpretation of lived experience, a developmental proposal and an ethical conclusion. Those parts need to be separated before evidence is selected.

Question zero

What kind of claim is this, at what level of human life, and what kind of knowledge could responsibly inform it?

Open the working integrative-grounding foundation →

  • Locate the level: biological, experiential, developmental, relational, behavioural and ethical, or material, cultural and systemic.
  • Name the timescale: moment, event, repeated, developmental, intergenerational or institutional and historical.
  • Choose the knowledge family: empirical research, clinical practice, lived experience, social or cultural scholarship, philosophical and ethical work, community knowledge, education, design or AI research.
  • Protect the boundary: one level may inform another, but it cannot silently replace it.

How the searches must differ

These are selection rules for future cell-by-cell grounding, not claims that every listed process is already established for every Position.

Search decisionFluid GradientChronic Survival
Primary questionHow does organisation change as safety, uncertainty, threat, persistence or imminence changes now?How do repeated or developmental conditions change what is expected, prioritised, inhibited, generalised or difficult to release?
Unit of analysisState and within-person transition: reactivity, allocation, action, updating and Return.Learning trajectory and recurrent organisation: threshold, default, persistence, generalisation, recovery and context sensitivity.
Time windowSeconds, minutes, hours and the immediate recovery period; days when using intensive within-person sampling.Repeated episodes, developmental periods, months or years; include timing, duration, recency and opportunities for repair.
Priority designsWithin-person experiments, condition manipulation, time-resolved physiology, repeated measures, ecological momentary assessment and recovery paradigms.Prospective longitudinal cohorts, repeated developmental observation, exposure-timing designs, learning/generalisation studies, natural experiments and intervention or recovery follow-up.
Required comparatorThe same person or comparable participants across clearly specified present conditions and time points.Exposure and non-exposure groups with appropriate controls; repeated measurement before and after learning, adversity or intervention; present threat separated from learned expectation.
What must be measuredEnvironmental condition plus one or more response channels: behaviour, report, autonomic/endocrine measure, cognition, neural activity and recovery.The conditions or learning history, timing and dose; the proposed process; present context; recurrence or persistence; functioning, change and alternative explanations.
Recovery testDoes organisation widen, update or relinquish protection when conditions change?Can the learned organisation discriminate new safety, vary by context and change through lived safety, support, accountability, practice or intervention?
Prohibited leapDo not turn an acute average into a fixed stage, universal sequence or total loss of a capacity.Do not turn an association, diagnosis or group mean into a person’s origin story, personality, inevitable outcome or unique biological state.

Science domains to recruit intentionally

Some domains bridge both tables, but their role changes with the question.

Fluid evidence families

  • Homeostasis, allostasis and moment-to-moment interoceptive processing.
  • Acute stress physiology, threat imminence and defensive action.
  • Attention, working memory, inhibition, pain modulation and action selection under present demand.
  • Safety learning, uncertainty, prediction error and rapid updating.
  • Social safety, co-regulation, affective sharing, mentalizing and empathic concern.
  • Recovery dynamics: reactivity, Return, restoration and renewed flexibility.

Chronic evidence families

  • Developmental psychobiology, attachment, caregiving and co-regulation.
  • Threat, deprivation and unpredictability considered separately rather than collapsed into one adversity score.
  • Emotion socialisation: coaching, dismissal, punishment, invalidation and suppression.
  • Repeated and prolonged stress, cumulative load and impaired recovery.
  • Conditioning, sensitisation, threat generalisation, safety discrimination and extinction.
  • Habit, perseverative cognition, learned controllability and context-insensitive prediction.
  • Dissociation, immobility, exhaustion, relational adaptation and recovery across time.
Bridge domains do not erase the distinction.

Stress neurobiology, learning, predictive processing, interoception, social cognition and autonomic regulation may inform both tables. Fluid asks how they allocate resources during a present event. Chronic asks how experience changes their thresholds, coupling, interpretation, recurrence and capacity to update.

Selection boundaries

A source belongs only when its methods can carry the claim assigned to it.

Include or prioritise when

  • The population, condition, timescale and comparator are explicit.
  • The measured variable matches the proposed process rather than merely sitting nearby.
  • State change is separated from stable individual difference.
  • Present threat is distinguished from learned threat expectation.
  • Null, mixed and resilience findings are retained alongside confirming results.
  • The source permits a narrower claim than the Gradient cell currently makes.

Stop, narrow or relabel when

  • A single autonomic, hormonal or neural marker is treated as proof of a whole Position.
  • Animal findings are presented as direct evidence of human subjective or relational experience.
  • A clinical diagnosis is treated as one mechanism or one Chronic organisation.
  • Cross-sectional correlation is written as developmental causation.
  • Actual ongoing danger is relabelled as an outdated response or context mismatch.
  • Reduced task performance is called absence of empathy, awareness or moral concern.
  • A protective origin is used to infer intent or erase impact, responsibility and repair.

Protected research programme: being taught to override emotion

This question belongs to the Chronic search, but it must be investigated before any Chronic process row is rewritten.

Do not treat “overriding” as one event or one outcome

Emotion socialisation may affect what is expressed, attended to, believed, named, regulated or acted upon. Hidden expression does not prove absent feeling, and absent naming does not prove absent bodily representation.

  1. 1 · Signal generation
    Did the emotional signal arise?
  2. 2 · Bodily representation
    Was its internal bodily pattern represented?
  3. 3 · Subjective access
    Could the person feel or identify it?
  4. 4 · Expression
    Could it be shown, spoken or made visible?
  5. 5 · Regulation
    Was it modulated, suppressed, amplified or avoided?
  6. 6 · Action
    Could its information influence behaviour or boundaries?
  7. 7 · Emotion belief
    Was the emotion treated as acceptable, safe, useful or dangerous?
  8. Required context
    Who responded, how consistently, under what cultural and material conditions, and whether repair was available?

Two evidence labels for every future cell

One axis records how closely the evidence matches the claim. The other records how strong, mixed or incomplete that evidence is. Keeping them separate prevents “indirect but replicated” from being confused with “directly tested.”

Axis 1 · Relationship to the claim

How far does the source travel before it reaches the cell?

DIRECT
Tests substantially the same process, condition and timescale.
BRIDGE
Supports a component mechanism, but not the complete cell or Position.
INDIRECT
Uses animal, clinical, trait-level or neighbouring-construct evidence requiring translation.
TEG-BLUE
Marks project integration or interpretation not directly tested.

Axis 2 · Strength and consistency

How stable is the result across the available evidence?

A · CONVERGENT
Replicated across strong studies or methods.
B · SUPPORTED
Supported by several studies but heterogeneous or context-dependent.
C · PRELIMINARY
Limited samples, single studies or an early literature.
M · MIXED
Credible findings disagree or moderators change the direction.
U · UNTESTED
No adequate direct test has been located.

Example: BRIDGE–B means several studies support a component mechanism, while TEG-BLUE–U clearly identifies an integration the project proposes but science has not directly tested.

Minimum record for a selected source

A citation should remain traceable to what it can—and cannot—support.

Claim
The exact cell sentence or clause being tested.
Population
Species, age, sample, clinical status and relevant context.
Condition
Present event, exposure history, manipulation and comparator.
Timescale
Acute, recovery, repeated, developmental or longitudinal.
Measure
Behaviour, report, physiology, hormone, neural signal or observation.
Finding
What changed, for whom, compared with what and with what uncertainty.
Inference limit
What the design cannot establish.
Evidence labels
Relationship label plus strength label, for example DIRECT–B or TEG-BLUE–U.
Claim level
Biological, experiential, developmental, relational, behavioural and ethical, or material, cultural and systemic.
Knowledge family
The kind of source and the role it can responsibly play in this claim.
Current construct
The present Model 1, Model 2 or wider TEG-Blue construct the source is being asked to inform.
Last reviewed
Date the search, source record and cell wording were last checked.

Initial evidence anchors

These studies demonstrate the kinds of designs each search needs. They are starting points, not a completed source trace.

Fluid design anchors

  • Mobbs et al. (2007) · threat imminenceHuman active-avoidance experiment showing organisation changes as a threat approaches. It supports graded present-condition effects, not fixed personality stages.
  • Hermans et al. (2011) · acute stress network reconfigurationExperimental human evidence linking acute stress and noradrenergic participation to large-scale network reconfiguration. It does not define one complete Gradient Position.
  • Kanske et al. (2016) · affective sharing and mentalizingBehavioural and neural evidence that empathy and mentalizing are dissociable. It supports separate rows, not perfect inference about either process in one person.
  • Toledo et al. (2024) · real-time stress dynamicsIntensive within-person sampling distinguishes reactivity, recovery and stressor pile-up. It illustrates why dynamic Fluid claims require moment-level data.

Chronic design anchors

  • Gee et al. (2014) · caregiver bufferingDevelopmental human evidence that caregiver presence can alter regulation and amygdala–prefrontal coupling during childhood. It supports developmental context, not a universal pathway.
  • Halligan et al. (2013) · longitudinal regulation developmentRepeated observation from infancy to age five links psychosocial risk, maternal sensitivity and regulation trajectories. Associations still require care with causal wording.
  • Stegmann et al. (2018) · fear generalisationExperimental comparison in adults with prolonged childhood-maltreatment-related PTSD separates risk judgement, uncertainty and generalisation components.
  • Edler et al. (2025) · parental responses to emotionLongitudinal family-profile evidence connects emotion-dismissing patterns with later adjustment while preserving heterogeneity across caregivers and families.

Counterevidence is part of grounding. A large prospective population cohort found no association between measured childhood adversity and facial-emotion recognition deficits (population-based study, 2018), while a longitudinal study found enhanced laboratory emotion-regulation capacity after moderate childhood adversity (Schweizer et al., 2016). Chronic research must therefore preserve adaptation, resilience, heterogeneity and null findings rather than assume uniform impairment.

Current model boundary

This protocol can guide responsible searching and wording. It does not establish the seven Fluid or seven Chronic Positions as validated biological categories, a clinical taxonomy or a method for reading a person’s history from behaviour. Where a claim goes beyond one study’s direct design, classify it as grounded synthesis or TEG-Blue integration according to whether the step is a convergent conclusion or the architecture used to organise it.
