How the body reads conditions

Before a person can name an emotion, the organism is already detecting change, combining information, reallocating resources and preparing possible action. This page makes the less visible processing before coordinated conscious access easier to see.

Governing questionHow does a change in conditions become a coordinated biological event that may become consciously available?

This page owns

The close view from conditions through detection, integration, coordination and possible conscious access.

It receives

Signal categories from the Signal Map and operating conditions from the Gradient.

It does not establish

That detection is conscious, that emotion is the first event, that a signal is factually complete or that processing follows one anatomical conveyor belt.

Information begins before words.

Detection, evaluation and bodily adjustment can occur without conscious report.

Emotion is not the first event.

It emerges within a wider pattern of biological relevance, organisation and action readiness.

The pathway is not a conveyor belt.

Its functions overlap, recur and influence what the organism notices next.

The processing field

Several currents become one changing organisation.

The blocks below separate functions so they can be examined. They do not describe five sealed anatomical compartments or a single direction of travel.

01

Conditions

Something changes inside the organism, around it, or within a remembered, imagined or anticipated situation.

Two informational questions

Body Signals and Situation Signals are not two sets of senses.

They name two problems the organism is solving from overlapping biological inputs.

Body Signals

What is happening within the organism?

These signals concern maintenance, viable internal conditions, bodily need and recovery. They may become especially visible through interoceptive information, but they are not identical to interoception.

breathingcirculationtemperaturepaingutenergyhydrationfatigue

Situation Signals

What is happening between the organism and its situation?

These signals concern relationship, opportunity, obstacle, belonging, danger, goal and context. They draw on the body as well as current, remembered and anticipated conditions.

facesvoicesdistancemovementcontextmemorypredictionsocial meaning
Both groups are embodied.

A racing heart can contribute to a Body Signal about physiological demand and to a Situation Signal about possible danger. The informational question changes; the underlying biological inputs can overlap.

A crucial distinction

Detection is not yet emotion.

A sudden sound can be transmitted and evaluated before the person can say “I am afraid”. The consciously reportable feeling is relatively downstream from earlier sensory and regulatory activity.

The body does more than generate a feeling.

It prepares for anticipated needs by changing the organism that will meet them.

Detection

Something changed. Sensory and physiological systems register it.

Evaluation

The change is processed in relation to context, need, learning and expectation.

Organisation

Resources, attention, perception and bodily systems are adjusted.

Experience

Part of the pattern may become available as bodily sensation, feeling and meaning.

Emotions are real information.

Fear carries information that danger has been detected or predicted. Shame carries information about a perceived threat to belonging or social standing.

Information is not an infallible conclusion.

Fear does not prove that the present situation is dangerous. Shame does not prove personal defectiveness. Cognition can test the signal, but it cannot test information that has been excluded from consideration.

TEG-Blue translation

ESS and CLS provide information. ME participates when access permits.

These names organise the working model. They do not divide the brain into three physical modules.

ESS · Emotional Somatic System

Embodied information

ESS names the distributed emotional–somatic stream through which biological relevance, bodily organisation, signal formation and action readiness can develop.

Much of this work occurs before reflective thought and cannot be directly commanded by ME.

Not one anatomical pathway. Not synonymous with conscious emotion.

CLS · Cognitive Logical System

Reflective information

CLS names the reflective and symbolic capacities through which available experience can be named, compared, contextualised, reasoned about and communicated.

Nonconscious prediction and evaluation are not automatically CLS simply because they process information.

Can interpret an emotion. Cannot make the upstream biological processes disappear.

ME · lived participant

Conscious participation

ME names the person who may experience, investigate and act using information from both systems when enough of it is available.

ME can turn towards or away from accessible information. Turning away can change participation without stopping internal processing.

Not a controller positioned outside the organism.

The conscious choice is not whether the underlying information initially appears. When organisation and learned access permit participation, ME can turn towards what becomes available, reflect on it, test it and decide how to respond.

From process to participation

A biological capacity can operate without being fully usable by ME.

The Gradient rows describe underlying processes. The participation card for ME describes how much of each process the person can recognise, understand and use.

Where the Gradient enters

Organisation changes what receives priority.

The same environment does not produce one fixed experience. Current organisation changes sensory weighting, attention, energy allocation, action readiness and what can become consciously usable.

X

Internal maintenance, recovery and viable bodily condition can receive priority.

A

Connection

Social engagement, exploration and reciprocal information remain widely available.

A↔B

Checking

Possible change in safety increases orienting, comparison and provisional readiness.

B

Defence

Established obstacle or threat gives mobilisation and protection greater priority.

C

Persistent or complex threat recruits monitoring, prediction and management.

D

Power

Severe demand can narrow processing around force, outcome and immediate survival.

Z

Conservation

When action is unavailable or too costly, reduced output and immobilisation can dominate.

These are TEG-Blue working organisations, not established neuroscientific states. The Fluid Gradient asks how organisation can change with present conditions; the Chronic model asks how learned weighting and restricted access may persist.

Scientific boundaries

What this page does—and does not—claim.

This is a TEG-Blue functional synthesis drawn from several research traditions. No single theory establishes the whole map.

Functional layer, not serial anatomy. The blocks distinguish questions and functions. Biological processing overlaps, recurs and travels through distributed pathways.

ESS, CLS and ME are working terms. They organise the model; they are not three scientifically established neural systems.

Detection is not conscious feeling. Defensive and regulatory processing can occur before a person reports fear or another emotion.

Evaluation is broader than threat. Need, relevance, opportunity, novelty, belonging, controllability and expected demand may all matter.

Action readiness is not behaviour. A prepared tendency may be inhibited, revised, opposed or combined with another tendency.

Emotion is information, not proof. A signal can be biologically meaningful while remaining incomplete, learned or mistaken about present conditions.

Body Signals are not interoception. Interoceptive information contributes to both Body Signals and Situation Signals.

The Position is not the signal. An emotion may occur across several organisations; organisation changes its weighting, expression and use.
