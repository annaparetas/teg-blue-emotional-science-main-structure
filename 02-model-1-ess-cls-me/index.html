The Two Biological Information Systems and ME

Emotional–somatic and reflective–symbolic information processing, conscious access and human participation. People can explain an experience clearly while unable to feel part of what is shaping it, and feel that something matters before they can name what changed. This model keeps those differences visible without dividing a person into a truthful body and an unreliable mind, or a rational mind and an irrational body.

Approved model territory

What can ME participate with, when organisation changes what becomes available?

Central proposition

Human experience involves distinguishable but interacting emotional–somatic and reflective–symbolic processing. Nervous-system organisation changes what becomes accessible, and conscious participation works with the information and capacities available at that moment.

Governing premise

ESS and CLS are equally important, biological, concurrent and recurrent forms of information processing. Neither is inherently wiser, safer or more ethical. What matters is what information becomes available to ME, how it is interpreted and what the person does with it.

Equally important protects the model from recreating the cultural hierarchy it examines. It does not mean identical function, equal weight in every moment or equal evidential authority for every conclusion.

Names

Accessible name: The Two Biological Information Systems and ME. Scientific-facing subtitle: emotional–somatic and reflective–symbolic information processing, conscious access and human participation. Short navigation label: ESS · CLS · ME. Here system is an educational organising term for a recurring family of distinguishable, interacting biological information-processing functions. It is not a claim that neuroscience has found two sealed systems.

Concise teaching pair: emotional–somatic information can show that something matters before its meaning is clear. Reflective–symbolic information can compare meanings and consequences without becoming neutral merely because it is articulate.

First system

ESS · Emotional Somatic System

Fast · embodied · largely outside conscious awareness. ESS registers internal, external and learned conditions. It can organise autonomic activity, emotion, attention and action before the reason becomes consciously understood. Its speed matters; it does not make every reading complete or infallible.

Working definition

TEG-Blue's name for the distributed biological processing through which internal, external, relational, remembered and anticipated conditions acquire emotional and bodily significance and participate in physiology, attention, felt experience, action readiness, recovery and restoration.

ESS keeps visible the part of human processing that cognition-only accounts miss: conditions can matter to the organism before their meaning is nameable or consciously understood. It is information-bearing and fallible. A real signal can require attention without supplying a complete account of its source, and it can organise from earlier learning when present conditions differ.

ESS is not

One anatomical module. All non-conscious processing. The body without the brain. An infallible source of truth. Conscious emotion. "Fast" describes a tendency, not exclusive ownership of speed. Its proposed functions are grounded through several research territories rather than one ESS literature.

Second system

CLS · Cognitive Logical System

Reflective · symbolic · able to work across time. CLS can name, compare, sequence, explain, predict and plan. When time and access permit, it can investigate what happened, connect present information with memory, and place an immediate response in a wider context. It can also override, rationalise or misunderstand embodied information.

Working definition

TEG-Blue's name for the distributed reflective–symbolic capacities through which available experience can be represented, named, compared, sequenced, contextualised, communicated, imagined, planned and deliberately reconsidered across time.

CLS keeps visible the work through which experience becomes discussable, comparable and revisable. It is narrower than all cognition and is never disembodied. Its defining limit is that it builds coherence from whatever reaches it. A coherent account can be accurate, partial, defensive or strategic, and it does not know which from the inside.

CLS is not

All cognition. Pure logic. One neural system. Consciousness itself. A command centre. "Reflective" describes its work, not a rule that it is always slow. Perception, prediction, memory, evaluation and action selection also occur outside reflective awareness, and they are not CLS just because they process information. A coherent explanation does not guarantee accuracy, emotional awareness, care or safe behaviour.

The participant

ME

Working definition

ME is the lived, first-person participant through whom ESS and CLS are experienced and expressed, and who may turn towards, examine, communicate and act with what becomes available.

ME is not a third information system. ME is not an executive above biology, a homunculus, a detached controller with permanent access, or a claim of unconstrained choice. ME names the person whose practical room to pause, compare, choose, learn and revise widens or narrows with organisation, development, health, disability, knowledge, skill, material conditions, relationship, power and time.

What ME can do, when access permits

Turn towards what becomes available. Differentiate it. Name it. Examine its context. Check it against evidence. Include another person's reality. Pause. Choose. Express or inhibit. Act. Observe consequences. Revise. Take responsibility.

What participation is not

Not total control. Not absent because a response has biological contributors. Not the moment the information first appears: ME does not create the emotion by noticing it. Participation begins with what can become readable, and may come after ESS has already moved.

Governing boundary

Nervous-system organisation shapes the field of information and action available to ME. It does not author, justify or excuse the behaviour that follows. Constrained participation may help explain an action. It does not erase impact or responsibility. Write ME, never "the ME".

The access relationship

Coordinated conscious access

Working definition

Coordinated conscious access names the relationship through which some of the organism's information may become experienceable or inspectable by ME: bodily information, differentiated feeling, situational meaning and possible direction becoming sufficiently readable together.

It is not the process that first detects or evaluates a condition. It begins where information becomes usable by the person. It is not a separate biological system, not continuously available, and not an infallible intuition. Access may be partial, delayed, conflicted or differently organised across contexts. Neuroception is Porges's theory-specific term for proposed below-awareness safety evaluation; it is not another name for this relationship. Until 26 August 2026 this relationship was called the Inner Compass; that name is now reserved for the public product.

  1. PresentInformation exists in the organism.
  2. AccessibleIt becomes consciously available to ME.
  3. DifferentiatedIt is specific, not fused into undifferentiated pressure.
  4. Interpreted and checkedIt is placed in context and compared with evidence.
  5. UsedIt informs action with care, restraint or responsibility.

Five different things

Information present is not information accessible, which is not information differentiated, which is not information interpreted, which is not information used well. The steps are conditional, not a ladder every event climbs. Difficulty at any one of them does not diagnose a person, establish a Gradient Position or determine ethical direction.

The full functional sequence, from conditions through detection, evaluation, signal, organisation and readiness to access, action, feedback and later entry, is on the access page. The processing before access is on the processing page.

One organism

Two forms of work, concurrent and recurrent.

Equal importance, different work

Immediate danger may require ESS to organise action before a reflective account is available. Time and sufficient access may allow CLS to compare, contextualise and reconsider. A strong feeling can reveal that something matters without establishing what caused it. A sophisticated explanation can identify evidence while still rationalising, omitting impact or protecting status. Neither leads in a fixed hierarchy; priority, influence and availability change recurrently.

The teaching line and its boundary

"ESS is fast, CLS is reflective" is the teaching line. It protects a real asymmetry: emotional–somatic significance, action readiness and even movement can begin before reflective interpretation becomes available to ME, which is why people protect, withdraw, freeze or speak before they have thought it through. The boundary is that this is a tendency, not a division. ESS is not complete before CLS begins; some ESS processes unfold slowly through hormones and recovery, some cognitive operations are quick, and the two interact throughout. No universal milestones.

Grouping is not ownership

Memory, prediction, attention, meaning, action and learning never reside in only one system. Naming can change significance. An imagined future can recruit present readiness. A bodily change can redirect attention. Action and consequence alter later conditions.

Why the distinction exists

The mismatches are the point of the model.

Each familiar tension is a different relationship between the two forms of processing. None is a diagnosis, and none makes either system the reliable one.

What a person saysWhat may be happening
"I know I am safe, but I do not feel safe."CLS holds a model of present safety while ESS still organises from a threat-weighted reading. Repeating the conclusion may not be enough; the organism needs conditions and experience through which it can update.
"I feel something is wrong, but I cannot explain why."ESS carries information not yet accessible or differentiated enough for CLS to represent. Worth investigating; not yet proof of the cause.
"I understand the pattern, but I keep doing it."Reflective insight has changed while weighting, action readiness or the available response route has not. Real change in one part of the event, not yet whole-event change.
"My explanation makes sense, so why am I still distressed?"Coherence and completion are different functions. A convincing account can organise experience cognitively without resolving the activation or the unmet signal.
"Thinking about it makes my body react again."The relationship runs from CLS to ESS as well. Memory, anticipation, imagery and interpretation can produce or sustain bodily change when the event is absent.
"My explanation is logical, therefore my behaviour is justified."Coherence within CLS does not establish that emotional pressure, actual conditions, alternatives, power or impact have been examined.
What integration means here

Integration is sufficient differentiated and revisable access for emotional–somatic information, reflective interpretation, conditions, evidence, impact and possible action to inform one another. It is not calm, permanent agreement, maximal awareness, fusion, or one system yielding to the other. Disagreement between ESS and CLS is a condition to understand, not a battle to win.

Access made concrete

Three ME access capacities that vary independently.

Each ME access capacity names how much of a biological process ME can notice, differentiate and use. The process continues whether or not access does. Bodily access and affective sharing draw primarily on emotional–somatic processing; mentalizing draws primarily on reflective–symbolic processing. These are primary contributions, not exclusive system ownership. The capacities may be uneven, partial, delayed and context-dependent. They do not define a Gradient Position, a mode, a personality, a diagnosis, morality or an overall level of awareness.

Feel myself

Bodily information access and use

How much internal bodily and emotional information ME can notice, differentiate, interpret and use. Underlying process: interoceptive processing.

Feel another

Affective-sharing access

How much of another person's affective state becomes consciously readable while self and other remain distinguishable. Without that distinction the experience is closer to contagion than empathy. Underlying process: affective sharing.

Read and revise another

Mentalizing access and use

How much ME can represent, question and revise an understanding of another person's perspective, feelings or intentions. Can remain detailed while the other two are low, and can serve reciprocal, defensive or instrumental action. Underlying process: mentalizing.

Empathic concern is separate, and access is never a verdict

Empathic concern is a relational output: whether another person's welfare can motivate, restrain or redirect action. It draws on the capacities but is not a fourth one and cannot be inferred from them. Availability does not establish accuracy, concern or ethical use. Do not infer innocence, guilt, safety, harmfulness, personality or diagnosis from any configuration.

The controls live on the add-on, the ME Access Dials: three graded sliders, one per capacity, showing what becomes readable and the configuration they combine into. The eight binary configurations are landmarks within the graded field, read through five quality dimensions: availability, differentiation, self–other distinction, accuracy and updating, and flexible recruitment. The configuration does not assign a mode. The Inner Compass reads the setting alongside Model 2 organisation, position and plane.

Ethical direction

Ethical direction sits after access, not inside either system.

  1. Information becomes usableThrough coordinated conscious access.
  2. CareProtection, communication, restraint, repair.
  3. Or controlPersuasion, concealment, strategic control, exploitation.
  4. Behaviour and impact decidePower, feedback and consequence show what occurred.
  5. Separate evidence planeAssessed directly, never inferred from access.

Knowledge has no automatic direction

Understanding another person is not sharing their feeling, which is not caring what happens to them, which is not choosing not to exploit what is understood. Emotional knowledge can support care or more precise manipulation. Reduced access can explain a pattern; it cannot establish innocence or diminished responsibility.

Hurt is not harm

Hurt names pain, offence, fear, loss, shame or disappointment. Harm concerns damage, violation, coercion, deprivation or restriction of safety and agency. Hurt can signal harm. Hurt can also arise when a boundary interrupts an expectation or entitlement. ESS makes the felt impact visible; CLS and ME can investigate; conduct, power, impact and repair are assessed on their own plane.

Coherence audit

When an account feels complete, ask: what information entered it; what was faint, unavailable, excluded or discounted; what present evidence supports it; whose experience, agency or impact is missing; what power or dependency protects it from contradiction; and what happens when feedback arrives.

Model boundary

What Model 1 owns, and what it does not.

Owns

ESS and CLS as functional groupings; coordinated conscious access; ME as the participant; the ME access capacities and their configurations; presence, access, differentiation, interpretation and use as separate questions; the processing configuration at each moment of an event.

Does not own

Emotion identity and the signal list (Signal Map). Gradient Positions, present organisation and Fluid–Chronic depth (Model 2). The temporal course of response, completion and carryover (Model 3). Developmental history (F2) and adult maintenance (F3). Conduct, power, impact and repair (Behaviour and responsibility).

Shared event record

Model 1 supplies the processing configuration at every moment of an event: ESS and CLS processing, conscious availability to ME, and possible ME participation. Model 2 reads how present organisation changes the accessibility, credibility and cost of that information. Model 3 compares the successive configurations through time. ESS, CLS and ME do not disappear when action begins.

Nine non-negotiable boundaries

The distinction without the split.

1 · Educational, not anatomical

Each system integrates several scientifically distinguishable processes. Neither is a discovered division.

2 · Distinction without separation

Emotion and cognition interact continuously and alter one another.

3 · No hierarchy of truth

A feeling is not automatically a fact; a coherent explanation is not automatically accurate.

4 · No fast–slow rule

ESS may change rapidly or persist; CLS may respond quickly or deliberate slowly.

5 · No conscious–unconscious ownership

Parts of both operate outside awareness; conscious access varies.

6 · No body–brain split

Both depend on distributed brain–body processing in material, relational and cultural conditions.

7 · No direct behaviour reading

Neither determines a single action, intention or moral meaning.

8 · No exoneration

Reduced awareness may explain behaviour without removing responsibility, power, impact or the need for protection and repair.

9 · No status inference

Authority, confidence, intelligence or verbal skill does not establish emotional awareness or integration.

Retired language

ESS as fast, subcortical and complete before CLS arrives. The millisecond timeline. Physically separate hardware. Interoception as the one bridge. ESS accurate and CLS the source of distortion. Cognition as mere narrator. ISSA as a third system. The M4, IA and RE–ER–SEA capacity architectures. "Cognitive override" as one mechanism; name suppression, inhibition, reinterpretation, avoidance, rationalisation or action against a signal instead. "Reunification" of the two systems.
