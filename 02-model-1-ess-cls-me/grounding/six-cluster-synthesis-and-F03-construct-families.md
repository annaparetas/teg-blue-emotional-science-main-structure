## Synthesis decision

The six clusters do not reveal six personalities or six deeper Gradient layers. They reveal recurring **adult maintenance-and-revision questions** that can appear across many capacity configurations, diagnoses, developmental histories and present conditions.

Emerging F3 should therefore own neither “False Coherence” as one master mechanism nor a catalogue of personality types. Its defensible centre is:

> **F3 examines how adults interpret, anticipate, organise and revise relational responses around currently available information, learned expectations, valued self-positions and experienced consequences.**

F3 can describe a maintenance route only after the relevant capacity and present-condition questions have been separated. It cannot establish developmental origin, biological state, diagnosis, truth, harm or coercion.

## What all six clusters establish

### 1 · Visible form does not identify function

Agreement, distance, confidence, intensity, structure, emotional understanding and apparent calm can each arise through several routes. The observed form is the beginning of inquiry, not its explanation.

### 2 · Availability does not establish quality or use

A capacity may be available while inaccurate, poorly differentiated, rigid, selectively recruited or resistant to feedback. Mentalizing does not guarantee accuracy; affective sharing does not guarantee concern; bodily access does not guarantee correct causal interpretation; integrated access does not guarantee ethical conduct.

### 3 · Internal pressure does not determine behaviour

Self-position threat, attachment alarm, uncertainty, rejection sensitivity, personal distress or low anticipated reward can alter what feels salient and available. None determines appeasement, withdrawal, retaliation, coercion or repair.

### 4 · Immediate relief does not establish updating

Reassurance, withdrawal, control, justification, compliance or restored closeness may reduce immediate pressure. The route may still leave the underlying prediction, option field, relational impact or future response unchanged.

### 5 · Explanation and outcome must be tracked separately

A new account may change language without changing felt access, expectation, available action, conduct or response to feedback. Conversely, behaviour may change before a person can narrate why. Coherence, accuracy and revision are distinct outcomes.

### 6 · Refusal is both an internal and relational question

**Refusal access** asks whether a person can locate, represent and express a no. **Viable refusal** asks whether that no can be enacted without coercive punishment or loss of essential options. F3 may examine the expectation around refusal; HPR must establish the surrounding power and consequences.

### 7 · Serious danger becomes visible at the conjunction

No capacity combination, diagnosis, intensity level or coping style supplies a dangerousness verdict. Concern rises through the conjunction of goal, capacity use, entitlement, power, behaviour, option-field restriction, impact, recurrence and response to feedback.

## The emerging F3 construct families

These are provisional scientific-facing families, not final public labels. Each family must later be decomposed into definition, phenomenon, mechanism, development, application and boundary claims.

### F3-A · Relational prediction and expected consequence

**Working question:** What does the person expect will happen if they disclose, disagree, depend, withdraw, make an error, lose status or allow another person to act independently?

This family includes anticipated rejection, abandonment, humiliation, engulfment, disappointment, punishment, uncertainty, loss of control and low expected social reward. It also includes condition-matched predictions when danger or cost is real.

**Does not establish:** that the prediction is developmentally acquired, inaccurate, unconscious or pathological.

### F3-B · Meaning assignment and felt certainty

**Working question:** What is a feeling, contradiction, delay, boundary, error or another person’s response taken to mean, and how certain does that interpretation become?

This family includes appraisal, attribution, ungrounded certainty, hypermentalizing where specifically supported, causal misattribution, threat meaning and the distinction between narrative coherence and evidential accuracy.

**Does not establish:** hidden bodily truth, deliberate deception, “denial”, or that another person’s account is correct.

### F3-C · Self-position and identity-contingent maintenance

**Working question:** Which valued self-position, role, status, belonging claim or identity continuity is being protected, and what would revision appear to cost?

This family includes contingent self-worth, approval dependence, correctness or competence positions, goodness, authority, indispensability, autonomy, relational belonging and identity clarity or continuity.

**Does not establish:** narcissism, fragility, a False Self, childhood origin or harmful behaviour.

### F3-D · Relational response-route selection

**Working question:** Given the information and options presently available, which route is recruited—pause, seek support, reassure, accommodate, appease, negotiate, structure, withdraw, conceal, impose, retaliate or repair?

The route must be described behaviourally and in context. The same person may use different routes across relationships and conditions.

**Does not establish:** one stable personality, one autonomic state or one inevitable sequence. When a route imposes cost or removes another person’s options, HPR becomes the governing owner.

### F3-E · Relief, reinforcement and replacement

**Working question:** What immediate effect makes the route more likely to recur, and what longer-term capacities, goals or relationships does it support or replace?

This family includes brief reassurance relief, conflict reduction, restored self-position, uncertainty reduction, reduced exposure, regained predictability, maintained closeness and avoided accountability. Relief may be adaptive, incomplete or costly; it does not prove that the interpretation was false.

**Does not establish:** a unitary regulation mechanism, “incomplete completion” or Chronic organisation. Chronic owns recurrence, generalisation, rigidity, accumulating cost and updating difficulty.

### F3-F · Feedback, revision and repair readiness

**Working question:** What happens when new information, contradiction, another person’s impact, a failed prediction or changed conditions enter the system?

Track whether the account can be checked; confidence can change; another perspective remains representable; behaviour stops; responsibility remains possible; options are restored; and later conduct changes under similar pressure.

**Does not establish:** that disagreement is defensiveness, that agreement proves insight, or that internal revision equals relational repair. HPR owns accountability, restitution, restored agency and recurrence after repair claims.

## Cross-cluster routing matrix

| Evidence cluster | Prediction / meaning question | Candidate maintenance route | Critical revision observation | HPR threshold or boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mentalizing and instrumental use | Is another person represented as a person, source of information, obstacle or instrument? | selective concern · compartmentalisation · concealment · leverage · repair | Does new information improve accuracy, concern or restraint? | Social understanding becomes harm-relevant only with exploitative use, power and conduct. |
| Self-position, entitlement and retaliation | What does contradiction threaten, and does preference become claimed right? | justification · aggression · withdrawal · victim-position restoration · retaliation | Can responsibility remain possible without collapse or counterattack? | Retaliation imposes a cost; domination requires enforced reduction of agency. |
| Appeasement, dependency and refusal | What is expected after disagreement, need or independent action? | reassurance seeking · self-silencing · accommodation · appeasement · testing | Does safety or support widen participation and refusal access? | Non-resistance is not consent; viable refusal depends on the external option field. |
| Avoidance, detachment and chosen distance | What is distance expected to prevent or protect? | withdrawal · deactivation · safety behaviour · solitude · boundary | Does distance preserve a chosen life or increasingly replace it across safer conditions? | Leaving may be necessary protection; renewed access or reconciliation is never owed. |
| Control, over-control and coercive control | What do uncertainty, error and independent action mean? | self-structure · planning · reassurance · monitoring · interpersonal demand | Can the strategy loosen when conditions, evidence or another person’s needs change? | HPR begins when authority, cost or threat restricts another person’s executable options. |
| Self–other distinction and attachment stress | What does strong affect appear to prove, and whose state is it? | certainty · proximity seeking · accusation · withdrawal · rapid repair seeking | Can source, state and confidence be re-separated as intensity changes? | Distress does not prove harm or excuse it; conduct and impact require direct assessment. |

## Replacing the legacy F3 umbrellas

| Legacy phrase | Current treatment | Safer decomposition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **False Coherence** | Retain only as a historical or early interpretive label. | available information · coherence · felt certainty · accuracy · revision · conduct |
| **Cognitive override** | Translate claim by claim. | attention · bodily access · reinterpretation · expression inhibition · action suppression · competing evidence |
| **Insight without restoration** | Preserve the observation; narrow the mechanism. | changed narrative · changed access · changed expectation · changed action · recovery · later conduct |
| **Cognitive dissonance as regulatory threat** | Treat as several hypotheses. | contradiction · identity relevance · affective intensity · uncertainty · revision cost · social consequence |
| **Growth narratives serving regulation** | Preserve as a revision question, not a verdict. | language change · behavioural change · feedback response · responsibility · repair |
| **Affective misattribution** | Use only when source and causal attribution are measured. | bodily state · emotion identification · external attribution · confidence · alternative account |
| **Discomfort-to-Harm Loop** | Retire as a named causal loop. | discomfort · harm appraisal · evidence · response route · behaviour · power · impact |
| **External regulation** | Narrow by behaviour and relational effect. | support seeking · reassurance · proximity · withdrawal · accommodation · demand · enforcement · option restriction |

## Ownership boundaries

| Owner | Governing question | Must not be assigned to F3 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **F1** | What relevant capacities are biologically available? | evolutionary origin or a fixed processing sequence |
| **F2** | How may development shape access, interpretation, expression, action and expectation? | a reconstructed personal history from current presentation |
| **Inner Compass / ESS–CLS** | What information becomes available, readable, representable and usable now? | a moral or diagnostic verdict from uneven access |
| **Fluid** | How is the organism presently organised in relation to current conditions? | adult narrative maintenance or developmental origin |
| **Chronic** | How recurrent, generalised, rigid, costly and difficult to update is the organisation? | one act, one intense state or one coping preference |
| **F3** | What adult prediction, meaning, self-position, response route and reinforcement pattern maintains or revises participation? | diagnosis, historical certainty, truth, harm or coercion |
| **HPR** | What was done, with what power, impact, recurrence and response to accountability? | inferred inner state as a substitute for behavioural evidence |
| **Return** | What capacities become available again as organisation updates? | reconciliation, restitution or restored trust |

## F3 claim discipline

Every future F3 construct must keep at least these variables visible:

1. **Trigger or changed condition:** what entered the situation?
2. **Available information:** bodily, affective, represented-other and contextual information.
3. **Prediction:** what outcome was anticipated?
4. **Meaning and confidence:** what was the event taken to mean, and how certain was the account?
5. **Valued position or direction:** what was protected or pursued?
6. **Response route:** what did the person attempt?
7. **Immediate effect:** what pressure, uncertainty or relational condition changed?
8. **Longer-term effect:** what widened, narrowed or remained unchanged?
9. **Feedback:** what contradictory information or reported impact became available?
10. **Revision:** what changed across explanation, confidence, action and later conduct?
11. **Power and option field:** did the route alter another person’s realistically executable choices?
12. **Alternative explanations:** present danger, illness, disability, neurodivergence, cultural rules, material conditions, deliberate strategy or ordinary preference.

## Active-page-ready language

The following statements are sufficiently bounded for later interface work:

- The three capacities describe what can participate; they do not predict personality, diagnosis or conduct.
- Similar outward behaviour can arise from different predictions, capacities, conditions and goals.
- A capacity can be available without being accurate, flexible, revisable or ethically used.
- Immediate relief is not the same as updating, Return or repair.
- Another person’s feeling can become information, relationship, pressure or leverage; the capacity configuration alone does not determine which.
- Refusal access and viable refusal are different: one concerns internal participation, the other the surrounding power and consequences.
- Distance may be protective, chosen, capacity-limited or recurrently avoidant; its function cannot be inferred from the movement alone.
- Structure can support agency; coercive control begins when power or imposed cost restricts another person’s options.
- Strong affective sharing does not itself mean loss of self–other distinction.
- Understanding another person does not guarantee concern or restraint.
- F3 examines maintenance and revision; HPR evaluates behaviour, power, impact and repair.

## Language not ready for active pages

Do not yet publish:

- a False Coherence score or type;
- F3 profiles attached to the eight capacity combinations;
- a Discomfort-to-Harm pathway;
- an external-regulation ladder;
- diagnostic or dangerousness interpretations;
- developmental origin stories inferred from adult responses;
- claims that insight fails because the body has not completed a process;
- claims that attachment alarm, insecurity, low empathy, people-pleasing, avoidance or control causes harm;
- any direct scale-up from adult maintenance to collective or institutional rules.

## Scientific status and next handoff

The synthesis is strong enough to govern architecture and terminology because it integrates the bounded findings and corrections already reviewed in Pilots 05–10. It is not yet a complete evidence review of adult maintenance as one field. The proposed F3 families are **provisional integrations** supported by component literatures, not validated TEG-Blue constructs.

The next scientific task is to decompose F3-A through F3-F into claim types and map existing canonical sources before commissioning new searches. Priority should go to:

1. meaning assignment, felt certainty and revision under self- or relationship-relevant pressure;
2. immediate relief versus durable updating;
3. feedback response across justification, withdrawal, appeasement, control and repair;
4. narrative change versus changes in access, action and later conduct;
5. branching response routes without a defence-to-domination sequence.

The next implementation task remains the combinatorial field. It should consume this architecture only after the F3 claim decomposition identifies which variables can be represented responsibly.
