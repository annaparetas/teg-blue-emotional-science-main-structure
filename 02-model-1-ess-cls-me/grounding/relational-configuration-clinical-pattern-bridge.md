## The missing bridge

The three ME access capacities can vary independently:

1. bodily information access and use;
2. affective-sharing access;
3. mentalizing access and use.

The eight binary configurations show the structural possibilities when each capacity is simplified as available or unavailable. Real configurations are more numerous because every capacity can also vary in clarity, differentiation, accuracy, self–other distinction, controllability, flexibility, context and action use.

Clinical and personality research becomes useful only after this decomposition. A diagnosis cannot be assigned to one combination. Research on narcissism, psychopathy, dependency, avoidance or personality disorder can instead test specific components, developmental routes, recurrent patterns and behavioural consequences.

```text

        ↓

        ↓

What explanation, goal, identity or regulation route keeps it in place?
        ↓

```

No arrow is inevitable. The same configuration can be temporary, protective, professionally useful, vulnerable, harmful or neutral depending on its quality, conditions and use.

## Mentalizing without affective participation

Mentalizing can remain highly developed when bodily self-information and affective sharing are weak or unavailable. This creates the capacity to infer another person's feelings, intentions, fears, dependencies and likely reactions without guaranteeing that their welfare becomes felt, motivating or action-constraining information.

That asymmetry can be extremely dangerous when it combines with instrumental goals, entitlement, control over resources, low concern, retaliatory response to feedback or repeated willingness to use another person's vulnerability.

It can also appear in roles requiring temporary emotional distance, technical analysis or emergency composure. The configuration supplies **capacity for interpersonal prediction**. F3 and HPR determine how that capacity is organised and used.

Research must therefore test several separable claims:

- cognitive perspective-taking can remain available when affective empathy is reduced;
- accurate other-reading does not establish empathic concern;
- affective resonance does not by itself establish restraint or helping;
- mental-state understanding can be used prosocially, defensively or instrumentally;
- manipulative behaviour requires evidence of strategic use, option-field engineering or exploitation, not mentalizing alone;
- repeated coercive or exploitative use may be informed by narcissism, psychopathy, Machiavellianism, antagonism, entitlement and coercive-control research without becoming identical to any one construct.

The first evidence pass supports this separation with one important correction: Machiavellianism and manipulation do not reliably imply superior cognitive empathy. Effective manipulation may depend on narrow social inference, learned scripts, vulnerability cues, repeated trial and error or direct control of another person's options. **Usable interpersonal information** is therefore the governing requirement; broad or accurate mentalizing is one possible route, not the definition of manipulation.

The second evidence pass establishes the next part of the route. A challenge to identity, status, competence, goodness or authority can create pressure to preserve a self-position. Narcissistic antagonism, entitlement and provocation are associated with greater aggression at population level, but the HPR threshold is crossed only when available power is used to make contradiction, refusal or another person's independent account costly. **Self-position threat** explains a possible pressure; observed retaliation and reduced agency establish the harmful relational use.

The third evidence pass separates the vulnerable side of the field. “People-pleasing” may describe self-silencing, excessive other-focus, dependency, rejection sensitivity, reassurance seeking, appeasement or externally constrained compliance. The same outward agreement can therefore reflect care, learned relational protection, acute non-resistance or no viable refusal route. **Refusal access** belongs to the person's available capacities; **viable refusal** belongs to the surrounding power and option field. Neither vulnerability nor accommodation makes the person responsible for another's coercion.

The fourth evidence pass separates the several meanings of distance. Behavioural withdrawal can reflect fear of evaluation, attachment deactivation, low anticipated reward, overload, shutdown, chosen solitude or a protective boundary. **Distance is the observed form, not its explanation.** Function, present conditions, voluntariness, flexibility, updating and cost determine whether a pattern is fear-maintained, capacity-limited, restorative or condition-matched. Configuration `100` supplies none of those conclusions by itself.

The fifth evidence pass separates the several targets of control. Structure may organise the self, a task, a shared process, another person's behaviour, their inner experience or their practical options. Intolerance of uncertainty, perfectionistic concern and over-controlled coping can explain pressure without establishing coercion. **Option-field restriction** begins where power, imposed cost or credible threat makes another person's refusal or independent action less executable. F3 examines the current justification; HPR establishes what was done and whose routes were reduced.

The sixth evidence pass replaces “fusion” with a condition-sensitive profile. Affective sharing, emotional contagion, personal distress, self–other distinction, mentalizing quality and identity continuity can change independently. Under attachment stress, mentalizing may become less balanced or too certain rather than simply disappearing. BPD research is useful for these components but remains too heterogeneous to define one configuration, empathy profile, developmental history or behavioural risk.

## F2 and F3 ownership

F2 can investigate why a capacity configuration developed. Candidate routes include:

- emotional neglect or invalidation;
- caregiving in which connection required monitoring rather than mutual feeling;
- environments where logic, performance, prediction or role compliance were safer than embodied emotional truth;
- repeated need to anticipate an unpredictable or dangerous caregiver;
- punishment of vulnerability, need, protest or dependence;
- modelling and reinforcement of dominance, entitlement or emotional dismissal;
- neurodevelopmental, sensory, health or disability-related variation;
- continuing threat requiring strategic distance or constant control.

F2 does not decide what the adult does with the resulting capacities.

F3 owns:

- the adult explanation that preserves the configuration;
- goals and preferred outcomes;
- identity, entitlement and self-justification;
- rationalisation, denial and selective interpretation;
- whether another person's response becomes a regulation instrument;
- whether feedback can revise the model;
- whether the person develops unused capacities or protects against them.

HPR then records behaviour, power, impact, accountability and repair.

## Expanded research families

### A · Harm-enabling and controlling patterns

1. cognitive versus affective empathy;
2. psychopathy and callous-unemotional traits;
3. narcissistic grandiosity and vulnerability;
4. threatened egotism and aggression;
5. psychological entitlement;
6. Machiavellianism and strategic manipulation;
7. antagonism, disinhibition and the dimensional trait literature;
8. coercive control and patterned restriction of agency;
9. instrumental and proactive aggression;
10. dominance, hierarchy preference and social power;
11. everyday sadism, with strong measurement and inference limits;
12. DARVO, epistemic harm and retaliatory response to feedback.

### B · People-pleasing, appeasing and dependent patterns

“People-pleasing” is an accessible description, not one established scientific construct. Relevant research must be separated into:

1. self-silencing;
2. unmitigated communion;
3. sociotropy and approval dependency;
4. interpersonal dependency and dependent traits;
5. attachment anxiety;
6. rejection sensitivity;
7. reassurance seeking;
8. submissive interpersonal behaviour;
9. appeasement and threat-related submissive strategies;
10. self-sacrifice and subjugation schemas;
11. conditional regard and psychological control;
12. coercive influence and reduced refusal viability.

The colloquial “fawn response” should be treated as a candidate translation until its component behaviours and evidence routes are specified.

### C · Avoidant, detached and withdrawn patterns

“Avoidant personality” can refer to several different fields. Keep separate:

1. avoidant personality disorder;
2. attachment avoidance;
3. social anxiety and fear of negative evaluation;
4. behavioural avoidance and safety behaviours;
5. experiential avoidance;
6. dismissing or deactivating relational strategies;
7. detachment and social withdrawal traits;
8. schizoid traits and social anhedonia;
9. dissociation, shutdown and conservation;
10. boundary-setting, solitude and safe disengagement.

Similar outward distance can therefore reflect fear, low reward, low capacity, protection, preference, exhaustion or a chosen boundary.

### D · Controlling and over-controlled patterns

“Controlling personality” is also not one construct. Review separately:

1. coercive control over another person's agency;
2. psychological control in caregiving or relationships;
3. compulsive control and intolerance of uncertainty;
4. obsessive-compulsive personality traits;
5. perfectionism and error intolerance;
6. dominance and interpersonal control;
7. authoritarian attitudes;
8. controlling caregiving and role enforcement;
9. strategic ambiguity, manipulation and information control;
10. protective planning under actual danger.

The critical distinction is whether control organises the self, a task or another person's options.

### E · Unstable, fused and identity-vulnerable patterns

1. borderline personality features and heterogeneity;
2. emotion-regulation difficulty;
3. mentalization under attachment stress;
4. identity disturbance and self-concept clarity;
5. unstable self–other representation;
6. emotional contagion and personal distress;
7. splitting and dimensional alternatives to psychodynamic shorthand;
8. invalidating environments;
9. abandonment sensitivity;
10. rapid changes in access across relationship conditions.

## Source-review sequence

1. **Empathy asymmetry:** cognitive mentalizing, affective sharing, empathic concern and moral action.
2. **Instrumental use:** Machiavellianism, psychopathy, narcissistic antagonism, entitlement and manipulation.
3. **People-pleasing and dependency:** replace the popular umbrella with measurable component families.
4. **Avoidance and detachment:** separate personality disorder, attachment, social anxiety, coping, shutdown and chosen distance.
5. **Control:** separate self-control, compulsive control, interpersonal dominance, psychological control and coercive control.
6. **Fusion and instability:** mentalization, self–other distinction, emotion regulation and identity research.
7. **Developmental routes:** connect only supported pathways to F2.
8. **Adult maintenance and behaviour:** route goals, rationalisation and feedback into F3 and observable impact into HPR.

## Non-negotiable boundaries

- A capacity configuration is not a personality disorder.
- A diagnosis is not a single mechanism or developmental history.
- Mentalizing without affective sharing can increase the capacity for calculated harm; it does not establish that harm occurred.
- All manipulative behaviour requires some effective reading of the other or the option field, but not necessarily accurate or sophisticated mentalizing in every domain.
- People-pleasing can increase vulnerability without transferring responsibility from the person using coercion or manipulation.
- Trauma and adversity do not predict harmful personality development in one direction.
- Developmental explanation does not remove adult responsibility.
- F3 owns goals, justification and adult maintenance; HPR owns action, power, impact and repair.
- The same person can show different configurations by context and time.
- Configuration `111` does not guarantee safe or ethical conduct.

## Architectural consequence

The three-capacity card is not one “middle layer” in the Fluid–Chronic field. Each configuration can develop its own persistence-and-flexibility profile. The visual architecture must therefore represent:

```text
configuration × quality × context × recurrence × use
```

The eight configurations are useful entry points. They are not the final number of human patterns.
