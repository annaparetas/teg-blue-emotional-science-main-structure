# ESS and CLS · Component Claim Decomposition

Status: first component-level claim architecture · 19 August 2026
Scope: IC-04 ESS · IC-05 CLS
Purpose: separate the functions grouped inside the two educational systems so each can be defined, grounded and bounded without requiring one theory to validate ESS or CLS as a whole

Companions: [ESS and CLS · Terminology and Architecture Decision](../notes/ESS-CLS-terminology-and-architecture.md) · [Current Construct Claim Decomposition](../../evidence/claims/current-construct-decomposition.md) · [Current Construct Grounding Registry](../../evidence/claims/current-construct-grounding-registry.md)

## How to read this decomposition

ESS and CLS remain TEG-Blue's two-system educational architecture. The components below are not smaller anatomical modules inside them. They are distinguishable functions that help explain what each system contributes and which research territories may ground that contribution.

Each component contains six claim types:

- **Definition:** what the component means inside TEG-Blue;
- **Phenomenon:** what may be observed, measured or reported without assuming one mechanism;
- **Mechanism:** processes that may contribute and require source-level evidence;
- **Development:** ways maturation, experience and conditions may change the component;
- **Application:** what the distinction helps someone notice or examine;
- **Boundary:** what the component must not be used to infer.

The research territories route later evidence work. Their presence does not mean that each territory validates the TEG-Blue component or its place inside ESS or CLS.

## Ownership rules

1. **Grouping is not exclusive ownership.** A function can be characteristic of ESS or CLS while drawing on processes that also contribute elsewhere.
2. **Prediction and memory cross both systems.** ESS concerns learned emotional-somatic weighting; CLS concerns representable recollection, anticipation and comparison. Neither owns prediction or memory in full.
3. **Felt access is a bridge.** ESS may participate in forming and carrying information; bodily information access and use, ME and Conscious Participation describe whether and how that information becomes consciously usable.
4. **Recovery is wider than ESS.** ESS may carry changes that participate in recovery and restoration. Return remains the whole-system construct for completion, updating, renewed availability and relational repair where relevant.
5. **CLS is narrower than cognition.** Perception, learning, evaluation and action selection do not become CLS merely because they are cognitive.
6. **Neither system determines behaviour.** Behaviour remains a person-in-context outcome involving available capacity, conditions, learning, power, consequences and participation.

# Part I · ESS components

## ESS-01 · Condition-reading

- **Definition:** the ESS grouping for how internal, external, relational, remembered and anticipated conditions become biologically available and relevant to the organism.
- **Phenomenon:** a person may orient, brace, soften, withdraw, mobilise or feel that something matters before they can explain which feature became relevant.
- **Mechanism:** sensory and interoceptive processing, attention, salience, appraisal, memory, prediction and affordance-related processes may contribute.
- **Development:** maturation, disability, health, repeated exposure, caregiving, learning and current organisation may change what is detected, selected or given weight.
- **Application:** encourages the question “What conditions might the organism be reading?” before treating a response as irrational or self-explanatory.
- **Boundary:** condition-reading is selective and constructive. It is not passive access to objective reality, proof of danger or evidence that the person consciously recognised the condition.
- **Research territories:** sensation and perception · interoception · ecological psychology and affordances · salience and attention · appraisal · predictive processing.

## ESS-02 · Emotional significance

- **Definition:** the ESS grouping for how information may acquire organism-relative meaning concerning need, viability, safety, threat, boundary, connection, loss, attraction, impact or repair.
- **Phenomenon:** an event may feel urgent, painful, welcome, threatening or important before the person has a complete account of why.
- **Mechanism:** affective valuation, core affect, appraisal, motivational relevance, reward and aversion learning, memory and bodily regulation may contribute.
- **Development:** emotional significance may change through maturation, language, culture, repeated outcomes, attachment, socialisation and changing material conditions.
- **Application:** treats emotion as structured information worth examining rather than noise, weakness, moral failure or automatic instruction.
- **Boundary:** emotional significance is not automatic fact, objective importance or proof of another person's intent. A signal may concern present conditions, earlier learning, prediction or several sources together.
- **Research territories:** affective science · appraisal and construction accounts · motivational salience · reward and aversion · emotion development and socialisation.

## ESS-03 · Bodily organisation

- **Definition:** the ESS grouping for coordinated changes in bodily and neural participation as conditions acquire significance and action or restoration becomes relevant.
- **Phenomenon:** breathing, cardiovascular activity, posture, movement, pain access, attention, digestion, temperature, fatigue, social availability and other functions may change together or on different timescales.
- **Mechanism:** autonomic, endocrine, immune, metabolic, sensory, motor, pain-modulation, salience and regulatory processes may interact.
- **Development:** illness, injury, sleep, medication, disability, repeated stress, practice, support and learning may alter thresholds, coupling and recovery.
- **Application:** makes visible that an emotion or state involves more than a thought or facial expression and may change what the person can presently access or do.
- **Boundary:** no single marker, chemical, brain region or autonomic pattern establishes ESS, one emotion or one Gradient Position. Bodily organisation does not specify behaviour or moral meaning.
- **Research territories:** allostasis and homeostasis · autonomic psychophysiology · neuroendocrinology · immunology and metabolism · motor control · pain and fatigue research.

## ESS-04 · Learned weighting

- **Definition:** the ESS grouping for how experience may alter which cues, bodily changes, memories, predictions and possible outcomes receive priority.
- **Phenomenon:** a small, ambiguous or represented cue may acquire strong relevance, while competing information becomes harder to notice or use.
- **Mechanism:** conditioning, reinforcement learning, threat generalisation, discrimination, context retrieval, habit, sensitisation and predictive weighting may contribute.
- **Development:** repeated consequences, uncontrollability, inconsistency, caregiving, trauma, practice and corrective experience may change thresholds and context sensitivity.
- **Application:** helps explain why present emotional-somatic organisation may reflect both current conditions and what earlier experience made important.
- **Boundary:** learned weighting is not a stored story that can be read directly from the body. A present pattern does not reveal one childhood event, trauma history or hidden belief.
- **Research territories:** associative and reinforcement learning · fear/threat learning · generalisation and discrimination · controllability · context learning · habit and sensitisation.

## ESS-05 · Felt access

- **Definition:** the ESS-side contribution to whether emotional and bodily information becomes available as noticeable sensation, feeling, urgency, need or differentiated experience.
- **Phenomenon:** information may influence physiology and action while feeling clear, fused, faint, delayed, difficult to name or unavailable to report.
- **Mechanism:** interoceptive representation, attention, precision-weighting, affective processing, conceptualisation, language and metacognitive processes may contribute.
- **Development:** caregiving, emotion language, punishment or dismissal, trauma, neurodevelopment, health, culture and practice may shape attention, clarity, differentiation and trust.
- **Application:** separates “the response is occurring” from “I can feel, identify, explain and use it”.
- **Boundary:** felt access is not owned by ESS alone and is not one high/low capacity. Self-report, task performance, confidence, differentiation, interpretation and action use must remain separate.
- **Research territories:** interoception and metacognition · alexithymia and emotional awareness · emotion differentiation · attention · language and conceptual development.
- **Cross-construct ownership:** RC-01 Bodily information access and use · IC-08 ME · IC-09 Conscious Participation · F2 access and identification layers.

## ESS-06 · Action readiness

- **Definition:** the ESS grouping for changes in bodily preparation, attention and motivational pressure that make some actions feel more immediate, possible or necessary than others.
- **Phenomenon:** a person may experience an urge to approach, withdraw, resist, appease, freeze, seek contact, protect, rest or act without carrying out that response.
- **Mechanism:** motor preparation, motivational systems, affordance perception, defensive circuits, autonomic mobilisation, inhibition, pain modulation and learned action values may contribute.
- **Development:** reinforcement, modelling, practice, coercion, available escape or support, disability and repeated consequences may widen or narrow the actions that feel viable.
- **Application:** helps distinguish emotional-somatic pressure from behaviour and preserves the possibility that the same pressure can lead to several actions.
- **Boundary:** action readiness is not action, intent, inevitability or justification. It cannot establish aggression, consent, domination, care or responsibility.
- **Research territories:** action tendency and action readiness · motivational neuroscience · defensive behaviour · affordances · motor preparation · inhibitory control.
- **Cross-construct ownership:** BE-01 Organising pull · BE-02 Behaviour · IC-09 Conscious Participation.

## ESS-07 · Recovery and restorative participation

- **Definition:** the ESS grouping for emotional-somatic changes through which immediate demand may reduce, protective readiness may loosen and bodily information concerning fatigue, pain, hunger, comfort or restoration may become more available.
- **Phenomenon:** visible behaviour, autonomic activity, endocrine activity, attention, pain, sleep, felt safety and relational openness may change at different speeds after an event.
- **Mechanism:** stress-response recovery, parasympathetic and sympathetic regulation, endocrine clearance and feedback, metabolic restoration, sleep, pain regulation, action completion and learning may contribute.
- **Development:** repeated interruption, ongoing demand, sleep, health, social support, learning and environmental safety may change recovery timing and future recruitment.
- **Application:** shows why reduced visible activation is not enough to conclude that the whole organism has recovered or updated.
- **Boundary:** ESS does not own or cause Return. Recovery is not one endpoint, one biomarker, calmness, compliance, forgetting or relational repair.
- **Research territories:** physiological recovery · stress-offset dynamics · sleep and restoration · social buffering · action completion · extinction/inhibitory learning · reconsolidation.
- **Cross-construct ownership:** IC-10 Updating · IC-11 Return · NG-01 Fluid organisation · NG-02 Chronic organisation.

# Part II · CLS components

## CLS-01 · Representation

- **Definition:** the CLS grouping for making some aspect of experience available in a form that can be held, referred to, compared or communicated.
- **Phenomenon:** a person may form an image, word, category, proposition or explicit recollection concerning what happened or what they feel.
- **Mechanism:** working memory, semantic memory, episodic representation, imagery, language and conceptual processing may contribute.
- **Development:** maturation, language exposure, culture, education, conversation, disability and practice may shape which representations become available.
- **Application:** explains how experience can become an object of reflection rather than remaining only an immediate pressure or diffuse feeling.
- **Boundary:** representation is not a copy of reality, complete conscious access or proof that the represented event occurred as remembered.
- **Research territories:** working and long-term memory · mental representation · imagery · concepts and categories · consciousness research.

## CLS-02 · Language and naming

- **Definition:** the CLS grouping for using words and other symbols to identify, organise and communicate available experience.
- **Phenomenon:** naming may make an experience easier to distinguish and discuss, while limited or imposed language may obscure part of what is happening.
- **Mechanism:** language processing, semantic memory, emotion concepts, lexical access, social communication and conceptual learning may contribute.
- **Development:** caregivers, culture, education, multilingual experience, neurodevelopment, disability and emotion socialisation may shape vocabulary and use.
- **Application:** gives ME a way to share experience, ask for help, set a boundary, compare meanings and revisit an event.
- **Boundary:** naming does not create full access, establish cause or guarantee shared meaning. A person may use an emotion word accurately, approximately, strategically or according to another person's imposed category.
- **Research territories:** language development · emotion vocabulary · semantic cognition · linguistic relativity · communication and discourse.

## CLS-03 · Comparison and differentiation

- **Definition:** the CLS grouping for placing representations beside one another so similarities, differences, alternatives and contradictions can become visible.
- **Phenomenon:** a person may distinguish feeling from fact, hurt from harm, present conditions from remembered conditions, or one possible explanation from another.
- **Mechanism:** working memory, cognitive control, relational reasoning, categorisation, metacognition and evidence evaluation may contribute.
- **Development:** conversation, education, modelling, practice, emotional safety, stress and access to competing perspectives may alter comparative capacity.
- **Application:** creates room between an immediate conclusion and what the person decides happened or should happen next.
- **Boundary:** comparison is not neutrality or accuracy. A person may compare selectively, omit relevant information or use sophisticated distinctions to avoid impact.
- **Research territories:** executive function · relational reasoning · metacognition · categorisation · judgment and decision-making.

## CLS-04 · Contextualisation

- **Definition:** the CLS grouping for placing available information within time, place, relationship, history, power, uncertainty and changing conditions.
- **Phenomenon:** an immediate signal may acquire a different meaning when the person considers what preceded it, what is happening now, whose options are constrained and what remains unknown.
- **Mechanism:** episodic and semantic memory, context processing, cognitive flexibility, perspective-taking, causal reasoning and mentalizing may contribute.
- **Development:** culture, relationship, education, trauma, repeated roles, institutional knowledge and access to other accounts may widen or narrow context.
- **Application:** helps test whether an emotional-somatic reading concerns present danger, earlier learning, a mixed situation or a pattern that another person is carrying.
- **Boundary:** contextualisation must not explain away harm, turn coercion into mutual misunderstanding or assume the calmer account contains more context.
- **Research territories:** context-dependent learning · cognitive flexibility · causal reasoning · autobiographical memory · mentalizing · social and cultural cognition.

## CLS-05 · Reflective prediction and anticipation

- **Definition:** the CLS grouping for consciously representable expectations, simulations and possible futures used to anticipate outcomes and prepare choices.
- **Phenomenon:** a person may imagine what could happen, rehearse a conversation, foresee consequences or construct a feared future that changes present bodily organisation.
- **Mechanism:** prospective memory, episodic future thinking, counterfactual reasoning, planning, imagery, working memory and valuation may contribute.
- **Development:** language, experience, anxiety, culture, education, controllability and feedback may shape which futures are imagined and trusted.
- **Application:** makes it possible to consider consequences and alternatives before acting, while also revealing how imagined conditions may reconstruct present demand.
- **Boundary:** CLS does not own prediction. Much predictive processing is non-conscious and may contribute to ESS. A vivid or coherent forecast is not evidence that the outcome will occur.
- **Research territories:** prospective cognition · episodic future thinking · counterfactual reasoning · anticipatory regulation · planning and decision-making.

## CLS-06 · Narrative and explanation

- **Definition:** the CLS grouping for arranging events, feelings, causes, identities and consequences into an account that makes experience communicable and coherent.
- **Phenomenon:** people may explain why they acted, construct a story about who they are or connect current experience with earlier events.
- **Mechanism:** autobiographical memory, narrative construction, causal inference, language, self-representation and social meaning-making may contribute.
- **Development:** family stories, culture, attachment, education, power, repeated feedback and access to alternative accounts may shape which narratives become available.
- **Application:** supports meaning, continuity, communication and revision while making rationalisation and imposed stories available for examination.
- **Boundary:** coherence is not accuracy. Narrative can omit bodily information, another person's reality, power or impact; a repeated story does not establish identity or historical fact.
- **Research territories:** autobiographical memory · narrative identity · causal inference · self-concept · cultural and discursive psychology.

## CLS-07 · Planning and response organisation

- **Definition:** the CLS grouping for holding goals, rules, alternatives and anticipated consequences in ways that can organise action across time.
- **Phenomenon:** a person may pause, sequence steps, inhibit one response, choose another, seek support, prepare a boundary or organise strategic control.
- **Mechanism:** executive function, working memory, planning, inhibitory control, goal maintenance, decision-making and social cognition may contribute.
- **Development:** maturation, education, practice, stress, disability, sleep, reinforcement, available resources and power may change planning and control.
- **Application:** makes deliberate alternatives and consequences visible without assuming that planning is emotionally integrated, empathic or safe.
- **Boundary:** planning is not wisdom, free choice without constraint or moral action. Reflective capacity can support care and repair or calculated domination and concealment.
- **Research territories:** executive function · cognitive control · goal-directed behaviour · planning · inhibition · decision science.

## CLS-08 · Reconsideration and revision

- **Definition:** the CLS grouping for returning to an interpretation, conclusion, plan or self-explanation and changing it when new information becomes usable.
- **Phenomenon:** a person may recognise uncertainty, revise what they thought happened, include another perspective or change a planned response.
- **Mechanism:** metacognition, cognitive flexibility, error monitoring, belief updating, memory updating, perspective-taking and reflective reasoning may contribute.
- **Development:** reliable feedback, emotional capacity, epistemic trust, education, safety, accountability and repeated repair experiences may support or restrict revision.
- **Application:** protects the possibility that the first cognitive account is provisional and that clarity can increase without dismissing the original feeling.
- **Boundary:** reconsideration is not the whole of Updating or Return. A changed explanation may not change emotional-somatic weighting, behaviour, impact or future action.
- **Research territories:** metacognition · cognitive flexibility · belief updating · error monitoring · perspective-taking · memory updating.
- **Cross-construct ownership:** IC-10 Updating · IC-09 Conscious Participation · BE-04 Responsibility and repair.

# Part III · Cross-system relations

## Shared functions that need two descriptions

| Shared territory | ESS-side question | CLS-side question | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Memory** | How does earlier experience change present weighting and readiness? | What can be explicitly recalled, sequenced, narrated or reconsidered? | Neither current response nor memory report provides direct access to the past |
| **Prediction** | What relevance or outcome is implicitly expected and embodied? | What future can be represented, compared, rehearsed or planned for? | Prediction crosses conscious and non-conscious processing |
| **Attention** | What becomes salient or urgent? | What can be deliberately held, compared or redirected? | Attention is not owned by either system and is not always voluntary |
| **Meaning** | What matters to the organism emotionally and bodily? | How is that significance named, explained and placed in context? | Felt meaning and narrated meaning may differ without one automatically being true |
| **Action** | What readiness, pressure or inhibition becomes available? | What goals, reasons, plans and consequences can be represented? | Behaviour is performed by the person, not by ESS or CLS |
| **Learning** | How do outcomes alter future weighting, thresholds and readiness? | How do explanations, categories, rules and anticipated consequences change? | Learning may occur without awareness and explicit insight may not change embodied learning |

## The integration problem TEG-Blue is trying to explain

The two systems become most useful when their information diverges or when access between them narrows:

- ESS carries strong emotional-somatic information that CLS cannot yet explain;
- CLS constructs a coherent account while relevant ESS information is unavailable;
- CLS names an experience but ESS weighting and action readiness do not update;
- ESS shifts, but CLS continues to organise around an older story;
- both systems carry partial information and ME treats one as complete;
- both systems cooperate in a pattern that protects the person while harming someone else.

The purpose of integration is not permanent agreement. It is enough access and distinction for signals, interpretations, conditions, evidence, impact and possible action to inform one another.

## Relationship with ME and Conscious Participation

ESS and CLS describe information processing. ME names the lived person who may participate with what becomes available.

Participation may include:

1. noticing bodily or emotional change;
2. recognising that a thought or feeling is information rather than the whole situation;
3. differentiating sensation, emotion, memory, prediction, interpretation and need;
4. comparing the first account with present conditions and evidence;
5. including another person's experience, power and impact;
6. pausing, communicating, choosing or seeking support where those actions are available;
7. observing consequences and revising future response.

This sequence describes possible participation, not a demand for immediate reflection and not proof of unrestricted free will.

## Relationship with behaviour, power and responsibility

The decomposition helps explain influences on behaviour. It does not read motives or responsibility directly from ESS or CLS.

For any behaviour that affects another person, the separate Harm–Power–Repair questions remain:

- What did the person do or omit?
- What alternatives and constraints were materially present?
- Whose options, safety or reality became restricted?
- Who carried the immediate and repeated cost?
- What happened when impact or a boundary was named?
- Did behaviour change, repeat, escalate, move out of sight or become more strategic?
- What protection, accountability or repair is now required?

# Part IV · Evidence plan

## First source-level priorities

The first evidence pass should not attempt all fifteen components at once. Begin where a distinction carries several downstream claims:

1. **ESS-01 + ESS-02:** condition-reading versus emotional significance;
2. **ESS-04:** learned weighting, generalisation, discrimination and context;
3. **ESS-05:** formation of information versus felt access and report;
4. **ESS-06 + CLS-07:** action readiness versus planning and behaviour;
5. **CLS-03 + CLS-04:** comparison versus contextualisation;
6. **CLS-06:** narrative coherence versus accuracy;
7. **CLS-08:** changed explanation versus broader Updating and Return.

## Source-record rule

Each source should be recorded once with:

- source type, population and design;
- the measured phenomenon;
- the relevant component and claim type;
- the result that may inform TEG-Blue;
- what the source cannot establish;
- whether it supports, partially supports, challenges, contextualises or leaves the claim uncertain.

Evidence for one component must not be promoted into validation of ESS, CLS or the two-system architecture as a whole.

## Concepts likely to improve through this decomposition

- the difference between a signal forming and a signal becoming consciously usable;
- the difference between implicit emotional prediction and explicit anticipation;
- the difference between insight and whole-pattern change;
- the role of language in making experience accessible without treating language as the source of the experience;
- the possibility that cognition can contextualise emotion or rationalise harm;
- the practical room ME has to participate without becoming a detached controller;
- the difference between emotional unawareness, reduced affective sharing, inaccurate mentalizing, entitlement and deliberate coercion.

The educational systems remain whole enough to explain the pattern. Their components are now small enough to investigate.
