## Dual-name working rule

Every TEG-Blue model should keep two forms of its name visible while it is being developed:

- an **accessible working name**, which makes it easy to recognise where we are;
- a **scientific-facing subtitle**, which states the processes and level of analysis more precisely.

The two names do different work. The accessible name supports orientation and continuity. The scientific-facing subtitle keeps the model close to what it can responsibly claim. Neither should quietly replace the other in working files.

## The observation the model is trying to explain

People do not live through reason alone or emotion alone. Conditions may already be changing physiology, attention, felt experience, expectation and action readiness before a person can explain what has become relevant. At the same time, naming, comparison, imagination, narrative and deliberate reflection can change what is noticed, what it means and what happens next.

Several familiar tensions follow:

- “I know I am safe, but I do not feel safe.”
- “I feel that something is wrong, but I cannot yet explain why.”
- “I understand the pattern, but I keep repeating it.”
- “Thinking about what might happen changes what my body is doing now.”
- “My explanation is coherent, but it may still omit fear, power, impact or another person's reality.”

The model is intended to make these tensions understandable without dividing a person into a truthful body and an unreliable mind, or a rational mind and an irrational body.

## Governing premise

> **ESS and CLS are equally important, biological, concurrent and recurrent forms of information processing. Neither is inherently wiser, safer or more ethical. What matters is what information becomes available to ME, how it is interpreted and what the person does with it.**

The phrase **equally important** protects the model from recreating the cultural hierarchy it is trying to examine. It does not mean identical function, equal weight in every moment or equal evidential authority for every conclusion.

**Concise teaching pair**

> **Emotional–somatic information can show that something matters before its meaning is clear. Reflective–symbolic information can compare meanings and consequences without becoming neutral merely because it is articulate.**

## Central question

> **How can a person participate with emotional–somatic and reflective–symbolic information when nervous-system organisation changes what becomes available, without treating either form of information as automatic truth or using biological explanation to excuse what follows?**

## What kind of model this is

ESS and CLS are TEG-Blue educational and functional groupings within one connected organism.

They are **not**:

- two discovered anatomical systems;
- two sealed neural pathways;
- body versus brain;
- emotion versus biology;
- unconscious versus conscious processing;
- fast versus slow processing;
- instinct versus intelligence;
- two personalities or moral types;
- an integration score by which people can be ranked.

The phrase **biological information systems** means that both groupings depend on embodied processes through which conditions can be detected, weighted, represented, interpreted and used. It does not mean that information is a message with one fixed meaning or that every component belongs exclusively to one system.

## The two educational groupings

### ESS · Emotional–Somatic System

**Working definition**

> TEG-Blue's educational name for the distributed biological processing through which internal, external, relational, remembered and anticipated conditions may acquire emotional and bodily significance and participate in physiology, attention, felt experience, action readiness, recovery and restoration.

ESS keeps visible the part of human information processing that cognition-only accounts can miss: conditions can matter to the organism before their meaning becomes fully nameable or consciously understood.

| ESS component | The work it makes visible | Essential boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Condition-reading** | Selective access to internal, external, relational, remembered and anticipated conditions | Partial and constructive, not objective truth detection |
| **Emotional significance** | How information may come to matter in relation to viability, need, harm, connection, loss, boundaries or possible action | Significance is information, not proof or instruction |
| **Bodily organisation** | Coordinated changes in neural, autonomic, endocrine, metabolic, sensory and motor participation | No marker establishes ESS, one emotion or one Gradient Position |
| **Learned weighting** | How experience changes which cues, outcomes, memories and predictions receive priority | A present response does not reveal one history or hidden belief |
| **Felt access** | Whether emotional and bodily information becomes noticeable, differentiated and usable | Access is a bridge across the architecture, not ESS property alone |
| **Action readiness** | Changes in pressure, attention and preparation that make some actions feel more available | Readiness is not behaviour, intent, inevitability or justification |
| **Recovery and restorative participation** | Changes through which demand may reduce and protective readiness may loosen | ESS does not own Return; visible calm does not establish recovery |

### CLS · Cognitive–Logical System

**Working definition**

> TEG-Blue's educational name for the distributed capacities through which available experience may be represented, named, compared, sequenced, contextualised, communicated, imagined, planned and deliberately reconsidered across time.

CLS keeps visible the reflective and symbolic work through which experience can become discussable, comparable and revisable. It is narrower than all cognition and is never disembodied.

| CLS component | The work it makes visible | Essential boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Representation** | Holding some aspect of experience in a form that can be referred to or compared | A representation is not a copy of reality |
| **Language and naming** | Identifying, organising and communicating available experience through symbols | Naming does not establish cause, completeness or shared meaning |
| **Comparison and differentiation** | Distinguishing feeling, interpretation, evidence, alternatives and contradiction | Comparison can remain selective or be used defensively |
| **Contextualisation** | Placing information within time, relationship, history, power and uncertainty | Context must not explain away harm or unequal agency |
| **Reflective prediction and anticipation** | Imagining possible futures and consequences in consciously representable form | CLS does not own prediction; vivid forecasts are not evidence |
| **Narrative and explanation** | Arranging events, feelings, causes and identities into a communicable account | Coherence is not accuracy and explanation is not exoneration |
| **Planning and response organisation** | Holding goals, rules, alternatives and consequences across time | Planning can support care, concealment or calculated harm |
| **Reconsideration and revision** | Returning to an account or plan when new evidence, impact or perspective arrives | Revision depends on conditions, feedback access and willingness; it is not guaranteed by intelligence |

## Equal importance does not mean identical work

ESS and CLS may contribute differently according to the conditions.

- Immediate danger may require action before a complete reflective account is available.
- Time and sufficient access may allow comparison, contextualisation and reconsideration.
- A strong feeling may reveal that something matters without establishing what caused it.
- A sophisticated explanation may identify important evidence while still rationalising, omitting impact or protecting status.
- Emotional–somatic information and reflective interpretation may change at different speeds without belonging to separate organisms or separate minds.

Neither system should be described as **leading** in a fixed hierarchy. Priority, influence and conscious availability change recurrently.

## Shared territories

The distinction is useful only if the overlap remains visible.

| Shared territory | ESS-side emphasis | CLS-side emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Memory** | Learned emotional–somatic weighting and reconstructed readiness | Representable recollection, sequence, narrative and comparison |
| **Prediction** | Anticipated significance, expected outcome and prepared action | Imagined futures, explicit forecasts and counterfactual comparison |
| **Attention** | What acquires urgency or salience | What can be deliberately held, compared or redirected |
| **Meaning** | Organism-relative emotional significance | Named, contextualised and narrated interpretation |
| **Action** | Readiness, pressure and available affordances | Planning, inhibition, sequencing and deliberate alternatives |
| **Learning** | Changed thresholds, weighting, readiness and recovery | Revised concepts, expectations, narratives and plans |

Grouping is not exclusive ownership. The model should never imply that memory, prediction, attention, meaning, action or learning resides in only one system.

## One recurrent organism, with ME participating

```text
conditions, bodily state and prior learning
                    ↓
        selective condition-reading
                    ↕
 emotional significance and learned weighting
                    ↕
 bodily organisation, felt access and action readiness
                    ↕
 representation, naming, comparison and context
                    ↕
      anticipation, narrative and planning
                    ↕
 ME participation: notice · examine · choose · act
                    ↓
 behaviour, consequence, impact and feedback
                    ↕
 revision, updating, persistence or Return
```

This is an explanatory map, not a serial anatomical pathway. Every arrow may recur. Action and consequence alter later conditions. Naming may change emotional significance. A bodily change may redirect attention. An imagined future may recruit present readiness.

### ME is not a third system

ME names the lived person, not an executive positioned above biology. ME may be able to turn towards, compare and use some of what becomes available through ESS and CLS. Nervous-system organisation, development, health, disability, material conditions, relationship, practice, power and time all affect that availability.

Conscious participation is therefore graded and situated. It is not total control, and it is not absent merely because a response has biological contributors.

> **Nervous-system organisation shapes the field of information and action available to ME; it does not author, justify or excuse the behaviour that follows.**

## Relationship to the Inner Compass and Nervous System Gradient

The Two Biological Information Systems model and the Nervous System Gradient answer different questions.

| Construct | Question it answers | What it must not become |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ESS and CLS** | What distinguishable forms of emotional–somatic and reflective–symbolic work may be occurring? | Two anatomies, two personalities or rival sources of truth |
| **Nervous System Gradient** | How may present organisation change the energy, attention, perception, feeling, connection and action available to the organism? | A direct reading of emotion, behaviour, character, morality or diagnosis |
| **Inner Compass** | What coordinated access to bodily information, differentiated feeling, situational meaning and direction becomes consciously usable? | The underlying signal, a special organ or guaranteed intuition |
| **ME** | How can the lived person participate with what becomes available? | A detached controller or a claim of unconstrained choice |

The model can therefore explain why the same person may have different access at different times, while the Gradient cannot tell us what that person did or why.

## Uneven availability without a false split

The model needs language for mismatch that does not suggest two sealed systems.

- emotional–somatic significance may be strong while a reflective explanation remains unavailable;
- a reflective account may be coherent while relevant bodily or emotional information is faint, fused or excluded;
- an explicit understanding may change before learned weighting and action readiness change;
- bodily organisation may change while an older explanation persists;
- both forms of processing may carry partial information and ME may mistake one for the whole;
- both may participate in a protective pattern that transfers cost or harms another person.

The desired outcome is not permanent agreement or “balance.” It is enough differentiated and revisable access for signals, interpretations, current conditions, evidence, impact and possible action to inform one another.

### Presence, access and use are not the same

```text
information present in the organism
        ≠
information consciously accessible
        ≠
information accurately differentiated
        ≠
information interpreted in context and checked against evidence
        ≠
information used with care, restraint or responsibility
```

The distinctions are conditional rather than a fixed ladder. Difficulty at one point does not diagnose a person, establish a Gradient Position or determine ethical direction.

## The Regulation thread · required warning

**This section is a placeholder for a full Regulation pass. It must not be treated as a finished theory.**

The word **regulation** is currently doing too many kinds of work across TEG-Blue. Before it is carried through ESS, CLS or F1–F12, the project must distinguish at least:

- organism regulation and viability maintenance;
- nervous-system organisation;
- emotion regulation;
- interpersonal and social regulation;
- immediate pressure reduction;
- suppression, avoidance and dissociation;
- imposed regulation through control, coercion or transferred cost;
- physiological recovery;
- completion, updating and Return.

Five questions recovered from the legacy architecture may help prevent those processes from collapsing into one:

1. **Condition:** Did the relevant internal, material or relational condition change?
2. **Information:** Did new evidence become available and alter the signal or interpretation?
3. **Recruitment:** Could the immediate protective or problem-solving recruitment reduce?
4. **Recovery:** Did physiological and cognitive resources recover after the demand?
5. **Return:** Did more flexible organisation, updating and relational availability reopen where relevant?

Pressure can reduce without all five occurring. Visible calm does not answer any one of them by itself.

A behaviour may reduce pressure without resolving the condition that recruited it. Consumption, overwork, achievement, status-seeking, avoidance, reassurance, appeasement, withdrawal, dissociation, surveillance, control or domination may sometimes alter immediate experience or surrounding conditions. The same visible behaviour may also have another function, several functions or no valid classification as regulation.

Every proposed route should therefore be tested through:

```text
condition · information and access · proposed function · actual behaviour
        · immediate outcome · duration · person-level cost
        · transferred cost · power used · response to feedback
        · recurrence · completion, updating or Return
```

Three boundaries are non-negotiable:

1. Immediate relief does not prove overall adaptation or completion.
2. A proposed internal function does not establish the meaning of a behaviour.
3. Explanation does not remove responsibility, impact, protection or repair.

## Truth, hurt, harm and responsibility

The model should teach a sequence of distinctions:

```text
What do I feel or notice?
        ↓
What interpretation am I making?
        ↓
What present and historical evidence is available?
        ↓
What did I or another person actually do?
        ↓
Who held power, viable refusal and alternatives?
        ↓
What impact and transferred cost followed?
        ↓
What happened when feedback or contradiction arrived?
```

Emotion is information, but not automatically complete evidence. Reflection can test evidence, but does not become neutral because it sounds logical.

### Coherence audit

When an account feels complete, the model should invite ME to ask:

- What information entered this account?
- What information was faint, unavailable, excluded or discounted?
- What present evidence supports it, and what remains uncertain?
- Whose experience, agency or impact is missing?
- What power or dependency may protect the account from contradiction?
- What happens when feedback arrives?

This preserves the legacy observation behind “false coherence” without claiming privileged access to another person's hidden truth or mechanism.

**Hurt** names felt pain, offence, fear, loss, shame, disappointment or another painful impact. **Harm** concerns damage, injury, violation, coercion, deprivation or restriction of safety and agency. Hurt may signal harm. Hurt may also occur when a boundary interrupts an expectation or entitlement. Harm, Power and Repair must assess behaviour, power, impact, recurrence, response to feedback and repair separately.

## Care, manipulation and ethical direction

Greater access to ESS and CLS information may widen discernment. It does not determine what a person will do with that access.

- A person may use emotional knowledge to care, communicate, protect or repair.
- The same knowledge may support persuasion, concealment, strategic control or more precise manipulation.
- Detailed cognitive understanding of another person can remain present when affective sharing or concern is limited.
- Strong emotional resonance can feel accurate while misreading the other person.
- Reduced access may help explain a harmful pattern but cannot establish innocence, intent or diminished responsibility by itself.

The educational aim is therefore not simply **more emotional awareness**. It is coordinated, differentiated and revisable access joined with evidence literacy, power literacy, viable refusal, accountable behaviour and institutions that respond to impact.

```text
personal access and differentiation
        ↓
shared language for signals, interpretation, behaviour and power
        ↓
corroboration, safe contradiction and viable refusal
        ↓
transparent decisions and accountable authority
        ↓
smaller rewards and narrower option fields for manipulation
```

This is a direction of possibility, not a promise of immunity.

## Evidence architecture

The model should separate four levels of claim.

| Status | Meaning in this model |
| --- | --- |
| **Established research construct** | A defined scientific process supported within stated limits, such as interoception, working memory or cognitive control |
| **TEG-Blue educational grouping** | ESS or CLS as a useful integration of several processes; not itself an established biological system |
| **TEG-Blue synthesis or hypothesis** | A proposed relationship among access, organisation, interpretation, participation and consequence that requires testing |
| **Public-facing shorthand** | A clear phrase or image retained only beside its scientific boundary |

Grounding cannot consist of a list of disciplines alone. Each component needs:

- a bounded claim;
- relevant research traditions and their disagreements;
- measures or observable phenomena;
- direct sources and population limits;
- alternative explanations;
- developmental, cultural, health and disability boundaries;
- behaviour, power and responsibility boundaries;
- an explicit evidence-status decision.

## Grounding science map

The model will need contributions from affective science; interoception and psychophysiology; autonomic, endocrine, immune and metabolic science; perception and attention; learning and memory; cognitive science; language and developmental science; social and cultural psychology; anthropology; sociology; communication and discourse; judgement and decision-making; mentalizing and empathy research; trauma and dissociation research; disability and neurodiversity studies; organisational science; power, coercion and violence research; moral and political psychology; philosophy of mind and epistemology; human–computer interaction; AI ethics; participatory and lived-experience research; measurement science and causal inference.

No one field validates the whole model. Convergence across components does not by itself establish ESS and CLS as two natural biological systems.

## Diagram requirements

A later public diagram should:

- show one organism rather than two containers;
- use overlap or recurrent arrows rather than a hard dividing line;
- place ME within the living process, not above it;
- show conditions and consequences entering the loop;
- distinguish biological processing, conscious access, interpretation and behaviour;
- make Gradient organisation a changing availability field, not another system;
- show that impact, power, feedback and repair remain outside any inference from inner state;
- avoid up/down, primitive/advanced, irrational/rational and red/green moral hierarchies;
- include a visible **educational model, not anatomical map** label.

## Provisional closing

The organism does not stop processing what a culture, relationship or person has learned not to name. Reflection does not stand outside that organism, and feeling does not stand outside interpretation. TEG-Blue's task is to make both forms of information more readable without turning either into a ruler. What becomes possible then depends on the person, the conditions, the power available, the action taken and what happens when reality answers back.
