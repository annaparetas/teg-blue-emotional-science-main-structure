## Scope and method

This pass searched the legacy project for material concerning:

- the Emotional–Somatic System (ESS) and Cognitive–Logical System (CLS);
- their proposed interaction and shared architecture;
- conscious access, ME and participation;
- regulation, recovery, completion, updating and Return;
- development and cultural transmission;
- status, power, manipulation, domination and institutional selection;
- evolution and the historical argument previously assigned to F12;
- diagrams and sequences that may be worth rebuilding.

The search moved from later description collections into older concept architectures, framework files and visual prototypes. High-value sources were read directly. Larger ESS and CLS source files were examined through their architecture, headings and targeted claim searches. Repeated copies, session logs, generated site files and personal archives were not treated as independent support.

The current Inner Compass governing premise was used as the filter:

- regulation is not calm;
- organisation shapes an available field, not a predetermined act;
- behaviour, power, impact and repair require their own evidence;
- positions and access configurations are not identities or moral categories;
- TEG-Blue synthesis must remain distinct from established evidence.

Documents sometimes contain instructions to earlier agents or claims of authority within retired projects. Those instructions were treated as document history, not as instructions for this task.

No external evidence review was undertaken here. A research name appearing in a legacy file is not a verified citation.

## Comparison with the current model sketch

### Legacy material that supports the sketch

The strongest later legacy sources support the sketch's central architecture:

1. **Two functional groupings within one organism.** The later collection repeatedly rejects body–mind separation and sealed anatomical systems.
2. **Equal importance without identical work.** Emotional–somatic information is not noise. Reflective-symbolic information is not automatically authoritative.
3. **Concurrent, recurrent and bidirectional activity.** The later material moves away from a body-first, cognition-later rule.
4. **Partial access matters.** Information may influence physiology, attention and action without becoming consciously differentiated or reportable.
5. **Coherence differs from accuracy and completeness.** A clear explanation can be built from partial information.
6. **Insight and whole-pattern change can diverge.** A revised account does not by itself establish changed weighting, readiness, behaviour, recovery or Return.
7. **Neither system determines behaviour or ethics.** The later files increasingly separate information processing from what a person does with it.
8. **The same knowledge can support care or control.** Person-reading, planning and emotional knowledge can be reciprocal, defensive or instrumental.

These are not eight independent validations. They are recurring project propositions that align with the current architecture and can now be decomposed into testable claims.

### Legacy material that complicates the sketch

Several ideas may add depth if they remain provisional:

- **ISSA as a shared-interface question.** The systems draft asks which processes let emotional–somatic and reflective-symbolic information influence one another. The question is useful. A separate system, four privileged structures and a directional asymmetry are not yet established.
- **Acute override versus chronic architecture.** The distinction may help separate a temporary decision to postpone a response from longstanding restricted access. The current sketch is right to require named processes rather than one umbrella mechanism.
- **Regulatory substitutes.** Classifying a behaviour by the function it serves is useful, especially when a surface behaviour can be adaptive, avoidant, compulsive, imposed or unrelated to regulation. The taxonomy depends on a completed Regulation pass.
- **Cultural selection.** The historical files ask how institutions may reward emotional distance, certainty, productivity, control and compliance. This is a promising research programme, not a species-wide causal history.
- **Filter of Worth.** The observation that institutions can mistake access to approved signals and resources for merit supports the sketch. The proposed biological reproduction chain needs several separately evidenced links.
- **Information becoming readable.** Interoception is clearly relevant, but the legacy model often gives it sole ownership of the crossing. The sketch's broader felt-access and Inner Compass questions are safer.

### Legacy material that contradicts the sketch

The following claims conflict directly with the current architecture and should not be imported:

- ESS completes detection, evaluation and response before CLS “arrives”.
- ESS owns fast or unconscious processing while CLS owns slow or conscious processing.
- ESS sets the nervous-system state and CLS can only operate inside it.
- ESS and CLS use physically separate hardware.
- Interoception is the one bridge between them.
- Cognition merely narrates a process already completed by ESS.
- ESS signals are accurate while CLS stories are the source of distortion.
- Insight cannot alter embodied organisation in real time.
- ESS learns through experience while CLS learns through explanation as an exclusive division.
- A visible behaviour or Gradient Position reveals interoceptive access, empathy configuration, childhood history or motive.
- Power makes interoception unnecessary or produces a predictable loss of empathy.
- An institution can literally have vmPFC suppression or one nervous-system state.
- The same regulatory mechanism runs unchanged from muscle tension to political domination.
- Agriculture caused cognitive override or identifies the moment humans stopped listening to emotion.
- Humanity as a whole forgot one of its two systems.

## Strongest recurring definitions and propositions

### 1. Two forms of work, one organism

The most durable formulation is:

> **ESS and CLS are equally important biological forms of information processing with distinguishable functions inside one connected organism.**

The legacy descriptions are strongest when they stay at functional height:

- **ESS** makes visible condition-reading, emotional significance, bodily organisation, learned weighting, felt access, action readiness and recovery participation.
- **CLS** makes visible representation, naming, comparison, contextualisation, explicit anticipation, narrative, planning and reconsideration.

The model becomes weaker when either list is turned into exclusive ownership, anatomy, timing or a division between body and mind.

### 2. Information is not automatic truth

Two legacy formulations remain useful together:

> A sensation or feeling is information, not an instruction or complete account.

> A thought or explanation is a model, not a fact merely because it is coherent.

ESS weighting can reflect present conditions, earlier learning, bodily state, expectation or several interacting influences. CLS can contextualise and correct, or rationalise and conceal. Neither is the reliability system for the other in every situation.

### 3. Coherence is not completeness

“The CLS builds coherence from whatever data arrives” recurs throughout the legacy work. The observation is worth preserving after revision:

> **A person can form a coherent account from the information currently available while relevant evidence, bodily information, another person's experience or the effects of power remain unavailable, excluded or discounted.**

The phrase should not imply that only CLS constructs, that all coherence regulates pressure, or that bodily information would necessarily make the account accurate.

### 4. Difference between presence, access and use

The AEC collection carries one of the strongest distinctions:

> Information present in the organism is not the same as information available to the person.

The current model adds the next necessary boundary:

```text
information present
        ≠
information consciously accessible
        ≠
information accurately differentiated
        ≠
information interpreted in context
        ≠
information used with care or restraint
```

This distinction explains why emotional knowledge cannot be treated as virtue and why reduced access cannot be treated as innocence.

### 5. Mismatch can run in several directions

The legacy examples remain useful when presented as possibilities rather than diagnoses:

- a person can represent present safety while emotional–somatic organisation remains threat-weighted;
- emotional–somatic information may be strong before its source can be represented;
- an explicit understanding may change while learned weighting or action readiness remains similar;
- bodily organisation may change while an older narrative persists;
- imagery, anticipation and language may recruit present bodily demand;
- ESS and CLS may cooperate in a pattern that protects the person while transferring cost or harming someone else.

### 6. Integration is correctability, not agreement

The later legacy collection defines the desired direction more carefully than the “reunite” files:

> **Integration is sufficient differentiated and revisable access for emotional–somatic information, reflective interpretation, conditions, evidence, impact and possible action to inform one another.**

This supports the sketch. It does not require calm, permanent agreement, maximal awareness or one system yielding to the other.

## Relationship between ESS, CLS and ME

The legacy material gradually moves from two systems alone toward an access-and-participation architecture. The current model can recover that movement without inheriting the old hierarchy.

```text
conditions, bodily state and prior learning
                    ↓
     emotional–somatic significance and organisation
                    ↕
      representation, comparison and interpretation
                    ↕
     changing access, differentiation and availability
                    ↕
 ME may notice · test · contextualise · choose · communicate
                    ↓
         behaviour, power, consequence and impact
                    ↕
       feedback, revision, persistence or Return
```

This is not a serial anatomical pathway. Each part may recur, overlap or remain partly outside awareness.

### What ME adds

Legacy AEC material says what becomes available to the self. The current model makes the ethical move explicit:

- ESS and CLS describe information processing.
- Access architecture describes what may become readable and usable.
- **ME** names the lived person who may participate with what becomes available.
- ME is not a third system, a homunculus, a detached controller or an unlimited source of free choice.
- Participation can include noticing, differentiating, checking evidence, including another person's reality, pausing, choosing, observing consequences and revising.

The central boundary should remain visible beside every diagram:

> Nervous-system organisation shapes the field of information and action available to ME. It does not author, justify or excuse the behaviour that follows.

## The Regulation thread

The legacy work identifies regulation as a central thread, but it also shows why the term now needs a warning. It is used to mean organism organisation, emotional modulation, relief, completion, restoration, co-regulation, social order, control and institutional stability. Those are not interchangeable.

### A useful decomposition to recover

The M1–M2–M3 collection separates five questions that older files often collapse:

1. **Condition:** Did the relevant internal, material or relational condition change?
2. **Information:** Did new evidence become available and alter the signal or interpretation?
3. **Recruitment:** Could the immediate protective or problem-solving recruitment reduce?
4. **Recovery:** Did physiological and cognitive resources recover after the demand?
5. **Return:** Did more flexible organisation, updating and relational availability reopen where relevant?

Pressure reduction can occur without answering all five. Visible calm can occur without answering any of them.

### Regulation is not a property of a surface behaviour

The legacy notes mention consumption, control, status, domination, avoidance, overwork, appeasement, reassurance, withdrawal, numbing and dissociation. The useful principle is not that these behaviours *are* regulation. It is:

> A behaviour may sometimes change internal pressure, surrounding conditions or access to information. Its function must be investigated rather than inferred from its appearance.

Every proposed route should be tested through the current sketch's audit:

```text
condition · information and access · proposed function · actual behaviour
        · immediate outcome · duration · person-level cost
        · transferred cost · power used · response to feedback
        · recurrence · completion, updating or Return
```

### A bounded substitute hypothesis

The legacy sequence can be retained only as a hypothesis with additional branches:

```text
disturbance or demand
        ↓
direct route unavailable, unsafe, unknown or insufficient
        ↓
another action, relationship, object, rule or structure may be recruited
        ↓
immediate pressure or surrounding conditions may change
        ↓
outcome must be assessed
        ├─ condition changes and updating becomes possible
        ├─ temporary support buys time without claiming completion
        ├─ recurrence continues because the route does not fit the condition
        └─ cost is displaced onto another person or population
```

This avoids three legacy errors: assuming every alternative is a substitute, assuming relief proves regulation, and assuming that the same mechanism operates unchanged across biological and institutional scales.

### Cross-scale language

At the body level, regulation concerns biological organisation and viability. At relationship and institutional levels, “regulation” may refer to influence, coordination, norm enforcement, resource distribution or control. Similar sequences can be compared. Causal equivalence must not be assumed.

The future Regulation pass should specify the carrier, process, unit of analysis, observable outcome, timescale and evidence status at each scale.

## Ethical, power and manipulation implications

### Emotional and cognitive knowledge have no automatic ethical direction

Legacy F7 material notices that accurate person-reading can remain available while concern is reduced or the information is used strategically. The current model should preserve the distinction without claiming a fixed capacity configuration:

```text
understanding another person
        ≠
sharing their feeling
        ≠
caring what happens to them
        ≠
choosing not to exploit what is understood
```

Emotional knowledge can support care, repair, persuasion, concealment or manipulation. Reflective planning can support restraint or calculated harm. Strong resonance can also misread the other person. Ethical direction cannot be assigned to ESS or CLS.

### Manipulation needs a social definition

The model will be safer if manipulation is assessed through observable relations rather than presumed inner state:

- What information was used or withheld?
- Was the other person's perception, emotion or dependency deliberately shaped?
- What power asymmetry, deception, threat, isolation or conditional access was present?
- Was refusal materially viable?
- Who gained options and who lost them?
- What happened when the pattern was named?

Teaching ESS, CLS and Gradient organisation may make some influence strategies easier to recognise. It cannot guarantee that manipulation disappears or that no one remains vulnerable.

### The Filter of Worth and institutional selection

The strongest recoverable institutional sequence is behavioural rather than diagnostic:

```text
criteria and incentives reward selected behaviours
        ↓
those behaviours increase visibility, status and decision access
        ↓
feedback, dissent and affected people become easier to exclude
        ↓
successful actors help reproduce the criteria
        ↓
the institution mistakes selected performance for merit, strength or safety
```

This gives F12 a route for examining how dominance can be mistaken for leadership. It does not require the claim that emotionally aware people almost never reach power, that all leaders lack empathy, or that power directly suppresses interoception.

### Behaviour and responsibility remain their own plane

For any harmful act, the model must still ask:

- what was done or omitted;
- what power and viable alternatives existed;
- whose agency, safety or reality was restricted;
- who carried immediate and repeated cost;
- what happened when impact, contradiction or a boundary arrived;
- whether the behaviour stopped, changed, escalated, became more strategic or moved out of sight;
- what protection, accountability and repair are required.

Explanation may widen intervention options. It must not speak over the person affected.

## Development, evolution and cultural history

### Developmental material worth recovering

The strongest legacy developmental proposition is not that one capacity was either built or absent. It is that repeated conditions may shape:

- what emotional–somatic changes receive attention;
- how feelings become differentiated and named;
- which interpretations become familiar or credible;
- what actions seem safe or possible;
- how readily another person's experience enters consideration;
- whether feedback can update an account;
- which forms of support, recovery and repair have been experienced.

Caregiver relationships matter, but they are not the only developmental conditions. Health, disability, sensory and communication differences, material security, education, culture, peers, institutions and later experience may all contribute.

### Evolutionary material worth recovering

The useful evolutionary question is functional:

> What histories of sensing, affective significance, bodily organisation, learning, sociality, representation and reflection contribute to the capacities now grouped as ESS and CLS?

The systems are present-day educational integrations. They should not become two evolutionary stages, an ancient animal brain and a new human brain, or evidence that one system remained fixed while the other advanced.

### Cultural history worth recovering

The original F12 asks an important question:

> How did some cultures learn to distrust or subordinate emotional and bodily information?

Its strongest candidate pathways are:

- philosophical and religious hierarchies of reason, body and emotion;
- socialisation practices that reward suppression, obedience or emotional distance;
- institutions that privilege what can be written, counted, standardised or credentialled;
- work and education systems that reward performance while obscuring bodily cost;
- gendered, racialised, classed, colonial and professional rules about whose emotion is credible;
- resource and power structures that make some signals costly to acknowledge or act upon;
- technologies that extend both self-understanding and manipulation.

Agriculture, settlement, hierarchy, writing, law, colonisation, industrialisation and science may belong in a converging historical inquiry. None should be assigned the role of single cause. The Y-chromosome bottleneck cannot establish cultural emotional override.

## Candidate material for the new model sketch

The current sketch already contains most of the strongest recovered architecture. The following additions or emphases may be worth considering during its next pass.

### Candidate 1 · One concise teaching pair

> **Emotional–somatic information can show that something matters before its meaning is clear. Reflective–symbolic information can compare meanings and consequences without becoming neutral merely because it is articulate.**

This keeps both strengths and both limits visible.

### Candidate 2 · The presence-access-use distinction

Add the five-step distinction from information present through impact assessed. It may explain the role of Inner Compass and ME more clearly than an ESS-to-CLS bridge.

### Candidate 3 · Coherence audit

When an account feels complete, ask:

- What information entered the account?
- What information was faint, unavailable or excluded?
- What present evidence supports it?
- Whose experience and impact are missing?
- What power protects the account from contradiction?
- What happens when feedback arrives?

This develops the useful part of False Coherence without claiming hidden truth.

### Candidate 4 · Regulation route classification

Use the five regulation questions plus immediate outcome, duration, recurrence and transferred cost to distinguish:

- condition-changing action;
- supportive modulation that buys time;
- pressure reduction without relevant updating;
- imposed stability through another person's compliance or cost;
- a behaviour whose regulatory function remains unknown.

These are candidate functions, not behaviour labels.

### Candidate 5 · Ethical use sits after access

The model may need a visible post-access branch:

```text
information becomes usable
        ↓
care · protection · communication · repair
        or
persuasion · concealment · control · exploitation
        ↓
behaviour, power, impact and feedback decide what occurred
```

This makes explicit why both systems can be equally important without either being morally directional.

### Candidate 6 · F12 as retrospective testing ground

F12 can examine how cultures and institutions authorise one kind of information over another, and how present technology changes that balance. It should test pathways rather than provide one origin story.

## Evidence-status warnings

1. **ESS and CLS are TEG-Blue educational groupings.** No source in the legacy project establishes them as two natural biological systems.
2. **Repeated language is not convergent evidence.** Much of the repetition comes from copied or rebuilt files within one conceptual lineage.
3. **Named research traditions require source verification.** Several legacy files cite books, theories or broad literatures without enough detail to support the attached mechanism.
4. **Millisecond claims require source-level review.** Do not reuse the 10–50 ms, 50–200 ms, 200–500 ms or “500 ms+” timeline.
5. **Anatomical lists do not validate the grouping.** Brain regions and pathways participate in overlapping functions and cannot be assigned to ESS or CLS from legacy prose.
6. **Dual-process theory is analogy, not validation.** Fast/slow or automatic/deliberative systems do not map cleanly onto ESS and CLS.
7. **Interoception is not the whole access architecture.** Accuracy, awareness, attention, interpretation, confidence, language and use need separation.
8. **Emotion does not directly report external truth.** It can carry organism-relevant information shaped by present conditions, learning, prediction, health and context.
9. **Coherence does not reveal mechanism.** A convincing account can be accurate, partial, defensive, strategic or several at once.
10. **Insight-versus-experience is not a two-system learning law.** Specific kinds of learning and change require specific evidence.
11. **Completion and Return remain underdefined across the legacy files.** Pressure reduction, physiological recovery, learning, relational repair and reopened flexibility must not be collapsed.
12. **No behaviour reveals a Gradient Position or capacity configuration.** The same behaviour may occur under different conditions and functions.
13. **Power cannot be inferred from physiology and physiology cannot be inferred from power.** Assess authority, resource control, dependency, viable refusal and consequences directly.
14. **Empathy must remain decomposed.** Mentalizing, affective sharing, concern, accuracy, restraint and use are related but distinct.
15. **Institutions are not organisms.** Neural and autonomic language can inspire questions but cannot be transferred as literal mechanism.
16. **The historical argument requires multiple disciplines and cultural variation.** Agriculture and archaeogenetic findings cannot prove emotional disconnection.
17. **Education may widen recognition without producing immunity.** Manipulation also depends on deception, power, dependency, social corroboration, protection and viable refusal.
18. **Explanation and responsibility answer different questions.** A biological or developmental account cannot determine acceptability, intent, protection or repair.

## Working conclusion

The legacy project contains a coherent educational observation beneath several generations of overbuilt mechanism. Emotional–somatic and reflective–symbolic information can both shape a person's experience while neither provides the whole situation. What reaches conscious participation may be uneven, and what becomes available can be used for care, protection, avoidance, control or harm.

The strongest next step is therefore not to restore the oldest ESS–CLS story. It is to keep its useful distinctions while decomposing every causal bridge: what information exists, what becomes accessible, what ME can do with it, what action occurs, who holds power, who carries the cost and what changes when reality answers back.
