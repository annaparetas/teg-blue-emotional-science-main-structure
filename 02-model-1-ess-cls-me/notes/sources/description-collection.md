# The Two Biological Information Systems: ESS and CLS

## Purpose of this collection

This is a working description bank for presenting the Emotional-Somatic System
(ESS) and the Cognitive-Logical System (CLS) as an educational model.

The distinction makes behaviours, processes and change easier to explain by
showing that a person can be processing the same event in two different ways at
the same time. Both are biological. Both carry information. They perform
different functions, and neither is automatically more truthful or more
important than the other.

The model separates the systems analytically so that their contributions can be
seen. It does not divide the organism into a body and a mind, or claim that
neuroscience recognises two anatomically sealed systems.

---

## 1. The central description

Human beings process conditions through two equal-standing biological forms of
information:

- The **Emotional-Somatic System (ESS)** reads and biologically weights what is
  happening through sensation, interoception, emotion, physiology, relational
  cues, memory and prediction. It participates in preparing the organism to
  respond and in carrying that response through activation, recovery and
  restoration.
- The **Cognitive-Logical System (CLS)** builds models of what is happening. It
  represents, compares, interprets, names, explains, imagines, predicts, plans
  and revises using the information available to cognition.

They are not competitors. The ESS provides embodied condition-information; the
CLS provides cognitive modelling. A fuller reading becomes possible when
information can move between them and both remain available for correction.

---

## 2. One-sentence definitions

### ESS

The ESS is the organism's distributed way of sensing, weighting and responding
to conditions through bodily, emotional, physiological and relational
information.

### CLS

The CLS is the organism's distributed way of representing, interpreting and
organising experience through concepts, language, reasoning, prediction,
planning and narrative.

### Together

The ESS tells us how conditions are being registered and organised in the
living body; the CLS builds an explanation and a possible course of action from
the information it can access.

---

## 3. Plain-language versions

### Very short

- **ESS:** What the body is registering and preparing for.
- **CLS:** What cognition makes of it and decides to do with it.

### Conversational

One system processes experience in embodied form: sensations, emotions,
activation, impulses, relational responses and changes in physiological state.
The other processes experience in cognitive form: words, interpretations,
comparisons, explanations, plans and stories. They are different expressions of
one connected organism.

### For an educational introduction

We often speak as if a person has one unified reading of a situation. In
practice, different forms of biological processing may be operating at once.
The body may register threat while cognition explains that everything is fine.
Cognition may predict danger while the present environment is safe. A person
may understand something intellectually while their physiology has not yet
updated. Distinguishing ESS and CLS gives us language for these differences
without treating either side as defective.

## 4. Functional comparison

| Question | ESS | CLS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary function | Embodied condition-reading, biological weighting and regulation | Cognitive modelling, interpretation and revision |
| Main forms of information | Sensory, interoceptive, physiological, emotional and relational | Representational, conceptual, linguistic, comparative and symbolic |
| Typical operations | Registers, weights, mobilises, configures, recovers and restores | Names, compares, explains, predicts, imagines, plans and revises |
| Typical outputs | Sensations, signals, activation patterns, action readiness and physiological change | Meanings, conclusions, narratives, decisions, strategies and plans |
| Relationship to awareness | Much of its activity may occur without conscious access | Produces much of what becomes consciously describable, but not necessarily from complete information |
| Characteristic strength | Rapid, whole-organism sensitivity to conditions | Flexible modelling across time, alternatives and abstract possibilities |
| Characteristic limitation | A signal may reflect past calibration or prediction rather than present Actual Conditions | A coherent explanation may still be incomplete, state-shaped or wrong |
| What supports accuracy | Current conditions, flexible updating, completion and restoration | Broad input, embodied access, reality contact, comparison and model revision |

This table describes different functions, not exclusive anatomy or fixed
ownership of individual brain regions.

## 5. The equal-standing principle

Equal-standing does not mean that ESS and CLS do the same work, contribute the
same amount in every moment, or should always be given equal decision-making
weight.

It means:

- bodily and emotional information is not dismissed as meaningless noise;
- cognitive interpretation is not granted automatic authority merely because
  it can be expressed clearly;
- neither a feeling nor a thought is automatically a fact, instruction or
  complete reading of Actual Conditions;
- discrepancies between the systems are information to investigate, not proof
  that one system must defeat the other;
- the useful question is often not "Which one is right?" but "What is each one
  registering, what information is available to it, and what would allow both
  to update?"

A concise formulation:

> Emotions and bodily states are information, not automatic facts. Thoughts and
> explanations are information, not automatic truth. Each can inform, distort,
> correct or be corrected by the other.

---

## 6. How the two systems work together

ESS and CLS are concurrent, recurrent and bidirectional. They should not be
taught as a rigid sequence in which the body acts first and cognition merely
arrives afterward in every event.

A useful simplified cycle is:

1. Conditions are registered across the organism.
2. Embodied information is biologically weighted and changes readiness,
   physiology and available response.
3. Cognitive modelling interprets the information it can access, relates it to
   memory and context, and generates meanings and possible actions.
4. Cognitive activity feeds back into embodied processing: an interpretation,
   image, prediction or plan can intensify, sustain, redirect or settle bodily
   organisation.
5. New sensations, outcomes and environmental information feed back into the
   model, allowing both forms of processing to update—or allowing an old pattern
   to continue when updating is unavailable.

The important teaching point is not which system starts the sequence. It is
that the event is shaped by their continuing exchange.

## 7. Why the distinction explains behaviour

### “I know I am safe, but I do not feel safe.”

The CLS has a model of present safety. The ESS is still organising from a
threat-weighted reading. Repeating the cognitive conclusion may not be enough;
the embodied system needs conditions and experience through which it can
update.

### “I feel that something is wrong, but I cannot explain why.”

The ESS is carrying information that has not yet become sufficiently accessible
or differentiated for the CLS to represent accurately. The feeling is worth
investigating, but it is not yet proof of what the cause is.

### “I understand the pattern, but I keep doing it.”

The CLS has developed insight, while the embodied organisation, action
readiness or available response route has not changed sufficiently. Cognitive
understanding is real change in one part of the event, but not yet whole-event
change.

### “My explanation makes sense, so why do I still feel distressed?”

Coherence and completion are different functions. A convincing account can
organise experience cognitively without resolving the physiological activation,
unmet signal or interrupted response carried by the ESS.

### “My body reacts before I can think.”

Some embodied evaluation and physiological recruitment can occur before
conscious description. The later cognitive model can contextualise, redirect or
revise the response, but it did not need to consciously author the first change
for that change to be biologically real.

### “Thinking about it makes my body react again.”

The relationship also runs from CLS to ESS. Memory, anticipation, imagery and
interpretation can produce or sustain physiological change even when the
original event is absent.

## 8. Why the distinction explains change

Change can fail when an intervention reaches only one form of information.

- **Cognitive change without sufficient embodied updating:** the person has new
  language, insight or intention, but old thresholds, physiological readiness
  or response patterns remain active.
- **Embodied change without cognitive integration:** the person experiences
  settling, release or new safety, but cannot yet represent what changed,
  generalise it, make meaning from it or use it in future decisions.
- **Restricted exchange:** information is present somewhere in the organism but
  is not sufficiently available across the embodied-cognitive interface.
- **Conflicting information:** the systems are both available but are working
  from different time horizons, memories, predictions or readings of current
  conditions.

More durable change becomes possible when:

- present conditions support enough safety and capacity for new information to
  be received;
- sensations and emotions can be differentiated rather than collapsed into
  noise or treated as commands;
- cognitive models can be questioned and revised rather than defended as
  identity;
- action, completion, recovery and restoration can occur where they are needed;
- the outcome of a new response becomes evidence that both systems can learn
  from.

The aim is not permanent agreement. It is greater access, flexibility and
correctability across the whole organism.

---

## 9. High-value formulations

- Two information systems, one connected organism.
- Different functions; equal biological importance.
- The ESS registers and organises; the CLS represents and interprets.
- The ESS carries embodied information. The CLS builds cognitive models.
- A sensation is information, not an instruction. A thought is a model, not a
  fact.
- The body can register what cognition cannot yet explain.
- Cognition can contextualise what the body cannot determine on its own.
- Coherence is not the same as completeness.
- Insight can change the model before it changes the embodied pattern.
- An embodied shift can occur before a person has words for it.
- Disagreement between ESS and CLS is a condition to understand, not a battle
  to win.
- Integration is not the disappearance of difference; it is the capacity for
  different information to remain available to the same organism.
- Regulation is not making the ESS obey the CLS. It is increasing the
  organism's capacity to receive, evaluate and respond to both forms of
  information.
- Change becomes more durable when both the embodied organisation and the
  cognitive model can update.

## 10. Scientific and educational guardrails

Use the model with these limits stated explicitly:

1. **It is a TEG-Blue educational and functional model.** ESS and CLS are not
   standard neuroscientific diagnostic categories.
2. **It is not a body-versus-brain or emotion-versus-reason split.** Both depend
   on distributed brain-body processes, and many structures participate in
   both kinds of function.
3. **The systems are distinguishable, not sealed.** Their activity is
   reciprocal and overlapping.
4. **Fast versus slow is a tendency, not a complete definition.** Some embodied
   processes are slow; some cognitive operations are fast and automatic.
5. **Neither system guarantees accuracy.** ESS may organise from earlier
   learning or prediction; CLS may build a coherent model from partial or
   state-shaped information.
6. **Equal-standing is not fixed weighting.** Immediate danger, complex
   planning, recovery, relationship and reflection may require different
   contributions at different times.
7. **Avoid absolute timing claims unless separately verified.** Older TEG-Blue
   documents use precise millisecond comparisons that require an evidence and
   citation review before public use.
8. **Avoid saying that the ESS always remains unchanged while only the CLS is
   variable.** Current TEG-Blue architecture treats the complete event as
   recurrent and mutually influencing; embodied processing also learns and
   recalibrates.
9. **Avoid saying that interoception is the only route by which bodily
   information affects cognition.** It is a central route in the model, but the
   exact biological boundary requires more precise scientific treatment.

---

## 11. Recommended core paragraph

TEG-Blue distinguishes two biological forms of information processing so that
we can see contributions that are difficult to explain when experience is
treated as one undivided process. The Emotional-Somatic System (ESS) reads and
biologically weights conditions through sensory, interoceptive, physiological,
emotional and relational information, participating in preparation, response,
recovery and restoration. The Cognitive-Logical System (CLS) represents and
interprets experience through memory, language, concepts, comparison,
prediction, imagination, planning and narrative. They are not separate organs
or opposing forces, but different functions of one connected organism. Both
are necessary; neither is automatically accurate. Behaviour and change become
more understandable when we ask what each system is processing, what
information can pass between them, and what would allow both to update in
contact with present conditions.
