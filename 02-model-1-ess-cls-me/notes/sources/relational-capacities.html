ME participation · three capacities · eight configurations

How three forms of access combine within ME

A configuration is not a personality. It is the current relationship between bodily information access and use, affective-sharing access, and mentalizing access and use. Personality-like patterns may begin to emerge when one configuration becomes recurrent, generalised, and difficult to update.

Working boundary. “Available” and “unavailable” are deliberately simplified here. A capacity may be faint, selective, distorted, fused, over-recruited, or intact but not currently needed. The binary field reveals structure; the quality dimensions below restore the complexity. Read the governing model premise →

A configuration is access information—not a behavioural verdict.

Restricted access may narrow what ME can notice or use. Greater access may widen participation. Neither determines what the person will do.

Do not infer: innocence, guilt, safety, harmfulness, personality or diagnosis from any configuration.

Assess separately: behaviour, power, impact, available alternatives, response to feedback, recurrence and repair.

Access is not optimality. A capacity can be available but inaccurate, fused, involuntary, or recruited for protection.

Low access is not absent processing. The biological process may be intact but unnecessary, inhibited, unreadable, or too costly for ME to use in the current organisation.

The eight-configuration field

Each card describes a pattern of ME access, not a person and not the presence or absence of the underlying biological processes. The same configuration can be adaptive for a moment, protective under pressure, or personality-like when it becomes the default across time and relationships.

No reliable bodily information access or use · no affective-sharing access · no mentalizing access

Relational unavailability

Neither one’s own felt state nor the other person’s felt or represented state is sufficiently available for relational participation.

May look like

  • Shutdown, numbness, exhaustion, dissociation, or relational withdrawal.
  • Minimal response because all available capacity is assigned to conservation or immediate survival.
  • A temporary pause in relational processing during overwhelm.

Empathic concern: the idea of care may remain, while motivation or capacity to act on it is unavailable.

Do not infer indifference, lack of attachment, or a fixed absence of empathy.

Your example 1 · mentalizing only

The mentalizing-only configuration

The other person can be read, predicted, or explained, while neither one’s own felt experience nor affective resonance with the other is available to ME.

May look like

  • Detached analysis, strategic interpersonal reading, or technically accurate perspective-taking.
  • Knowing what another feels without feeling moved by it.
  • Professional composure in a role that temporarily requires affective distance.

Empathic concern: cannot be inferred. Understanding may support care, restraint, management, persuasion, or exploitation.

Do not infer manipulation, psychopathy, or narcissism from this configuration alone.

access & use010

Your example 2 · affective-sharing access dominant

The absorbed-other configuration

Another person’s affect enters strongly, but one’s own experience is difficult to locate and mentalizing cannot reliably interpret whose feeling it is or what it means.

May look like

  • Emotional contagion, overwhelm, fusion, or rapidly taking on another person’s state.
  • Compulsive soothing without checking what the other actually needs.
  • Mistaking resonance for accurate knowledge of the other.

Empathic concern: may become compulsory action or self-erasure rather than freely chosen, proportionate care.

“Too much empathy” may be better described as affect sharing without sufficient self–other distinction, mentalizing, or regulation.

access & use011

Affective sharing and mentalizing accessible · bodily information not reliably accessible or usable

The other-centred configuration

The other person can be felt and understood in detail, while one’s own feeling, need, limit, and preference remain outside usable reach.

May look like

  • Highly attuned caregiving combined with self-silencing or absent boundaries.
  • Organising choices around the other person’s state and anticipated reaction.
  • Relational expertise that does not include one’s own position in the relationship.

Empathic concern: may be strong but difficult to distinguish from obligation, appeasement, or connection-preservation.

Do not assume generosity is false; the question is whether the self can also participate.

access & use100

Bodily information accessible and usable · other neither shared nor represented

The self-contained configuration

One’s own feeling and need are available, while the other person’s state is not affectively shared or cognitively represented.

May look like

  • Healthy solitude when no other person requires representation.
  • Protective self-focus under pain, acute demand, or boundary defence.
  • A relational blind spot if the configuration persists while another person is affected.

Empathic concern: may be irrelevant in solitude or reduced when the other’s condition cannot enter the field.

Do not infer selfishness when the social capacities are simply not recruited or needed.

access & use101

Interoceptive and mentalizing access · affective-sharing access unavailable

The interoceptive–mentalizing configuration

One’s own experience remains available and the other can be understood, but the other person’s affect does not resonate within oneself.

May look like

  • Clear negotiation, boundary-setting, or calm decision-making without emotional merging.
  • Compassionate understanding with low affective resonance.
  • Instrumental social reasoning when concern and accountability are also reduced.

Empathic concern: is the differentiator between respectful distance and cognition used only for personal outcome.

Low affect sharing does not by itself mean low care or poor moral action.

access & use110

Self and other felt · other not mentally represented

The shared-affect configuration

Both one’s own feeling and resonance with the other are present, but the other person’s perspective, cause, or intention cannot yet be represented reliably.

May look like

  • Deep emotional participation with limited perspective-taking.
  • Knowing that both people are affected without understanding why or what each needs.
  • Projection, misattribution, or action based on shared intensity rather than checked meaning.

Empathic concern: may be sincere and strong while the chosen response remains inaccurate or unhelpful.

Feeling with someone is not the same as knowing their experience.

access & use111

Your example 3 · all three forms of ME access available

The integrated configuration

One’s own experience, affective resonance with the other, and a revisable understanding of the other can all participate without collapsing into one another.

May look like

  • Reciprocal connection with self–other distinction and room for correction.
  • Feeling moved by another while retaining one’s own limit, perspective, and agency.
  • Repair that integrates impact, intention, context, restraint, and action.

Empathic concern: can become proportionate, freely chosen action that respects both welfare and agency.

All three being present is not always optimal; flexible recruitment remains more important than permanent maximum openness.

Binary access is only the first pass

To connect the eight structures to the Fluid and Chronic Gradients, each form of access must also be read through five quality dimensions.

Availability

Can the capacity participate at all, and at what cost?

Differentiation

Is the information specific, or fused into undifferentiated pressure?

Self–other distinction

Can my experience and your experience remain related but separate?

Accuracy and updating

Can the representation be corrected by present evidence?

Flexible recruitment

Can the capacity enter and leave according to actual conditions?

Bridge back to the Gradient

The Fluid Gradient describes how the underlying processes are recruited by present conditions. This matrix asks how much of those processes becomes available to ME in combination. Chronic Survival can then examine which pattern of access has become the familiar relational solution. Personality-like expression belongs to recurrence and generalisation—not to a single state or binary combination.
