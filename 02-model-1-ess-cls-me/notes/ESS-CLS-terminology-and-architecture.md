## The intention that must remain visible

ESS and CLS were created to explain a pattern that is difficult to see when human behaviour is described through cognition alone.

People can believe they are acting from reason while emotional-somatic organisation is shaping what feels urgent, threatening, necessary or justified. A person may understand something intellectually while their body continues to prepare for danger. They may suppress an emotion without removing the process that produced it. They may explain a behaviour coherently without recognising the fear, hurt, shame, need or learned protection influencing it.

The model gives these simultaneous processes a visible architecture.

Its purpose is not to make emotion and cognition opponents. It is to show why neither provides a complete reading alone, why they can carry different information at the same time, and why conscious participation becomes narrower when one side is dismissed or unavailable.

## The social observation behind the model

Many people have learned to treat articulate reasoning as evidence that a response is rational. Emotional and bodily information is then treated as noise, weakness or something to control. It does not follow that the emotional process disappears. It may continue to influence attention, interpretation, action and repetition without being recognised in the explanation a person gives.

This matters beyond individual wellbeing. A person can use status, certainty, expertise or force while remaining unable or unwilling to examine the emotional organisation influencing their behaviour. Their explanation may sound coherent while fear, entitlement, shame, hurt or a need for control remains unexamined.

TEG-Blue therefore keeps two questions visible:

1. What explanation is the person giving through cognition and language?
2. What emotional-somatic information and organisation may also be shaping the response?

Neither question determines the answer alone. Together they make more of the behaviour available for examination.

## The decision

### Keep the names

Retain:

> **ESS · Emotional Somatic System**

> **CLS · Cognitive Logical System**

These names remain internal TEG-Blue working terms while the constructs are defined, tested and grounded. No public release is assumed. A future naming review remains possible, but it is not required now.

The older foundation folder currently expands ESS as *Embodied Somatic System*. The present Inner Compass and Foundational Diagrams use *Emotional Somatic System*. This naming divergence should remain recorded for later resolution; it does not change the decision to retain ESS.

### Keep the educational architecture

Retain the description:

> **Two biological information systems, one connected organism.**

Here, *system* names an educational and functional architecture inside TEG-Blue. It does not claim that neuroscience has identified two sealed anatomical systems, two exclusive neural networks or a complete division of human processing.

### Add scientific-facing descriptions without replacing the names

- **ESS:** emotional-somatic, embodied-affective condition-reading and organisation;
- **CLS:** cognitive-logical, reflective-symbolic modelling and interpretation.

These descriptions clarify the intended functions. They are not replacement names and are not presented as standard scientific constructs.

## What ESS means in the current model

### Working definition

> The Emotional Somatic System is TEG-Blue's educational name for the distributed biological processing through which internal, external, relational, remembered and anticipated conditions may acquire emotional and bodily significance and participate in physiology, attention, felt experience, action readiness, recovery and restoration.

### What this definition protects

- bodily and emotional organisation can begin before conscious explanation;
- information can influence behaviour without becoming clearly felt or named;
- earlier learning can change what receives weight in the present;
- emotional signals can remain active when expression is suppressed;
- a signal can contain important information without providing a complete or infallible account of its cause.

### Boundary

ESS is a TEG-Blue educational integration. It is not one anatomical module, all non-conscious processing, a body separate from the brain, or an infallible source of truth. Its proposed functions require grounding through several research territories rather than one ESS literature.

## What CLS means in the current model

### Working definition

> The Cognitive Logical System is TEG-Blue's educational name for the distributed capacities through which available experience may be represented, named, compared, sequenced, contextualised, communicated, imagined, planned and deliberately reconsidered across time.

### What this definition protects

- language and concepts can make experience available for examination;
- reflective thought can compare an immediate response with wider conditions and consequences;
- a person can revise an interpretation rather than treating the first explanation as fact;
- cognition can help create meaning, communicate experience and plan a different action;
- cognition can also rationalise, suppress, distort or strategically organise behaviour.

### Boundary

CLS is not all cognition, pure logic, one neural system or a conscious executive. Perception, prediction, memory, evaluation and action selection also occur outside reflective awareness. A coherent explanation does not guarantee accuracy, emotional awareness, care or safe behaviour.

## The emotion–cognition distinction

The framework should not avoid this distinction. It should make the distinction without turning it into separation, opposition or hierarchy.

| ESS makes visible | CLS makes visible |
| --- | --- |
| How conditions are registered and weighted emotionally and bodily | How available experience is represented, explained and organised through concepts |
| Changes that may begin before conscious understanding | Capacities that can compare, contextualise and reconsider |
| Signals, feelings, physiological organisation and action readiness | Meanings, narratives, reasons, strategies and plans |
| Learning that may continue without language | Learning that may become linguistically and symbolically organised |

The correction is not “emotion and cognition are the same”. It is:

> **Emotion and cognition are distinguishable but interacting forms of processing within one organism. Neither is outside biology, neither works alone, and neither is automatically the truth.**

This distinction is the framework's teaching height. The scientific boundary protects it from becoming a literal body–mind split.

## Four claims the ESS–CLS model holds together

### Emotion is information

Emotion is structured biological and relational information. It can carry signals concerning body state, safety, threat, need, boundary, memory, connection, impact and repair. The signal matters, but it is not automatically complete evidence about the external situation.

### State changes what feels true

Body and nervous-system organisation can shape perception, certainty and the information available for interpretation. A real inner signal can become fused with an external conclusion: *I feel hurt, therefore harm occurred.* The purpose is not to dismiss the hurt. It is to keep feeling, interpretation, evidence, impact and response distinguishable.

### Emotional disconnection is not objectivity

When body state and emotion are unavailable, cognition may remain articulate and coherent while modelling with incomplete information about need, boundary, impact, care or repair. *I can explain it* does not necessarily mean *I am in contact with the whole situation*.

### Mechanism does not erase responsibility

ESS and CLS may help explain why a behaviour became available or why its impact was not registered. They do not establish innocence, necessity or intent. Power, behaviour, impact, repetition, response to feedback and repair remain separate questions.

## How the two systems interact

ESS and CLS are concurrent, recurrent and bidirectional.

```text
conditions and prior learning
        ↓
emotional-somatic registration, weighting and organisation
        ↕
cognitive representation, explanation and anticipation
        ↕
ME participation, action and consequence
        ↓
feedback, updating, persistence or Return
```

This is a TEG-Blue educational sequence, not one established anatomical pathway. Different parts may overlap, recur, remain outside awareness or become available at different times.

Examples include:

- **“I know I am safe, but I do not feel safe.”** The cognitive account and emotional-somatic organisation carry different present information.
- **“I feel something is wrong, but I cannot explain why.”** Emotional-somatic information is present without sufficient differentiation or representation. It deserves investigation, not automatic belief or dismissal.
- **“I understand the pattern, but I keep doing it.”** Reflective insight has changed while action availability, expectation or emotional-somatic organisation remains narrower.
- **“Thinking about it makes my body react again.”** Memory, language, imagery and anticipation can reconstruct emotional-somatic demand.
- **“My explanation is logical, therefore my behaviour is justified.”** Coherence within CLS does not establish that the person has examined emotional pressure, actual conditions, alternatives, power or impact.

## Relationship with ME and practical agency

ME names the lived person through whom both systems are experienced and expressed. ME is not a third information system and not an executive standing above biology.

Recommended formulation:

> **The ME may turn towards, examine and use what becomes available through ESS and CLS. As emotional awareness and reflective capacity become more connected, the person may gain more room to pause, compare, communicate, choose and revise.**

This is the grounded form of the concern about free will. TEG-Blue should not claim that free will is simply present or absent. It can examine how access, learning, emotional pressure, available alternatives and conscious participation may widen or constrain a person's practical room to act.

The scientifically proportionate translation of “emotions rule our lives” is therefore:

> **Emotional and nervous-system organisation can shape perception, attention, interpretation and action outside awareness. When that information becomes more accessible and distinguishable, a person may gain more room to examine what is happening and influence what follows.**

## Hurt and harm

The original insight that people often confuse hurt with harm belongs in this architecture, but the two must remain separate.

- **Hurt** describes pain, offence, loss, disappointment, shame, fear or another felt impact.
- **Harm** concerns damage, violation, coercion, deprivation, injury or a pattern that restricts safety, agency or wellbeing.

Hurt can signal real harm. Hurt can also arise when a boundary is set, an entitlement is interrupted or an expectation is not met. ESS helps make the felt impact visible. CLS and ME may help investigate conditions, evidence, perspective and consequence. Harm–Power–Repair remains the layer that examines what was done, who carried the cost and what happened after feedback.

## Claim decomposition

### IC-04 · ESS

- **Definition:** TEG-Blue's educational system for emotional-somatic processing of organismic significance, bodily organisation, felt experience, attention and action readiness.
- **Phenomenon:** bodily and emotional changes can influence attention and action before a person can name or explain what has become relevant.
- **Mechanism:** interoceptive, exteroceptive, affective, memory, predictive, autonomic, endocrine, salience and action-selection processes may contribute.
- **Development:** maturation, health, learning and repeated relational or material conditions may change weighting, thresholds, felt access and available action.
- **Application:** makes emotional-somatic influence visible when a cognitive explanation alone cannot account for a response.
- **Boundary:** not a discovered anatomical system, all non-conscious cognition, an infallible bodily truth or proof of present conditions or past history.

### IC-05 · CLS

- **Definition:** TEG-Blue's educational system for cognitive-logical representation, language, comparison, contextualisation, communication and deliberate reconsideration.
- **Phenomenon:** people may describe an experience, compare it with other evidence, imagine consequences and revise an earlier account.
- **Mechanism:** language, working memory, semantic and autobiographical memory, cognitive control, metacognition, counterfactual reasoning and social cognition may contribute.
- **Development:** language, culture, education, relationship, practice, disability, stress and access needs may shape which reflective capacities become available and trusted.
- **Application:** makes the explanatory and model-building side of experience visible without granting it automatic authority.
- **Boundary:** not all cognition, pure logic, a single neural system, a conscious ruler or a guarantee of truth, care or safe behaviour.

## Non-negotiable scientific boundaries

1. **Educational systems, not discovered anatomical divisions.** Each construct integrates several scientifically distinguishable processes.
2. **Distinction without separation.** Emotion and cognition interact continuously and can alter one another.
3. **No hierarchy of truth.** A feeling is not automatically a fact; a coherent explanation is not automatically accurate.
4. **No fast–slow rule.** ESS may change rapidly or persist over time; CLS may respond quickly or deliberate slowly.
5. **No unconscious–conscious ownership rule.** Parts of both systems may operate outside awareness, while conscious access varies.
6. **No body–brain split.** Both depend on distributed brain–body processing in material, relational and cultural conditions.
7. **No direct behaviour reading.** ESS and CLS may influence behaviour; neither determines a single action, intention or moral meaning.
8. **No exoneration.** Reduced emotional awareness may help explain behaviour without removing responsibility, power, impact or the need for protection and repair.
9. **No status inference.** Authority, confidence, intelligence or verbal skill does not establish emotional awareness or integration.

## Consequences for the wider architecture

### F1

F1 may trace the foundations and expansion of the capacities grouped by ESS and CLS. It must continue to state that these are present-day TEG-Blue integrations, not two evolutionary taxa or seven biological stages.

### F2

F2 should preserve the developmental asymmetry at the centre of the model: a child can be affected, organise action and learn emotional-relational patterns before they can explain the situation through mature language and reflection. Broad early cognition should not be denied simply because later reflective capacities belong to CLS.

### Inner Compass

The Inner Compass should show how information from ESS and CLS may become available to ME and how access, differentiation, comparison and action can widen. It should not imply that awareness permanently controls either system.

### Fluid and Chronic

Fluid and Chronic organisation may change what ESS weights, what CLS explains, what becomes available to ME and what actions seem possible. The systems remain active across both; neither belongs to only one organisation.

### Harm–Power–Repair

The educational model can help expose rationalisation and unexamined emotional pressure. It cannot conclude that a leader, institution or harmful actor behaves from one state. Behaviour, coercion, power, impact, response to feedback and repair still require their own evidence.

## Development route

### Keep now

1. ESS and CLS as the current names.
2. The two-biological-information-systems educational architecture.
3. Equal standing without equal function or equal weight in every moment.
4. Emotional-somatic influence outside awareness as a central explanatory question.
5. Cognitive interpretation as useful but not automatically authoritative.

### Develop next

1. Use the completed [component claim decomposition](../grounding/ESS-CLS-component-decomposition.md) as the unit for evidence work.
2. Identify the source records and measurement level needed for each component claim.
3. Test where the current two-system diagram clarifies experience and where it accidentally implies separation.
4. Ground practical agency as variable room for participation rather than an all-or-nothing free-will claim.
5. Connect emotional unawareness, rationalisation, status and harmful behaviour through Harm–Power–Repair without inferring an internal state from social position alone.

### Revisit later

The full names may be reconsidered if the definitions become more accurate than the labels. That future choice should come after the architecture is stable, not before it has done its explanatory work.

The model keeps the distinction because cognition alone cannot explain the whole person. It keeps the boundary because emotion alone cannot explain the whole person either.
