Behaviour, responsibility and the shared premise

Two distinctions that more than one concept needs. Behaviour and responsibility is a separate evidence plane: what was done, under what conditions, with what power and impact, and what happened when feedback or repair became possible. Human sociality and the two survival functions is the shared biological premise: for a social organism, relationship can be one of survival's conditions. Neither is inferred from the other, and neither is inferred from an emotion, a Position or a capacity profile.

Does not establish
Conduct, intention, guilt, safety or accountability from an emotion, a Gradient Position, a capacity configuration or a physiological measurement. Nor a person's history, organisation or motive from their behaviour. Nor that human sociality prescribes company, safe relationships or what another person owes.

The distinction this concept protects

Organisation, behaviour pattern and power use are three questions.

The body can organise around protection. A person can then act in many possible ways. Some actions protect without governing another person. Some use power to make another person carry the cost, surrender agency or accept an imposed outcome. Internal organisation may help explain pressure on behaviour. Power, agency, impact, feedback and recurrence help examine whether the behaviour became harmful.

Six rules that follow

Extreme defensive mobilisation does not establish Domination. Chronic organisation does not establish abusive behaviour. Calm behaviour does not establish safety. High activation does not establish coercion. A protective history does not remove impact or responsibility. Harmful behaviour does not allow one internal mechanism or developmental history to be inferred. The planes are examined together only after each has been described on its own terms.

Behaviour that can stand down

On the Fluid plane the behaviour is doing something the present conditions ask for, and it ends when they change: force is relinquished, attention widens, the appointment matters again, what happened can be gone back to and named.

Behaviour that has become the default

On the Chronic plane the same behaviour runs before the conditions call for it and continues after they pass. Guarding, pleasing, checking, managing, pressuring or withdrawing is reached faster than rest or open connection, and repair is delayed, traded or refused.

Where the two tables separate

Afterwards. Whether the stance comes down when the pressure does, whether the reading updates when new information arrives, whether feedback reaches, and whether repair becomes possible. Two rows carry this: response to feedback and repair after conflict.

What the tinted rows are

Two blocks on the page are not observation. What organises the behaviour is inferred from the pattern rather than seen in it, and the from-inside rows are a working hypothesis nobody can verify from outside.

A Position is read from a pattern over time, not from one row

Any cell can describe a person for an afternoon for reasons the table does not cover: a bad night, illness, grief, an unfamiliar language, or a culture with different rules about volume, directness and eye contact. What makes a reading usable is repetition, the conditions it happens under, and what follows when those conditions change. A protective origin does not make harmful behaviour harmless, and naming the mechanism is not agreeing to the behaviour.

Where the conduct plane begins

The enforcement threshold, feedback as a change test, four repair territories.

The enforcement threshold

Power is present in many ordinary relationships and roles, and authority can be legitimate but bounded. The threshold is crossed where power, threats, punishment, surveillance, restriction, deprivation or force make another person's refusal or independent action meaningfully less possible. F7 owns the question in full.

Feedback as a change test

What happens when impact is named, a boundary arrives or refusal is voiced is evidence about the pattern. Reaching, delaying, trading or refusing are observable. Harm can be loud or quiet; the test does not depend on volume.

Repair is several territories

Accountability, restitution, restored agency and changed conduct are distinguishable and none stands in for the others. Internal revision, insight or an apology does not complete them. F8 separates recovery, reconnection and repair as routes.

The twelve claim families, the six practical distinctions and the research families recovered from the old engine's dossier are in the Harm, Power and Repair grounding, a working foundation and claim register.

The shared biological premise

Relationship is not outside survival.

Humans survive and develop within social ecologies. Other people can alter access to care, resources, information, protection, effort and recovery. Humans did not evolve as fully self-sufficient individuals who later added relationships for comfort; across human life history, other people have participated in provisioning, childcare, learning, defence, cooperation, risk sharing and the regulation of demand. This does not make every relationship necessary or beneficial. It makes social availability biologically consequential.

  1. 1 · Established evidenceHuman dependency, attachment behaviour, cooperation, social buffering, interpersonal influence, and consequential responses to social availability, loss and exclusion.
  2. 2 · Grounded synthesisMaintaining access to socially consequential others is a biologically meaningful problem for humans, even though its processes are distributed across fields and levels.
  3. 3 · TEG-Blue integrationConnection-preservation names that functional territory; organism-protection names the interacting territory of direct maintenance and protection.

Bounded from the start

An architectural survival function is not an anatomical structure. Human sociality does not imply universal sociability, safe relationships, prescribed behaviour or what another person owes. Introversion, chosen solitude and independence remain ordinary: "social animal" describes a species history and ecology, not a requirement for constant company. Social rejection and physical pain do not share one proven neural system; the distress is real, the simple "pain matrix" is not supported. Full claim-status ledger and sources on the carried page.

Two interacting ways viability is served

Organism-protection and connection-preservation.

Through direct protection

Organism-protection

Conditions require maintenance, restoration, distance, defence, escape, shelter, nourishment or another action that changes bodily demand or exposure. Bodily integrity and viable internal conditions; resources, distance, shelter and effective action.

Through access to others

Connection-preservation

Access to a consequential person or group affects care, cooperation, recognition, information, shared effort, protection or the possibility of relational restoration. Availability, belonging and reciprocal access; recognition, accountability and repair where mutuality is required.

Another person can be part of the completing condition without being the only route to ReturnThree outcomes stay distinguishable: relational restoration, in which the relevant people participate; organism-level updating and Return, in which demand changes enough for recruitment to release; and Return without relational restoration, through grief, protection or revised expectation.

One premise, several scales

A species-level dependency becomes a developmental condition, then a present event.

  1. F1 · repertoireCaregiving, signalling, attachment, cooperation, learning and shared action belong to an inherited and culturally elaborated repertoire.
  2. F2 · developmentOther people change conditions while the child's own organism senses, acts, reorganises and learns from what follows.
  3. Models 1 and 2Another person's availability, expression and action enter the information field while present organisation changes access, priority and capacity.
  4. Model 3 · the eventWhat the other person does may change the condition, support updating, leave demand open or become carryover into the next cycle.

Keeping the levels separate prevents evolutionary plausibility from becoming a childhood story or a prediction about present behaviour. Social dependence explains why relational information matters; it does not determine what repetition will produce. The premise reaches the Chronic plane and F3 only through learning, persistence and updating evidence, never by direct evolutionary inference. Development can shape which relational routes become easier to recruit; it cannot be reconstructed from one adult response.
