Behaviour Across the Gradient

The same seven nervous system organisations, read as what someone could observe: what is done, said, attended to, decided, and what happens once the conditions change. This is the layer tools are written from.

Page responsibility · Behaviour and responsibility evidence plane

Scientific-facing scope · observable conduct, power, impact, alternatives, feedback, recurrence, repair and accountability

Governing questionWhat was done, under what conditions, with what power and impact, and what happens when feedback or the possibility of repair appears?

This page owns

The separate record of behaviour, consequences, available alternatives, responsiveness to feedback, recurrence, repair and responsibility.

It receives

Bounded descriptions of organisation, access and change through time from the three models.

It does not establish

Conduct, intention, guilt, safety or accountability from an emotion, Gradient Position, capacity profile or physiological measurement.

The same behaviour appears in both tables

Someone can be short, guarded, silent, watchful, or forceful in either table. A single moment shows the behaviour and the conditions it is happening under, and both tables answer the same seven conditions. What separates them is what happens afterwards: whether the stance comes down when the pressure does, whether the reading updates when new information arrives, and whether repair becomes possible.

Behaviour that can stand down

In the Fluid Gradient, the behaviour is doing something the present conditions ask for. It ends when they change: force is relinquished, attention widens again, the appointment matters again, and what happened can be gone back to and named.

Behaviour that has become the default

In Chronic Survival, the same behaviour runs before the conditions call for it and continues after they pass. Guarding, pleasing, checking, managing, pressuring, or withdrawing is reached faster than rest or open connection, and repair is delayed, traded, or refused.

A Position is read from a pattern over time, not from one row. Any cell here can describe a person for an afternoon for reasons this table does not cover — a bad night, illness, grief, an unfamiliar language, or a culture with different rules about volume, directness, and eye contact. What makes a reading usable is repetition, the conditions it happens under, and what follows when those conditions change. A protective origin does not make harmful behaviour harmless, and naming the mechanism is not agreeing to the behaviour.

Behaviour
What is done, said, attended to, and decided — the part another person could witness and describe without interpreting it. It is the applied layer, not the mechanism: the internal capacities behind these rows are in How Humans Respond to Safety and Threat.
What the tinted rows are
Two blocks are not observation. What organises it is inferred from the pattern rather than seen in it. From inside is a working hypothesis about how the same organisation may be experienced, including a first-person narrative row nobody can verify from outside.
How to read a Position here
Each column — X, A, A↔B, B, C, D, and Z — is a Position, and each Position names a different nervous system organisation, not a person, a personality, or a permanent identity. The Position row, the Environmental conditions row, and the Nervous system organisation row connect this page to the main Gradient. In the Fluid Gradient, organisation changes with present conditions. In Chronic Survival, an organisation persists or is rebuilt when the present environment no longer corresponds to it, so every column there is defensive even when the person looks calm or connected.

What a Fluid cell claims. A cell describes what is characteristic while the condition above it holds, not what must happen, and not something a person is. Two people in the same Position will not produce the same sentence, the same volume, or the same silence. The behaviour rows describe the observable layer only; which internal capacity narrowed, stayed intact, or was simply not recruited is a separate reading, and it is in the capacities table.

What a Chronic cell claims. Chronic does not mean severe, and Fluid does not mean mild — the two tables differ in whether the organisation is answering present conditions, not in how much it costs. A person may look calm or connected while guarding, monitoring, pleasing, managing, or conserving is still running. A capacity may stay highly active while becoming inaccurate, uncorrectable, fused, compulsory, or instrumental; reduced access is not the only form chronic change takes. The C and D columns describe behaviour and its cost to another person. Understanding what organises that behaviour does not reduce the harm of it, is not a reason for the person receiving it to doubt their own experience, and is never a condition of their setting a boundary, seeking support, or leaving.

What a behaviour tool has to carry

These rows are written for tools that help someone recognise a pattern. Each item below is a distinction the table depends on, and each one is cheap to lose once the rows become questions.

  • Ask what happened afterwards, not only what happened. One behaviour is in both tables. Whether the stance came down when the pressure did is the question that separates them, and a tool that only samples the moment cannot answer it.
  • Never return a Position from a single row. A Position is a pattern across rows, across time, and under known conditions. A cell match is one observation, not a result.
  • Show the conditions with any reading. A behaviour without what it was answering says nothing, and the Environmental conditions row is why the same cell means different things in the two tables.
  • Keep Chronic apart from severe, and Fluid apart from mild. Fluid D is maximum protective mobilisation under life threat. It is not a milder version of coercive control, and the tool must not let one read as the other.
  • Name behaviour and its cost, not another person’s Position. A self-reading belongs to the person doing it. Reading someone else stays with what they did, how often, under what conditions, and what it cost — the observable layer, which is the only one another person has access to.
  • Keep the mechanism from doing the work of a verdict. Explaining what organises a behaviour is not excusing it, and a tool that offers an explanation where someone needs a boundary has changed sides without saying so.
  • Let a reading update. Nothing here is a type. Any result a tool returns should be reversible by new information, in the same way the organisation it describes is.
