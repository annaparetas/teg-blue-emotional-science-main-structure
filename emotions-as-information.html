Emotions as Information

Emotions contribute information about what is happening within the body and situation, what may matter, and which conditions may require attention. This information can shape perception and readiness, but it does not dictate behaviour or provide a complete verdict about reality.

Approved foundation

Emotion is part of how the organism reads its conditions.

Working definition

In TEG-Blue, an emotion is understood as structured biological and relational information. It participates in how the organism reads bodily and situational conditions, identifies what may matter, directs attention and prepares possible responses.

Some emotional processing may not become a consciously recognised feeling. When a feeling does become available, it is real as an inner event. Its meaning, source and interpretation of the outer situation can still be incomplete, revisable or mistaken.

Emotion can inform action, but it does not authorise it. Action-readiness is not behaviour. What a person does, its impact and the responsibility that follows must be assessed separately.

Signal Map architecture · TEG-Blue working language

The Signal Map contains two signal groups.

What signal means here

TEG-Blue uses signal as a functional umbrella term for an organised change that carries information about a condition and can influence regulation, attention or readiness. A signal may or may not become consciously felt.

Calling something a signal does not make it a fact, a single biological object or an instruction.

Internal regulated condition

Body-condition signals

These begin with conditions inside the body that regulation tracks, including breathing, possible bodily harm, temperature, hydration, energy availability, visceral capacity and restorative capacity.

When they reach conscious access, they may be experienced as sensations, urges or bodily feelings such as breathlessness, pain, heat, cold, thirst, hunger, urgency or tiredness.

Significance of a condition

Emotion signals

These carry information about the organism's relation to a current, remembered, imagined or anticipated condition. Connection, loss, uncertainty, obstruction, possibility, threat and achievement are examples of what may become significant.

When part of the emotional process becomes conscious, it may be felt as fear, love, anger, sadness, pride or another emotional feeling.

The group identifies the starting question

Body-condition signals ask: what internal condition is the organism regulating? Emotion signals ask: what does this present or represented condition mean for the organism?

The groups are not separate biological systems. They can recruit overlapping processes, affect one another and participate at the same time.

Keep the levels separate

Condition → organism-level signalling and processing → possible conscious feeling, sensation or urge.

Hydration-related processing may become conscious as thirst. Respiratory processing may become conscious as breathlessness or air hunger. Nociceptive and other protective processing may contribute to pain, but pain and nociception are not interchangeable.

TEG-Blue working distinction

Emotion and feeling are related, but not interchangeable.

Scientific traditions do not all use these words in the same way. TEG-Blue uses the distinction below to keep organism-level processing separate from conscious access.

Organism-level signal and process

Emotion

Emotion names the coordinated process through which bodily or situational significance participates across the organism. It can shape physiology, attention, perception and action-readiness without becoming conscious.

Conscious access to part of the process

Feeling

Feeling names the conscious experience of some of that emotional information when it becomes available to ME. It may be partial, mixed, faint or not yet named.

Where ME enters

ME does not create the emotion by noticing it. When emotional information becomes available as feeling, ME may turn towards it, differentiate it, name it, examine its context and decide how to respond. A person can feel something before knowing what it is or what it means.

Conscious access makes emotional information experienceable; it does not make its interpretation automatically factual. A feeling establishes that something is happening within the person's experience. It does not, by itself, establish the signal's source, target, scale, cause or required response.

Signal Map grounding hook

Emotions involve chemistry, but no chemical is an emotion.

Emotional signalling involves interacting neural, autonomic, endocrine, immune and metabolic processes. Neurotransmitters, hormones and other biological mediators can participate in forming, carrying, regulating and updating a signal across different timescales.

Biological participation

Look for interacting processes

Chemistry is part of how an emotion happens in an organism. Different processes can participate in detection, arousal, attention, action-readiness, regulation, learning and recovery.

Interpretive boundary

Do not infer a chemical fingerprint

Oxytocin is not love. Dopamine is not pleasure. Adrenaline is not fear or anger. No single chemical measurement identifies a person's emotion, its cause, their organisation or the behaviour that will follow.

What the evidence must establish

Ask which interacting biological processes participated, what researchers measured, when and where they measured it, and how far the evidence allows us to infer. Association is not identity, necessity or proof that one chemical uniquely belongs to a signal.

Approved dynamics

Keep the signal separate from what happens around it.

These distinctions keep emotional information legible as it becomes felt, interpreted, used and carried forward.

Information is not automatic fact

A feeling is real as inner information. Its source, target, scale and interpretation may still need checking.

No feeling is not no information

Emotional and bodily information can shape attention and readiness without becoming consciously felt. Numbness or calm does not prove that nothing is happening.

Emotions can coexist without changing identity

Several signals may participate at once. One can become prominent while others remain faint; one emotion does not transform into another.

Emotion is neither a moral verdict nor behaviour

No feeling proves character or grants permission to act. Behaviour, power, impact, alternatives and responsibility require separate evidence.

Reduced pressure is not necessarily resolution

A response may change the internal state while the need, danger, rupture or loss remains. Completion, Return, recovery and repair are separate questions.

Feedback changes the next read

What happens next can alter the condition, add evidence, create another signal or leave the original requirement open.

F1 bridge · working architectural language

Two functions help explain what may be at stake.

Emotional information can participate in two interacting biological problem-spaces. These functions help identify the larger condition that may matter; they do not divide emotions into two fixed groups.

Direct maintenance and protection

Organism-protection

Organism-protection concerns maintaining the organism's viable condition, bodily integrity, boundaries, resources and capacity. It can include maintenance, restoration, distance, defence, escape or protective action.

Access to consequential others

Connection-preservation

Connection-preservation concerns maintaining access to people or groups whose availability affects care, belonging, cooperation, information, shared effort, protection or relational restoration.

The functions interact without becoming the same function

During early dependency, preserving connection to a caregiver also helps preserve warmth, nourishment, protection, orientation and regulation. Relationships can remain biologically consequential later in life, although the form and degree of dependency change.

Connection-preservation is not conformity, compliance or self-erasure. Organism-protection is not selfishness. Neither function authorises behaviour, establishes what another person feels, or reclassifies the established emotion families.

Two kinds of inner family

Each signal group is organised by its own question.

Body-condition families organise the internal condition being regulated. Emotion families organise the functional meaning that a condition may have for the organism. The two classifications are related, but they are not interchangeable.

A working regulatory structure

These are provisional TEG-Blue organising families, not seven established scientific natural kinds. A family identifies a regulatory domain and can later contain further signals. The familiar felt form does not name the whole underlying process.
