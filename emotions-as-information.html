Emotions as Information

Emotions contribute information about bodily and situational conditions. They do not dictate behaviour or provide a complete verdict about reality.

Approved foundation

Information, not instruction.

Working definition

An emotion is information participating in how a living organism reads a bodily or situational condition and prepares possibilities for participation.

A feeling can be real and important while its interpretation remains incomplete, revisable or mistaken. Emotion never authorises behaviour.

Approved dynamics

The signal remains distinct from its access, interpretation and outcome.

No fixed ladder

Emotion families do not form a temporal ladder, and one emotion does not transform into the next.

Coexistence remains possible

Several emotions, bodily signals and represented conditions may remain active or become differently foregrounded.

Feedback changes the read

When a relevant condition remains unresolved, outcomes become feedback and the organism reads the still-relevant condition again.

Behaviour remains separate

Action-readiness is not action. Conduct, power, impact, accountability and repair require their own record.

Protected architecture

The established families remain unchanged.

BelongingSignals participating where connection, relational value and shared life are relevant.
Belonging-at-RiskSignals participating where belonging, value, availability or relational continuity may be threatened or lost.
Threat-ProtectionSignals participating where danger, violation, contamination or defensive preparation is relevant.
Survival-RageSignals participating where maximum-force survival mobilisation becomes relevant.

Canonical development example

Love is the first structural reference case.

Accepted now, detailed work deferred

The Fluid–Chronic Love package demonstrates how one emotion can be followed without changing its identity. It is development architecture, not public authority, and Love-specific content is not a template for another emotion.
