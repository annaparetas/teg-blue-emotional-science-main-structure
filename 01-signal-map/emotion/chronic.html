Governing questionHow may the same signals be recruited, felt, interpreted, expressed and updated when participation has become chronically narrowed?

This page owns

The far Chronic companion reading: how emotion identity is preserved while access, salience, interpretation and use may vary across recurrent or difficult-to-update patterns.

It receives

The Fluid signal taxonomy; Model 1’s account of access and participation; Model 2’s present-organisation profiles; Model 3 (ESC)’s account of feedback, carryover and updating through time; and the shared Fluid–Chronic and human-sociality boundaries.

It does not establish

A diagnosis, one causal history, inevitable behaviour or a permanent condition. Chronic names a pattern, not a kind of person.

What “chronic” means here
Chronic Survival is the display term used here for far Chronic reference patterns. These patterns can involve recurrent narrowing, generalisation, easier recruitment, difficult revision, interrupted recovery, repeated reactivation or continuing adverse conditions across access, interpretation, action and recovery. Protection recruited before present threat is established and restoration that does not fully occur are important, and may be common, routes. They are not the only ones. Chronic does not mean severe, chosen, fake or permanent.
Emotion remains information
The ESS continues to register bodily, relational and environmental significance when relevant conditions arise. Chronic organisation changes whether that information becomes differentiated feeling, how CLS explains it, whether expression is permitted, which actions appear available and whether the original requirement can update. No feeling determines which action is taken.

Four questions for every feeling under Chronic Survival

The Fluid page reads every feeling through four questions — function, body, conditions, completion. Those questions remain the comparison point here. Chronic organisation adds another reading: how emotional information is weighted, accessed, interpreted and used, and how regulation continues when updating or Return becomes difficult.

What becomes easier to recruit or sustain
Which organisation repeatedly becomes available? What persists, recurs or is reconstructed, in which contexts, and what problem does it appear to be organising for?
How access to the feeling changes in experience
How does participation change while identity remains? Participation with the same emotion may involve clear felt access, fusion with other information, performed expression, active management, disowning, strong foregrounding or difficulty reaching it. These differences concern access, interpretation, expression and use; they do not turn it into another emotion.
What can still be felt
What remains readable from inside? Which part of the signal reaches ME as differentiated feeling, and what CLS makes the rest mean.
How regulation is organising — and what could change
What change could alter the present organisation? This may involve conditions, interpretation, action, recovery, support or repair. Lower pressure and Return are not always the same.

The cells on this page are organised around these questions. They describe characteristic possibilities — not required experiences, diagnoses, person-types, or proof that an emotion is distorted. Real danger, loss, injustice, pain, entrapment and absent accountability can keep a clear emotion active; awareness cannot make an unsafe condition safe.

Identity and trajectory boundary. A Chronic profile does not create an emotion, and an emotion does not prove a profile. Several signals may coexist, remain influential or move in and out of felt access. The cells describe possible intersections with present organisation, not stages in a sequence. How response and outcome become feedback through time belongs to Model 3 (ESC); this page does not infer that one emotion transforms into the next.

One proposed route by which participation may narrow

The main Gradient page names one learned route in four steps. It remains useful, but it is not the only Chronic route. Interrupted recovery, repeated reactivation, later reconstruction and continuing adverse conditions can also keep emotional information active or difficult to use.

Owner-review boundary. This route is a proposed interface, not a mechanism owned by the Emotional Signal Map. Model 1 must settle what reaches ME and remains influential; Model 2 the present organisation and Return support; Model 3 (ESC) the sequence, carryover, feedback and updating; and the Fluid–Chronic foundation the persistence profile.

  1. Signal formsThe organism registers that something matters.
  2. A cost or constraint has been learnedAttention, expression or action may previously have threatened safety or belonging, failed, or brought no repair.
  3. Participation narrowsThe feeling may be dismissed, mistrusted, fused, redirected or kept outside reflective use.
  4. The requirement remains openBehaviour can still be organised by information the person cannot yet use deliberately.

Not every override is a present conscious decision. A person may deliberately push a feeling away, but the route can also become learned and automatic. A real emotion establishes organism relevance; it does not by itself prove the external interpretation or authorise an action.

Reading scope. These rows describe how participation may change within each far Chronic reference profile — not an all-or-nothing switch, and not a description of every chronic adaptation. A capacity may remain highly active while becoming threat-biased, rigid, fused, compulsory or instrumental; reduced access is only one possible form of chronic change. Relief is not repair: management, narrative, control, closeness, or collapse can all lower pressure while the unresolved load stays underneath.

How participation can differ without changing signal identity

Emotional information may remain active while its weighting, felt access, interpretation or use changes. Five recurring participation patterns appear in the TEG-Blue material. They are working patterns for organising what has been observed, not five emotion types, biological stages, fixed trajectories or single mechanisms.

Fusion · signals arrive together

Connection and risk become difficult to separate

Warmth towards another person may arrive with tightening, self-consciousness or anticipation of rupture. The person may experience these as one response, particularly when connection and threat have repeatedly appeared within the same relationship.

The accompanying information can vary with context and depth. Working examples include love with shame, guilt or fear; compassion with shame; pride with threat; and belonging with vigilance.

Additional experience · a proposed relationship remains under review

An established signal may be difficult to recognise or use

Emotional information can influence attention, interpretation and action without arriving as a clear, differentiated feeling. TEG-Blue proposes several possible relationships between established signals and additional experiences. Envy, Arrogance, Contempt, Vindication and Resentment are gathered in a separate hypothesis section below; the proposed routes still require claim-by-claim research.

Compensation · connection does not reliably reduce protective demand

Closeness and continued monitoring can coexist

A kind word, touch or moment of warmth can be real while the person continues scanning, bracing or working to preserve the relationship. Visible connection therefore does not by itself establish felt safety, relational trust or physiological recovery.

Earlier learning, present conditions, limited trust and repeated relational uncertainty may each contribute. The observed pattern does not establish that one developmental capacity was permanently absent.

Intensification · mobilisation becomes easier to recruit or slower to settle

Anger remains available after repeated or continuing obstruction

When protection, an effective boundary, acknowledgement or repair repeatedly remains unavailable, anger may become easier to recruit, more intense or slower to settle. Continuing danger or injustice can also keep it active.

Anger and rage retain distinct identities and family memberships. TEG-Blue treats their possible relationship under repeated or continuing obstruction as a research question, not as one emotion transforming into the other or as one established circuit.

Reduced access · differentiated feeling may be faint, delayed or difficult to reach

Numbness, fog or unreadable pressure

During some persistent low-output, immobilisation or disconnection patterns, a person may report numbness, fog, heaviness or undifferentiated pressure. Emotional and bodily information may be less differentiated or less accessible; reduced report does not prove absent bodily representation.

Similar felt absence can arise through different routes. High mobilisation may narrow attention away from bodily information; depletion, dissociation, low arousal or other processes may reduce access in different ways. The experience alone does not identify the mechanism.

Feelings often change as conditions, interpretation, action and bodily organisation change. When a pattern repeats across time, it may become harder to recognise as one response among several. It can begin to feel like the situation itself, or like part of who the person is, even while remaining context-dependent and open to later revision.

The four families under Chronic Survival

The same families as the Fluid page — Belonging Emotions, Belonging-at-Risk, Threat-Protection, Survival-Rage — read across the seven far Chronic reference profiles. The Fluid function remains the comparison point; felt access, weighting, interpretation, expression and use may change. The matrix is a working TEG-Blue synthesis, not a settled scientific taxonomy or a way of placing a whole person in one column.

Survival-Rage

Emotion signals → Survival-Rage: why these emotions belong together

Fluid family · life threat and maximum survival action

Keep the three working distinctions visible. Rage concerns maximum force when ordinary protection is registered as insufficient. Fury keeps that force directed towards a perceived source of continuing danger. Frenzy concerns maximum mobilisation when no viable organised route is available. These are TEG-Blue framework distinctions, not established scientific natural kinds. None proves objective life threat, identifies a Gradient Position or authorises force.

How chronic organisation reads its own feelings

The tables above describe changes in access, weighting and use. One layer remains: how a feeling participates in interpretation. Two interpretive risks can appear when access or flexibility narrows, and they pull in opposite directions.

Feeling-Fact Collapse

“I feel it, therefore it is true.”

In some chronic patterns, a feeling can become the main evidence for an external conclusion. The feeling is real as experience and may carry important information about present conditions, earlier learning, needs or danger. Its intensity does not by itself establish the external cause. Relational risk increases when another person is required to accept, obey, mirror or organise around that conclusion.

Emotional-State Disconnection

“I do not feel it, therefore it is not real.”

The opposite risk appears when emotional and bodily information becomes faint, delayed or difficult to use. Interpretation can then proceed without information that might otherwise change it. Numbness, detachment, calmness and certainty can all be shaped by present organisation. Reduced felt access does not establish that nothing is happening, and it is not the same as objectivity.

Both risks can appear in the same person across different signals and contexts. Neither means the person is lying. Interpretation may be working with information that is incomplete, differently weighted or difficult to revise. The distinction also cuts both ways: chronic threat can involve genuine danger that other people do not recognise. The purpose is to improve resolution without dismissing the feeling.

Boundaries this page keeps

  • Chronic Survival does not automatically mean distorted. Real danger, loss, injustice, pain, entrapment and absent accountability can keep a clear emotion active.
  • Cells are possibilities, not verdicts. No cell is a diagnosis, a person-type, or proof of distortion — and no particular fusion is a signature of any Position.
  • Numbness does not prove that signalling stopped. It may describe an experience of reduced differentiated access; it is not evidence of absent impact, absent care or objectivity. The report alone does not identify the biological or psychological route.
  • Emotion explains signal; it never authorises harm. Reduced access to feeling does not erase responsibility for impact, repair, and changed behaviour.
  • Relief is not repair. Management, narrative, control, closeness, or collapse can lower pressure while the unresolved load stays underneath. Compliance is not consent; fear is not safety.
  • Do not force emotional access. Contact with emotion may need to return slowly, with enough safety, support, distance, or repair — and repair may need capacity before insight.
