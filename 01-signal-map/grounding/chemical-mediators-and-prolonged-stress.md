# Chemical Mediators And Prolonged Stress - Working Science Note

Status: accepted internal science architecture requiring continued evidence audit
Layer: shared physiology grounding beneath M1, M2, M3 and ESC
Date: 2026-07-11

## Central Rule

Do not map one emotion to one hormone or neurotransmitter.

```text
embodied emotional-regulatory event
= coordinated activity across neural circuits
+ autonomic activity
+ endocrine and neuroendocrine signalling
+ immune and inflammatory activity
+ metabolism, respiration and circulation
+ muscular and sensory organisation
+ learning, prediction and present context
```

M1 reads the emotional-information signal component of this event; M2 reads the
whole-organism configuration. Chemical mediators help implement the event and
configuration. They are not the emotion, the signal family or the Formation.

`Emotional hormones` is not a recognised scientific class. The substances
commonly grouped under that label also belong to different biological classes:

| Substance | More accurate class |
| --- | --- |
| Serotonin | Monoamine neurotransmitter / neuromodulator |
| Dopamine | Monoamine neurotransmitter / neuromodulator |
| Oxytocin | Peptide hormone in circulation and neuropeptide in the nervous system |
| Endorphins | Family of endogenous opioid peptides |
| Cortisol | Steroid glucocorticoid hormone |
| Adrenaline / epinephrine | Catecholamine hormone in the rapid sympatho-adrenal response |

## Audit Of Common One-Chemical Claims

### Serotonin is not happiness

Serotonin participates in mood regulation, punishment-sensitive inhibition,
aversive learning, patience, behavioural flexibility, sleep and appetite. No
serotonin level uniquely represents happiness, calm, stability or joy.

### Dopamine is not pleasure

Dopamine is strongly involved in reward prediction, learning, movement,
attention, motivation and incentive `wanting`. Hedonic `liking` is not reducible
to dopamine and involves endogenous opioid and other systems.

### Oxytocin is not love

Oxytocin participates in social attention, recognition, reciprocity, bonding
and attachment-related processes. Effects vary with person and context. It can
alter the salience of social information or response bias without creating
love, trust or accurate social understanding.

### Endorphins are not empowerment

Endogenous opioid systems participate in pain modulation, relief, reward and
some pleasurable experiences. Confidence, empowerment, energy and motivation
are not specific endorphin outputs. Peripheral beta-endorphin measurements also
do not provide a direct measure of central opioid activity.

### Cortisol is not stress

Cortisol is an essential HPA-axis mediator with ordinary circadian, metabolic,
immune and energy-allocation functions. Stress can change cortisol secretion,
timing and tissue response, but a cortisol value is not the experience of
stress.

### Adrenaline is not fear or anger

Adrenaline participates in rapid cardiovascular and metabolic mobilisation.
Fear, anger and anxiety involve distributed neural, autonomic, endocrine,
muscular, perceptual and contextual processes. Acute defensive action can begin
through fast neural routes before circulating adrenaline or cortisol could be
the cause of the first movement.

## Better Working Associations

| System | Defensible participation language | Do not say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Serotonergic | emotional and behavioural regulation, learning, patience, aversive processing, sleep and appetite | serotonin produces happiness |
| Dopaminergic | reward prediction, motivation, incentive salience, learning, attention and movement | dopamine is pleasure |
| Oxytocinergic | social salience, bonding-related processes, reciprocity and some attachment functions | oxytocin is love or universal trust |
| Endogenous opioid | pain modulation, relief, hedonic response and reward | endorphins are empowerment or confidence |
| HPA / cortisol | slower energy mobilisation, immune modulation and adaptation around demand | cortisol is stress or is only chronic |
| Sympatho-adrenal / catecholamine | rapid cardiovascular, respiratory and metabolic mobilisation | adrenaline is an emotion or only `fight-or-flight` |

Many listed support functions are real but overlapping. Sleep, appetite,
learning, bonding, pain, alertness and immune activity are each regulated by
multiple interacting systems rather than one chemical owner.

## Immediate Collision Example

The car example demonstrates that fast action is not caused by a conscious
emotion or by waiting for a hormonal stress response.

```text
looming collision information reaches the visual system
-> one embodied read forms in which
   - ESS describes biological registration of trajectory, time-to-impact and
     consequence
   - M1 describes extremely high-priority Threat-Protection information
   - M2 describes the immediate defensive configuration shift
-> defensive, postural, attentional and muscular organisation changes
-> M3 uses the available condition-matched escape route
-> motor commands and neuromuscular signalling produce the jump
```

Adrenaline and noradrenaline can support the wider rapid response. Cortisol is a
slower HPA-axis mediator. Neither needs to become a consciously felt emotion
before the person moves. The consciously reportable fear may arise during or
after the action.

The jump is also not one fixed reflex. Human escape can integrate trajectory,
time-to-impact, obstacles, body position and available routes without requiring
explicit verbal deliberation. Another person may step, run, brace, pull someone
else, freeze briefly or fail to move in time.

## Normal Acute Cortisol Arc

```text
Actual or predicted demand
-> hypothalamic CRH signalling
-> pituitary ACTH signalling
-> adrenal cortisol secretion
-> energy mobilisation and immune, metabolic and cognitive modulation
-> negative feedback through glucocorticoid-sensitive systems
-> further secretion reduces
-> cortisol is metabolised
-> physiological recovery proceeds
```

Cortisol is continuously produced, circulated, metabolised and removed. The
same cortisol material is not stored indefinitely because an emotion failed to
leave.

## What `Cortisol Stays` Can Mean

Under prolonged or recurrent demand, several different patterns are possible:

1. **Continued elevation** - secretion continues because actual or predicted
   demand remains active.
2. **Repeated peaks** - cortisol reduces after one event but recurrent events,
   anticipation or cognitive re-entry recruit new responses.
3. **Delayed recovery** - secretion and downstream effects decline more slowly.
4. **Altered diurnal timing** - morning rise, daily slope or evening level
   becomes flatter, elevated, reduced or more variable.
5. **Blunted responsiveness** - a later stressor produces a smaller response;
   this does not prove regulation or absence of cost.
6. **Glucocorticoid resistance** - cortisol is present, but target cells become
   less responsive to expected signalling, including anti-inflammatory effects.

Therefore use:

> Under prolonged or recurrent demand, cortisol regulation can become elevated,
> repeatedly recruited, slow to recover, mistimed, blunted or resistant.

Do not use:

> Cortisol remains because the stress was not discharged.

## Prolonged Stress Is Not Uniform Hyperactivation

Chronic organisation must not be equated with every mediator staying high.
Possible chronic patterns include:

- high output
- repeated short-lived output
- delayed termination
- altered circadian timing
- low or blunted output after prior over-recruitment
- altered receptor sensitivity
- mismatch between systems
- ordinary mediator level with abnormal downstream response

The relevant chronic question is:

> Has timing, recruitment, termination, recovery, sensitivity or coordination
> become persistently altered?

## Other Repeated Or Dysregulated Systems

### Adrenaline and noradrenaline

Catecholamines are generally cleared rapidly. Under prolonged stress, the more
defensible pattern is repeated bursts, elevated sympathetic tone, incomplete
cardiovascular recovery or altered receptor and vascular responsiveness—not the
same adrenaline remaining stored.

### Immune and inflammatory mediators

IL-6, TNF-alpha, CRP and related mediators are not all hormones, but they are
important to prolonged-stress physiology. Repeated stress responses and reduced
glucocorticoid sensitivity can permit low-grade inflammatory activity to
persist or increase. Evidence varies by population, duration and method, so do
not claim that every chronic stressor creates systemic inflammation.

### Glucose and insulin systems

Cortisol and catecholamines help make energy available. Repeated mobilisation
can alter glucose regulation, insulin demand, appetite and fat distribution and
may contribute to insulin resistance. These are downstream regulatory effects,
not emotional signals.

### DHEA

DHEA is another adrenal mediator and may counterbalance some glucocorticoid
effects. Cortisol-to-DHEA ratios are scientifically interesting but currently
too inconsistent to use as an individual chronic-stress diagnostic marker.

### Serotonin, dopamine, oxytocin and endogenous opioids

Under chronic conditions these systems may show altered synthesis, release,
receptor sensitivity, connectivity, learning effects or cue responsiveness.
Avoid saying that they simply `stay in the body`. Peripheral blood levels also
do not directly report activity inside relevant brain circuits.

## Stressor Type And Individual Variation

Physical danger, infection, pain, sleep loss, social evaluation, relational
threat, caregiving, deprivation and cognitive anticipation can all recruit
overlapping regulatory systems. They do not produce one identical hormonal
profile.

Response depends on:

- intensity, duration, novelty and predictability
- controllability and available action
- time of day and sleep
- developmental calibration and previous exposure
- current illness, pain, nutrition and medication
- age, sex-related physiology and individual differences
- available relational, material and environmental support
- whether the stressor has actually ended

## TEG-Blue Ownership

Chemical mediators are mechanism-level implementation, not new signal families
or Formation labels.

| TEG owner | Chemical-physiology relation |
| --- | --- |
| **ESS** | Detects interoceptive and external conditions, including downstream bodily change. Body hardware and present biological weighting remain here. |
| **M1** | Names the emotional-information priority; it does not name a hormone as the emotion. |
| **M2** | Names the coordinated multisystem configuration in which neural, autonomic, endocrine, immune, metabolic and muscular mediators participate. |
| **M3** | Tracks whether further recruitment can terminate, physiological recovery can proceed and baseline access can reopen. |
| **ESC** | Tracks repeated peaks, delayed recovery, altered timing, carryover and accumulation across cycles. |
| **GC** | Supplies the shared map vocabulary for configuration load, recovery, allostatic load and possible overload. |

## Connection To Load Vocabulary

| TEG term | Biological examples |
| --- | --- |
| **Configuration load** | Current sympathetic, endocrine, muscular, attentional and metabolic recruitment |
| **Recruitment termination** | New HPA, sympatho-adrenal or motor recruitment stops increasing |
| **Incomplete recovery** | Cortisol declines slowly, cardiovascular activation persists or inflammatory signalling remains active |
| **Regulatory carryover** | Altered rhythm, residual activation, reduced capacity, receptor resistance, carried threat prediction or easier reconstruction |
| **Allostatic load** | Accumulated dysregulation across neuroendocrine, autonomic, immune, cardiovascular, respiratory and metabolic systems |
| **Allostatic overload** | Demands and cumulative costs exceed adaptive capacity enough that health, access or functioning deteriorates |

Regulatory carryover is still not identical to allostatic load. One altered
cortisol response is not proof of either a chronic Formation or allostatic
overload.

## Accepted Working Statement

> Under prolonged or recurrent demand, stress mediators do not necessarily
> remain uniformly elevated. Regulatory systems can become repeatedly
> recruited, slow to recover, mistimed, blunted, resistant or poorly
> coordinated. These patterns create biological forms of regulatory carryover
> and can contribute to cumulative allostatic load.

## Guardrails

- Do not call chemicals emotions.
- Do not create one-emotion / one-hormone mappings.
- Do not infer an emotional state from one blood, saliva, urine or hair value.
- Do not infer central neurotransmitter activity directly from peripheral
  measurements.
- Do not equate chronic stress with permanently high cortisol.
- Do not treat blunted output as proof that the system is healthy or calm.
- Do not treat elevated cortisol as proof of a particular psychological cause.
- Do not turn one biomarker into a Formation diagnosis.
- Do not claim that every prolonged stressor produces the same endocrine or
  inflammatory pattern.
- Do not use physiological explanation to excuse coercion, impact or harm.
