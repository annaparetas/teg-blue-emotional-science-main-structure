Chronic Emotional Signals — TEG-Blue Collection

This page gathers every substantive description of how emotions are felt and understood under chronic conditions, collected from across the teg-blue project folders — the current engine, the .org site and drafts, the foundation documents, and the legacy vaults. It is a working source collection for the future Chronic emotions page, and the chronic counterpart to Emotions and Feelings — The Fluid Gradient. Each section names its source file; nothing here is newly authored theory.

What “chronic” means here
The canonical .org definition: chronic names “organisation that has become rigid or repeatedly available — where protection engages before threat is confirmed, and rest no longer restores. In chronic mode every position is defensive: a person may look calm or connected while the body is still bracing, monitoring, pleasing, managing, or conserving energy.” Acute does not mean mild; chronic does not mean severe, chosen, fake, or permanent. The question the two views separate is “whether movement, return and repair are still available.”
The central claim
Chronic organisation changes participation, not the original biological reason for signalling. The ESS continues to register what matters. What changes is whether that information becomes differentiated feeling, how cognition explains it, whether expression is permitted, which action follows, and whether the original requirement can update.
Source statuses
Material is marked current (the live engine and .org data), draft (working files not yet published), or legacy (earlier eras of the project, kept because their descriptions remain the richest available). Where versions disagree, the current engine wins.

A response that passes, or a pattern that has stayed

Before any chronic reading, the sources ask one discernment question: is this an emotion moving through, or a pattern that has stayed? Both can feel intense. Both can feel real. The difference is whether there is an after.

Dimension Moving through Staying
DurationRises and passes — there is a before and an after. The feeling has a shape.Always there — no beginning, no end. It feels like weather.
ReturnComes back to something else — other states are available. The system shifts.This is the baseline — nothing to return from. Other states feel unreal.
Trigger matchMatched to what’s here — proportional to what happened.Matched to something older — carrying weight from before.
AwarenessKnows what’s happening — can name it, see it while inside it.Invisible from inside — feels like “just who I am.”
RangeOne response among many — other options are available.The only response available — what looks like choice is a constraint.
The bodySettles after — tension releases, breathing returns, sleep comes.Never fully settles — tension is the resting state.

A response that moves has information — even when intense, it does not need fixing. A pattern that has stayed cannot be thought away, because thinking is often part of how it stays. When a pattern stays long enough it stops feeling like a pattern — it feels like “just who I am,” and that invisibility is part of how it stays. The question is not what is felt. The question is whether it can finish.

How signals change form under chronic conditions

The acute emotional chain walks the gradient and walks back: a signal fires, is read, completes, and yields for the next. Under chronic conditions the chain walks forward and does not walk back — the crossings weld. The M1-Chronic architecture catalogues five distinct forms this rewriting takes.

“Fused signals cannot be experienced as temporary: felt the same in every state, with no recalibration hint and no temporal separation, the fused threat-bonded configuration is not experienced as a passing signal but as the structure of reality itself.”

The rewriting begins at rest. In a nervous system that completes its cycles, the resting signal profile is minimal — near-neutral between events. In chronic configuration the rest carries something else: “a continuous undertone of shame, or watchfulness, or brace. The body generates signals across the day without any specific event triggering them.” At Chronic Threat & Defence, belonging-at-risk signals run at rest — shame, guilt, watchfulness for relational risk. At Chronic Strategy & Management, threat-protection signals run at rest — sustained low-grade fear, brace, anticipatory mobilisation.

Form 1 · Signal Fusion

The bonding signal carries its own risk

Warmth toward another person — and in the same physiological event, a tightening alongside the warming, a self-criticism arriving inside the reach itself. The two are not sequential; the body produces them as one. The fusion is “the accurate readout of a nervous system whose developmental environment produced bond signals and rupture signals from within the same relational configuration.”

The fusion partner tracks chronic severity: at lower severity the embedded signal is belonging-at-risk — love-fused-with-shame. At higher severity it is threat-protection — love-fused-with-fear. There are no intermediate gradations; the body produces the fused output directly.

Working examples from the M1 file: love fused with guilt, compassion fused with shame, pride fused with threat, belonging fused with vigilance.

Form 2 · Distortion

The body generates one signal and experiences another

Admiration toward a friend’s accomplishment — a warm pull toward the other’s good. When the interoceptive access delivering the body’s findings to cognition is not available, the signal cannot complete. “What the body produced was admiration. What reaches experience is envy.”

Two mechanisms produce the distorted family. Reception Blockage fails at the body–cognition interface: the signal arrives at the boundary and gets nothing back. Restoration Blockage fails at completion: the signal is received, but the conditions it needs to complete are absent for so long that it hardens into a distorted form.

Diagnostic claim: a nervous system with interoceptive access intact, living substantially in connection, safety, and belonging, does not generate these signals.

Form 3 · Compensation

Pretended Bonding-Belonging — closeness that does not settle

“A hand on the shoulder, a kind word, warmth between two people, but no slowing of the heart, no opening of the breath, no moment where the nervous system marks safe and stops scanning.” When the substrate for genuine bonding was never developmentally built, cognition scans for approximated-safety markers and the body generates signals shaped like bonding-belonging — without the ventral-vagal settling genuine bonding requires.

This was the only signal shape the organism learned could produce relational stability. The signals hold proximity the body reads as safer than rupture. The cost is structural: the cycle does not close because the substrate that would close it does not exist.

Form 4 · Intensification

RAGE — anger that cannot pass

“Anger that does not pass — the heat that arrives, holds, and stays running.” Across enough cycles where the protective mobilisation cannot complete — no boundary set, no acknowledgment received — the circuits sensitise: the threshold for firing drops, the amplitude rises, settling takes longer even when conditions resolve.

RAGE is distinct from the distorted signals: it keeps its rooting in the body’s signal-generation hardware. Anger remains anger, intensified and sustained. What differs is the chronic substrate that amplifies its magnitude and obstructs its completion.

Form 5 · Collapse

Signal collapse — numbness, fog, unreadable pressure

At persistent shutdown, “signals may present as numbness, fog, absence, collapse or inaccessible pressure.” Emotion may be there as inaccessible pressure or absence before it can become a differentiated feeling. The interoceptive channel itself collapses; self-read becomes faint, fragmented, or numb.

The sources keep one distinction sharp: “Absence has two opposite causes. In high mobilisation the body holds its own state back because registering it would work against survival; in shutdown there is nothing left to hold it with. Same blank cell, opposite mechanism.”

Eight current organisations of a feeling

The live engine describes present emotional organisation under Chronic Survival through eight recognisable configurations — what the person may experience, and what has changed underneath. “Nervous-system states may remain unknown while cognition overrides, replaces or misinterprets embodied information.”

Current organisation What the person may experience What has changed
Felt but distrusted“I feel it, but I should not believe or show it.”Signal trust and expression safety.
Felt but unnamedPressure, heaviness, agitation, ache or urgency without a differentiated emotion.Recognition and emotional vocabulary.
FusedThe source feeling and the fear, shame or relational consequence of expressing it arrive together.Source differentiation.
Suppressed or inaccessibleNumbness, distance, blankness or awareness only after the event.Felt access and usable awareness.
PerformedThe permitted emotion is displayed while the underlying feeling remains unavailable or unsafe.Correspondence between feeling and expression.
Mis-sourced or mis-targetedThe feeling is real, but its cause, target, timing, scale or responsibility is assigned incorrectly.Correspondence with Actual Conditions.
Cognitively overriddenAn explanation contradicts, dismisses or prematurely closes embodied information.Cooperation between body information and cognition.
Differentiated but unupdatedThe person understands the feeling, but danger, injustice, pain, entrapment or absent accountability continues.The required evidence, route or material condition remains unavailable.
embodied emotional information forms → awareness cannot reliably recognise, tolerate or use it → cognition supplies a safer or familiar explanation → action follows the protected model or a substitute route → the original emotional requirement may remain unupdated

Chronic Survival does not automatically mean distorted. Material danger, unresolved injustice, pain, entrapment, route removal or absent accountability can keep a clear emotion active. Awareness cannot make an unsafe condition safe. The last row of the table exists precisely for this: a feeling can be fully differentiated and correct while the world still refuses its completion.

The master matrix — 24 emotions across 7 Chronic Formations

The densest artefact in the corpus: how each emotion may feel across Chronic Survival Organisation. “The emotion keeps its primary Acute Source information and direction; the Chronic Survival Formation changes state pressure, felt access, expression, weight and the route through which it can be used.” The matrix carries a working-hypothesis label: cells are characteristic possibilities, not required experiences, fixed person-types or proof that the emotion is distorted.

Reading instruction (verbatim): “Read horizontally to compare one emotion across states. Read vertically to see how one Chronic Survival Formation filters different emotions.”

How chronic organisation reads its own feelings

The tables above describe what feelings become. The foundation documents describe what the person then does with them — how a chronic configuration interprets its own signals, and where that interpretation loses contact with the situation.

The four distortion chains

The legacy M2 data compresses each chronic mode’s misreading of its own emotional signal into one chain. Each starts from the same real feeling.

Connection
“I feel bad → I caused it → I must fix myself”
Protection
“I feel bad → you’re threatening me → I must defend”
Control
“I feel bad → you’re destabilising me → I must manage you”
Domination
“I feel bad → you’re challenging me → I must eliminate”

Source: _extracts/site-at-2026-04-12/src/data/m2-data.js legacy

Two reality distortions

The foundation files hold two distortions at once. Both damage reality contact; neither means the person is lying. “The person may not be trying to lie. They may be trying to survive inside the model their state has built.”

Feeling-Fact Collapse

“I feel it, therefore it is true.”

In chronic states the separation between feeling and fact can collapse. A feeling becomes a fact; an interpretation becomes evidence; a body prediction feels like external truth. Examples from the source:

  • “I feel unsafe, therefore danger is present.”
  • “I feel ashamed, therefore I am wrong or worthless.”
  • “I feel angry, therefore the other person intended harm.”
  • “I feel certain, therefore no further evidence is needed.”

The coercive risk begins when felt reality becomes something another person must accept, obey, mirror, or organise around: “You must treat what I feel as what is true.”

Emotional-State Disconnection

“I do not feel it, therefore it is not real.”

The opposite failure: emotion and body state become unavailable as information, and cognition models reality from incomplete data while still sounding coherent — the source names this numb coherence. Examples:

  • “I do not feel affected, therefore it does not matter.”
  • “I do not feel grief, therefore nothing was lost.”
  • “I am calm, therefore I am accurate.”
  • “I can explain it, therefore I have understood it.”

“Not feeling something does not mean the person is objective… Disconnection from emotion is not objectivity.”

Sources: what-is-teg-blue/foundation/06-state-shaped-perception.md · foundation/13-emotional-access-and-reality-contact.md current

Why the same emotion means different things

“Anger from safety and anger from chronic threat are not the same signal. Both are called anger. Both feel like anger. But one is a whole-organism configuration that can still move, and the other is one that has stopped moving — and that changes what the anger is reporting.”

“From chronic threat, anger may be reporting the same crossed line — or it may be reporting a pattern the body learned years ago and is now predicting again. Often both at once. The signal is real either way. What it is pointing at is harder to locate.”

The same file adds the counterweight the framework insists on: “this is not a reason to distrust yourself… Someone in chronic threat is often reading a genuine danger that people around them cannot see. The distinction is there to give the signal more resolution, never to give anyone else grounds for dismissing it.”

Source: what-is-teg-blue/foundation/18-detailed-synthesis.md §4–5 current

Signals that cannot complete become new load

At the far end of the gradient, the sources describe emotional signals that are generated but never received. Harm on the harming side still produces shame, guilt, and remorse — “in a complete system these are the course-correction. In this configuration the equipment that would process them is offline, so they cannot complete. They become new activation.” The engine states the same fact per-formation: at Chronic Strategy & Management, “shame/guilt generated but not received”; at Chronic Power & Dominance, “shame/guilt generated but not received, so no remorse-as-self-signal.”

The empathy configuration this produces is precise: “Cognitive empathy stays sharp — redirected to leverage. Emotional resonance collapses — the other’s pain does not land. Interoceptive self-awareness is absent — there is no internal signal that any of this is happening.” The framework’s one-sentence summary of the whole escalation: “the person is not regulating despite the harm; the harm is the regulation.”

Boundaries the sources keep repeating

Every era of the material carries the same guardrails, stated in slightly different words. They are collected here because the future Chronic emotions page will need all of them.

  • Chronic Survival does not automatically mean distorted. Real danger, loss, injustice, pain, entrapment and absent accountability can keep a clear emotion active. Awareness cannot make an unsafe condition safe.
  • Cells are possibilities, not verdicts. The matrices describe characteristic possibilities — not required experiences, diagnoses, person-types, or proof of distortion. No particular fusion is a mode signature.
  • Do not over-name one emotion as the cause. The chronic modes describe fused signal fields; care, fear, shame, need and anger can be active together.
  • Numbness is still a signal. It should not be read as absence of impact, absence of care, or objectivity. Do not shame people who are numb, detached, or shut down — disconnection may be a survival adaptation.
  • Do not force emotional access. Sometimes contact with emotion has to return slowly, with enough safety, support, distance, or repair. Do not demand insight or accountability before capacity for contact returns.
  • Emotion explains signal; it never authorises harm. A real emotion establishes organism relevance. It does not prove the external interpretation, and reduced access to feeling does not erase responsibility for impact, repair, and changed behaviour.
  • Relief is not repair. Management, narrative, control, closeness, or collapse can all lower pressure while the unresolved load stays underneath. Compliance is not consent; fear is not safety.
  • Not every override is a present conscious decision. A person may deliberately push a feeling away, but the route can also be learned and automatic — “the override is experienced as stability, not as effort.”
