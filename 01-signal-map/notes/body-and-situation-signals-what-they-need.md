# Body Signals and Situation Signals: What the Signal Needs

Status: completion–Return vocabulary audit · row-level Situation Signal implementation · 23 August 2026
Owner: Emotional Signal Map · signal concern and possible completing conditions
Companions: [Fluid Signal Map](../emotion/fluid.html) · [Emotional-Somatic Cycle](../../04-model-3-esc/cycle.html) · [Return and Recovery](../../03-model-2-gradient/return.html) · [Shared vocabulary decision](../../foundations/notes/three-model-architecture.md#shared-vocabulary-and-model-ownership)

## Why this distinction matters

The difference between Body Signals and Situation Signals becomes particularly clear in the column **What the signal needs**.

For a Body Signal, the need is often relatively direct because the signal begins with a regulated bodily condition.

- Thirst needs water balance restored.
- Hunger needs usable energy and nutrients.
- Breathlessness needs breathing conditions restored.
- Urination needs the bladder emptied when it is safe and possible.
- Overheating needs heat reduced.
- Cold needs warmth.
- Tiredness needs recovery.
- Pain needs the source of possible tissue harm located, stopped or protected.

The conscious feeling is not identical to the underlying physiology, but the relationship between the signal and the condition it needs is often visible.

For a Situation Signal, the need is less obvious from the feeling alone. It becomes clearer when we understand the signal’s function.

- Fear does not simply need fear to decrease. It needs danger accurately located and protection or safety restored.
- Shame does not simply need reassurance. It needs accurate information about belonging, exposure, standing and whether repair is possible.
- Anger does not simply need discharge. It needs the crossing, obstruction or violation understood and an effective boundary or route restored.
- Grief does not need the lost condition recreated when that is impossible. It needs the loss recognised and enough support and time for reorganisation.

This is why the **Emotion function** column is essential to interpreting **What the signal needs**. The emotion name or bodily sensation alone does not tell us what would allow the signal to update.

## What the final column owns

The public phrase **What the signal needs** is accessible shorthand. More precisely, the column asks:

> **What change, evidence or available condition may allow this signal to update or complete its present recruitment?**

It does not answer every outcome question that follows.

| Term | Question | Owner | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Completing condition** | What bodily, environmental, relational or represented condition would have to change, become sufficiently clear or be recognised as irreversible? | Emotional Signal Map | A possible condition is not a behavioural instruction or something another person automatically owes. |
| **Completion** | Did the relevant condition change sufficiently for the present recruitment to change course? | Signal Map + ESC | Completion is demand-specific, not discharge, calm or a whole-person endpoint. |
| **Updating** | Did new evidence change signal priority, interpretation, expectation, readiness or response? | ESC, with Model 1 participation | A changed explanation does not establish changed physiology, conduct or repair. |
| **Return** | Did a more flexible, less defensively dominated and more restorative Gradient organisation become available? | Nervous System Gradient | Return may begin before recovery is complete and is not guaranteed by one signal changing. |
| **Recovery** | What resources, tissues, sleep, pain, metabolic capacity or other channels still need rebuilding? | Gradient support + ESC outcome | Different channels can recover at different rates. |
| **Carryover** | What demand, weighting, depleted capacity, expectation or cost enters the next condition? | ESC | Carryover is decomposable; it is not an emotion stored intact. |
| **Repair** | What action addresses impact, responsibility, restitution, protection or possible relational rebuilding? | Behaviour / Harm–Power–Repair | Repair can remain necessary after completion, Return or recovery. |

The handoff is therefore:

> **signal concern → possible completing condition → observed event outcome → updating or carryover → possible Return → recovery that may still remain**

The arrows organise questions. They do not claim a fixed biological sequence.

## A biological distinction

Body Signals are organised primarily around detecting and correcting internal physiological conditions.

Situation Signals are organised primarily around registering what current or represented conditions mean for connection, safety, action and survival.

That gives them different neurochemical structures.

### Body Signals

A Body Signal can often be followed through four distinguishable layers:

1. **Regulated variable**
   What changed: water balance, energy availability, CO₂/O₂, temperature, bladder volume, tissue condition or sleep pressure.

2. **Detection**
   What registers the change: osmoreceptors, chemoreceptors, thermoreceptors, mechanoreceptors, nociceptors or specialised metabolic-sensing neurons.

3. **Transmission and regulation**
   Which neural transmitters, peptides, hormones, inflammatory mediators and autonomic pathways carry or regulate the information.

4. **Correction**
   What bodily or behavioural response can restore the condition: drinking, eating, breathing, cooling, warming, emptying, resting or protecting injured tissue.

Because many Body Signals begin with specialised physiological detection, they may be more neurochemically distinguishable from one another than Situation Signals. They still do not have one-signal/one-chemical fingerprints.

### Situation Signals

Situation Signals do not usually begin with one specific regulated variable. Their neurochemistry belongs to a wider process combining:

- perception;
- memory and learning;
- current and represented conditions;
- bodily state;
- prediction and appraisal;
- nervous system organisation;
- action preparation;
- social and environmental information.

Fear is not a reading of adrenaline. Shame is not a reading of cortisol. Love is not a reading of oxytocin.

For Situation Signals, the useful scientific question is how the relative recruitment, timing and function of interacting systems change as the organism moves through connection, uncertain safety, direct threat and life threat.

## Preliminary Body Signal neurochemistry map

| Body Signal | Physiological and neurochemical starting points |
| --- | --- |
| Breathlessness | CO₂, O₂ and pH sensing; peripheral and central chemoreceptors; brainstem respiratory circuits; serotonergic, noradrenergic and other modulatory participation |
| Thirst | Osmolality, blood volume and sodium balance; lamina terminalis circuits; angiotensin II and vasopressin-related regulation |
| Hunger | Energy and nutrient sensing; ghrelin, leptin, insulin, CCK, PYY, serotonin, NPY/AgRP and melanocortin systems |
| Urination | Bladder stretch and urothelial signalling; ATP/purinergic signalling, afferent nerves and autonomic control |
| Bowel emptying | Mechanical distension, enteric nervous-system activity, serotonin and multiple gastrointestinal peptides |
| Pain | Nociceptors, glutamate, substance P, prostaglandins, bradykinin, inflammatory mediators and descending opioid modulation |
| Overheating and cold | Peripheral and central thermosensors, TRP channels, hypothalamic control and autonomic/endocrine effectors |
| Tiredness | Sleep pressure, adenosine, circadian signalling, metabolic state, inflammatory cytokines and multiple arousal systems |

This is an orientation map, not yet the paper-grounded account for each signal.

## Where the two groups meet

Body Signals and Situation Signals remain distinct, but they interact continuously.

Breathlessness may begin as a Body Signal. The organism may then register smoke, an obstructed airway, inability to escape or possible death. Those are Situation Signals about the conditions producing the bodily change.

The reverse also happens. Fear may alter breathing, heartbeat, temperature, pain, bladder activity and hunger. A Situation Signal therefore creates additional bodily information that may become consciously available through interoception.

Both groups can converge through:

- brainstem and hypothalamic regulation;
- ascending interoceptive pathways;
- insular and cingulate processing;
- autonomic and endocrine responses;
- attention and salience;
- dopaminergic, serotonergic, noradrenergic and opioid modulation;
- learning, prediction and contextual interpretation.

Bodily information access and use can make aspects of both groups subjectively available. Emotional awareness differentiates Situation Signals as emotions. Situational awareness helps locate the conditions those emotions concern. Detection, attention, felt clarity, interpretation and action use remain separate questions.

## Working research rule

The two groups need different scientific questions.

For **Body Signals**, map:

> regulated variable → sensor → afferent pathway → central integration → conscious sensation → corrective response

For **Situation Signals**, map:

> condition → signal function → nervous system organisation → relative system recruitment → action priority → updating or completion

This explains why **What the signal needs** is immediately legible for many Body Signals but depends on understanding function for Situation Signals.

## Body Signal calibration

Body Signals provide the clearest test of the vocabulary because the relevant physiological condition is often more directly measurable. Even here, correction, completion, Return and recovery remain different observations.

| Body Signal | Possible completing condition | What may change in the current recruitment | Return, recovery and carryover boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Breathlessness** | Ventilation and gas exchange sufficiently meet present demand, or the condition interfering with breathing changes. | Urgent respiratory recruitment and interruption of other activity may reduce. | Wider organisation may release while respiratory, muscular or illness-related recovery continues. |
| **Pain** | Possible harm is checked; continuing danger is reduced; the affected region is protected or treated where possible. | Protective attention, guarding or avoidance may change course. | Pain may persist after tissue danger changes, and injury may require recovery without ongoing pain. |
| **Overheating** | Heat loss restores a workable temperature range. | Cooling recruitment and the pull to stop or seek a cooler environment may reduce. | Fluid balance, fatigue or illness-related recovery may remain. |
| **Cold** | Warmth restores a workable temperature range. | Heat-conservation and heat-production recruitment may reduce. | Energy expenditure or tissue effects may still require recovery. |
| **Needing to urinate** | A safe opportunity allows bladder emptying. | The escalating interruption signal can reduce. | Relief does not establish treatment where pain, obstruction or another condition remains. |
| **Thirst** | Water and electrolyte availability move the relevant regulated conditions toward a workable range. | Search and drinking priority can reduce as new bodily information arrives. | Rehydration continues over time; illness, heat exposure or depleted capacity may carry forward. |
| **Needing to empty the bowels** | A safe opportunity allows waste to pass. | The escalating interruption signal can reduce. | Relief does not establish resolution of pain, constipation or another continuing condition. |
| **Hunger** | Nourishment makes usable energy and nutrients sufficiently available. | Food-search priority may reduce as ingestion and post-ingestive information arrive. | Digestion and metabolic restoration continue after the conscious signal changes. |
| **Tiredness** | Rest and sleep rebuild enough spent capacity for demand and available resources to become workable again. | The pull to stop can reduce as capacity returns. | Recovery is itself extended here; quietness or forced wakefulness is not completion. |

The Body Signal pattern is:

> **regulated condition changes → signal priority can update → present recruitment may complete → whole-organism Return may become available → recovery can continue**

No single row establishes every transition.

## Situation Signal audit

Situation Signals need a wider question because the relevant condition may exist in the material environment, one person's action field, a relationship, a collective system or a remembered or anticipated event. The signal can be accurate while the condition remains unchanged. It can also update when evidence revises the original read.

### Belonging and favourable conditions

| Signal | What it concerns | Example in context | What may allow updating or completion | If the completing condition does not occur | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Love** | The value of a person and bond. | Someone expresses love to a partner and invites the bond to remain mutually known and cared for. | The bond's value becomes expressible or mutually recognisable enough to guide care and participation. | Love may remain internally known while the relational bid remains unanswered. Repeated non-response may bring disappointment, loneliness, uncertainty or protective distance into later cycles. | Love need not end to complete one moment; mutuality cannot be inferred from one person's feeling. |
| **Trust** | Whether reliance is sufficiently safe. | A person shares something vulnerable with a friend or relies on them to keep an agreement. | Ongoing evidence supports, narrows or revises the degree of reliance placed in the person or situation. | Vigilance may return and trust may narrow. Disappointment, fear, anger, a boundary or greater distance may become relevant if reliability remains unavailable. | Settling is not proof of safety; vigilance is not proof of danger. |
| **Gratitude** | Care, support or benefit received. | Someone notices that another person helped during a difficult week and feels moved to acknowledge it. | What was received becomes consciously registered; expression may support reciprocity where appropriate. | The benefit can remain real even when gratitude cannot be expressed or received. If the help carried pressure or an expected debt, trust, guilt or a boundary may also need attention. | Expression is not owed, and gratitude does not erase cost, dependence or power. |
| **Compassion** | Another person's suffering and significance. | A friend is grieving, and someone offers to listen or help without taking over. | Suffering is accurately recognised and any care offered is useful, receivable and preserves self–other distinction. | Compassion may remain without becoming action. Refused or unavailable care may bring sadness, helplessness or frustration while the other person's autonomy remains intact. | Compassion does not author action, guarantee accuracy or require self-erasure. |
| **Admiration** | A valued quality, skill or contribution. | A colleague's careful work draws attention and becomes something another person wants to learn from. | The quality is recognised and can inform appreciation or learning. | Appreciation may remain private and learning may continue. If access to the person or route closes, the observer can revise how the valued quality is pursued. | Admiration does not require imitation, hierarchy or surrender of judgement. |
| **Happiness** | Favourable, fitting or nourishing present conditions. | A quiet afternoon after finishing a demanding task feels easy and sufficient. | Enough attention and safety allow the favourable condition to register. | The experience may pass unnoticed or be interrupted when attention and capacity remain elsewhere. The favourable condition can still have existed. | The experience can complete without the good condition ending or being displayed. |
| **Joy** | Available play, movement, celebration or shared aliveness. | A reunion, game or piece of music opens movement and a wish to share the moment. | Enough capacity and permission allow aliveness to be experienced or expressed. | Joy can remain private. When sharing was part of the moment, non-response may bring disappointment or self-consciousness without making the original joy false. | Expression is one route, not proof of intensity or completion. |
| **Pride** | Meaningful effort, growth, contribution or integrity. | Someone finishes difficult work and shares the achievement with a person whose recognition matters to the event. | The achievement or value becomes consciously registered; relevant recognition may be received where the achievement is genuinely shared. | The achievement can remain real without recognition. Where recognition was part of the condition, disappointment, shame or anger may arise and the relationship or audience may need to be reconsidered. | External approval is not universally required, and pride does not establish impact or entitlement. |
| **Hope** | A valued future that remains possible. | A treatment, application or conversation offers a credible route towards something that matters. | Possibility remains credible enough to guide participation, or expectation revises when the route changes or closes. | If the route closes, disappointment, sadness or grief may help expectation change. Continuing effort may shift to another route rather than remain attached to an unavailable outcome. | Hope is not evidence that the outcome will occur. |

### Belonging at risk, obstruction and loss

| Signal | What it concerns | Example in context | What may allow updating or completion | If the completing condition does not occur | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Shame** | Exposure, standing, belonging or the acceptable self. | Someone is corrected publicly and cannot yet tell whether they made a repairable mistake or lost their place in the group. | Evidence clarifies whether belonging or impact changed; proportionate repair occurs where responsibility exists, or the self/relationship read revises where it does not. | Shame may remain, recur or generalise. Hiding, appeasement, withdrawal, anger or greater protection may become available, especially when exposure or rejection continues. | Reassurance alone may not answer the condition; shame is not proof of wrongdoing. |
| **Guilt** | Possible harm or cost produced by one's action. | A person forgets an agreement and learns that the omission created a real cost for somebody else. | Impact is established, responsibility is proportionate, and protective change or repair is attempted where possible. | Uncertainty or guilt may persist when impact cannot be clarified or repair is refused. Repair can remain open even if internal pressure falls, and defensiveness is possible but not inevitable. | Felt guilt is not proof of harm; internal completion does not complete repair for another person. |
| **Loneliness** | Missing meaningful connection, witness or belonging. | Someone repeatedly reaches out but does not feel known, received or included. | Connection becomes available and receivable, or expectation and participation reorganise around what is currently unavailable. | Loneliness may persist or recruit sadness, anxiety, renewed seeking, withdrawal or protective distance. Other relationships may support Return without supplying the missing person's participation. | No particular person is thereby obligated to provide connection. |
| **Disappointment** | An expected bond, outcome, support or possibility that did not arrive. | A promised visit, response or opportunity does not happen. | The mismatch is registered and the expectation, goal or relationship read revises. | Repeated expectation may continue to meet the same mismatch. Sadness, frustration, resentment or a revised route may enter later cycles. | Revision can preserve the goal, change the route or recognise that the condition is unavailable. |
| **Sadness** | Absence, hurt, unmet need or loss. | A friend dismisses an expressed hurt, or an important possibility ends. | The condition is acknowledged and enough time or support allows response and expectation to reorganise. | Sadness may remain or return and may recruit loneliness, grief or withdrawal. Support elsewhere can help without completing missing recognition or restoring the lost condition. | Care can help without recreating what is absent. |
| **Grief** | Irreversible loss. | A person, relationship, role or expected future is no longer recoverable in its previous form. | Irreversibility becomes increasingly registerable and life reorganises around what cannot be restored. | Grief may recur in waves while reorganisation remains incomplete or support is limited. Continuing pain, love or renewed grief does not by itself show failed updating. | Grief has no single completion point; ongoing love or pain does not mean failure to update. |
| **Confusion** | Conflict among available information, meaning or expectation. | Two credible accounts conflict, or another person's words and behaviour do not fit together. | Enough reliable information or orientation distinguishes what is known, unknown and still ambiguous. | Scanning, pausing, anxiety or suspicion may continue while ambiguity remains. The person may keep uncertainty open rather than adopt a false explanation. | Completion does not require false certainty. |
| **Frustration** | Blocked progress towards a valued outcome. | A tool repeatedly fails, a process stalls or another person's action blocks the next step. | The obstacle changes, another route becomes usable, the goal revises or impossibility becomes clear. | Renewed effort, anger, exhaustion, withdrawal or goal revision may follow. Repeated unresolved obstruction can carry additional stress into later attempts. | Mobilisation or discharge alone does not move the obstacle. |
| **Anxiety** | Possible rupture, exposure, loss or instability before it is clear. | An important message goes unanswered and several outcomes remain possible. | Evidence, protection or increased capacity makes uncertainty more discriminable and allows prediction to update. | Scanning, reassurance-seeking, avoidance or repeated prediction may continue. Anxiety may persist or shift towards fear, sadness or protective distance as conditions become clearer. | Reassurance can reduce pressure without resolving uncertainty; continuing concern may remain accurate. |
| **Stress** | Demand pressing against available capacity. | Work, caregiving or material demand exceeds the time, support or energy currently available. | Demand reduces, capacity or support increases, priorities reorganise or the continuing mismatch becomes accurately known. | Activation and recovery debt may accumulate while attention, patience and action range narrow. Quietness or temporary relief can occur without the demand–capacity mismatch changing. | Low arousal does not establish that demand and capacity are workable. |

### Threat and protection

| Signal | What it concerns | Example in context | What may allow updating or completion | If the completing condition does not occur | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Fear** | Possible present danger. | A car swerves into one's path, or another person's behaviour creates an immediate possibility of harm. | Danger is sufficiently located; threat ends, protection succeeds, credible safety evidence arrives or uncertainty remains accurately represented. | Protective attention, escape, resistance or freezing may remain recruited. If danger intensifies or no route is available, organisation may move towards life-threat mobilisation or conservation. | Feeling safer is not proof of safety, and continuing fear is not proof that danger remains. |
| **Anger** | A crossing, obstruction, violation or loss of agency. | A stated boundary is ignored and the unwanted action continues. | The crossing stops, a boundary becomes effective, agency or route is restored, or impossibility is recognised and participation changes. | Anger may persist or recruit firmer boundaries, distance, withdrawal or protection. Frustration or fear may also become clearer; calming alone does not restore agency. | Anger does not prescribe confrontation, discharge or harm. |
| **Disgust** | Possible contamination, degradation, violation or unsafe proximity. | Food appears spoiled, or an unwanted act or proximity feels contaminating or degrading. | Separation becomes possible or credible evidence revises the contamination or violation read. | Recoil, nausea, avoidance, vigilance or anger may remain while separation is unavailable. What happened, and whether coercion or harm occurred, still requires separate evidence. | Disgust is not proof of another person's moral character. |
| **Suspicion** | Possible mismatch between appearance and underlying conditions. | Explanations repeatedly change while observable behaviour does not fit what is being said. | Evidence becomes sufficient to confirm, revise or maintain concern proportionately. | Scanning, provisional trust, checking or protective boundaries may continue. Concern may persist or recruit anxiety, fear or anger without becoming proof by repetition. | Suspicion is not evidence by itself; certainty is not required where risk remains unresolved. |

### Life threat and maximum mobilisation

| Signal | What it concerns | Example in context | What may allow updating or completion | If the completing condition does not occur | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Rage** | Life threat where ordinary protection appears insufficient. | A person is cornered during a violent threat and ordinary defence or escape has failed. | Danger stops, escape or effective protection becomes available, and maximum force is no longer required. | Maximum mobilisation may persist until the route changes, then leave substantial depletion, pain, vigilance or recovery demand. Disorganisation or shutdown may follow when capacity is exceeded. | Recovery may be substantial; internal force does not establish behaviour or justification. |
| **Fury** | A continuing perceived source of life threat. | A source of danger remains present and repeatedly defeats ordinary protective action. | The danger ends or another viable route makes sustained maximum force unnecessary. | Attention and force may remain fixed on the perceived source while capacity depletes and other information narrows. Escape, assistance or changed conditions may still be required. | Narrowed certainty does not establish the source or proportionality of threat. |
| **Frenzy** | Survival demand without a viable organised route. | A person faces immediate danger but cannot locate an exit or organise an effective response. | A viable route, assistance or changed conditions allow coordination to re-enter and danger to end. | Uncoordinated movement may continue until a route appears or capacity fails. Exhaustion, collapse or conservation can reduce movement without completing protection. | Reduced movement can reflect depletion rather than completion or Return. |

Across the four families, the non-completion column records a possible next condition, not a prediction. A signal may persist, change intensity, recruit another signal, support a different action, become less consciously available or coexist with partial Return. ESC owns what actually happens through the event.

## Public implementation rule

The public Signal Map keeps the larger cycle outside each row. The **What the signal needs** column names a possible completing condition. The next column stops at the possible persistence, transition or carryover when that condition remains unavailable:

- completion is assessed through the event;
- updating may be partial or absent;
- Return is a whole-organism Gradient change;
- recovery may continue after Return begins;
- carryover records what enters later conditions;
- behaviour and repair remain separate.

The public page adds an example and a bounded non-completion column to each Situation Signal. The cells stop at the next possible event condition; ESC still owns the full temporal cycle.
