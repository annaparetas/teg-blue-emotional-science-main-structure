Accessible orientation: **What happens before a feeling becomes consciously available, what ME can do with it and what may still change without conscious understanding**

## Governing observation

The organism can register, weight and respond to information before that information becomes consciously felt, recognised or understood.

> **ESS and CLS name two forms of biological information processing. Coordinated conscious access names the Model 1 relationship through which some participating information may become available to ME. ME names the person who may participate with what becomes available.**

ME does not switch the underlying processing on. ME participation becomes possible when some of the organism's information and capacities become sufficiently available to notice, differentiate, examine or use.

## The wording rule

Avoid:

> ME participation is not required.

Prefer:

> **This process can occur without becoming consciously available to ME.**

Where deliberate participation is the question, use:

> **This process does not depend on conscious recognition or deliberate participation by ME.**

ME is the lived person, not a biological permission system. The question is whether information becomes available to the person and what participation becomes possible from there.

## Five questions to carry through every layer

Every individual-signal record should make these questions visible:

1. **ESS contribution:** What emotional-somatic significance, weighting, felt information or action-readiness may be involved?
2. **CLS contribution:** What reflective-symbolic representation, comparison, prediction, language or explanation may be involved?
3. **Processing outside conscious access:** What can continue without becoming consciously felt, recognised or understood?
4. **Conscious availability to ME:** At what point, and in what form, may information become consciously readable?
5. **Possible ME participation:** What may the person be able to notice, differentiate, question, interpret, express, inhibit, choose or learn from what becomes available?

These are functional questions, not claims that ESS and CLS occupy separate anatomical pathways or take turns in a fixed sequence.

## The four participation territories

| Territory | Layers | What the filter is asking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Organism-level processing** | **1–4** | What condition, significance, bodily participation and information weighting can occur without conscious access? |
| **Conscious-access threshold** | **5** | Does participating bodily and emotional information become consciously experienceable? |
| **ME recognition and use** | **6–8** | Can ME differentiate, name, interpret and deliberately use what becomes available? What automatic readiness or expression continues alongside that participation? |
| **Coupled response and feedback** | **9** | What do action, changing conditions and other people's responses alter through time, with or without conscious understanding? |

Layer 5 is the first explicit conscious-access question in this filter. It is not a claim that awareness appears at one universal biological moment. Processes overlap, recur and change at different rates. Information may reach ME in fragments, become felt without being named, or become cognitively represented while bodily and affective access remain limited.

## Model 1 contribution and ME availability across the nine layers

| Layer | ESS contribution | CLS contribution | Conscious availability to ME | Possible ME participation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1 · Relevant condition** | Bodily or situational information may enter emotional-somatic processing. The condition is not itself created by conscious recognition. | Memory, imagination, expectation or symbolic representation may form part of a represented condition. | The condition may remain unavailable, faint or indirectly represented. | ME may or may not know that the condition is present, represented or relevant. |
| **2 · Significance registration** | Emotional-somatic information is weighted relative to bodily requirements, learning and present or represented conditions. | Concepts, predictions and existing explanations may contribute to how the condition is represented without determining the emotion by themselves. | Significance can register without becoming consciously foregrounded. | ME participation is not established merely because significance has registered. |
| **3 · Physiological participation** | Emotional, bodily, motivational and action-readiness processes may participate within a wider organism-level reorganisation. ESS does not own the whole physiology. | Remembered, anticipated or interpreted conditions may contribute to continuing recruitment through their interaction with emotional-somatic processing. | Physiological change can occur without becoming consciously felt or understood. | ME may later notice some effects but does not initiate the underlying recruitment by recognising it. |
| **4 · Information representation and salience** | Bodily, emotional and motivational information may receive weight and direction across attention, memory and prediction. | Reflective-symbolic processing can represent, compare, predict and begin constructing explanations. | Information can influence attention and action while remaining faint, fragmented or unavailable to conscious inspection. | ME may orient or act before being able to inspect why the information has become salient. |
| **5 · Felt access** | Some participating bodily and emotional information may become available as felt experience. | Words, categories or provisional explanations may become available alongside the feeling, agree with it or pull attention away from it. | This is the first explicit conscious-access question: some information may become experienceable without yet being identified accurately. | ME may turn towards, notice or begin examining what has become available. Access enables participation but does not guarantee it. |
| **6 · Experiential differentiation and naming** | Felt qualities, needs and competing emotional information may become more distinguishable. | Comparison, categorisation and language can help separate and provisionally name the experience. | The experience may become more differentiated or linguistically available, although access can remain partial or uneven. | ME may recognise what is happening and revise the name when other information becomes available. |
| **7 · Contextual interpretation and meaning** | Felt significance, value, need and possible direction continue to contribute. | Context, memory, evidence, alternatives and representations of other minds can be compared and reconsidered. | Bodily, affective and reflective information may become jointly available without guaranteeing an accurate interpretation. | ME may hold information from both systems together, test an interpretation and distinguish feeling from factual verdict. |
| **8 · Use, expression, inhibition and action** | Motivational pressure, action-readiness and automatic expression may continue whether or not they become clearly understood. | Planning, inhibition, symbolic communication and prediction of consequences may widen or narrow the available response. | Relevant information and response capacities may be available fully, partly or not at all. | ME may deliberately use, express, restrain or act on what is available. Behaviour can also occur with limited conscious access, and emotion never prescribes or authorises it. |
| **9 · Response, feedback, completion and later course** | Outcomes may alter emotional-somatic weighting, bodily response and later recruitment. | Outcomes may revise predictions, explanations and expected consequences. | Feedback may become consciously available immediately, later, partially or not at all. | ME may consciously notice, learn, reconsider or attempt repair. Biological and cognitive updating can also occur without conscious understanding; Model 3 (ESC) follows these changes through time. |

The contributions are approximate and overlapping. A layer does not belong exclusively to ESS, CLS or ME. Initial interpretation can arise before conscious inspection; reflective interpretation becomes possible only when enough information reaches ME. Automatic expression can occur without deliberate participation; deliberate restraint or communication can occur when the relevant capacity remains available.

## The access progression

```text
information is processed
        ↓
information influences organisation without awareness
        ↓
some information may become felt by ME
        ↓
ME may differentiate and name it
        ↓
ME may interpret and test it
        ↓
ME may use it deliberately
        ↓
ME may recognise and learn from the outcome
```

This is an orientation, not a mandatory order. Later action and feedback can occur when earlier information remains inaccessible. Information can become available retrospectively. A person may explain an event without felt access, feel it without naming it, name it without interpreting it accurately, or understand it without choosing to act on that understanding.

## Layer 8 requires two questions

1. **What readiness, expression or action may occur without conscious recognition or deliberate ME participation?**
2. **What can ME deliberately express, inhibit, choose or use when the relevant information and capacity become available?**

Action-readiness is not behaviour. Deliberate participation is not guaranteed by access. Neither an emotion nor a capacity configuration establishes acceptable conduct.

## Applying the filter across Fluid and Chronic organisation

Hold the individual emotion's identity constant. Then examine two parallel lanes:

| Organism-level processing | Conscious access and ME participation |
| --- | --- |
| What condition is registered? | What becomes consciously readable? |
| What emotional and bodily processing continues? | What can ME feel and differentiate? |
| What receives salience or protective weighting? | What meaning can ME construct and revise? |
| What action-readiness is recruited? | What can ME test, express, inhibit, choose or learn? |

Fluid organisation does not require perfect awareness. Chronic organisation does not mean that the underlying signal or biological process has stopped. The comparison asks how access, differentiation, interpretation, action availability, feedback use, persistence and recovery change around the same signal.

The three Model 1 capacities—bodily-information access and use, affective-sharing access, and mentalizing access and use—describe how unevenly relevant processes may become available to ME. Their settings do not define an emotion, Gradient Position, Fluid or Chronic organisation, personality, diagnosis or behaviour.

## Ownership and evidence boundary

This filter is an ESM-S1 interface for asking consistent questions of each signal. It does not take mechanism ownership from the models it joins.

- **Emotional Signal Map** owns the signal identity, functional-family interface and individual-signal application.
- **Model 1** owns ESS, CLS, coordinated conscious access, conscious availability to ME and possible ME participation.
- **Model 2** owns present organism organisation, resource allocation, action-readiness, transition and Return.
- **Model 3 (ESC)** owns response becoming feedback through time, completion, carryover, updating, learning and later entry.
- **Fluid–Chronic foundation/profile** owns present-responsive flexibility versus recurrent narrowing, generalisation, persistence and recovery profile.
- **Behaviour/HPR** owns conduct, consent, power, impact, accountability and repair.
- **Later bounded evidence work** owns clause-level evidence status, supporting and limiting literature, alternatives and gaps.

The filter is a TEG-Blue integration. It does not establish one validated biological sequence, one mechanism for every emotion or an anatomical division between ESS and CLS. Owner and evidence review must determine which clauses can be strengthened, limited or retained as architectural synthesis.

## Consistent-use rule

For every emotion or signal:

- keep the relevant condition and signal identity distinct from conscious access;
- describe what can continue without becoming consciously available to ME;
- mark Layer 5 as the first explicit conscious-access question;
- distinguish encounter, recognition, interpretation, deliberate use and conscious learning;
- keep automatic action-readiness separate from behaviour and deliberate choice;
- keep feedback and updating possible with or without conscious understanding;
- never infer conduct from emotion, organisation or access configuration.

The body does not wait for conscious recognition before it begins responding. Conscious participation changes what the person may be able to understand and do with the information that reaches them.
