# Emotion families and the two F1 problems

Status: proposed wording and internal consistency review · 4 September 2026.

## What F1 distinguishes

**Organism-protection:** maintaining or restoring the conditions that allow an
organism to remain viable and able to act: bodily integrity, energy, mobility,
resources and protection from harm. Another person's practical help can be
essential in a particular event; mutual recognition or reciprocity is not
inherent in every protective problem.

**Connection-preservation:** maintaining or restoring access to a consequential
relationship, including care, belonging, cooperation, information and shared
action. Where the missing condition is another person's availability,
recognition, reciprocity or willingness to repair, one person cannot create
mutual restoration alone.

Connection being available is not the same condition as connection needing to
be preserved because it appears at risk. Valuing, enjoying, exploring and
participating should not all be described as defending a connection.

The two problems overlap. Connection can protect the organism; protection can
require another person's help; preserving a particular relationship can also
conflict with protection. Neither problem is a moral classification. F1 also
explicitly says that play, exploration, reproduction, opportunity and collective
coordination do not fit completely inside either problem.

A relationship may remain unrepaired while the organism updates to that reality.
Relational restoration, organism-level updating and Return are separate outcomes.

## Proposed family descriptions

### Belonging — value, care and possible participation

**Proposed description:** These emotions register value in a person,
relationship, experience, achievement or possibility, and orient participation
toward caring, relying, appreciating, enjoying, learning or investing.

**Why these members belong together:** their present Signal Map descriptions
centre on what matters and how engagement with that value may remain relevant.
Love concerns a valued bond; trust concerns reliance; gratitude concerns received
benefit; compassion concerns another's welfare; admiration concerns valued
qualities. Happiness and joy concern favourable experience or participation;
pride concerns valued effort, contribution or identity; hope concerns a valued
possibility.

**What the description must not imply:** the condition is already favourable,
secure, reciprocal or fully available. Compassion can concern suffering and
hope can concern an outcome that has not occurred. Love does not establish
reciprocity. “Something valued is sufficiently available and participation is
viable” is therefore too strong as a universal summary of the current members.

“Belonging” is broader here than social inclusion. Keep the established name,
but explain its functional scope alongside it. The two existing internal
working clusters—relational value and favourable participation—help explain
the range without creating new families.

### Belonging-at-Risk — disruption of something that matters

**Proposed description:** These emotions register uncertainty, absence, loss,
obstruction, mismatch or excessive demand affecting a valued condition, and
make the need for clarification, support, adjustment, repair or reorientation
relevant.

**Why these members belong together:** the current descriptions concern a
problem in access, continuity, fit or capacity. Shame and guilt concern social
standing or possible harm; loneliness concerns missing connection;
disappointment concerns an unmet expectation; sadness and grief concern absence
or loss; confusion concerns conflicting information; frustration concerns an
obstacle; anxiety concerns possible disruption; stress concerns demand pressing
against capacity.

**What the description must not imply:** every member is about social rejection,
every event is merely uncertain, or restoration is always possible. Grief can
concern an irreversible loss. Confusion, frustration and stress can concern
nonrelational conditions. The family includes disrupted access more broadly
than the ordinary meaning of “belonging at risk”.

### Threat-Protection — possible harm and protective relevance

**Proposed description:** These emotions register a possible source of harm,
violation or contamination, or uncertainty about a source's trustworthiness,
and make protective attention, distance, checking or resistance relevant.

**Why these members belong together:** fear concerns possible harm; anger
concerns a crossed or obstructed boundary, value, need or route; disgust concerns
contamination or unacceptable proximity in the current account; suspicion
concerns a possible mismatch between appearances and underlying reality.
Their shared centre is the protective significance of a condition or source.

**What the description must not imply:** danger has been established, the source
is accurately identified, or any particular protective action is warranted.
Protection can concern oneself, another person or a valued relationship. It is
not restricted to an isolated organism or to one Gradient Position.

The boundary with Belonging-at-Risk needs examples, not an absolute rule:
anxiety and suspicion both involve uncertainty; frustration and anger can both
involve obstruction. The proposed distinction is uncertainty or disruption of
a valued condition versus a possible source becoming relevant to protection.
It remains a functional reading, not a diagnostic decision rule.

### Survival-Rage — ordinary protection registered as insufficient

**Proposed description:** These experiences are grouped around a registered
failure, blockage or insufficiency of ordinary protective options, with urgent,
forceful or poorly directed mobilisation becoming prominent.

**Why these members belong together in the current model:** rage is described
as mobilisation when ordinary defence is insufficient; fury as sustained force
toward a perceived source; frenzy as movement where no viable coordinated route
is available. Their proposed shared centre is the relationship between perceived
demand and available protection, not simply “more anger”.

**What the description must not imply:** objective life threat, actual maximum
physiological output, a necessary action, or a scientific distinction already
established among rage, fury and frenzy.

This family needs the most conceptual review. Unlike the other families, its
definition relies strongly on mobilisation and protective capacity—the present
organisation questions owned by Model 2. A later review must establish what
makes this a distinct signal family rather than only an intensified or altered
protective organisation. Wording alone cannot settle that question. Membership
remains unchanged during this review.

## What should change if this wording is adopted

1. Keep the four names and all existing members. Replace the short family
   descriptions with the proposed centres and an explanation of the shared
   relationship, including the exceptions above.
2. Replace the Fluid table's categorical survival-function cells with
   event-dependent participation. Its current shorthand can imply that
   Belonging means connection-preservation while Threat-Protection means
   organism-protection, despite F1's explicit overlap.
3. Keep three questions separate in every record: what the emotion registers;
   which broad problem matters in this event; how the organism is organised.
4. Carry the family rationale into the generated map records. Regenerate both
   `data/signals.js` and `signal-records.md` after the source descriptions change.
5. Keep the Survival-Rage / Model 2 boundary as an explicit review question.
   Do not claim that the four families are established scientific natural kinds.

This proposal reconciles the current member descriptions and F1 boundaries.
It does not establish one evolved purpose, one chemistry or one regulation
mechanism for each emotion.
