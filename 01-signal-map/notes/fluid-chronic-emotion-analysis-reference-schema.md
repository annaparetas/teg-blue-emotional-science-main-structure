# Fluid–Chronic Emotion Analysis Reference Schema

Accessible orientation: **A reusable way to follow one emotion without confusing the emotion with access, organisation, behaviour or outcome**

Scientific-facing orientation: **A comparative record structure for emotion identity, condition registration, information-system contribution, conscious availability, organism organisation, participation, response and later course across Fluid and Chronic profiles**

Status: Anna-approved canonical development source · cross-owner and bounded evidence review pending · not public authority · 27 August 2026

Scope: Emotional Signal Map · Body and Situation Signals · individual-emotion analysis · Fluid–Chronic comparison · Nine-Layer interface · owner and claim-status routing

Purpose: provide the same analytic fields for each emotion while preventing the content, physiology, response routes or evidence assumptions of one worked example from being transferred to another

Companions: [Nine-Layer Emotional Signal Participation Filter](nine-layer-emotional-signal-participation-filter.md) · [Love in Fluid Organisation](fluid-love-nine-layer-signal-structure.md) · [Love in Chronic Organisation](chronic-love-nine-layer-signal-structure.md) · [Fluid–Chronic Love Comparison](fluid-chronic-love-nine-layer-comparison-and-owner-handoff.md) · [Love Reference-Case Completion and Public Boundary](love-reference-case-completion-and-public-boundary.md)

## What this schema is

This schema is a set of questions and record fields. It makes the same distinctions visible whenever the Emotional Signal Map examines one emotion across Fluid and Chronic organisation.

It is not a validated biological sequence. The nine layers do not claim that processes occur once, in order or at the same speed. They may overlap, recur, proceed outside conscious access or become available retrospectively.

> **The schema is reusable. The answer entered into each field belongs to the emotion being examined and must be established separately.**

Love is the first complete worked example. Its registered condition, relational meanings, possible response routes and claim placeholders are not defaults for Fear, Anger, Shame or any other emotion.

## Governing invariants

Every completed record must preserve these statements:

- emotion identity is not created or changed by a Gradient Position;
- a Fluid or Chronic profile does not redefine an emotion;
- one emotion does not transform into the next;
- several emotions, bodily signals and represented conditions may coexist;
- processing can occur without becoming consciously available to ME;
- conscious availability may enable participation but does not guarantee recognition, accurate interpretation, deliberate use or action;
- a feeling is informative but not a complete factual verdict;
- emotion and action-readiness do not prescribe behaviour;
- pressure reduction, condition-specific completion, Return availability, physiological recovery and repair are different outcomes;
- conduct, consent, power, impact, accountability and repair require a separate record;
- mixed, context-dependent and unknown relationships remain possible.

## Record A — identity and classification

Complete this record before constructing either Fluid or Chronic lanes.

| Field | Question | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A01 · Signal group** | Is this a Body Signal or Situation Signal? | Signal group is not functional family. |
| **A02 · Established functional family** | Which protected family already contains this emotion? | The schema does not reopen membership. |
| **A03 · Working cluster, if used** | What narrower relationship is being examined inside the established family? | A working cluster is not a new family or a statistically validated natural kind. |
| **A04 · Individual emotion** | Which emotion is the record following? | The name does not by itself define function. |
| **A05 · Invariant signal identity** | What information does this emotion contribute across both lanes? | Must remain unchanged in Fluid and Chronic records. |
| **A06 · Registered condition or relational territory** | What bodily condition, situation or relationship makes the signal relevant? | Does not establish that ME recognises it or that the external interpretation is correct. |
| **A07 · Proposed function** | What does registering this information make possible for the organism or ME? | Function is not a behavioural instruction. |
| **A08 · Possible direction or action tendency** | What orientation may become more available? | Tendency is not action, permission or outcome. |
| **A09 · Explicit non-inferences** | What does the emotion not establish? | Include safety, factual certainty, reciprocity, entitlement, diagnosis and conduct where relevant. |
| **A10 · Claim status** | Which parts are ESM architecture and which require evidence or another owner? | Do not assign established-evidence status without review. |

An emotion record cannot proceed by copying the identity or registered condition from an existing example. Those two fields are the main protection against making the Nine-Layer lens a generic story imposed on every emotion.

## Record B — fields carried through every layer

Each of the nine layers must be readable against the following fields, even where the entry is “not yet an explicit question at this layer”. The fields may sit in one table or across a linked emotion record and shared owner overlay, but none should disappear from the analysis.

| Field | What it records | Owner boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **B01 · Emotion-specific formulation** | What this layer means for the individual emotion. | ESM owns the signal interface; mechanism claims remain routed. |
| **B02 · ESS contribution** | Emotional–somatic significance, weighting, felt information or readiness that may participate. | Model 1 reviews ESS wording and mechanism. |
| **B03 · CLS contribution** | Representation, comparison, prediction, language or explanation that may participate. | Model 1 reviews CLS wording and mechanism. |
| **B04 · Processing outside conscious access** | What may continue without being felt, recognised or understood. | Lack of report does not establish lack of processing. |
| **B05 · Conscious availability to ME** | What may become experienceable or inspectable. | This is not complete awareness or deliberate participation. |
| **B06 · Possible ME participation** | What ME may be able to notice, differentiate, question, interpret, express, inhibit, choose or learn. | Access enables possibilities; it guarantees none. |
| **B07 · Present organisation and action-readiness** | Which present priorities, capacities or tendencies may be more or less available. | Model 2 owns organism organisation; readiness is not behaviour. |
| **B08 · Response and time interface** | What response, outcome or later change becomes relevant. | Model 3 (ESC) owns temporal relations. |
| **B09 · Boundary preserved** | Which tempting inference must be blocked at this layer. | Include conduct and factual-verdict boundaries where relevant. |
| **B10 · Owner and claim marker** | Who must review the clause and what evidence triage remains. | An architectural placeholder is not an evidence conclusion. |

## Record C — the nine analytic layers

| Layer | Reusable question | Required output | What the layer does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1 · Relevant condition** | What present or represented condition makes this emotion relevant? | Emotion-specific condition statement and representation boundary. | Conscious recognition, external accuracy or one Gradient Position. |
| **2 · Significance registration** | What aspect of that condition matters to the organism? | Emotion-specific significance and the signals that may be weighted alongside it. | Felt intensity, urgency or behaviour. |
| **3 · Physiological participation** | What distributed bodily processes may participate when this signal is relevant? | Proportionate, non-unique physiological wording and explicit evidence placeholder. | A unique hormone, circuit, autonomic state or chemical identity. |
| **4 · Information representation and salience** | What may receive priority across attention, memory and prediction? | Salience formulation, present-information sensitivity and reality boundary. | Factual truth, another person's mind or inevitable action. |
| **5 · Felt access** | In what forms might participating information become consciously experienceable? | Variable felt descriptions, absent-report boundary and first explicit access question. | Emotion identity, physiological proof or action prescription. |
| **6 · Experiential differentiation and naming** | What must be distinguished from this emotion when several experiences coexist? | Provisional naming contrasts and revision conditions. | Transformation between emotions or certainty from a label. |
| **7 · Contextual interpretation and meaning** | What can this emotion validly contribute, and what conclusions exceed it? | Evidence, alternative and context checks. | A complete verdict about safety, reciprocity, blame, intention or viability. |
| **8 · Use, expression, inhibition and action** | What readiness may occur automatically, and what may ME deliberately do when information and capacity are available? | Separate automatic readiness, possible deliberate participation and response-route functions. | Behavioural prescription, entitlement or ethical acceptability. |
| **9 · Response, feedback, completion and later course** | What changed after response, and what remains open? | Separate outcome ledger, feedback route, carryover and later-entry questions. | One mandatory sequence, next emotion or global completion. |

Layer 5 is the first explicit conscious-access question in this analytic lens. It is not a claim that consciousness begins at one universal biological stage. Layers 1–4 may influence present organisation and behaviour while their information remains faint, fragmented or unavailable to ME.

## Record D — Fluid lane

The Fluid lane asks how the same emotion may operate where present conditions and feedback can continue to influence weighting, access, differentiation, interpretation and response.

For every layer, record:

- what remains available outside conscious access;
- what may become consciously available to ME;
- what ME may be able to do with it;
- what present information can revise;
- which alternatives remain distinguishable;
- what event-specific completion would mean;
- what can remain unresolved after that completion.

Fluid does not mean complete awareness, perfect accuracy, permanent calm, prosocial behaviour or absence of protection. A Fluid record must retain partial access, mixed emotion and context dependence.

## Record E — Chronic lane

The Chronic lane holds Record A constant and asks what may become more persistent, readily re-recruited, generalised, difficult to differentiate or less responsive to present updating.

For every layer, record:

- what remains active or influential;
- what may be unavailable, delayed, mixed or indirectly felt;
- what joined bodily or emotional information may become foregrounded;
- what present evidence may receive less weight;
- what interpretation or familiar route may become easier to recruit;
- what relief may occur without answering the original condition;
- what carryover may shape later entry.

Chronic does not mean a different emotion, fixed person type, diagnosis, moral category, severity scale or predetermined harmful behaviour.

## Record F — response-route possibilities

Response routes describe possible functions, not people or outcomes.

| Route field | Reusable definition | Required boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **F01 · Condition-responsive route** | The response uses the emotion alongside present context, alternatives and relevant limits. | Does not guarantee the desired outcome. |
| **F02 · Regulation-support route** | A person, activity or resource changes pressure or capacity so more information and alternatives may become usable. | Regulation support is not condition-specific completion. |
| **F03 · Substitute response route** | A familiar response reduces, redirects or overrides pressure while the original condition or participating information remains unanswered or unavailable. | A substitute is a route, not an outcome; examples require emotion-specific review. |
| **F04 · Conduct record link** | Observable action is assessed for consent, power, impact, alternatives, recurrence and response to feedback. | Internal function never settles ethical status. |

Do not populate F03 by copying Love's reassurance, contact or bond-preservation examples into another emotion. The route definition is reusable; the possible response must be derived from the new emotion, its co-signals and context.

## Record G — outcome ledger

Layer 9 must answer each question separately.

| Outcome | Question | Does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **G01 · Pressure reduction** | Did immediate internal pressure rise, fall or change form? | Completion, safety, accuracy, reciprocity, recovery or repair. |
| **G02 · Condition-specific completion** | Did the condition that recruited this signal change enough to support updating? | That the emotion ended or all other conditions resolved. |
| **G03 · Return availability** | Is transition toward a less costly present organisation available now? | That Return has occurred or that recovery is complete. |
| **G04 · Physiological recovery** | What bodily restoration remains in progress? | Relational repair or conscious understanding. |
| **G05 · Repair** | Was harm acknowledged, stopped and addressed through adequate conduct? | Model 2 organisation or internal relief. |
| **G06 · Updating and learning** | What biological or consciously understood information may alter later expectation and response? | Accurate learning or one inevitable future pathway. |
| **G07 · Carryover and later entry** | What unresolved condition, altered expectation, bodily demand or response history enters the next event? | A fixed loop or permanent baseline. |
| **G08 · Emotion's later course** | Does the original emotion remain relevant, become less salient or coexist with new signals? | Transformation into the next emotion. |

Repair is deliberately present as a separate question rather than an emotion outcome. Behaviour/HPR owns whether repair was attempted, adequate, accepted or completed.

## Record H — architecture, owner and claim status

Every substantive clause receives two independent labels.

### Architecture label

- **ESM-owned:** emotion identity, family interface, registered condition, proposed function and the distinctions among signal, felt access, meaning and possible direction.
- **Model 1 proposed:** ESS and CLS contribution, coordinated conscious access, conscious availability to ME and possible ME participation.
- **Model 2 proposed:** present organisation, resource allocation, salience, readiness, controllability, transition and Return availability.
- **Model 3 (ESC) proposed:** response becoming feedback through time, completion, carryover, updating, learning and later entry.
- **Fluid–Chronic proposed:** persistence, recurrence, generalisation, context sensitivity, responsiveness to updating and recovery profile.
- **Behaviour/HPR separate record:** conduct, consent, power, impact, accountability, boundaries and repair.
- **Developmental dependency:** any proposed origin in learning, caregiving or developmental history belongs to the relevant framework owner.

### Claim-status label

- **Architectural statement:** a definition, distinction, field or ownership rule established inside TEG-Blue.
- **Empirical claim placeholder:** a clause that requires direct supporting, limiting and competing evidence.
- **Grounded-synthesis candidate:** a possible cross-literature conclusion awaiting formal review.
- **TEG-Blue integration:** the final organisation of responsibly grounded parts under TEG-Blue language.
- **Unknown or unresolved:** a relationship that should remain open.

An architectural statement can be settled as architecture without being presented as an empirically validated mechanism. An empirical claim does not become established merely because it fits the architecture.

## Record I — bounded evidence triage

The evidence handoff for each emotion should be no larger than necessary to assess its actual claims.

For every placeholder, record:

1. the exact clause;
2. the level of analysis;
3. the construct or process involved;
4. the required supporting literature;
5. limiting and competing accounts;
6. population, context and timescale limits;
7. whether the clause should be retained, narrowed, relabelled as synthesis or removed;
8. the final evidence level.

The schema itself needs evidence review only where it makes claims about real processes. Its use as a record structure is an architectural decision.

## Completion checklist for one emotion

A reference case is structurally complete when:

- Record A contains one identity statement used unchanged in both lanes;
- the linked record package exposes fields B01–B10 across all nine layers;
- Fluid and Chronic lanes answer the same questions;
- the comparison shows what changes and what stays invariant;
- response routes remain possibilities rather than predictions;
- the outcome ledger separates all eight outcomes;
- every non-ESM clause has an owner;
- every empirical claim has a bounded placeholder;
- public-candidate and development-only material are identified;
- family membership is unchanged;
- no clause infers conduct from emotion, access or organisation.

Structural completion does not mean owner approval, evidence validation or public readiness.
