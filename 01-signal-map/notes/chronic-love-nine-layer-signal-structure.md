## Governing invariant

> **Love registers that a particular person carries deep emotional value and that connection with them is significant.**

Love does not become distorted into fear, shame, grief, anger, dependence, possession or control. Those signals, meanings or response routes may coexist with Love, become more consciously available than Love, or alter what ME can understand and do with it.

> **Chronic organisation may alter which Love-related and protective information is weighted, felt, differentiated, interpreted, used and carried forward. It does not change Love's identity.**

No layer establishes reciprocity, safety, compatibility, consent, relational viability or knowledge of another person's mind. No emotion authorises behaviour.

## How to read the Chronic lane

Chronic does not mean permanently unconscious, biologically fixed, severe, disordered or morally harmful. It names a profile in which recruitment may be more persistent or readily repeated, present information may have less influence, competing signals may be harder to separate, and Return or recovery may be less readily available.

The nine layers remain questions, not stages. Several signals can coexist. One emotion does not transform into the next. A response can occur while the relevant feeling remains unavailable, and later feedback can be processed without becoming consciously understood.

The Model 1-facing fields are deliberately separate:

- **Conscious availability to ME** asks what information becomes experienceable or inspectable.
- **Possible ME participation** asks what the person may be able to differentiate, question, interpret, express, inhibit, choose or learn from that information.

Access may enable participation. It does not guarantee accurate interpretation, deliberate use or action.

## Layer 8 response routes

The same visible activity can serve different functions. Function cannot be inferred from appearance alone.

### 1. Condition-responsive route

Love is used alongside information about reciprocity, consent, safety, compatibility, boundaries and circumstances. ME may express Love, seek clarification, accept a response, choose distance or take no immediate action. This route does not guarantee the hoped-for outcome.

### 2. Regulation-support route

Another person, activity or resource helps reduce pressure or widen capacity so that more information and alternatives can become usable. Co-regulation, rest, movement, conversation and structured support may serve this role. Pressure reduction can support participation; it does not answer whether Love is reciprocal or whether the relationship is viable.

### 3. Substitute response route

A familiar route reduces, redirects or overrides pressure while the original condition, emotion or participating signals remain unrecognised or unresolved. Possible routes include repeated reassurance-seeking, monitoring or testing, compulsive contact, imagined interaction, self-silencing, caretaking, rumination, substances or repetitive activities.

A substitute is a route, not an outcome. It may bring real short-term relief. That relief does not establish condition-specific completion, Return, recovery or repair.

Another person's involvement in regulation is ordinary. The boundary becomes ethically important when their attention, affection, access, compliance or emotional state must be produced for the route to work. Pressure, punishment, humiliation, coercion and domination belong to the separate Behaviour/HPR record. They are not neutral regulation strategies and are never authorised by Love or distress.

## Layer 9: when relief can feed the next cycle

One possible route is:

    Love and joined protective information are difficult to use
            ↓
    pressure becomes foregrounded
            ↓
    a familiar substitute response route is recruited
            ↓
    relief, distraction, numbness, certainty or control occurs
            ↓
    the original condition remains unanswered
            ↓
    consequences create additional conditions and signals
            ↓
    later processing begins with more carryover or a narrower familiar route

This is conditional, not inevitable. A route can change when present information, capacity, support, consequences or learning alter what becomes available.

For example, another person's reassurance may reduce pressure without resolving whether Love is reciprocal or the relationship is viable. The immediate learning may become “their response stops this pressure”. If reassurance is later withheld, the unresolved relational condition and the learned route can both be recruited again. New conditions may also arise: uncertainty or loss, shame or guilt if impact becomes recognised, fear of consequences, anger at a boundary, grief, mistrust or rupture. These signals do not automatically occur, and conduct does not prove that any particular emotion was consciously felt.

Where conduct harms another person, the actor's relief and the other person's impact are two separate records. Explanation does not replace accountability, consent, boundaries, stopping harm or repair.

## Bodily demand without a stored-emotion claim

> **Unresolved conditions, repeated physiological recruitment, incomplete recovery and newly created consequences may contribute to cumulative bodily demand. Later events may recruit the organism from an altered baseline even when the original emotions remain poorly differentiated or unavailable to ME.**

This does not mean emotions accumulate as stored substances. Allostatic load is a heterogeneous scientific construct, not a direct measure of “unprocessed emotion”. The exact physiological and evidential claims remain deferred for bounded evidence triage and Model 2, Model 3 (ESC) and Fluid–Chronic review.

## A non-reciprocated Love example

Love can remain genuine when it is not reciprocated.

- In a more Fluid configuration, Love may become felt and differentiated from grief, shame, fear or anger; the other person's response can clarify reciprocity; ME may accept the answer and choose conduct consistent with consent and boundaries while Love or grief continues.
- In a more Chronic configuration, Love and protective information may be only partly available or may arrive as pressure, urgency, longing or a compelling explanation. The other person's response may be treated as something that must change before internal pressure can change.
- Neither configuration determines behaviour. Present organisation and access shape the field of participation; conduct remains separately assessable.

## Non-inference boundaries

- Love remains Love across Fluid and Chronic organisation.
- One emotion does not transform into another or prove one Gradient Position.
- Processing may continue without becoming consciously available to ME.
- Conscious availability does not guarantee accurate naming, interpretation, use or action.
- Mentalizing does not give direct access to another person's mind.
- Relief does not establish completion, safety, reciprocity, Return, recovery or repair.
- Organisation and access do not establish personality, diagnosis, morality or conduct.
- Emotion never prescribes or authorises behaviour.
- Mixed, context-dependent and unknown relationships remain possible.

The Chronic question is not whether Love became something else. It is what remained available, what joined it, which route became possible and what the outcome allowed the organism and ME to learn next.
