Reading structure

Keep each level visible.

A record begins with the condition the organism may be registering. It then follows the process towards possible conscious access and asks what new condition or evidence could allow updating.

  1. ConditionWhat may be changing?
  2. Signal and regulationWhat processes may carry or organise the information?
  3. Possible conscious accessHow might part of it become felt?
  4. Updating conditionWhat change or evidence could alter priority?
  5. BoundaryWhat does the signal not establish?

This is a reading order, not a fixed biological sequence

Detection, regulation, anticipation, conscious access and action can overlap and affect one another. The structure keeps the questions separate; it does not claim that every signal moves through five stages.

Body-condition group

Five working families organise regulatory problems.

These are TEG-Blue organising families, not five established scientific natural kinds. Each family is developed only after its shared problem and internal boundaries have been examined.

A working regulatory structure

Pain and tiredness remain single-member families while the Body-condition inventory is limited to its current scope. The nine Body Signals with their Fluid readings are listed in the Fluid roster.

Working Body-condition family

Essential-supply signals concern access to necessary inputs.

Family definition

Essential-supply signals concern the organism's access to breathable conditions, water and usable nourishment.

The three regulatory systems are biologically distinct. TEG-Blue groups them because each can gain priority when an essential input may not sufficiently meet present or anticipated demand.

Shared problem does not mean shared physiology

These signals do not use one detector, chemical system or timescale. They do not have equal urgency, and conscious intensity is not a direct measurement of the underlying condition. Reduced feeling does not by itself establish restoration.

Essential-supply · regulatory signal domain

Respiratory-sufficiency signalling

Initial synthesis
Condition being integrated
Respiratory drive and demand, ventilation and gas exchange, airway and chest mechanics, respiratory effort and incoming bodily feedback.
Possible conscious access
Breathlessness. This can include air hunger or unsatisfied inspiration, increased work or effort, and chest tightness. These qualities need not occur together.
What may raise priority
Respiratory demand rises, breathing capacity is constrained, airway conditions change, gas regulation is disrupted or achieved ventilation does not match the drive to breathe.
What may allow updating
Ventilation and gas exchange sufficiently meet present demand, respiratory effort reduces or the condition interfering with breathing changes.
Interpretive boundary
Breathlessness is a conscious experience, not an oxygen gauge. Underlying respiratory regulation can change without conscious breathlessness, and breathlessness can persist or become intense through several different physiological and contextual routes.

Essential-supply · regulatory signal domain

Hydromineral-sufficiency signalling

Initial synthesis
Condition being integrated
Extracellular osmolality, sodium concentration, circulating volume and pressure, together with anticipatory information from the mouth and gastrointestinal system.
Possible conscious access
Thirst. Oral dryness can participate in the experience, but dryness and thirst are not interchangeable.
What may raise priority
Current or anticipated fluid imbalance makes water acquisition and conservation more relevant.
What may allow updating
Drinking provides rapid oral and gastrointestinal information; absorbed fluid and electrolyte regulation change the underlying condition over a longer period.
Interpretive boundary
Thirst is not a direct dehydration meter. It can begin to decrease before absorbed water restores blood composition, so felt relief and physiological rehydration must remain separate observations.

Open the thirst neurochemistry record

Essential-supply · regulatory signal domain

Energy-and-nutrient-sufficiency signalling

Initial synthesis
Condition being integrated
Energy and nutrient availability, gastrointestinal contents, stored energy, sensory information, learning and anticipated access to food.
Possible conscious access
Hunger. Hunger can overlap with appetite, wanting and food reward, but these are not the same process.
What may raise priority
Metabolic and gastrointestinal information, learned timing, sensory cues and the expected availability of food can all alter food-related priority.
What may allow updating
Food-related cues, ingestion and gastrointestinal information can begin updating the process before absorbed nutrients change metabolic availability.
Interpretive boundary
Hunger is not a direct fuel gauge. Its presence, absence or intensity does not by itself establish immediate energy deficit, nutritional adequacy or the action a person should take.

Open the hunger neurochemistry record

Family-level dynamic

Essential condition changes or is anticipated to change → regulatory processes alter → signal priority may increase → conscious urgency may become available → incoming information can begin updating the signal → physiological restoration may continue.

The arrows keep observations in order. They do not establish one fixed biological sequence.

Working Body-condition family

Bodily-integrity signals concern possible harm or disruption.

Family definition

Bodily-integrity signals concern conditions that may harm or disrupt the body and make attention or protection relevant.

Pain is the current mapped form of conscious access. The family identifies the organising problem; it does not establish that harm is present or that every protective process becomes painful.

Bodily-integrity · current mapped access

Pain

Initial synthesis
Condition being integrated
Actual or possible bodily harm, nociceptive and other bodily information, inflammation, the state of the affected region, prior learning and the current situation.
Possible conscious access
Pain. It may be experienced in different ways, including aching, burning, pressure, stabbing or tenderness.
What may raise priority
Possible or continuing harm, inflammation, altered sensitivity, movement, context and attention can increase protective priority.
What may allow updating
The source is checked, continuing harm is reduced, the body is protected or treated where needed, and new bodily or contextual information becomes available.
Interpretive boundary
Pain is not nociception or a direct damage meter. Damage can occur without pain, and pain can occur or persist without continuing tissue damage. The experience is real even when its cause or meaning remains uncertain.

Open the pain neurochemistry record

Family-level dynamic

Possible harm or disruption is detected or anticipated → protective processing changes → pain may become conscious → attention and protective readiness may increase → new information may update priority → recovery may continue.

The signal can influence readiness without prescribing behaviour. Reduced pain does not by itself establish that recovery is complete.

Deliberately bounded at this stage

This initial record does not map pain types, clinical diagnoses, detailed biological pathways, treatment or chronic pain and sensitisation. Those questions remain available for later development if the wider system requires them.

Working Body-condition family

Thermal-balance signals concern heat gain, heat loss and temperature defence.

Family definition

Thermal-balance signals concern changes in skin, body and environmental temperature that make heat loss, heat conservation or heat production more relevant.

Overheating and cold are grouped because both participate in temperature defence. They do not share one detector, threshold, pathway or conscious quality.

Thermal-balance · current mapped access

Overheating

Initial synthesis
Condition being integrated
Skin and internal temperature, environmental heat, heat produced by activity or metabolism, and the body's present capacity to lose heat.
Possible conscious access
Feeling too hot, thermal discomfort or an urge to cool. Sweating can accompany the experience but is a regulatory response, not the feeling itself.
What may raise priority
Heat gain or production exceeds effective heat loss, warm sensory information increases, or anticipated exposure makes cooling more relevant.
What may allow updating
A cooler environment, reduced heat production and effective heat loss make temperature defence less necessary; physiological recovery may continue after discomfort falls.
Interpretive boundary
Feeling hot is not a thermometer. Skin temperature, internal temperature, humidity, activity, illness, medication, expectation and context can contribute differently. Reduced discomfort does not prove that internal temperature is safe.

Open the overheating neurochemistry record

Thermal-balance · current mapped access

Cold

Initial synthesis
Condition being integrated
Skin and internal temperature, environmental cold, heat loss, available insulation and the body's present capacity to conserve or produce heat.
Possible conscious access
Feeling cold, thermal discomfort or an urge for warmth. Shivering can accompany the experience but is a heat-producing response, not the feeling itself.
What may raise priority
Heat loss exceeds effective conservation or production, cold sensory information increases, or anticipated exposure makes warmth more relevant.
What may allow updating
Warmer conditions, insulation, movement or other effective heat conservation and production make cold defence less necessary.
Interpretive boundary
Feeling cold is not a direct reading of core temperature. Skin and internal signals can diverge, and sensation, discomfort, vasoconstriction, shivering and behavioural responses can begin at different points.

Open the cold neurochemistry record

Family-level dynamic

Thermal conditions change or are anticipated to change → sensory and central thermoregulatory processes alter → heat-loss or cold-defence responses may gain priority → thermal discomfort may become conscious → new thermal information can update the response.

The arrows organise the reading. Temperature sensing, autonomic response, conscious experience and behaviour can overlap.

Working Body-condition family

Visceral-capacity signals concern storage, release and a viable opportunity to empty.

Family definition

Visceral-capacity and elimination signals concern filling, distension and movement within the bladder or bowel, together with whether release is presently possible.

The bladder and bowel use distinct organs, reflexes and control systems. TEG-Blue groups their conscious urges because both can make storage limits and elimination conditions relevant.

Visceral-capacity · current mapped access

Need to urinate

Initial synthesis
Condition being integrated
Bladder filling, wall stretch and pressure, local tissue conditions, afferent signalling, storage capacity and the coordination required for continence and voiding.
Possible conscious access
Bladder sensation or an urge to urinate, which can range from an early awareness of filling to urgent need.
What may raise priority
Continued filling, increasing or sensitised afferent activity, irritation, learned timing, attention or reduced confidence that a suitable opportunity will remain available.
What may allow updating
A suitable opportunity to urinate and coordinated bladder contraction and outlet relaxation allow emptying and reduce filling-related signals.
Interpretive boundary
Urinary urgency is not a volume gauge. Bladder mechanics, inflammation, neural signalling, pelvic-floor function, attention, learning and context can alter what is felt and when.

Open the urination neurochemistry record

Visceral-capacity · current mapped access

Need to empty the bowels

Initial synthesis
Condition being integrated
Movement of bowel contents, rectal filling and distension, stool properties, local tissue conditions, visceral afferent signalling and sphincter and pelvic-floor coordination.
Possible conscious access
Rectal sensation or an urge to defecate. The urge can change when defecation is delayed even though the underlying contents have not disappeared.
What may raise priority
Mass movement into the rectum, distension, local irritation, changes in motility or reduced confidence that a suitable opportunity will remain available.
What may allow updating
A suitable opportunity and coordinated rectal contraction, pelvic-floor and sphincter relaxation allow contents to pass and distension-related signalling to decrease.
Interpretive boundary
The urge is not a direct measure of stool amount or one chemical signal. Motility, stool properties, sensation, autonomic and voluntary control, learned routines and local pathology can all change the experience.

Open the bowel neurochemistry record

Family-level dynamic

Contents move or storage conditions change → visceral and central signalling alters → an urge may become conscious → access to a suitable place and coordinated release can update the signal.

Feeling less urgency after delay does not by itself establish that the bladder or bowel has emptied or that the underlying condition has resolved.

Working Body-condition family

Restorative-capacity signals concern whether continuing demand remains workable.

Family definition

Restorative-capacity signals concern accumulated wakefulness, exertion and recovery demand that may make reduced demand, rest or sleep more relevant.

Tiredness is the current mapped access. It is a broad everyday term, not one established biological variable.

Restorative-capacity · current mapped access

Tiredness

Initial synthesis
Condition being integrated
Time awake and sleep history, circadian timing, recent exertion, current energy demand, immune and inflammatory activity, illness, recovery state and expected opportunity to rest.
Possible conscious access
Tiredness: reduced felt capacity or increased need for rest. Sleepiness—the tendency to fall asleep—and fatigue related to effort or illness can overlap with it but are not interchangeable.
What may raise priority
Extended wakefulness, circadian timing, insufficient or disrupted sleep, sustained exertion, illness, inflammation or demand that continues to exceed available recovery.
What may allow updating
Reduced demand, rest and sufficient sleep can contribute through different routes. Which condition matters depends on why tiredness became prominent.
Interpretive boundary
Tiredness is not a direct adenosine, energy or sleep-debt meter. Similar feelings can arise through different processes; a brief increase in alertness does not establish recovery. Persistent, sudden or disabling tiredness can require medical assessment rather than framework interpretation.

Open the tiredness neurochemistry record

Family-level dynamic

Wakefulness, exertion, illness or recovery demand accumulates → restorative processes and present demand are integrated → tiredness may become conscious → rest, sleep, reduced demand or recovery can provide new information.

No single route explains every report of tiredness, and no single intervention establishes that capacity has been restored.

Emotion-signal group

Four established families organise functional meaning.

Membership is protected. The families group signals by the kind of condition relationship they may make available. They are not stages, do not prescribe an order, and no family, emotion or non-completion establishes a person's Gradient Position.

Characteristic association is not exclusive content

A family may be especially readable under one Fluid reference position (Belonging around A, Belonging-at-Risk around A↔B, Threat-Protection around B, Survival-Rage around D). The association is illustrative. It is not a rule of membership, and it does not run in reverse: no signal proves a Position.

Cross-group reading

The two groups can participate together without changing identity.

Breathlessness and fear

Respiratory information can become urgent while fear registers possible danger. Fear can also change breathing. Neither signal becomes the other.

Blocked supply and protection

Unavailable food, water or breathable conditions can recruit anxiety, frustration, anger or direct threat information alongside the Body-condition signal.

Supply through connection

Receiving nourishment or protection from another person can recruit gratitude, trust or belonging information without making the bodily requirement a relational emotion.

Keep both questions

What is happening in the regulated bodily condition? What does the surrounding condition mean for connection, agency, protection or survival? One answer must not replace the other.
