Reading structure

Keep each level visible.

A record begins with the condition the organism may be registering. It then follows the process towards possible conscious access and asks what new condition or evidence could allow updating.

  1. ConditionWhat may be changing?
  2. Signal and regulationWhat processes may carry or organise the information?
  3. Possible conscious accessHow might part of it become felt?
  4. Updating conditionWhat change or evidence could alter priority?
  5. BoundaryWhat does the signal not establish?

This is a reading order, not a fixed biological sequence

Detection, regulation, anticipation, conscious access and action can overlap and affect one another. The structure keeps the questions separate; it does not claim that every signal moves through five stages.

Five working families organise regulatory problems.

Essential-supply signals concern access to necessary inputs.

Family definition

Essential-supply signals concern the organism's access to breathable conditions, water and usable nourishment.

The three regulatory systems are biologically distinct. TEG-Blue groups them because each can gain priority when an essential input may not sufficiently meet present or anticipated demand.

Bodily-integrity signals concern conditions that may harm or disrupt the body and make attention or protection relevant.

Thermal-balance signals concern heat gain, heat loss and temperature defence.

Family definition

Thermal-balance signals concern changes in skin, body and environmental temperature that make heat loss, heat conservation or heat production more relevant.

Overheating and cold are grouped because both participate in temperature defence. They do not share one detector, threshold, pathway or conscious quality.

Visceral-capacity signals concern storage, release and a viable opportunity to empty.

Family definition

Visceral-capacity and elimination signals concern filling, distension and movement within the bladder or bowel, together with whether release is presently possible.

The bladder and bowel use distinct organs, reflexes and control systems. TEG-Blue groups their conscious urges because both can make storage limits and elimination conditions relevant.

Emotion-signal group

Four established families organise functional meaning.

Grouping question: What relationship between the organism and a significant condition do these signals register?

Membership is protected. The families group signals by the kind of condition relationship they may make available. They are not stages, do not prescribe an order, and no family, emotion or non-completion establishes a person's Gradient Position.

Characteristic association is not exclusive content

A family may be especially readable under one Fluid reference position (Belonging around A, Belonging-at-Risk around A↔B, Threat-Protection around B, Survival-Rage around D). The association is illustrative. It is not a rule of membership, and it does not run in reverse: no signal proves a Position.

Why the signals belong to their families

Group → family → signal. Each family groups signals around a shared kind of condition, not a shared feeling or chemistry. These are TEG-Blue organising relationships; their scientific and conceptual boundaries are not equally settled.

Essential-supply signals

Breathlessness, thirst and hunger concern whether necessary inputs—breathable conditions, water and nourishment—meet the organism’s needs. Their shared regulatory problem does not make their physiology the same.

Family in the map · Breathlessness · Thirst · Hunger

Bodily-integrity signals

Pain concerns possible bodily harm or disruption and the relevance of protection. It is the current single member of this family; pain does not directly measure damage.

Family in the map · Pain

Thermal-balance signals

Overheating and cold concern temperature conditions that make heat loss, conservation or production relevant. They share a regulatory problem while retaining different detection and response processes.

Family in the map · Overheating · Cold

Visceral-capacity and elimination signals

The need to urinate and the need to empty the bowels concern filling, storage limits and opportunities for release. Bladder and bowel regulation remain distinct.

Family in the map · Needing to urinate · Needing to empty the bowels

Restorative-capacity signals

Tiredness concerns whether continuing demand remains workable or rest and recovery become relevant. It is the current single member of this family, not a direct measurement of one biological variable.

Family in the map · Tiredness

Belonging

Love, trust, gratitude, compassion and admiration concern valued relationships, welfare or qualities. Happiness, joy, pride and hope concern valued experiences, contributions or possibilities. They belong together because value makes care, reliance, appreciation, enjoyment or investment relevant. Compassion can concern suffering, hope an unrealised possibility, and love a bond without reciprocity; a favourable or secure situation is not required.

Family in the map · Love · Trust · Gratitude · Compassion · Admiration · Happiness · Joy · Pride · Hope

Read this family under Fluid organisation · Read this family under Chronic organisation

Belonging-at-Risk

Shame and guilt concern standing or possible harm; loneliness concerns missing connection; disappointment an unmet expectation; sadness and grief absence or loss; confusion conflicting information; frustration obstruction; anxiety possible disruption; and stress demand pressing against capacity. Their shared relationship is disruption affecting something that matters. It extends beyond social belonging and includes losses that cannot be restored.

Family in the map · Shame · Guilt · Loneliness · Disappointment · Sadness · Grief · Confusion · Frustration · Anxiety · Stress

Read this family under Fluid organisation · Read this family under Chronic organisation

Threat-Protection

Fear, anger, disgust and suspicion concern different forms of possible harm, violation, contamination or concealed threat. They belong together because a condition or possible source makes protection, checking, distance or resistance relevant. Feeling the signal does not establish that the perceived threat is accurate or that a particular action is warranted.

Family in the map · Fear · Anger · Disgust · Suspicion

Read this family under Fluid organisation · Read this family under Chronic organisation

Survival-Rage

The current model groups rage, fury and frenzy around different forms of urgent mobilisation when ordinary protection is registered as insufficient or blocked. This does not establish objective life threat or simply mean more intense anger. The boundary between a distinct signal family and a change in protective organisation remains under review with Model 2.

Cross-group reading

The two groups can participate together without changing identity.

Breathlessness and fear

Respiratory information can become urgent while fear registers possible danger. Fear can also change breathing. Neither signal becomes the other.

Blocked supply and protection

Unavailable food, water or breathable conditions can recruit anxiety, frustration, anger or direct threat information alongside the Body-condition signal.

Supply through connection

Receiving nourishment or protection from another person can recruit gratitude, trust or belonging information without making the bodily requirement a relational emotion.

Keep both questions

What is happening in the regulated bodily condition? What does the surrounding condition mean for connection, agency, protection or survival? One answer must not replace the other.
