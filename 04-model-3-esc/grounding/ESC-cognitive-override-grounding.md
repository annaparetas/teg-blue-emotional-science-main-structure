# ESC Cognitive-Override Grounding

Status: focused first evidence pass · 23 August 2026
Scope: ESC-05 · ESC-06 · ESC-08 · ESC-13 · the seven protected override distinctions
Purpose: test what research can support about emotional-somatic information remaining influential while becoming unevenly accessible, expressible, actionable or authoritative during feedback and updating

Companions: [ESC Claim Decomposition](ESC-component-decomposition.md) · [ESS and CLS Component Claim Decomposition](../../02-model-1-ess-cls-me/grounding/ESS-CLS-component-decomposition.md) · [Science Grounding Protocol](../../evidence/protocol.html#override-title) · [Shared Source Record Index](../../evidence/sources/shared-source-record-index.md) · [Canonical Emotional-Somatic Cycle](../cycle.html)

## Review decision

The evidence supports the **component distinctions required by Cognitive Override**, but it does not establish the complete TEG-Blue mechanism as one measured natural process.

Research supports several narrower propositions:

- physiological, expressive, experiential and behavioural components of an emotional response can vary together without moving as one perfectly coherent unit;
- bodily-signal performance, self-reported bodily sensitivity and metacognitive knowledge of performance are separable measurements;
- affective processing can alter evaluation or action without a corresponding reported feeling in some laboratory tasks;
- expression can be intentionally reduced while subjective experience and some physiological responding remain;
- emotion beliefs can affect regulation effort or strategy selection, although associations weaken or disappear for some daily-life measures;
- established judgements can asymmetrically weight confirming and disconfirming evidence in specified tasks;
- interpretation, attention, retrieval and belief persistence are distinguishable routes with different evidence and limitations;
- contradictory information can still change beliefs. Backlash, non-updating and Chronicity are not universal consequences of disagreement.

The exact sequence—**ESS remains active → ESS information becomes insufficiently usable or authoritative → CLS supplies the dominant account → ME participates through that account → feedback fails to revise it → carryover enters the next cycle**—remains **TEG-BLUE–U**. No located study measures this full assembly, uses the ESS–CLS–ME architecture or establishes that one bias causes the Chronic Cycle.

## Method and limits of this pass

This is a focused, claim-led first pass, not a systematic review or meta-analysis. It combines the repository's existing interoception and Chronic source records with selected primary human studies chosen because their designs can test one protected distinction or one feedback route.

Selection priorities were:

- direct separation of response components rather than global “emotion awareness” scores;
- human experimental, longitudinal or intensive repeated-measure designs;
- explicit measures of expression, report, physiology, action, attention, interpretation, retrieval or belief revision;
- primary studies that limit a stronger claim, including null or correction findings;
- task, population and timescale boundaries that can be carried into ESC wording.

This pass does not complete the developmental, cultural or adversity-specific review. It does not establish the cause of a pattern in an individual and cannot determine whether an emotional interpretation, cognitive account or protective response is accurate in current conditions.

# Part I · ESS availability and the seven distinctions

## Why availability must replace an on/off account

“Information is present” can refer to several different observations: a stimulus affects physiology, a bodily signal is represented, a participant performs above chance, a feeling is reported, an emotion is named, an expression is visible, a boundary is acted upon or a belief grants the emotion authority. These observations are related but not interchangeable.

The evidence therefore favours **uneven availability** over language suggesting that ESS either sends a complete message or disappears. It also prevents a reverse inference: a strong report does not establish accurate interpretation, and strong bodily-signal performance does not establish emotional clarity or wise action.

## Availability profile for ESC

ESC can now use the following research-facing questions without claiming a seven-stage mechanism:

1. **Generation:** What observation indicates that affective or emotional significance participated?
2. **Representation:** What bodily or neural variable was measured, and does it require conscious report?
3. **Access:** Was performance, felt clarity, differentiation, confidence, naming or report assessed?
4. **Expression:** Was visible, verbal or physiological expression measured, and under what social instruction?
5. **Regulation:** What changed—attention, appraisal, expression, experience, physiology, action or reported effort?
6. **Action:** Did information alter a simple task response, a deliberate choice or an observed real-world behaviour?
7. **Belief:** Was the study about controllability, malleability, acceptability, usefulness, danger or another belief?
8. **Context:** What power, relationship, culture, material condition and viable alternative shaped what could be expressed or acted upon?

The profile is not a score. More conscious access is not automatically more accurate, safer or more ethical, and less expression is not automatically Chronic.

# Part II · CLS accounts, feedback and belief maintenance

## The feedback question

The Chronic Cycle does not require feedback to be physically absent. The sharper question is:

> **What information was available, how was it selected and interpreted, how much evidential weight did it receive, and did it alter the working account or later response?**

Research does not support “confirmation bias” as a single force that explains every failure to update. At least search, attention, interpretation, evidence evaluation, confidence updating, retrieval, belief persistence and coherence after action must remain separate.

## Route-by-route grounding

| Route | Bounded finding | Current label | Source routes | ESC use and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Confirmation bias / asymmetric updating** | In an estimation-and-wager task, the strength of agreeing opinions influenced revised confidence more than the strength of disagreeing opinions. The effect was designed around commitment to an initial judgement. | **BRIDGE–C** | [ECO-S11](../../evidence/sources/shared-source-record-index.md#src-eco-s11) | Supports asking whether contradictory feedback received less weight. | **Biased assimilation / disconfirmation bias** | Participants with opposing capital-punishment attitudes evaluated mixed purported evidence through their prior positions; later political experiments also found stronger counterargument against opposing claims. | **INDIRECT–B** | [ECO-S12](../../evidence/sources/shared-source-record-index.md#src-eco-s12) · [ECO-S18](../../evidence/sources/shared-source-record-index.md#src-eco-s18) | Supports unequal evaluation as a possible CLS route. Topic, identity, knowledge and commitment are material moderators. |
| **Belief perseverance** | Self- and social perceptions persisted after false feedback was discredited; process-focused debriefing reduced the erroneous perception more effectively than outcome debriefing. | **BRIDGE–B** | [ECO-S13](../../evidence/sources/shared-source-record-index.md#src-eco-s13) | Supports persistence after correction and the possibility that the form of feedback matters. It does not establish permanent closure. |
| **Interpretation under ambiguity** | Training participants to resolve ambiguous information in threatening or benign directions changed later interpretation and, under some conditions, anxiety. | **BRIDGE–B** | [ECO-S14](../../evidence/sources/shared-source-record-index.md#src-eco-s14) | Supports a learned interpretation route and its revisability. It cannot decide whether a real-world threat interpretation is biased or accurate. |
| **Attentional selection and disengagement** | Early dot-probe work found threat-related group differences, but later behavioural and ERP work found poor reliability and no clear anxiety correlation for standard scores. | **BRIDGE–M** | [ECO-S15](../../evidence/sources/shared-source-record-index.md#src-eco-s15) · [ECO-S16](../../evidence/sources/shared-source-record-index.md#src-eco-s16) | ESC may ask what received attention, but must not assign a stable “threat bias” from one task or diagnosis. |

Correction can occur even when prior commitments are present. |

## What “feedback is absorbed” may mean

The phrase should not function as an unexplained step. At least six outcomes are possible:

1. **Not sampled:** relevant feedback is not encountered or attended to.
2. **Not discriminated:** the information is present but not distinguished from noise or ambiguity.
3. **Reinterpreted:** the information is incorporated as support for the existing account.
4. **Discounted:** its reliability or strength receives less weight.
5. **Accepted but not generalised:** the account changes for one event or context without changing later retrieval elsewhere.
6. **Accepted and revising:** the account, confidence, action or organisation changes.

ESC should record which outcome is proposed. “Confirmation bias” cannot substitute for this decomposition.

# Part III · Integration with the ESC two-cycle model

## What the Fluid–Chronic distinction can now claim

The evidence supports defining Fluidity through **responsiveness**, not through the absence of priors or bias.

| ESC question | More Fluid possibility | More Chronic Cognitive-Override possibility | Evidence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What becomes available? | Several information sources remain differentiable and usable. | Relevant information may remain influential while access, expression or authority is restricted. | The full ESS–CLS–ME assembly is TEG-Blue. |
| What happens to ambiguity? | Competing interpretations can remain open and be revised. | A familiar interpretation is more readily selected or reconstructed. | Ambiguity effects are task- and context-specific. |
| How is feedback weighted? | Confirming and contradictory information can alter confidence or account. | Some feedback receives reduced weight, is reinterpreted or remains context-bound. | Stable prior belief can be appropriate; unequal weighting must be demonstrated. |
| What changes after response? | Account, action, organisation or retrieval can update. | Immediate pressure may change while the established route persists or reconstructs. | Relief, belief change, physiology, behaviour and repair are separate outcomes. |
| What enters next? | Revised weighting, wider access or new learning may become available. | Residual demand, restricted access, established expectation or route availability may carry forward. | Carryover remains an umbrella construct requiring route-specific measurement. |

## Wording decisions for the prototype

The [canonical Emotional-Somatic Cycle](../cycle.html) uses appropriately bounded language and does not need immediate causal strengthening.

Retain:

- **“ESS information becomes unevenly usable.”** This is a model-level description supported by component distinctions.
- **“ESS is not absent.”** Use as an architectural correction, not as a claim that ESS activation has been measured in every instance.
- **“Feedback may be absent, unsafe, discounted, delayed or absorbed.”** Keep only alongside the updated-situation-read question and the possibility of revision.
- the seven-layer availability audit as research distinctions, not stages.
- Cognitive Override as one working Chronic route, not the explanation of all Chronic organisation.

Do not strengthen to:

- “confirmation bias prevents updating”;
- “the body knows but cognition refuses”;
- “suppressed expression traps the emotion”;
- “CLS takes over the nervous system”;
- “contradictory feedback causes beliefs to become stronger”;
- “emotion dismissal in childhood produces adult Cognitive Override.”

## Remaining evidence gaps

1. Direct within-person studies measuring bodily representation, feeling access, interpretation, action and feedback across the same event.
2. Emotion-belief studies that distinguish usefulness, safety, acceptability, controllability and epistemic authority rather than only malleability.
3. Developmental studies connecting emotion socialisation to later access, expression and feedback use without inferring one causal pathway.
4. Cultural and material-context research showing when non-expression or reliance on a cognitive account is adaptive, required or unsafe to revise.
5. Naturalistic work separating actual continuing danger from biased interpretation or context-insensitive prediction.
6. Evidence about how revised explicit belief does—or does not—change emotional-somatic weighting, response availability and later behaviour.

## Implementation decision

This first pass is sufficient to retain the prototype's seven-layer audit and to promote Cognitive Override as an explicitly labelled **TEG-Blue working route**. It is not sufficient to present the route as an established biological mechanism, diagnose it in a person or use a cognitive-bias finding to infer that ESS was omitted.

The next ESC decision is therefore architectural rather than evidential: review the prototype against this pass, make only required wording changes, and decide whether the two-cycle rebuild becomes the canonical Model 3 page.
