Shared architecture · coupling interface

How three models describe one changing event.

Model 1 describes how ESS, CLS and ME process information. Model 2 describes how the organism is organised and which capacities are available. The Emotional–Somatic Cycle (ESC) records how both configurations change through time.

The coupling rule

At each recorded moment, ESC identifies the Model 1 processing configuration and Model 2 organisation configuration that are known—and preserves what remains unknown.

Why the models need a shared record

Links between model pages do not show how the models describe the same event.

A model page can mention another model without recording any of its variables. The shared event record keeps the signal, Model 1 processing, Model 2 organisation and ESC timing visible together, so “signal”, “organisation” and “updating” do not become unspecified steps between pages.

Avoid

Three parallel mini-cycles

Model 1, Model 2 and ESC each repeat conditions, action, feedback and learning in their own language. A later framework then has to guess where its variables belong.

Keep

Three distinct model responsibilities inside one event

Model 1 describes processing and participation. Model 2 describes present organisation. ESC compares their changing configurations through time.

Canonical rule

At each ESC moment, record the conditions and signal, the Model 1 processing configuration, the Model 2 organisation configuration and the observable event separately.

As an event changes, processing and organisation remain part of the record.

How feedback can alter different parts of the event record

Record which part changed, when it changed and what remained unchanged.

After an action or response produces feedback, the conditions, signal weighting, ESS or CLS processing, ME access, Gradient organisation and available action routes may not all change together. Naming the part that changed prevents “the system updated” from hiding what the feedback altered—and what it did not.

One event recorded through all three models

A sound interrupts sleep before its cause is known.

This ordinary event shows how Model 1 processing, Model 2 organisation and ESC timing can be recorded together. It does not assign a fixed Position or use one response to explain the whole person.

Event

Something falls in another room.

A loud sound becomes relevant before its source is known. The person listens, looks and finds a book on the floor.

Model 1

Processing and access

ESS may recruit orienting and bodily readiness before a clear explanation. CLS compares possibilities. ME may notice activation, listen and choose to check.

Model 2

Present organisation

A↔B checking or B protection may become more available. The exact organisation depends on the conditions and cannot be inferred from the action alone.

ESC

Change through time

The sound, checking action and new visual evidence form successive moments. ESC compares what changes immediately and what remains active later.

Coupled outcome

Several destinations

The external uncertainty changes. CLS may revise the prediction, ESS may reduce priority, organisation may Return and later learning may alter how similar sounds are weighted.

Three questions remain open throughout an event.

Signal: What condition or need has become relevant, and what information could update it? Use the Signal Map.

Organisation: How are resources, attention, perception and action readiness being allocated, and how flexibly can they change? Use Model 2.

Completion and updating: What condition, action, evidence, boundary or recovery would let the event change course or finish? Follow Model 3.

Signal and organisation can change at different rates. Completion conditions matter throughout; lower pressure alone does not establish completion. Compare the hunger, fear and shame examples.

What the shared event record does not combine

Connecting the models does not merge their responsibilities.

The shared record places the model readings beside one another while keeping their different questions intact: Model 1 describes processing, Model 2 describes organisation and ESC compares change through time.

Model 1 does not own the event timeline.

It can describe processing before, during and after action. ESC owns the comparison of those configurations through time.

Model 2 does not prescribe behaviour.

It describes the field of resources and capacities currently available. Action, power and impact require direct evidence.

ESC does not invent missing states.

It indexes changes supplied by the other owners. If the Model 1 or Model 2 field is unknown, the record retains that uncertainty.

The three models become one architecture when the same event can change through time without losing sight of how information is being processed or how the organism is organised.
