## Decision

Do not reintroduce M1, M2 and M3 as three independent systems, three serial
stages, or a competing architecture. Extract their strongest contribution as
**three simultaneous questions that can be asked of one changing event**:

1. **Signal — what has become relevant?** What condition or need is being
   registered, how much priority has it gained, what direction does it make
   available, and what information could update it?
2. **Organisation — how is the organism reallocating around it?** What is
   happening to physiology, energy, attention, perception, cognition,
   relational availability and action readiness, and how flexibly can this
   organisation change?
3. **Completion and updating — what would let the event change course or
   finish?** What condition, action, evidence, boundary, relational response,
   recovery or reorganisation is required for recruitment to stop and Return
   to become available?

These are cross-sections through the existing Inner Compass sequence, not new
steps added to it. Signal remains active while organisation changes, and the
conditions for updating matter throughout the event rather than only at its
end.

Regulation is not a fourth question or a later stage. It is examined across all
three questions by asking what changed, through which process, with what
immediate and longer outcome, and at whose cost.

## What this adds to the current system

### 1. A direct bridge between emotions, Gradient Positions and regulation

The three-question lens prevents three common collapses:

- an emotion signal is not the same thing as a Gradient Position;
- a change in visible behaviour or arousal does not establish completion;
- regulation is not the suppression of a signal or a return to calm.

The current architecture can now state the relationship compactly:

> Signals indicate what may matter. Nervous-system organisation reallocates
> what the organism can perceive and do around that priority. Regulation,
> updating, completion, Return and recovery describe different parts of how
> that organisation changes again.

### 2. Better questions for the Fluid–Chronic depth axis

The chronic collections contribute useful variables, provided they are treated
as graded, context-dependent properties rather than diagnoses or person types.

| Lens | Fluid-to-Chronic variables worth carrying |
| --- | --- |
| Signal | access, differentiation, trust, source/target accuracy, weighting, expression and responsiveness to new evidence |
| Organisation | recruitment threshold, starting configuration, breadth of repertoire, dwell, exit, flexibility and expectation of recovery |
| Completion / updating | availability, safety and fit of the needed route; whether relief is mistaken for completion; whether Return and recovery are repeatedly interrupted |

Two distinctions should be kept visible:

- **Previously available but now restricted** is not the same as **never
  reliably developed or assembled**. Similar surfaces can require different
  forms of support.
- A chronic organisation may be continuously held or repeatedly
  reconstructed. Chronicity does not require one unchanging state or one
  chemical mediator to remain elevated.

### 3. A more precise account of carryover

Use **carryover** only as a collective label that can be decomposed into:

- residual physiological activation;
- signal priority that has not updated;
- persistence or rapid reconstruction of an organisation;
- learned predictions carried into the next condition-read;
- temporarily or recurrently reduced capacity;
- altered recruitment thresholds; and
- accumulated physiological cost.

Do not describe an emotion as a substance stored in tissue. Do not use the
retired terms *debris* or *somatic debt* as if they were biological entities.

### 4. Route and outcome must be separated from surface behaviour

The same behaviour can contribute to different outcomes in different contexts.
Its appearance does not establish its function. Begin with what was observed,
then ask what the route changed:

- **condition-relevant action or evidence** changes what the signal concerns;
- **supportive modulation** reduces pressure enough to preserve or widen access,
  time or capacity while the relevant condition remains visible;
- **pressure reduction without relevant updating** changes experience,
  attention, urgency or activation while the recruiting condition or
  expectation remains;
- **interpersonal support or relational evidence** changes demand, options or
  understanding through another person's consensual presence, information,
  comfort, practical help or coordinated action;
- **imposed stability** reduces open conflict or uncertainty through another
  person's compliance, silence or restricted options;
- **recovery or restoration** rebuilds one or more channels after demand; and
- **unknown or mixed function** preserves uncertainty when the evidence does
  not support one classification or several processes occurred together.

Do not infer an actor's private relief from coercive behaviour. Establish
coercion through behaviour, power, viable refusal, impact, recurrence and
response to limits. Any proposed actor-level function remains a separate
hypothesis.

Every route should therefore be audited through the same fields:

```text
condition · information and access · actual behaviour · proposed function
        · immediate outcome · duration · updating · recovery and Return
        · person-level cost · transferred cost · power · recurrence
        · evidence status
```

This classification belongs beside the existing five-part distinction between
regulation, Return, recovery, restoration and repair.

## What is not being carried forward

- M1, M2 and M3 as anatomically distinct systems or literal stations.
- The claim that a signal must cross one fixed threshold before organisation
  changes; weighting and organisation are graded, recurrent and mutually
  influencing.
- The claim that a state simply “holds until something changes it.” Different
  processes may update at different rates, and an organisation may be actively
  reconstructed.
- A one-to-one mapping between one emotion, one Position, one behaviour or one
  chemical profile.
- “Completion” as automatic discharge, pressure reduction, catharsis, or a
  guarantee of no later cost.
- “Interoceptive access + environmental safety” as the only two requirements
  for completion. Access, condition, route, capacity, feedback and time can all
  matter.
- The claim that cognitive override is the universal or most common reason an
  event fails to complete. It is one important coordination pattern among
  several possible interruptions.
- The proposed linear-versus-compounding difference between somatic and
  relational substitutes as established fact. It can be retained only as a
  TEG-Blue hypothesis whose component mechanisms require separate support.

## Integration completed in this pass

- the [Shared Event Record](../shared-event-record.html) now holds the
  common fields, time slices and update destinations used across the models;
- the public orientation and the canonical Model 1, Model 2 and Model 3 pages
  link to that interface while retaining their separate ownership;
- the Inner Compass sequence no longer presents action as the point where
  Model 1 hands the event to ESC; ESC indexes the full event while processing
  and organisation remain present;
- the canonical ESC page presents the models as simultaneous readings rather
  than an arrow chain;
- [Signal Recruitment, Persistence and Recovery](../../01-signal-map/grounding/recruitment-persistence-and-recovery.html)
  supplies the scientific trajectory vocabulary behind completion, carryover,
  recovery and changed later recruitment;
- the project map, model README, content manifest and architecture decision now
  record the coupling interface as current architecture.

## Standing scientific boundary

The three-question assembly and the ESC/escalation assembly are TEG-Blue
syntheses. Their component literatures can support condition detection,
emotion episodes, organism-wide stress responding, state-dependent processing,
allostasis, recovery, social buffering, reinforcement, coercive control and
capacity changes. No single source establishes the whole architecture. Public
pages should keep component evidence, supported interpretation and TEG-Blue
synthesis visibly distinct.
