## Purpose

This file is the scientific grounding source pack for the **Emotional-Somatic Cycle (ESC)**.

ESC is a TEG-Blue theoretical synthesis. The complete assembled architecture — including the four-step cycle, Fluid and Chronic forms, Cognitive Override, Completion, Carryover, the Chronic Cascade, and the exact interfaces among ESS, CLS, ME and the Nervous System Gradient — should **not** be presented as an already established biological mechanism.

Instead, CODEX should ground ESC **claim by claim** in established component literatures.

### Core scientific rule

For every ESC statement:

1. Identify the smallest scientific claim being made.
2. Map it to an established research construct.
3. Prefer systematic reviews, meta-analyses, major reviews and primary experimental evidence.
4. Distinguish:
   - **Established component science**
   - **Evidence-compatible TEG-Blue synthesis**
   - **TEG-Blue hypothesis requiring further grounding**
5. Do not use a source to support a stronger claim than the source actually tests.
6. Do not treat conceptual similarity as proof of the full ESC architecture.

# 2. Pillar A — Appraisal, relevance and dynamic emotion processes

## A1. Scherer, K. R. (2005)
**What are emotions? And how can they be measured?**<br>
*Social Science Information, 44*(4), 695–729.<br>
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/0539018405058216

### Source type
Major theoretical / integrative paper.

### Supports
- Emotion as a **process**, not a single static state.
- Emotion episodes as coordinated changes across multiple organismic subsystems.
- Internal or external events become emotionally relevant through evaluation.
- Appraisal, physiological change, action preparation, expression and subjective experience can be treated as distinguishable but coordinated components.
- Supports ESC's decision to model temporal unfolding rather than equating emotion with feeling.

### Does not establish
- ESS, CLS or ME.
- The four ESC steps.
- Cognitive Override.
- Completion or Carryover as ESC-specific mechanisms.

---

## A2. Scherer, K. R. (2009)
**Emotions are emergent processes: they require a dynamic computational architecture.**<br>
*Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B, 364*(1535), 3459–3474.<br>
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2009.0141<br>
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19884141/

### Source type
Major theoretical review / synthesis.

### Supports
- Emotion as a dynamic, unfolding, multicomponent process.
- Emotional responses emerge when events are relevant to organismic needs, goals and well-being.
- Appraisal can drive coordinated changes in attention, physiology, action tendency and expression.
- Different components can unfold at different speeds.

### ESC relevance
Strong grounding for:
**condition → relevance evaluation → multi-system reorganisation → action preparation**

---

## A3. Gentsch, K., Grandjean, D., & Scherer, K. R. (2015)
**Appraisals Generate Specific Configurations of Facial Muscle Movements in a Gambling Task: Evidence for the Component Process Model of Emotion.**<br>
*PLOS ONE, 10*(8), e0135837.<br>
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0135837<br>
PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4546426/

### Source type
Experimental study.

### Supports
- Appraisal-related changes can unfold dynamically over time following an event.
- Different appraisal dimensions are associated with patterned response changes.
- Empirical support for temporal sequencing within the Component Process Model.

### ESC relevance
Useful experimental support for the claim that **detection/evaluation and organismic response are temporally coupled but not instantaneous identical events**.

# 3. Pillar B — Interoception and internal biological information

## B1. Khalsa, S. S., Adolphs, R., Cameron, O. G., et al. (2018)
**Interoception and Mental Health: A Roadmap.**<br>
*Biological Psychiatry: Cognitive Neuroscience and Neuroimaging, 3*(6), 501–513.<br>
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bpsc.2017.12.004<br>
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29884281/<br>
PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6054486/

### Source type
Expert consensus / major review.

### Supports
- Interoception as nervous-system sensing, interpretation and integration of signals originating inside the body.
- Interoceptive processing occurs at both conscious and unconscious levels.
- Interoceptive signalling contributes to urges, feelings, adaptive responses, emotion, cognition, regulation and survival.
- Internal biological information is not equivalent to consciously accessible feeling.
- Sensation, perception and conscious awareness should be distinguished.

### ESC relevance
Central source for the claim:
**biological information can be generated and affect processing without necessarily becoming clearly available to conscious participation.**

### Important caution
This source does **not** establish the ESC proposition that "ESS is omitted while CLS dominates." It grounds the more basic claim that internal bodily information can exist across different levels of processing and conscious accessibility.

# 4. Pillar C — Allostasis, predictive regulation and resource allocation

## C1. Sterling, P. (2012)
**Allostasis: A model of predictive regulation.**<br>
*Physiology & Behavior, 106*(1), 5–15.<br>
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physbeh.2011.06.004<br>
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21684297/

### Source type
Major theoretical / integrative paper.

### Supports
- Regulation as continuous adjustment rather than preservation of a single fixed internal state.
- Anticipatory regulation.
- Brain integration of current variables with prior knowledge.
- Prediction of needs and setting of priorities.
- Coordination of effectors and mobilisation of resources.
- Flexible trade-offs across bodily systems.
- Regulatory errors/outcomes can influence future prediction.

### ESC relevance
One of the strongest sources for:
**entry conditions + prediction → resource organisation → response → outcome-informed future regulation**

### Important caution
Allostasis should not be reduced to "stress." It is a broader model of adaptive predictive regulation.

---

## C2. McEwen, B. S. (1998)
**Stress, adaptation, and disease: Allostasis and allostatic load.**<br>
*Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences, 840*, 33–44.<br>
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1749-6632.1998.tb09546.x<br>
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9629234/

### Source type
Major review / theoretical synthesis.

### Supports
- Adaptive challenges recruit neural, endocrine and immune mechanisms.
- Acute biological mobilisation can be protective and adaptive.
- Repeated or poorly terminated activation can contribute to cumulative biological burden.
- Supports distinction between **adaptive mobilisation** and **chronic cost**.

### ESC relevance
Core grounding for:
**mobilisation is not pathology**<br>
and<br>
**chronicity depends on recurrence, persistence, recovery failure and cumulative cost—not simply intensity.**

**Protective and Damaging Effects of Stress Mediators.**<br>

### Source type
Major review.

### Supports
- Stress mediators have short-term adaptive/protective functions.
- The same systems can contribute to damage when activation becomes chronic or poorly regulated.
- Allostatic load as cumulative wear-and-tear concept.

**Physiology and neurobiology of stress and adaptation: central role of the brain.**<br>

# 5. Pillar D — Regulatory flexibility, context sensitivity and feedback

## D1. Bonanno, G. A., & Burton, C. L. (2013)
**Regulatory Flexibility: An Individual Differences Perspective on Coping and Emotion Regulation.**<br>
*Perspectives on Psychological Science, 8*(6), 591–612.<br>
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691613504116<br>
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26173226/

### Source type
Major theoretical review.

### Key framework
Regulatory flexibility includes:
1. **Sensitivity to context**
2. **Availability of a diverse repertoire of regulatory strategies**
3. **Responsiveness to feedback**

### ESC relevance
This is one of the strongest scientific neighbours of the **Fluid Cycle**.

Supports the idea that adaptive regulation is not one strategy, one arousal level or "being calm"; it depends on the capacity to:
- read context,
- access more than one possible response,
- revise behaviour as feedback arrives.

### Does not establish
- Fluid Cycle as a biological entity.
- ESS/CLS joint usability.
- ESC Completion.

# 6. Pillar E — Persistence, prolonged activation and cognitive carryover

## E1. Brosschot, J. F., Gerin, W., & Thayer, J. F. (2006)
**The perseverative cognition hypothesis: A review of worry, prolonged stress-related physiological activation, and health.**<br>
*Journal of Psychosomatic Research, 60*(2), 113–124.<br>
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpsychores.2005.06.074<br>
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16439263/

### Source type
Major review / theoretical hypothesis.

### Supports
- Cognitive representation of stress can prolong physiological activation.
- Physiological effects can continue before or after the immediate presence of a stressor.
- Worry, rumination and anticipatory cognition can extend the time during which stress-related systems remain recruited.

### ESC relevance
Strong support for:
**a represented condition can contribute to persistence beyond the immediate event.**

### Important caution
This is not evidence for the full **CLS override** mechanism. It supports prolonged physiological recruitment associated with perseverative cognition.

---

## E2. Ottaviani, C., Thayer, J. F., Verkuil, B., et al. (2016)
**Physiological concomitants of perseverative cognition: A systematic review and meta-analysis.**<br>
*Psychological Bulletin, 142*(3), 231–259.<br>
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000036<br>
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26689087/

### Source type
Systematic review + meta-analysis.

### Supports
Perseverative cognition is associated with measurable changes across:
- blood pressure,
- heart rate,
- cortisol,
- heart-rate variability.

### ESC relevance
Empirical grounding for:
**persistent cognitive processing can have continuing physiological consequences.**

### Evidence status
High-value source because it quantitatively synthesises 60 studies.

# 7. Pillar F — Avoidance, relief, reinforcement and corrective information

## F1. Krypotos, A.-M., Effting, M., Kindt, M., & Beckers, T. (2015)
**Avoidance learning: a review of theoretical models and recent developments.**<br>
*Frontiers in Behavioral Neuroscience, 9*, 189.<br>
DOI: https://doi.org/10.3389/fnbeh.2015.00189<br>
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26257618/<br>
PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4508580/

### Source type
Major review.

### Supports
- Avoidance can be adaptive or maladaptive.
- Avoidance responses can be acquired and maintained through learning.
- Reduction of fear / omission of expected aversive outcomes can reinforce avoidance.
- Excessive avoidance can prevent exposure to anxiety-correcting information.
- Behaviour that reduces immediate aversive experience does not necessarily revise the underlying threat expectation.

### ESC relevance
Very strong grounding for the distinction:
**temporary relief ≠ sufficient updating / resolution**

### Important caution
Do not generalise avoidance-learning findings to every somatic substitute, relational behaviour or every emotional process. Use it as one well-established demonstration that **immediate pressure reduction and corrective updating can diverge**.

# 8. Pillar G — Extinction, context and persistence of older learning

## G1. Hermans, D., Craske, M. G., Mineka, S., & Lovibond, P. F. (2006)
**Extinction in Human Fear Conditioning.**<br>
*Biological Psychiatry, 60*(4), 361–368.<br>
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2005.10.006

### Source type
Review.

### Supports
- New learning does not necessarily erase prior threat learning.
- Fear can return through renewal, reinstatement and other context effects.
- Updating can be context-dependent.

### ESC relevance
Supports caution against representing successful updating as deletion of prior learning.

Useful for:
**learning changes future entry conditions, but old routes may remain available and can re-emerge.**

---

## G2. Vervliet, B., Craske, M. G., & Hermans, D. (2013)
**Fear extinction and relapse: state of the art.**<br>
*Annual Review of Clinical Psychology, 9*, 215–248.<br>
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-clinpsy-050212-185542

### Source type
Major review.

### Supports
- Extinction learning is vulnerable to relapse phenomena.
- Context affects whether old versus newer learning controls behaviour.
- Strong grounding for non-linear and context-sensitive updating.

# 9. Pillar H — Social buffering and relational regulation

## H1. Kikusui, T., Winslow, J. T., & Mori, Y. (2006)
**Social buffering: relief from stress and anxiety.**<br>
*Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B, 361*(1476), 2215–2228.<br>
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2006.1941<br>
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17118934/<br>
PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1764848/

### Source type
Review across mammals, including humans.

### Supports
- Presence of conspecifics can alter recovery from distress.
- Social buffering can affect neuroendocrine stress responses.
- Relationship and social context can modify physiological regulation.

### ESC relevance
Strong source for the relational branch:
**another person's presence or response can genuinely change stress physiology.**

### Important caution
Social buffering does not imply that another person is obligated to regulate someone, nor does it establish dependency, coercion or relational harm.

---

## H2. Hennessy, M. B., Kaiser, S., & Sachser, N. (2009)
**Social buffering of the stress response: diversity, mechanisms, and functions.**<br>
*Frontiers in Neuroendocrinology, 30*(4), 470–482.<br>
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.yfrne.2009.06.001<br>
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19545584/

### Source type
Major review.

### Supports
- Social companions can moderate HPA-axis responses.
- Social buffering is not universal.
- Relationship quality, developmental stage, prior experience, social organisation and other factors affect whether buffering occurs.

### ESC relevance
Important corrective against simplistic claims such as:
"contact with another nervous system always regulates the organism."

---

## H3. Hostinar, C. E., Sullivan, R. M., & Gunnar, M. R. (2014)
**Psychobiological mechanisms underlying the social buffering of the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenocortical axis: A review of animal models and human studies across development.**<br>
*Psychological Bulletin, 140*(1), 256–282.<br>
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1037/a0032671<br>
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23607429/

### Source type
Major developmental review.

### Supports
- Social buffering of stress physiology across development.
- Caregiver buffering is especially important earlier in development.
- Mechanisms and effectiveness change developmentally.

### ESC relevance
Key source if ESC discusses infant dependence on caregiver-supported regulation or developmental learning of relational regulation.

# 11. Proposed scientific operationalisation of ESC terms

These are **TEG-Blue terms**. CODEX should map them onto measurable component processes rather than presenting them as standard scientific constructs.

## Regulation
TEG-Blue use:
> Ongoing organisation and allocation of biological resources and capacities in relation to current or predicted demand.

Ground with:
- Sterling 2012
- McEwen 1998 / 2007
- Bonanno & Burton 2013

Do not define regulation as "calming down."

---

## Completion
TEG-Blue umbrella construct.

Possible measurable components:
- relevant external/internal condition changed,
- threat/demand prediction updated,
- previous response recruitment is no longer needed in the same form,
- behavioural flexibility expands,
- physiological recovery becomes possible,
- new learning modifies subsequent responses.

Ground components with:
- Sterling 2012
- Bonanno & Burton 2013
- McEwen 1998 / 2007
- extinction / learning literature
- avoidance literature

Do **not** claim science recognises one unitary biological "completion mechanism."

---

## Return
TEG-Blue use:
> Reorganisation toward a wider/more flexible Gradient configuration when previous demand no longer requires the same resource allocation.

Possible grounding:
- stress-response termination,
- regulatory flexibility,
- allostasis,
- recovery literature.

---

## Recovery
TEG-Blue use:
> Replenishment, repair and physiological settling after or alongside reorganisation.

Ground with:
- stress recovery literature,
- McEwen,
- autonomic/endocrine recovery studies.

Keep distinct from Return.

---

## Carryover
TEG-Blue umbrella construct.

Possible components:
- residual physiological activation,
- altered prediction,
- learned response availability,
- altered context weighting,
- lowered/raised recruitment threshold,
- reduced available capacity,
- incomplete recovery,
- accumulated physiological cost,
- relational consequences.

Ground component-by-component.

Do **not** describe carryover as a literal substance or as an emotion physically stored intact in tissue.

# 12. Chronic Cascade — claim-by-claim grounding requirements

CODEX should never search for one paper that "proves the Cascade."

Break it into these claims:

## Cascade Claim 1
**Internal/emotional-somatic information can influence processing without becoming clearly consciously accessible.**

Ground with:
- Khalsa et al. 2018
- Craig 2002
- interoception-awareness literature

Status: supported at component level.

---

## Cascade Claim 2
**Cognition can sustain or reconstruct physiological activation beyond the immediate event.**

Ground with:
- Brosschot et al. 2006
- Ottaviani et al. 2016

Status: strong.

---

## Cascade Claim 3
**A behaviour can reduce immediate distress while leaving relevant expectations or conditions insufficiently updated.**

Ground with:
- Krypotos et al. 2015
- extinction literature

Status: strong in avoidance/fear-learning domains; generalisation requires care.

---

## Cascade Claim 4
**Repeated or poorly terminated activation can produce cumulative physiological cost.**

Ground with:
- McEwen 1998
- McEwen 2007
- allostatic-load literature

Status: strong.

---

## Cascade Claim 5
**Social contact can genuinely reduce stress physiology.**

Ground with:
- Kikusui et al. 2006
- Hennessy et al. 2009
- Hostinar et al. 2014

Status: strong, context-dependent.

---

## Cascade Claim 6
**Relief obtained through another person is not equivalent to evidence that the underlying material or relational condition changed.**

Scientific basis:
- social buffering shows real physiological relief,
- learning/avoidance literature shows relief and updating can diverge.

Status:
Evidence-compatible TEG-Blue synthesis.

---

## Cascade Claim 7
**Relational relief can become coercive, exploitative or harmful.**

This cannot be inferred from physiology.

Requires separate behavioural evidence:
- power,
- viable refusal,
- coercive behaviour,
- recurrence,
- impact,
- response to limits,
- repair,
- restricted agency.

Status:
Must be grounded in coercive-control / interpersonal violence / behavioural literature, not autonomic science.

---

## Cascade Claim 8
**Repeated cycles can contribute to a more rapidly reconstructed or persistent organisation later.**

Ground with:
- allostasis / allostatic load,
- stress neuroplasticity,
- associative learning,
- context learning,
- fear relapse/renewal,
- habit and sensitisation literature.

Status:
Strong component basis; exact ESC/Gradient interface is TEG-Blue synthesis.

# 13. Evidence-status labels for CODEX

Use these exact labels where useful.

### ESTABLISHED COMPONENT
The underlying scientific process is well recognised and supported across substantial evidence.

### SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION
The wording combines established findings but is not itself a standard named mechanism.

### TEG-BLUE SYNTHESIS
TEG-Blue assembles several supported processes into a new functional architecture.

### TEG-BLUE HYPOTHESIS
The proposed relationship is plausible and partly grounded but requires direct or stronger evidence.

### DO NOT INFER
Evidence in one domain does not justify the stronger conclusion.

Example:
> Physiological activation → coercive behaviour

**DO NOT INFER.** Behaviour and responsibility require independent evidence.

# 16. CODEX instruction — how to use this file

When revising or scientifically grounding the ESC page:

1. Preserve the distinction between **Model 1 information**, **Model 2 organisation**, and **Model 3 temporal sequence**.
2. Do not rename a scientific construct as if TEG-Blue invented it.
3. Do not rename a TEG-Blue synthesis as if science already recognises it.
4. Cite sources directly next to major scientific claims.
5. Add evidence-status labels to theoretical sections where helpful.
6. Prefer language such as:
   - "consistent with..."
   - "draws on research showing..."
   - "TEG-Blue uses the term X to integrate..."
   - "the component processes are established; their exact assembly here is a TEG-Blue synthesis."
7. Avoid:
   - "science proves the ESC"
   - "the nervous system stores unfinished emotions"
   - "physiology causes coercion"
   - "regulation means calm"
   - "relief proves resolution"
   - "chronic activation means the original threat is no longer real"
8. Continuing danger, deprivation, pain or relational threat can accurately sustain protective recruitment. Do not redescribe continuing real-world threat as a failure of internal regulation.
9. Keep behaviour, responsibility, coercion and harm on a separate evidence plane from internal physiology.
10. Treat **Completion** and **Carryover** as decomposable functional constructs until stronger direct validation exists.
