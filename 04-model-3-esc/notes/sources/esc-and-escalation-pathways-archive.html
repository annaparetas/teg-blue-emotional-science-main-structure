01 · Definition

A time-view of the whole event

ESC does not add another organ, pathway or nervous-system state. It asks what happens to an organised response as conditions, action, feedback and learning continue.

The Emotional Systems Cycle is TEG-Blue’s accessible name for following an event through condition-reading, signal and whole-organism change, conscious participation, action, feedback, updating, Return, recovery and learning. The model contains two comparison cycles: Fluid, when relevant present information can influence what happens next, and Chronic, when the organising pattern is recurrent, difficult to revise or still required by continuing conditions.

Its scientific-facing subtitle—emotional–somatic and reflective–symbolic processing across conditions, organisation, action, feedback, updating, recovery and learning—keeps the participating processes visible without treating them as separate stages. Earlier TEG-Blue material expanded ESC as Emotional-Somatic Cycle; that name remains part of the model’s provenance.

The ESC scaffold is recurrent and bidirectional. Processes overlap; cognition can intensify, redirect or settle bodily organisation; new bodily and environmental information can revise cognition; and several parts of the event can update at different rates. The scaffold is therefore a teaching map, not a claim that biology moves through fixed stations.

ESC’s central question is not simply, “Did the person calm down?” It is: What changed, what remained active, what became learnable, and what does the next event begin inside?

Not a calm-down sequenceAdaptive regulation can include intense mobilisation.
Not automatic dischargeExpression or pressure reduction does not by itself establish update, Return or recovery.
Not a character modelA recurrent route identifies a pattern to investigate, not a fixed type of person.

02 · Shared scaffold

Both cycles begin before the visible event.

Every pass enters from a coupled Model 1 and Model 2 configuration—restored, mobilised, depleted, connected, guarded or carrying unfinished demand from earlier events.

01

Entry

ESC records the starting Model 1 configuration, Model 2 Position and depth, available resources, learning and carryover.

02

Conditions register

ESS and other sensory processes register change while CLS predictions may already participate. Organisation shapes salience and readiness.

03

Signal + organisation

Model 1 specifies processing and possible access. Model 2 specifies the current problem, resource allocation, Position and context-specific depth.

04

Access + meaning

Model 1 records what reaches ME and how ESS and CLS agree or conflict. Model 2 records the capacities made more or less available.

05

Action + response

ME participation and the Gradient’s available action field meet observable action, environmental change and other people’s responses.

06

Feedback + updating

ESC compares moments and names the destination: condition, signal, ESS, CLS, ME access, Gradient organisation or action route.

07

Return, recovery + learning

Model 1 specifies changed processing; Model 2 specifies changed recruitment and capacity; ESC records what enters the next event.

↶ Nothing exits the architecture. In both cycles, completion, partial update, carryover or added cost becomes part of the next entry. What differs is whether relevant feedback can change the configuration.

03 · Two forms of movement

The same scaffold can remain Fluid or become Chronic.

The difference is not whether the event contains distress, mobilisation or strong emotion. It is whether present information can influence the coupled configuration and whether organisation can change when the relevant conditions change.

Fluid Cycle

Present information can change what happens next.

Processing and organisation remain sufficiently responsive to current conditions. The cycle may be intense, incomplete or slow; Fluid does not mean calm.

  1. Entry remains connected to the present.Prior learning participates, but current conditions can still alter weighting, expectation and organisation.
  2. ESS and CLS contribute information.Embodied-affective and reflective-symbolic processing may agree, conflict or unfold at different speeds without one being discarded automatically.
  3. ME can use enough of the difference.The person may feel, reflect, check, pause, seek support or act from a field that retains more than one source of information.
  4. Action meets the relevant condition.Action, inhibition, evidence or another person’s response changes the situation—or reveals that it has not changed.
  5. Feedback reaches the configuration.ESS weighting, CLS prediction, ME participation or Gradient organisation can change in response to credible information.
  6. Recruitment can change course.Completion may occur, Return may begin and recovery may continue across its own timescale.
  7. The next entry carries usable learning.The event may change later expectations, access, skills, action routes or readiness without fixing one permanent response.
Fluidity is responsiveness, not success. A Fluid Cycle can end in loss, uncertainty or continued protection when those conditions remain real.

Chronic Cycle

The organising pattern is reconstructed or remains difficult to revise.

The cycle begins with recurrent weighting, narrowed access, altered thresholds, reduced capacity, unfinished demand or continuing adverse conditions.

  1. Carryover enters as part of the starting field.Expectation, residual activation, depleted capacity or a familiar organisation is already more available.
  2. Information becomes unevenly usable.Signals may be fused, faint, delayed, over-weighted, reinterpreted, distrusted or difficult for ME to differentiate.
  3. Processing and organisation narrow together.CLS may serve the protective prediction; ESS information may dominate, remain inaccessible or be treated as irrelevant; the Gradient constrains the available field.
  4. ME participates from what remains available.A familiar explanation, role, inhibition or response route may feel like the only coherent or workable option.
  5. The route may change pressure without changing the organising condition.Relief, control, compliance, withdrawal, certainty or collapse can make the immediate event feel more settled.
  6. Relevant feedback has reduced influence.It may be absent, unsafe, inaccessible, discounted, reinterpreted, punished or insufficient to change the underlying organisation.
  7. The outcome becomes the next cycle’s starting condition.The route, expectation, recruitment threshold, cost or reduced capacity may become more available when similar conditions arise.
Chronic does not mean false. Ongoing danger, deprivation, pain, injustice or relational threat can keep protection accurately recruited.

The Chronic Cycle is defined by recurrence, persistence, restricted updating or continuing conditions—not by one ESS–CLS relationship. Cognitive override is one important Chronic route. Other routes include affective capture, undifferentiated signalling, reduced reflective access, dissociation, exhaustion and environmental maintenance.

Specific Chronic pathway

The Chronic Cognitive-Override Cycle

Accessible description: ME proceeds by relying on the CLS account while ESS information is omitted, inaccessible or treated as noise.

Scientific-facing description: recurrent reduction in the conscious usability or authority of embodied-affective information alongside reflective-symbolic dominance in interpretation and participation.

01 · ESSEmotional–somatic relevance registers.

Bodily, affective or relational information continues to influence salience, physiology and action readiness.

02 · ACCESSThe signal does not become sufficiently usable.

It may be faint, undifferentiated, costly, delayed, distrusted, reinterpreted or actively suppressed.

03 · CLSA rule or explanation provides coherence.

CLS may organise around a role, prediction, ideal, goal or account that makes the feeling irrelevant, wrong or merely noisy.

04 · MEME proceeds without usable ESS participation.

The person acts from the information and options that reach conscious participation, sometimes deliberately and sometimes automatically.

05 · ROUTEAction follows the coherent account.

ME may comply, perform, explain, manage, withdraw, persist or act against the signal while the original information remains unresolved.

06 · OUTCOMEStability or relief may follow.

Uncertainty, conflict or pressure may reduce. Relief alone does not establish reinforcement, completion or accurate interpretation.

07 · NEXT ENTRYThe disconnection becomes easier to reconstruct.

Signal cost, reduced access, the CLS account, the response route and Gradient organisation may carry into later events.

ESS is not deleted.Omission names reduced conscious use or authority, not the absence of embodied-affective processing.
CLS is not inherently false.Reflection can clarify ESS information and present conditions. The problem is closed dominance, not cognition itself.
ME may not be consciously choosing the split.The route can be learned, automatic and experienced as stability, competence, duty or identity.

04 · Cross-sections

Three questions remain live throughout

These questions connect emotion and nervous-system organisation without turning Regulation into a separate mechanism, stage or handoff between ESS and CLS.

SIGNAL

What has become relevant?

Which bodily need or situational condition has gained priority? How readable and differentiated is it, and what evidence could update it?

ORGANISATION

What is the organism preparing to do?

How are energy, attention, perception, cognition, relationship and action being reallocated? Can that organisation change with conditions?

COMPLETION + UPDATING

What would let recruitment change?

Which condition, action, evidence, relational response or recovery would allow priority to update and a less costly organisation to reopen?

Regulation is examined across all three questions. The audit begins only when the changing condition, process, outcome, timescale and cost can be named.

05 · Regulation route audit

What changed, for whom, and at what cost?

A route cannot be classified from its surface form. The same action may protect, postpone, support recovery, reduce pressure or impose a cost, depending on the conditions and outcome.

01 · EVENT + CONDITIONSWhat was happening?Name the internal, material, relational and represented conditions without assuming what they meant.
02 · SIGNAL + INFORMATIONWhat became relevant?Identify the candidate signal, what it may report and what evidence could clarify or complete it.
03 · MODEL 1 CONFIGURATIONWhat was processed and available?Record ESS and CLS participation, their relation, ME access and how ME participated.
04 · MODEL 2 CONFIGURATIONHow was the organism organised?Record Position, depth, resources, capacity access, fit and flexibility at that moment.
05 · ACTION + RESPONSEWhat actually occurred?Describe action, inhibition, environmental response, power, impact, transferred cost and viable refusal directly.
06 · TRANSITION + LATER OUTCOMEWhat changed, where and when?Compare immediate and longer outcomes across updating, completion, Return, recovery, learning and carryover.

Condition-changing action

The relevant demand, danger, deprivation, uncertainty or need changed. Fit, proportionality and longer cost still require examination.

Supportive modulation

Pressure reduced enough to create time, access or capacity while the unresolved condition remained visible.

Pressure reduction without relevant updating

Experience, attention or urgency changed while the recruiting condition or expectation remained.

Imposed stability

Conflict or uncertainty reduced through another person’s compliance, silence or restricted options. The actor’s private function is a separate hypothesis.

Recovery or restoration

One or more channels rebuilt after demand. Quiet behaviour or reported relief does not establish whole-person recovery.

Unknown or mixed function

The evidence does not support one classification, or several processes occurred together. Uncertainty is retained rather than filled in.

These are inquiry classes, not labels for behaviours. Work, rest, reassurance, exercise, withdrawal, confrontation and connection can each participate in more than one route.

06 · Completion

Lower pressure is only one change

An event can look finished while its organising condition, prediction or physiological cost remains. Completion is better examined through five separable questions.

01

Did the relevant condition change?

Danger ended, a need was met, a boundary became effective, loss was registered, or the situation was reorganised.

02

Did information update?

Credible information became available and altered the signal, interpretation, expectation or future readiness.

03

Could recruitment stop?

Further mobilisation, checking, management or conservation was no longer required for the previous task.

04

Could recovery continue?

Spent resources could be restored across the channels and timescales required.

05

Could Return begin?

More flexible organisation and wider capacities became available. Return may begin before recovery is complete.

Different conditions can require different forms of action or evidence. Solitary settling cannot provide another person’s recognition; reassurance cannot end material danger; and visible compliance is not relational evidence because it was produced through reduced agency. No single route is required for every emotion or event.

07 · The hinge

Immediate relief and longer updating can diverge

Relief is meaningful information, but it does not establish why it occurred or what will happen next. The route audit compares the immediate and longer outcomes.

Fluid Cycle possibility

Feedback changes the coupled configuration

Action or new evidence changes the condition, Model 1 processing or Model 2 organisation. Pressure may fall, recruitment may reduce, and the outcome may become usable learning.

condition-relevant response → credible feedback → ESS / CLS / ME and Gradient variables can revise → recovery and Return may become available

Outcome sampled

Chronic Cycle possibility

Pressure changes while the coupled configuration remains

A route changes sensation, attention, uncertainty or another person’s behaviour while the recruiting condition, processing pattern or organisation remains. Recurrence cannot be inferred from relief alone.

pressure reduction → Model 1 / Model 2 configuration remains or reconstructs → recurrence tested separately → carryover enters the next event

These are comparison routes, not an exhaustive binary. An event may produce partial, delayed, mixed or presently unknown outcomes.

CARRYOVER 01Residual physiological activation
CARRYOVER 02Signal priority not yet updated
CARRYOVER 03Persistent or rapidly reconstructed organisation
CARRYOVER 04Prediction carried into later condition-reading
CARRYOVER 05Reduced currently available capacity
CARRYOVER 06Changed recruitment threshold
CARRYOVER 07Accumulated physiological or relational cost

Carryover is a decomposable description, not a substance. ESC does not claim that an emotion is stored intact in tissue.

Specific Chronic pathway

Cognitive override

A rule, role, explanation or goal gains priority while emotional–somatic information becomes less accessible, is reinterpreted or is acted against. In the detailed cycle above, ME proceeds through the CLS account without sufficiently usable ESS participation.

This can be deliberate, learned or automatic. It does not delete ESS, make CLS inherently false or establish that cognition and embodiment are opposing systems. Return to the seven-moment Chronic Cognitive-Override Cycle ↑

08 · Route comparison

A route can change one’s own experience or another person’s options

Both routes may reduce immediate pressure. They require different ethical and evidential treatment when another person’s viable refusal, expression or movement is restricted.

Self-directed route

Changes in state, attention or pressure

Work, intensity, withdrawal, repetitive checking, substances, food, exercise, fantasy or ritual can support capacity, postpone action or become costly. Surface form does not decide the route.

  1. Name the condition and the action separately.Do not assume the behaviour was recruited because the relevant route was unavailable.
  2. Observe the immediate change.Attention, arousal, sensation, effort or available time may change enough to produce relief or capacity.
  3. Check the condition and information again.The route may have changed the situation, supported later action or left the original demand open.
  4. Examine recurrence rather than assuming reinforcement.Ask whether the action becomes earlier, more rigid, more intense or simply remains useful.
  5. Track cost and widening capacity.Health, recovery, relationships and range of action may improve, remain stable or narrow over time.
Boundary: none of these behaviours is inherently a substitute. Ask what changed, what remained, what it cost, and whether flexibility widened or narrowed.

Relational power route

Stability imposed through restricted agency

Threat, surveillance, punishment, compelled reassurance or demanded compliance can secure an outcome by narrowing another person’s options. Whether this also changes the actor’s internal pressure is a separate question.

  1. Name what was demanded or restricted.Identify the behaviour, power available and whether refusal, disagreement, pause or departure remained viable.
  2. Observe the immediate outcome.Open conflict may reduce, the preferred result may be secured, or the other person may become quieter or less able to refuse.
  3. Keep actor-level function separate.Relief, certainty, entitlement, strategy and institutional reward are candidate explanations, not facts established by compliance.
  4. Track transferred cost.Fear, labour, deprivation, reduced agency, injury, isolation or recovery cost may move onto another person.
  5. Examine feedback and recurrence.Ask whether impact, boundaries and consequences revise the behaviour or whether its intensity, reach or enforcement widen.
Boundary: coercion is established through behaviour, power, viable refusal, impact and response to limits—not an inferred nervous-system state or private need.

09 · Relational escalation

A pattern may widen when power blocks correction

This is an observational sequence for examining recurrence, not a biological cascade or prediction. Each move is a question, and no move proves that the next must occur.

QUESTION 01

Is another person’s behaviour being made responsible for the outcome?

Look for demands concerning availability, reassurance, agreement, expression, movement, resources or access.

QUESTION 02

What power supports the demand?

Authority, money, status, information, dependency, isolation, threat or force can make apparent agreement difficult to refuse.

QUESTION 03

What changes when the other person complies?

Open conflict or uncertainty may fall, but the condition, actor-level function and transferred cost must be examined separately.

QUESTION 04

Does feedback revise the behaviour?

Observe what happens when distress, disagreement, boundaries, consequences or contradictory information become visible.

QUESTION 05

Does the pattern recur or widen?

Track frequency, intensity, reach, monitoring, punishment, restriction and the growing cost of refusal over time.

QUESTION 06

What protection and constraint are required?

When feedback does not stop the pattern, safety cannot depend on insight, emotional access or the harmed person providing further evidence.

Evidence boundary: recurrence, power, restricted refusal, impact and response to limits can be investigated directly. Relief, reinforcement, emotional access and nervous-system organisation may be relevant hypotheses, but they must not define or excuse the pattern.

10 · Feedback capacity

“Empathy loss” is too blunt

Different relational capacities may vary independently. Their configuration cannot be inferred from harmful behaviour, and none of them determines what a person will do.

Self-state accessArticulate explanation, goals and strategic awareness.Differentiated access to bodily demand, fear, shame, grief, need or vulnerability.If access is reduced, some internal information may be harder to use. This cannot be inferred from control.
Mentalizing / cognitive empathyAccurate inference about another person’s fear, motives, needs or likely response.Uncertainty, curiosity and willingness to let the other person correct the model.Social understanding can serve leverage as readily as care.
Affective sharingResonance in some relationships or contexts.The other person’s distress may carry little felt weight under threat, entitlement or power.Impact can be read without becoming sufficient stop-information.

Establish a harmful pattern through observable behaviour, power, coercion, reduced agency, impact and response to feedback. Do not diagnose an internal empathy configuration from one act or one relationship.

11 · Interruption

The response changes with the route

Earlier work may increase access, options and usable support. Once another person’s agency is being restricted, their protection cannot depend on an inferred mechanism or the controlling person’s readiness to change.

Before the route narrows

Increase readability and options. Differentiate the signal from the first explanation; identify the actual condition; make delay, boundary, rest, help, grief or relational evidence safer and more available.

When relief is repeating

Separate modulation from completion. Preserve useful short-term settling while tracing what remains unresolved, what the route costs and what alternative evidence or action could update the cycle.

When stability is imposed

Protection and constraint come first. Uphold boundaries and consequences, reduce access to harm where possible, and do not use contact, forgiveness or the harmed person’s reassurance as the treatment route.

12 · Ethical guardrails

Explanation and accountability answer different questions

A mechanism can help locate interruption points. It cannot determine innocence, intention, diagnosis, safety or what another person owes.

Mechanism is not justification

How a route formed does not make its impact acceptable. Accountability asks what must stop, be named, be repaired where possible, and be prevented from recurring.

The harmed person owes nothing to the cycle

No one is required to provide safety, contact, compliance, trust or forgiveness so that another person can feel settled, obtain relief or recover.

Protection can precede understanding

When risk is active, containment and reduced access to harm are valid before there is agreement about the mechanism or motivation.

Locate patterns, not people

Use graded, context-dependent language. A recurrent pathway is not a personality, diagnosis, moral essence or irreversible identity.

13 · Scientific boundary

The components have research homes. The full ESC and escalation architecture is TEG-Blue’s synthesis.

COMPONENT EVIDENCE

Emotion episodes, condition detection, autonomic and endocrine recruitment, state-dependent processing, action readiness, stress-response termination, physiological recovery and learning.

COMPONENT EVIDENCE

Allostasis, perseverative cognition, reinforcement, habit and tolerance dynamics, social buffering, interpersonal regulation, coercive control, power and moral disengagement.

SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION

Pressure can fall without the original condition, processing pattern or organisation updating. Repeated short-term relief may contribute to later route availability when recurrence and learning are demonstrated over time.
