The Emotional–Somatic Cycle (ESC)

Time-indexed emotional–somatic and reflective–symbolic processing across conditions, organism organisation, conscious participation, action, feedback, recovery and learning. An emotional response does not end when the feeling is expressed or the visible behaviour stops. ESC follows what changes through one event and how its outcome becomes part of the conditions the next event begins inside.

Approved model territory

What changes when the event continues?

Central proposition

An emotional–somatic event is a recursive process in which conditions register, ESS and CLS process information, nervous-system organisation changes, ME participates, response produces feedback, and the outcome becomes part of the next condition.

ESC is not another organ, nervous-system state or information system. It adds no position and no system. It is the temporal model that connects the existing models across an event and follows how the outcome enters the next.

Working definition

The Emotional–Somatic Cycle is TEG-Blue's temporal model for how emotional–somatic and reflective–symbolic information, organism organisation, conscious participation, response and feedback move through an event and contribute to completion, carryover, updating, learning, Return and recovery.

Governing question: what changes, what remains active, what becomes learnable, and what does the next event begin inside? ESC is the canonical abbreviation. Model 3 is ESC; there is no fourth model beside it.

Regulation continues throughout

Regulation is not a late stage of the cycle. Resources and capacities are organised from the moment conditions register, and keep reorganising during detection, interpretation, action, feedback, Return and recovery. Calm is one possible experience, not the definition of regulation or the required endpoint.

The shared event spine

Five hinges that a Fluid or a Chronic cycle both pass through.

The two forms do not have different stages. They move through the same hinges while differing in information usability, response flexibility and whether feedback can revise the coupled configuration. Functional distinctions inside a recursive process, not biological stations.

  1. Current conditionInternal, environmental, relational and represented conditions the event begins from, including what was carried in.
  2. Coupled readingConditions register while Model 1 processing, Model 2 organisation and capacity availability change together.
  3. ME participation and responseME participates through the information and capacities currently available; action, inhibition, communication, withdrawal or continued observation meets the situation.
  4. Feedback and updated readThe body, environment, action outcome and other people provide new information. It may be available, absent, ambiguous, unsafe, discounted, delayed or strong enough to revise.
  5. Outcome and next conditionCompletion, carryover or a mixed outcome changes the next entry. Return, recovery, updating, learning and integration follow on their own timescales.
At every moment, three readings

Model 1 supplies ESS, CLS and ME: what registers, what is interpreted, what becomes usable and how ME participates. Model 2 supplies position, depth and resources: what the organism is organised for and which capacities are available. Model 3 owns moment, transition and later outcome: how successive configurations change through time and what becomes part of the next entry. Unknown fields stay unknown rather than becoming a generic state. The shared event record is the coupling interface; the expanded sequence is on the cycle page.

Two forms

The difference is responsiveness.

Fluid Cycle

Information remains available for revision.

ESS, CLS and ME stay sufficiently connected for present conditions and feedback to alter what happens next. Current conditions can change the read. Difference remains usable: embodied and reflective information can agree, conflict or unfold at different speeds. ME retains more than one route. The response can meet the relevant condition, or confirm that protection is still needed. The next entry carries usable learning.

Fluid does not require agreement between ESS and CLS, and it does not mean calm. A Fluid cycle can include intense mobilisation, uncertainty, action before reflection, later understanding, partial completion and recovery that outlasts the event. Fluidity is the capacity to update.

Chronic Cycle · Cognitive Override

ME builds coherence through CLS while ESS is treated as noise.

ESS is not absent. Emotional–somatic information is generated and continues to affect physiology, salience and readiness, but it becomes unavailable, undifferentiated, distrusted or given too little authority to participate sufficiently in what ME understands and does. CLS supplies the coherent account through a rule, role, explanation, narrative or goal. The outcome may stabilise pressure without updating the cycle; the route, cost and narrowed organisation enter the next entry.

Cognitive override is not the existence of thought, planning or restraint. It becomes Chronic when one account repeatedly dominates and remains difficult to revise with relevant emotional–somatic information, conditions and feedback. The account may be intelligent, protective or temporarily necessary; the route can be learned and automatic, and feel like competence, duty, certainty, self-control or reality.

The Chronic Cycle is not every Chronic route

ESC gives detailed architecture to one recurrent route, cognitive override. Wider Chronic organisation also involves prolonged persistence, interrupted recovery, repeated reconstruction, generalisation, altered thresholds, restricted action, context-bound updating and continuing adverse conditions, without override being central. Use Fluid and Chronic Organisation for the family; write Chronic Cognitive-Override Cycle wherever a reader could mistake the cycle for the whole of Chronic organisation.

Seven override distinctions

The signal can remain alive while its participation changes.

Research distinctions, not seven stages. Each asks a different question, and evidence at one cannot stand in for another. They keep "override" from becoming one vague event.

LayerChronic questionDo not infer
1 · Signal generationDid emotional significance arise and organise salience, physiology or readiness?A later cognitive account does not show ESS failed to activate.
2 · Bodily representationWas internal bodily information represented, even without clear feeling?Low report or low felt clarity does not prove absent participation.
3 · Subjective accessCould ME feel, identify or differentiate what was happening?Information can stay influential while conscious usability narrows.
4 · ExpressionCould the information be shown, spoken, named or made visible here?Concealment and non-expression are not non-feeling.
5 · RegulationWas the response modulated, suppressed, amplified, avoided or repeatedly reconstructed?Different routes look alike and need different evidence.
6 · ActionCould emotional–somatic information influence a boundary, choice, request or response?Present information may not become usable action guidance.
7 · Emotion beliefWas emotion treated as acceptable, safe, useful, dangerous, irrational or noise?Belief can shape authority without proving absence at earlier layers.

Context belongs in every row: who responded to the emotion, how consistently, under which cultural and material conditions, with what power and viable options, and whether repair or contradictory experience became available. The Chronic cascade on the cycle page follows these layers into two outcomes: revision becomes possible, or carryover remains. A Chronic route can become more Fluid; no person or cycle is permanently assigned to one form.

Outcomes

Completion, carryover, or both at once.

Completion

The relevant condition, demand or task has changed sufficiently for the previous recruitment to change course: effective action, escape, nourishment, information, recognition, grief, boundary enforcement, changed conditions or relational repair, depending on the event. Condition-specific. Not one universal discharge, not full recovery, not proof that every part of the event resolved.

Carryover

What enters later conditions when part of the demand, organisation or outcome remains active or has changed future readiness: residual activation or unmet demand; changed prediction or weighting; altered recruitment threshold; a learned route; reduced capacity or recovery debt; revised concepts; relational or material cost. Decomposable. Not an emotion stored intact in tissue, and not proof of past adversity. Continuing danger may also keep protection accurately recruited.

Mixed outcome

Completion and carryover coexist. One part of a condition resolves while physiology, relationship, cost, grief or recovery remains open. The outcome becomes input: the next cycle begins from what is now available.

Carryover is not one system switched on

A condition may continue; one channel may recover slowly; later demand may interrupt recovery; cognition may reconstruct the event; learning may change later recruitment. The recruitment, persistence and recovery bridge connects these outcomes to their scientific terms.

Completion profile

Not a yes-or-no verdict.

Before classifying an outcome, ask the questions separately. The answers can differ: a condition can end while physiology is still recovering; pressure can fall while the condition remains; Return can begin before recovery is complete; repair may still be required after every question is answered.

Condition · Information · Organisation

Did the relevant internal, material or relational condition change? Did credible feedback alter the signal or interpretation? Could the previous recruitment change course?

Participation · Response

What information and capacities could ME use? What happened, and what feedback did the response produce?

Completion · Carryover

Which demand or task, if any, became sufficiently answered? What remained active, learned, depleted or changed?

Return · Recovery · Next condition

Did wider organisational flexibility begin to reopen? Which channels continued settling or rebuilding? What now shapes the entry into the following cycle?

Bridge to Model 2

How a later cycle can begin.

Three relationships between demand, organisation and recovery. Not three kinds of person and not a scale from calm to distressed.

Opening condition 1

Physiological baseline

A resting configuration in which resources are not recruited for a specific demand. Not itself one Gradient position. A wider range may be available, but that cannot be assumed from low arousal.

Opening condition 2

Fluid activation

An acute, event-timescale reorganisation in response to present or predicted demand. Detection, response and feedback are active; intense mobilisation may be adaptive; updating and Return remain possible when conditions change.

Opening condition 3

Chronic-shaped entry

A recurrent, persistent or difficult-to-revise organisation shaped by carryover, learned weighting, reduced capacity, repeated reconstruction or continuing adversity. The override relationship may be more readily reconstructed; other Chronic routes remain possible outside this cycle.

Ownership across the bridge

The Gradient describes the organisation present at each moment. ESC explains how outcomes and learning contribute to the organisation encountered later. Fluid activation becomes relevant to Chronicity because of persistence, recurrence, restricted updating, accumulated cost or continuing conditions, not because mobilisation was intense. In research, acute names the event timescale; in ESC, Fluid names the cycle's responsiveness to present conditions and feedback.

Model boundary

Time does not absorb the other models.

Owns

Movement through an event; the shared spine; the Fluid and Chronic Cognitive-Override comparison; response and feedback; completion, carryover and mixed outcomes; the temporal handoff into Return, recovery, updating, learning and later entry; recurrence and what becomes the next cycle's input.

Does not own

The taxonomy of emotions (Signal Map). ESS, CLS, access and participation (Model 1). Present organisation and Return (Model 2). Persistence and recovery profile (Fluid and Chronic). Developmental and evolutionary explanation (F1, F2). Conduct, impact, accountability and repair (Behaviour and responsibility). A fixed sequence, a universal clock, one pathway for every emotion, or an inference from relief to reinforcement, recovery or Return.

Name under review · provisional, not adopted

Anna proposed Emotional–Somatic–Cognitive–Logical Cycle as a fuller name that makes CLS participation visible. It must be tested against Model 3's temporal ownership, readability and the established ESC abbreviation before adoption. The canonical name remains The Emotional–Somatic Cycle. The earlier public label, The Emotional Systems Cycle, is provenance; the old folder name still carries it.

Scientific and ethical boundaries

The components have research homes; the assembly is TEG-Blue's.

Boundaries

ESC is not an organ, neural circuit or universal sequence. The spine is recursive and overlapping. Expression, catharsis or pressure reduction does not equal completion. Carryover does not prove trauma, stored emotion or one persistence mechanism. Chronic cognitive override does not mean ESS stopped processing. Continuing protection can be accurate to current danger. No internal cycle configuration establishes coercion, harm, innocence, intent, diagnosis or what another person owes.

Consistent use

Use The Emotional–Somatic Cycle (ESC) as the canonical name; Fluid Cycle for the present-responsive route; Chronic Cognitive-Override Cycle for the specific recurrent route; completion, carryover, Return and recovery for their distinct changes. Describe what carried over rather than treating carryover as one substance. Keep the next condition visible. Never use regulation as a synonym for a calm or completed cycle.
