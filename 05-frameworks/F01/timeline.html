Deep time → present participation

How the living repertoire expanded.

Eight broad expansions made more kinds of condition detectable, more responses possible, and more routes for learning, coordination, updating and recovery available. They accumulated and recombined; they did not become eight stages or map one-to-one onto the seven Fluid Positions.

Deep time asks what became available. Different capacities have overlapping histories across lineages.
The Fluid Gradient asks how capacities organise now. A Position is a present whole-organism configuration, not an evolutionary age.
Eight broad expansions

A capacity timeline, not a dated ladder.

Accumulation across the line

Later capacities join, modify and depend on already living forms of sensing, coordination and action. The line shows continuity, not progress from lesser to greater organisms.

01

Viability maintenance
and internal sensitivity

The living requirement

Remain within viable limits.

Living systems detect and respond to changes relevant to temperature, energy, hydration, chemical balance, tissue integrity and other conditions of continued living. Nervous systems later extend this work; they do not create the underlying viability problem.

Made more manageable

Internal change that could otherwise move the organism outside conditions compatible with continued life.

Capacity added or elaborated

Maintenance, internal sensitivity, resource adjustment and response to favourable or damaging gradients.

Present Gradient relevanceGrounds the model in a living organism whose resources and internal conditions continually change.
Participates beyond one PositionX foregrounds restoration, but viability regulation continues across every Position.
Boundary. X is not identical to homeostasis, a cellular stage, or the only organisation in which maintenance occurs.

02

Coordinated sensation,
movement and orientation

Changing contact

Alter the relationship to conditions.

Coordinated sensing and movement make approaching, avoiding, pausing, feeding, seeking shelter and selecting among environmental possibilities increasingly possible. The organism can change not only its internal activity, but its contact with what matters.

Made more manageable

Opportunities and constraints distributed across a changing environment.

Capacity added or elaborated

Orientation, movement selection, action readiness and the ability to sample the consequences of action.

Present Gradient relevanceContributes to perception, orienting, action availability and the capacity to alter distance, exposure or contact.
Participates beyond one PositionOrientation and movement can serve exploration, care, protection, strategic action or recovery.
Boundary. Present behaviour cannot be reduced to one ancient reflex, circuit or evolutionary purpose.
↑ Return to timeline
03

Conservation, mobilisation
and bodily coordination

Resource direction

Spend, redirect or conserve capacity.

Wider coordination allows energy use, cardiovascular activity, respiration, endocrine participation, perception and action to change together under differing demands. Mobilisation and conservation are both possible forms of regulation, not opposites of regulation.

Made more manageable

Conditions requiring rapid action, sustained effort, maximum survival action, or reduced expenditure when action is unavailable or too costly.

Capacity added or elaborated

Whole-body mobilisation, constrained-action conservation and changing allocation across immediate and continuing demands.

Present Gradient relevanceHelps make the differentiated configurations of B, C, D and Z biologically possible.
Also participates elsewhereA↔B prepares before active mobilisation; restorative and social activity also require coordinated physiology.
Boundary. Conservation, mobilisation and immobilisation must not be arranged into an unsupported fixed phylogenetic sequence or one-pathway state story.
↑ Return to timeline
04

Care, affiliation
and social coordination

Relationship as a condition

Meet some demands with and through others.

Caregiving, affiliation, play, cooperation and coordinated activity allow another organism to change access to warmth, protection, information, learning and action. Relational availability can reduce demand; uncertainty or loss can make that availability survival-relevant.

Made more manageable

Dependent care, obtaining help, shared protection, learning from others, play, cooperation and collective action.

Capacity added or elaborated

Caregiving, attachment, reciprocal coordination, social buffering and the use of relationship as information and resource.

Present Gradient relevanceA holds relationship available for engagement; A↔B checks whether safety, belonging or access may be changing.
Participates beyond two PositionsCare, help-seeking, cooperation and social information can remain relevant across protection and Return.
Boundary. Care is not exclusive to mammals. Connection is not automatically safe, reciprocal, non-defensive or regulating.
↑ Return to timeline
05

Learning, memory,
prediction and flexible action

Using prior outcomes

Prepare from more than the immediate cue.

Learning and memory allow organisms to discriminate conditions, use prior outcomes, anticipate change and revise action. Prediction lengthens the horizon of regulation while flexible action makes more than one response available.

Made more manageable

Recurring, uncertain or delayed conditions whose meaning becomes clearer through experience.

Capacity added or elaborated

Discrimination, anticipation, sequencing, outcome comparison, inhibition and revision of action.

Present Gradient relevanceSupports present-responsive comparison rather than a response tied only to an immediate stimulus.
Participates across the GradientC foregrounds sustained anticipatory management, but cognition, learning and prediction do not belong only to C or D.
Boundary. Cognition did not begin with language, and present Gradient order is not an evolutionary scale of cognitive sophistication.
↑ Return to timeline
06

Prelinguistic social-affective
communication

Meaning before words

Coordinate attention and action without explanation.

Voice, face, gaze, gesture, orientation, touch, proximity, withdrawal, rhythm and movement can coordinate attention, distance, care, alarm, play, invitation, refusal and shared activity. These channels remain active after language appears.

Made more manageable

Social coordination when what matters cannot be named, or when speed and shared timing matter more than explanation.

Capacity added or elaborated

Multi-channel embodied communication of socially consequential conditions, intentions and readiness.

Present Gradient relevanceBodies, faces, voices, gaze, touch and rhythm continue to shape Situation Signals and relational organisation.
Not all feeling is communicationCold, hunger and many emotional events can organise the organism without primarily functioning as messages to another person.
Boundary. Humans did not communicate through emotion alone. Communication was—and remains—multi-channel.
↑ Return to timeline
07

Language, symbolic modelling
and cumulative culture

Reflective-symbolic extension

Make absent conditions jointly available.

Language and symbolic modelling expand the ability to refer to absent events, share explanations, transmit knowledge, coordinate plans, build norms and question an initial interpretation. Culture extends what can be accumulated and handed on.

Made more manageable

Conditions displaced across time and space, complex coordination, cumulative knowledge and collectively maintained possibilities or constraints.

Capacity added or elaborated

Naming, explanation, shared abstraction, long-horizon planning, negotiation and cultural transmission.

Present Gradient relevanceExpands reflective-symbolic participation and the ways remembered, predicted or socially interpreted meaning can alter organisation.
ESS · CLS · ME boundaryEmbodied and reflective-symbolic information remain concurrent biological processes; ME may gain changing access to both.
Boundary. Language did not create cognition, replace emotional–somatic information, or install a detached controller above the organism.
↑ Return to timeline
08

Expanded flexibility,
updating and recovery

Ending costly demand

Change course when conditions change.

An adaptive repertoire must do more than recruit action. It must sample outcomes, revise weighting and expectation, release costly demand, restore resources and make other forms of participation available again.

Made more manageable

Conditions that change, actions that succeed or fail, finite resources, and the need to become available for another task after demand ends.

Capacity added or elaborated

Flexibility, reversibility, recovery, restoration, renewed action availability and continued responsiveness to evidence.

Present Gradient relevanceExplains why Fluid organisation depends on updating and renewed availability, not on calmness or one preferred state.
Return connectionCompletion, release, re-entry of capacities and physiological recovery are related but non-identical processes.
Boundary. Return is not one universal pathway. Restoration is not exclusive to X, and visible quiet does not establish that updating occurred.

Where the timeline hands over

Capacities became available; present organisation combines them.

The next question is not “which Position evolved next?” It is “how does a present organism allocate these overlapping capacities as internal, environmental, relational and represented conditions change?”
