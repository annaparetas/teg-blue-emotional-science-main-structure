F1 · Foundations of the Fluid Gradient

Evolutionary foundations of present-responsive whole-organism organisation across changing internal, environmental and relational conditions. F1 explains why a Fluid Gradient is biologically plausible: how a layered repertoire of sensing, coordination, action, care, communication, learning, reflection and recovery became available, and how those capacities participate together now.

The question F1 owns

How did the repertoire become available, and how does it organise now?

How did organisms acquire and retain capacities to detect consequential conditions, reorganise physiology, attention, feeling, thought, relationship and action around them, and update, recover and Return as conditions change? And how does the Fluid Gradient organise those capacities in the present without turning its positions into evolutionary stages?

F1 owns the deep-time availability of

Viability maintenance and internal condition sensing; sensitivity to environmental opportunity and constraint; coordinated movement, conservation, mobilisation and action readiness; affective and motivational organisation; autonomic, endocrine, immune and metabolic participation; caregiving, attachment, play, cooperation and social coordination; prelinguistic communication; learning, memory, prediction, planning and flexible action; language, symbolic representation and cumulative culture; updating, recovery, restoration and renewed availability.

F1 does not own

How the repertoire is calibrated during one person's development (F2). How a present event is organised (Model 2). Why an organisation becomes Chronic (Fluid and Chronic). What a person does to someone else (Behaviour and responsibility).

Three surfaces, three jobs

The capacity timeline owns the provisional deep-time orientation. The F1 to Fluid Gradient crosswalk owns the compact many-to-many handoff into present organisation. Model 2 remains the canonical source for the positions and their rows. The bridge stays beside F1 so that there is never a second table of truth.

Two timescales, one explanation

Capacities became available; capacities organise together.

Deep time

Capacities became available

Different capacities have different and overlapping evolutionary histories. Later capacities did not replace earlier ones. They joined, modified and depended upon already living forms of sensing, coordination and action.

Present time

Capacities organise together

X, A, A↔B, B, C, D and Z are positions within a current whole-organism model. They are not geological periods, species levels, brain layers or steps from primitive to advanced.

The central distinction

Evolutionary capacity field: viability sensing, bodily coordination, action, care, communication, learning, prediction, symbolic extension, updating, recovery. Many-to-many participation. Present Fluid organisation: X, A, A↔B, B, C, D, Z.

No one evolutionary capacity belongs to only one position. No position is made from only one evolutionary layer.

Before symbolic language

Embodied, social-affective, multi-channel. Never emotion alone.

Before symbolic language, and still today, social-affective communication could involve vocalisation, tone and rhythm; facial and bodily expression; gaze, gesture and orientation; touch, proximity and withdrawal; coordinated movement and timing; and attention to what another individual attends to. These channels organised approach, avoidance, care, alarm, play, conflict, reassurance and collective action without a spoken explanation.

Not every feeling is primarily communication to another person. Feeling cold makes a thermal condition available and organises movement to shelter. Hunger makes energy need relevant and organises attention to food. Emotional events can make a situation, relationship, loss, opportunity or boundary matter to the organism and alter what becomes available. Language later expanded what humans could do with that information: name, compare with the past, imagine absent and future conditions, negotiate meaning, transmit explanations, question a first interpretation. It did not replace the embodied channels.

Two overlapping problem-spaces

Organism-protection and connection-preservation, and where the condition must change.

Organism-protection is not inherently relational

Preserving tissue integrity, energy, mobility, resources and continued action. The relevant condition may change through the organism's own action, environmental change or practical help. Shelter reduces exposure; food changes energy; distance or an effective boundary changes contact with danger. Another person may be essential in a particular event, but reciprocity is not built into the problem.

Connection-preservation can be relationally contingent

Access to care, belonging, cooperation, information and coordinated action can affect survival and development. When the unresolved condition is another person's availability, recognition, reciprocity, accountability, trust or willingness to repair, one organism cannot produce mutual restoration alone. The other's response is new information and may be part of the condition that needs to change.

Dependent offspring couple the two

For a young child, access to a caregiver may also be access to warmth, nourishment, protection, orientation and the conditions within which recovery becomes possible. Preserving connection can therefore participate directly in organism-protection. This is the developmental handoff into F2.

Three outcomes that must stay separate

Relational restoration: mutual recognition, reciprocity, accountability, trust or repair becomes available. Organism-level updating and Return: the demand changes enough for organisation to release. Return without relational restoration: the relationship stays unavailable or unrepaired, but grief, an effective boundary, revised expectation or reorganisation lets the organism update to that reality. Another nervous system may be necessary for the first; it is not therefore necessary for every form of regulation or Return.

The two problem-spaces overlap: connection can protect the organism; protection may need help from others; caregiving organises one organism to protect another; preserving connection can conflict with self-protection; a relationship can be valued and unsafe; play, exploration, reproduction and collective coordination do not fit either category completely. This is a TEG-Blue synthesis, not a division of emotions into two natural kinds. Position A includes availability, play, exploration and cooperation; it is not the defence of attachment.

Each position is an integrated present configuration in which neural, autonomic, endocrine, immune, metabolic, perceptual, cognitive, motor and relational processes may participate. The labels make differences discussable; they do not name seven biological systems. The row-by-row bridge is the crosswalk.

Emotion within F1

One domain through which conditions become relevant.

1 · Information, not instruction

A feeling may indicate that something matters without proving the cause, establishing the meaning or authorising one response.

2 · The whole organism

Neural, autonomic, endocrine, immune, metabolic, motor, perceptual, cognitive and relational processes may participate. No one chemical, organ or pathway is the emotion.

3 · Signal groups, not sealed systems

Body Signals and Situation Signals identify the condition being registered. Cold and hunger have different initiating conditions from fear, grief or relational uncertainty, but all are embodied, contextual and open to learning and conscious participation.

4 · Fluid is not unlearned, calm or pure

Fluid describes a present-responsive comparison in which information, action and new evidence can still alter organisation.

The detailed account of named emotions, families, functions and updating belongs to the Signal Map. F1 explains why such capacities can participate at all; it does not assign every emotion one evolved purpose, one position or one completion route. Follow group → family → signal and why the members belong together. The family identifies what the signal concerns. Organism-protection and connection-preservation identify overlapping problems that may matter in an event; they do not divide the families into fixed groups.

ESS, CLS and ME

Not an old emotional brain and a new rational brain.

ESS and CLS are equally important, biological, concurrent and recurrent forms of processing. Emotional–somatic capacities have deep histories; so do learning, memory, discrimination, prediction, planning and social inference. Human language and reflective-symbolic modelling expanded particular possibilities, but cognition did not arrive as a detached system built to rule an irrational body. ESS and CLS are educational groupings, not two anatomical systems and not two evolutionary eras. ME is the present participant and must not be projected backwards as a stage or placed above the organism.

The origin account for four modes still holds at the level of capacities: C and D can be read as cognition recruited into an older defensive branch, new routes for the same mobilisation drive. That is TEG-Blue integration, marked as such.

Fluidity, updating and Return

The evolutionary value of a repertoire is not only that it can mobilise but that organisation can remain sensitive to changing evidence: conditions become detectable, significance and priority change, organisation and action availability change, outcomes are sampled, weighting and expectation update, costly demand ends, resources and wider participation become available again. F1 can ask why flexibility, reversibility and recovery would matter to organisms with finite resources. Exact pathways belong to Model 2, Model 3 and the grounding pages.

F1 ends with an available, layered repertoire.

  3. Present information and participationModel 1.
  4. Present organisationModel 2.

F1 supplies the deep-time conditions of possibility. It does not explain every later outcome, and it makes no inference to conduct, coercion, responsibility, morality or repair.

Boundaries and provenance

Retired wording, and the grounding route.

Narrowed or retired

One evolutionary origin per position. "Conservation and shutdown first, mobilisation later" as a settled autonomic phylogeny. Mammals as the inventors of safety through relationship. An infant "borrowing a regulated adult" except as public shorthand beside a precise account. "Cognition, arriving last". Humans communicating through emotion alone before language. "Old hardware in new conditions" as an explanation. "The body protects connection" where it obscures harmful or coercive relationships.
