# F1 and F2 Connections to Four Support Pages

Status: first-pass integration architecture · 20 August 2026

This review asks how F1 and F2 can help explain four existing pages without taking over the questions those pages already answer.

The pages describe present organism organisation from different angles. F1 and F2 sit upstream. They can explain how relevant capacities became available and how participation in them may be shaped over time. They cannot determine a person's present Position, reconstruct a developmental history from current behaviour, or turn a TEG-Blue organising synthesis into an established biological mechanism.

## The connection in one view

```text
F1 · evolutionary availability of capacities
             ↓
F2 · possible developmental shaping of participation
             ↓
present organism organisation in relation to detected or predicted demand
             ├── autonomic coordination
             ├── neurochemical participation
             └── emotional information, access and use
             ↓
Fluid responsiveness or Chronic persistence
```

This is an explanatory order, not a universal causal sequence. F2 is one possible contributor to later organisation. Continuing adversity, recurrent demand, physical conditions, medication, sleep, pain, social context and other present influences may also matter.

## The question each layer owns

| Layer | Question it can answer | Question it must not claim to answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **F1 · Evolutionary Foundations** | How did organisms come to have capacities for viability regulation, sensing, mobilisation, conservation, affective signalling, relationship and reflection? | Which Gradient Position a person occupies, why one named emotion occurs, or whether a TEG-Blue family is an established evolutionary taxonomy. |
| **F2 · Developmental Shaping** | How may repeated experience shape what is noticed, how a signal is understood, whether it can be expressed or acted on, and what consequences are expected? | A person's actual history, the only route to Chronic organisation, or the anatomy and chemistry of a present state. |
| **Four support pages** | What appears to be participating now, viewed through autonomic, neurochemical and emotional lenses? | The full origin of the organism's capacities or a complete explanation of why this particular pattern developed. |
| **Fluid / Chronic distinction** | How responsive, persistent, revisable and able to Return the organisation appears to be in context. | A diagnosis, personality type, moral category or fixed identity. |

F1 therefore explains **availability**. F2 explains possible **shaping of participation**. The support pages explain **present participation**.

## 1. Autonomic Organisation and Pathways

### What the page already owns

This page explains how sympathetic, parasympathetic, enteric, central, endocrine and metabolic processes may coordinate across the fourteen reference organisations. Its most important boundary is already clear: a Position is not one branch of the autonomic nervous system and not one body-wide setting.

### What F1 adds

F1 can explain why the organism has a mixed repertoire rather than a single regulation pathway. Mobilisation, conservation, internal sensing, endocrine coordination and social participation draw on capacities with different evolutionary histories that now work together.

This does not create an evolutionary ladder across the Positions. Chronic XIV is not more ancient than Fluid I, and a sympathetic or parasympathetic pattern does not by itself establish meaning, history or adaptive fit.

### What F2 adds

F2 can explain why cues, anticipated consequences and perceived controllability may change how readily an organisation is recruited. Repeated experience may also shape attention to bodily signals and a person's ability to identify or describe them.

F2 should not be used to say that childhood sets an autonomic profile permanently, that every narrowed response is developmental, or that behaviour reveals which autonomic pathway is active.

### Recommended placement

Add one short **Upstream foundations** block after the page's scope or reading guidance. Do not repeat F1 and F2 inside each of the fourteen Position cards.

Suggested bridge copy:

> This page describes autonomic participation in present organisation. Two upstream frameworks add context without replacing that description. F1 considers how capacities for mobilisation, conservation, internal sensing and relational coordination became available across evolutionary history. F2 considers how repeated experience may shape cue detection, recruitment thresholds, expected consequences and access to bodily information. Neither framework allows a present autonomic pattern to reveal a person's developmental history, and neither maps one Gradient Position to one autonomic branch.

## 2. Emotion Neurochemistry

### What the page already owns

This page asks which chemical mediators may participate in a detected condition and makes a necessary distinction between central and peripheral signalling, contextual variation and different levels of evidence. It also rejects the idea that an emotion has a single chemical fingerprint.

### What F1 adds

F1 can explain why old signalling systems are reused across many functions. Mediators involved in energy allocation, arousal, pain, learning, attachment or recovery were not created for one modern emotion label. Evolutionary continuity supports overlap, not a one-chemical–one-emotion account.

The exact evolutionary history of an individual mediator-emotion relationship remains a research question. F1 should not be used as evidence for every proposed row in the table.

### What F2 adds

F2 can explain how learning and context may change which cues are salient, what is predicted, how readily a response is recruited, and whether bodily changes enter conscious feeling or language. It does not change the identity of the mediator itself, and a mediator measurement cannot reveal trauma, attachment history or the meaning of a current situation.

### Recommended placement

Add a compact bridge after the research rule. Keep claim status attached to the neurochemical entries themselves; F1 and F2 do not raise the evidence level of a hypothesis.

Suggested bridge copy:

> The same mediator can participate in many conditions because biological signalling systems are repeatedly reused and coordinated. F1 places that shared biological repertoire in evolutionary context. F2 adds that experience may shape what is detected, predicted and consciously available when the repertoire is recruited. These frameworks do not give an emotion a unique chemical signature, and chemistry alone cannot identify the meaning or developmental origin of a response.

## 3. Emotions and Feelings · Fluid Gradient

### What the page already owns

This page describes emotion-signals while participation remains present-responsive: what a signal may indicate, what becomes available to awareness, what conditions matter and what may help the organisation update or Return.

### What F1 adds

F1 provides a broad foundation for viability sensing, protective action, resource conservation, social-affective participation and reflective-symbolic capacities. It can support the observation that emotion involves old organism-level problems as well as later social and reflective organisation.

It cannot establish that each named emotion evolved for one purpose, that the current emotion families form a settled evolutionary taxonomy, or that all emotion can be reduced to two survival problems.

### What F2 adds

F2 explains why the same possible signal may be easy to notice and use in one person or context but difficult to identify, express or act on in another. Development may shape participation at several points:

1. what is noticed;
2. what meaning is made of it;
3. whether it can become conscious feeling;
4. whether it can be expressed;
5. whether action or help is expected to be possible;
6. what consequences are anticipated.

This is more precise than saying that development creates or suppresses the emotion itself.

### Language that still needs a boundary

The current page is coherent, but several formulations may sound more settled than the evidence allows:

- **“One condition, one direction”** may suggest a one-to-one code. A signal is better described as bounded information whose meaning depends on bodily state, context, interpretation and time.
- **“The evolved function of each emotion-signal”** can become **“a working functional classification informed by evolutionary and affective research.”**
- **“Two survival problems”** should be labelled as a TEG-Blue organising synthesis, useful for comparison but not presented as an exhaustive scientific taxonomy.
- **“Completion”** should remain a display term paired with more specific processes: change in conditions, proportionate action, recovery, contextual updating, grief, repair or reorganisation. It should not imply one universal discharge sequence.

### Recommended placement

Add a **Two upstream foundations** block before the four guiding questions. Label the functional family structure as TEG-Blue synthesis where it is introduced, rather than repeating caveats in every emotion entry.

Suggested bridge copy:

> Emotion-signals draw on capacities with long biological histories, but present feeling also depends on development, context and interpretation. F1 asks how capacities for sensing, protection, connection and reflection became available. F2 asks how experience may shape access to those capacities: what is noticed, named, expressed, acted on or expected to be safe. The functional groupings on this page are a TEG-Blue working synthesis. They organise comparison; they do not claim that every emotion has one evolved purpose or one required completion route.

## 4. Emotions and Feelings · Chronic Survival

### What the harmonised page has already resolved

The revised page now makes several important distinctions:

- **Chronic Survival** is a display term for far-Chronic reference patterns, not a diagnosis or identity.
- developmental shaping is one route among several;
- repeated reactivation, continuing adverse conditions, interrupted recovery and recurrent narrowing can also contribute;
- the five signal forms are working patterns, not fixed stages or established mechanisms;
- derived-signal relationships are hypotheses rather than proven one-to-one transformations.

These changes make the page substantially more compatible with F1, F2 and the governing premise.

### What F1 adds

F1 contributes only the broad availability of the repertoire: sensing, mobilisation, conservation, affective participation, social regulation and reflection. It does not explain chronicity and should not map far-Chronic Positions to supposedly older evolutionary stages.

### What F2 adds

F2 is more directly relevant here. Repeated relational or environmental consequences may shape whether a signal can be recognised, expressed, acted on or revised. A person may learn that some forms of participation carry costs, while other forms are more likely to preserve connection, reduce conflict or maintain predictability.

That is still a conditional account. Current danger may be real. Physical and social conditions may keep demand present. Repetition may narrow organisation without a single formative episode. F2 should explain a possible history of participation, not become the master cause of every Chronic pattern.

### Remaining connections to clarify

- **“The requirement remains open”** could become: **“Relevant information, need, expected consequence or action problem may remain unresolved or be reconstructed when similar conditions recur.”**
- Rows describing ESS formation or CLS interpretation should be visibly labelled as TEG-Blue reference hypotheses. They should not imply that the exact inner process of a person can be read from a Position.
- Rows that move from feeling into pressure, punishment, coercion or relational control need a hand-off to F3 and the Harm, Power and Repair work. F2 can explain possible shaping; it does not explain the adult maintenance or interpersonal effects of the pattern by itself.
- **“Unresolved load”** should name the process being proposed—continuing demand, recurrent prediction, inhibited action, incomplete recovery, grief, conflict or another condition—rather than act as a mechanism on its own.
- **“Numbness is still a signal”** could become: **“Numbness is meaningful experience and may provide information about present organisation, but it does not identify one mechanism or history.”**
- The footer phrase **“when they can complete”** should point to the more specific language of updating, recovery, repair, reorganisation and Return used elsewhere in the system.

### Recommended placement

Add a short **How F1 and F2 enter this page** block after the vocabulary section. Add explicit hand-offs to F3 and Harm, Power and Repair beside the derived-signal and relational-behaviour material.

Suggested bridge copy:

> F1 provides the broad capacities that can participate in these reference patterns; it does not explain why organisation becomes Chronic. F2 describes one possible route by which repeated experience may shape detection, access, expression, action and expected consequences. Other routes remain possible, including continuing adversity, recurrent demand, physical conditions and interrupted recovery. A present pattern therefore cannot be used to reconstruct a person's history. Where interpretation, self-justification or adult relational behaviour maintains the pattern, the explanation passes from F2 to F3 and to the Harm, Power and Repair layer.

## Cross-page integration rules

1. **Use one bridge per page.** F1 and F2 should orient the reader before the detailed material, not appear as badges in every row.
2. **Keep the levels distinct.** F1 concerns capacity availability; F2 concerns possible shaping; the four pages concern present participation.
3. **Do not infer backwards.** A present feeling, behaviour, mediator or autonomic pattern cannot establish evolutionary purpose or developmental history.
4. **Keep Chronic multicausal.** Developmental learning may contribute, but current conditions and non-developmental routes must remain visible.
5. **Label TEG-Blue synthesis.** Functional families, derived-signal relationships and Position-specific interpretations need claim-status language where they exceed established findings.
6. **Decompose completion.** Where possible, name the actual process: changed conditions, action, recovery, updating, grief, repair, reorganisation or Return.
7. **Hand behaviour onward.** F2 may explain shaped participation. F3 addresses adult interpretation and pattern maintenance. Harm, Power and Repair addresses effects, responsibility, power and relational safety.

## Recommended implementation order

1. Add the short upstream bridge to the autonomic page.
2. Add the short upstream bridge to the neurochemistry page.
3. Bound the Fluid page's functional-family and completion language, then add its bridge.
4. Add the Chronic page bridge and the F3 / Harm, Power and Repair hand-offs; narrow the remaining row-level inferences.
5. Add the resulting connections to the central project map after the page edits have stabilised.

This keeps F1 and F2 visible without making them responsible for everything that follows. They explain where capacities come from and how participation may be shaped. The later pages can then remain focused on what appears to be happening now.
