F12 · The Human Retrospective: Emotional Information, Power and Possibility

How some cultures learned to mistrust emotional and bodily information, what became easier to sustain when those signals were poorly understood, and what may become possible when people and institutions learn to read them more accurately. F12 stands slightly outside the F1 to F11 sequence. It turns around and asks what the whole collection can help us notice about human history and present social organisation.

F12 exposes a regulation question that belongs across all twelve frameworks, Model 1 and the Gradient; the collection does not yet define or trace that thread with enough precision.
Does not establish
One history of humanity, a fall from an emotionally integrated past, proof that agriculture caused disconnection, a body and mind dualism, an account of every culture, a diagnosis of leaders, a claim that emotional awareness guarantees empathy or ethics, or a promise that education will eliminate manipulation or violence.

The decision

Keep F12 as the reflective framework; replace the species-wide disconnection story with a testable, plural and forward-looking inquiry.

"Retrospective ESS–CLS Disconnection and Cultural CLS Elevation" told one story about how a whole species ended up running one arrangement. The original framework does identify a consequential pattern: influential strands of Western philosophy, religion, science, law, administration and organisational life have often treated reason as detached, trustworthy and properly human while treating emotion as irrational, private, feminine, primitive, disruptive or in need of control. Those traditions were neither universal nor internally consistent. They existed alongside philosophical, cultural, clinical, artistic, spiritual and Indigenous traditions that understood bodily and relational knowledge differently.

The biological processes involved in affect, interoception, motivation, attention and action did not disappear when a culture distrusted them. They continued participating in organism organisation. What could change was conscious access, language, interpretation, permission, response and whose account was accepted as knowledge. F12 asks what followed, and what could now change.

Collection-wide dependency

The regulation thread is not yet complete.

A warning F12 carries for the whole collection

Consumption, status, control, overwork, appeasement, avoidance, dissociation and domination can sometimes reduce pressure, preserve belonging, prevent contact with pain, restore a sense of certainty or secure an immediate outcome. They must not therefore be grouped casually as "ways humans regulate". The word can hide organism-level resource allocation, emotion regulation as influence on experience or expression, interpersonal and social regulation, a learned behaviour that produces short-term relief, pressure reduction through compliance, control or avoidance, completion, updating and Return after a condition changes, and imposed regulation in which another person carries the cost. These are not interchangeable.

The later regulation pass should carry one sequence through F1 to F12: detected or predicted condition; organism-level organisation and available information; conscious access, interpretation and organising pressure; possible action routes; short-term pressure change or relief; cost to self, other, relationship and system; evidence of completion, persistence, updating or recurrence. F12 can establish why this pass is needed and preserve its boundaries. It should not pretend the missing architecture has already been completed.

The question F12 owns

How did some societies learn to mistrust emotional and bodily information, what followed, and what may become possible?

How did some human societies learn to mistrust emotional and bodily information, what followed when those signals continued operating without being adequately understood, and what may become possible when people and institutions learn to read them more accurately?

F12 owns questions about

Where and when emotion became associated with irrationality, weakness, disorder or lesser authority; which embodied, relational, local, oral or experiential knowledge was subordinated or excluded; how institutions selected for detachment, certainty, productivity, obedience, domination or control; how emotional and bodily processes continued when access or permission was constrained; which behaviours reduced pressure without resolving the condition; how personal protection routes were rewarded, normalised or scaled through roles and institutions; how strength, safety, dominance, confidence, competence, care and leadership became confused; how emotional, nervous-system and power literacy might change recognition and collective tolerance; what present evidence shows humans can learn, differentiate, revise and organise differently; how technology supports education and inquiry while also increasing surveillance, classification and manipulation.

Three movements, and what F12 does not own

Look back: how particular cultures, institutions and knowledge traditions distinguished reason from emotion and assigned them unequal authority. Look around: what personal, relational and institutional patterns became easier to sustain when emotional and bodily information was hard to access, name, test or act upon. Look forward: what becomes possible when emotional information, reflective cognition, evidence, technology, responsibility and institutional protection work together. F12 does not own one history of humanity, a fall from an integrated past, proof that agriculture caused disconnection, a body and mind dualism, an account of every culture, a diagnosis of leaders, or a promise that education will eliminate manipulation or violence.

The governing separation

Reconnect emotional information with reflective reasoning; do not reverse the hierarchy.

Six distinctions F12 preserves

Emotion participates in biological information; it is not an infallible verdict. Limited conscious access does not mean the underlying processes stopped operating. Short-term relief or pressure reduction is not completion, safety, repair or ethical acceptability. Behaviour rewarded by an institution is not behaviour required for effective leadership. Understanding another person's emotions is not caring about or protecting their welfare. Greater emotional and nervous-system literacy is not immunity from manipulation.

F12 should reconnect emotional information with reflective reasoning. It should not place feeling above evidence.

Look back

The history begins with a question, not a verdict.

How did some cultures learn to distrust or subordinate emotional and bodily information? The record does support traditions in which reason was elevated against passion or emotion. It also shows repeated disagreement over what emotion is, whether it helps or obstructs judgement, how it relates to the body, and which emotions or expressions are acceptable for whom. The category "emotion" itself has a history; its present English-language psychological meaning should not be projected unchanged into Classical philosophy, medieval theology, non-Western languages or prehistoric societies. F12 needs histories of concepts, practices and institutions, not a search for one moment when humanity "stopped listening".

Possible historical pathways

Philosophical and theological distinctions among reason, passion, virtue, appetite and self-command; gendered, racialised, classed and colonial assignments of rationality and emotionality; writing, quantification, bureaucracy and law privileging what can be recorded and standardised; medical and psychological classification of acceptable and disordered feeling; industrial requirements for time discipline, productivity and emotional presentation; scientific methods that protect inquiry from individual impression while sometimes treating situated knowledge as noise; colonisation and the suppression, appropriation or reclassification of Indigenous and local knowledge; organisational cultures that reward certainty, output and control while concealing dependence, uncertainty and impact. These routes may interact. None is the history of all cultures.

Agriculture is a possible condition, not the single cause

Settlement, storage, property, inheritance, population density, labour specialisation, warfare, administration and control of land or surplus may alter what people can leave, refuse or reorganise. Agriculture can participate in some of these changes without being necessary or sufficient for hierarchy. The old map's example, that when survival depends on staying the signal to leave may become difficult to act upon, works as a possible human situation, not a historical law. People may stay because of care, season, land, dependence, coercion, identity, ecology, danger elsewhere or many conditions at once. Mobile foragers vary and should not be treated as living fossils, uniformly egalitarian, emotionally integrated or representative of the Pleistocene.

The Y-chromosome bottleneck cannot carry the cultural argument

Post-Neolithic reductions in Y-chromosome lineage diversity indicate a demographic pattern requiring explanation; models involving patrilineal kin groups and competition are one proposed route. The genetic pattern does not show what individuals felt, that cultures distrusted embodied information, that men or women had one psychological organisation, that domination began at that point, that one social system existed across regions, or that a change in lineage diversity caused the later authority of abstract reasoning. It can remain one bounded clue about social organisation. It cannot become proof of F12.

Look around

The emotional and somatic system kept operating.

A culture can discourage emotional language without stopping affective, interoceptive, motivational or autonomic processes. Information may continue influencing salience and attention, approach, avoidance and action readiness, memory and prediction, bodily state and perceived urgency, social orientation and belonging, judgement and decision-making, and what becomes easier or harder to notice consciously. TEG-Blue names part of this work ESS, the Emotional–Somatic System, an educational and functional model rather than a standard neuroscientific system. CLS, the Cognitive–Logical System, is not disembodied reason; reflective, symbolic and linguistic processing is also biological, emotionally influenced and recurrently connected with the rest of the organism.

Emotional and bodily information can continue shaping organisation and action even when the person or culture has limited language, permission or conscious access for understanding it. This differs from saying the information "moved below awareness" as one historical event. Much organism-level processing is ordinarily outside awareness. What varies is which signals become accessible, differentiated, interpreted, trusted and usable by ME.

  1. ConditionWhat internal, relational, material or institutional demand was present?
  2. Information and accessWhat registered, and what could the person notice, name or interpret?
  3. Organising pressureWhat appeared urgent, necessary, threatened or unavailable?
  4. BehaviourWhat did the person actually do?
  5. Immediate consequenceWhat pressure reduced, what access returned or what outcome was secured?
  6. Transferred costWho carried the labour, fear, deprivation, restriction or recovery cost?
  7. Longer courseDid the condition resolve, persist, recur, escalate or change form?
  8. Responsibility and repairWhat happened when impact, refusal or corrective information arrived?

Unread signals do not become one kind of behaviour, and relief can preserve the cycle

When pain, fear, shame, grief, fatigue, loneliness, anger or belonging threat is difficult to recognise or act upon, people may find other routes that change the immediate experience or the surrounding conditions: consumption, work, achievement, status, explanation, avoidance, reassurance, appeasement, withdrawal, dissociation, surveillance, control or domination. F12 should not infer the route from the feeling or the feeling from the route. A behaviour can produce real relief without resolving the condition that recruited it. Overwork may delay contact with grief while increasing exhaustion; appeasement may end immediate conflict while leaving refusal unsafe; control may reduce uncertainty for one person by narrowing another person's agency; consumption may alter feeling briefly without addressing pain, deprivation or isolation. Immediate relief is evidence of an outcome. It is not proof that the behaviour was adaptive overall, that the signal was correctly interpreted or that nobody else paid the cost.

Look forward

What becomes possible when the information is readable.

F12 needs more than a vocabulary of feelings. Emotional literacy names, differentiates and locates affective experience, but a feeling is information, not proof of cause, fact or entitlement. Bodily and interoceptive literacy notices changing internal signals, though strong sensation does not provide one correct interpretation. Nervous-system literacy shows how organisation may alter salience, cognition, action readiness and access, while a Gradient Position does not diagnose character or excuse behaviour. Relational literacy distinguishes self and other, request and demand, connection and compliance, repair and restored access, without creating a duty to remain connected. Power literacy sees asymmetry, viable refusal, coercive cost, credibility and transferred burden; unequal power is not automatically abuse. Evidence literacy separates feeling, interpretation, factual claim, uncertainty and revision; data do not become neutral by being formalised. Technology literacy understands how tools measure, classify, persuade, connect, amplify and extract; a system's confidence score is not direct access to another person's inner state.

Together these literacies help distinguish

Hurt from harm; confidence from wisdom; authority from care; protection from control; quiet from safety; compliance from consent; explanation from exoneration; emotional resonance from accurate understanding; understanding from concern; immediate relief from completion; leadership from domination.

A less manipulable society is a direction, not a guaranteed result

Learning the Two Biological Information Systems model and the Gradient may help people notice when urgency, shame, fear, belonging threat, relief or certainty is influencing what seems true and what action seems available. The evidence supports a bounded possibility: emotion knowledge can improve some forms of differentiation, social and emotional learning can improve specified outcomes, and training that explains common misinformation techniques can improve some measures of discernment. Emotional intelligence alone does not reliably create accurate deception detection, and understanding another person's feelings can also support instrumental influence. The stronger pathway is collective: personal access and differentiation, shared language for signals, behaviour, power and evidence, safe contradiction, corroboration and viable refusal, transparent decisions and accountable authority, and so fewer rewards and smaller option fields for manipulation. Literacy helps; collective conditions determine how usable it becomes.

Knowledge and technology expand possibility without guaranteeing direction

Humans now have methods for studying physiology, development, learning, relationships, institutions and history at resolutions and scales unavailable to earlier periods. The same tools can infer, target, rank, persuade, monitor and restrict. Emotion-recognition systems may assign internal states beyond what the evidence supports; platforms may learn which signals hold attention without helping people understand why; a language of nervous systems can be used for care or for more efficient behavioural control. Of every new tool F12 asks: what does it measure, what does it infer beyond the measurement, who chose the categories, whose language, body and culture shaped the training data, who can inspect, correct or refuse the inference, who benefits, what happens when it is wrong, and does it expand agency or make behaviour easier to manage from outside? Technology increases capacity. It does not choose the purpose.

The evidence for "we can do better"

F12 should not promise that knowledge produces moral progress. It can show that relevant capacities are learnable and institutions are changeable. People can acquire emotion concepts, improve some forms of differentiation, learn alternative responses, revise interpretations and develop practices for checking impact. Groups can change rules, distribute authority differently, protect reporting, make decisions reviewable and measure outcomes older systems ignored. Cultures already contain emotional, relational and ecological knowledge that survived despite being devalued. This is evidence of possibility, not inevitability. Whether more coordinated access becomes care, manipulation, avoidance, protection or repair still depends on behaviour, power, choice, feedback and consequence.

The Filter of Worth as an institutional selection corridor

Can the filter also describe how institutions select who looks suitable for authority?

F5 retains Filter of Worth as a recognition label for the confusion through which social access, recognition or protection is treated as evidence of inherent human value, while exclusion is treated as evidence of inadequacy. F12 adds a retrospective question: can the filter also describe how institutions repeatedly select which presentations and behaviours look suitable for authority?

  1. Institutional criteria, incentives and existing powerSome presentations and behaviours receive reward.
  2. Reward increases status, credibility, resources and decision accessPossibly rewarded: confident self-presentation, competitive persistence, emotional restraint, availability for overwork, tolerance for imposing costs, loyalty to existing rules, strategic relationship-building, rapid decisive action.
  3. Disconfirming information is filtered out or made costlyPeople and practices that carry it lose access.
  4. Those who advance reproduce the criteriaA working TEG-Blue hypothesis, not an established mechanism.

The meaning of each rewarded feature depends on context. Emotional restraint may reflect skill, culture, fear, role discipline or concealment; decisiveness may protect a group or close revision; confidence may reflect competence or exceed evidence. Research provides bounded support: narcissistic characteristics relate more consistently to leadership emergence than to effectiveness; psychopathic tendencies show on average only a weak positive association with emergence and negative associations with effectiveness-related outcomes; dominant leaders may become more attractive under some experiences of conflict or threat, while prestige, expertise, generosity and group commitment provide other routes to influence. This does not establish that emotionally aware people rarely reach the top, or that leaders can be classified by empathy from their role. It supports examining selection separately from effectiveness. F12 observes advancement criteria, who nominates and evaluates, which behaviours create visibility, whether care and corrective work remain invisible, what happens to people who refuse harmful norms, whether confidence is checked against accuracy, whether effectiveness includes effects on less powerful people, whether contradiction stays safe after authority is gained, whether the person revises when impact becomes visible, and how the institution responds when control produces results and harm at once. The Filter of Worth is a visible selection pattern, not a claim about the private emotional life of every person who advances.

When society confuses strength with safety

Strength, safety, domination and leadership answer different questions.

TermWhat it may describeWhat it does not establish
StrengthCapacity to tolerate demand, protect, persist, recover, tell the truth or use power with restraintInvulnerability, certainty, emotional distance or entitlement to decide for others
SafetyConditions in which threat, deprivation and coercive cost are sufficiently contained and viable agency remainsQuiet, obedience, absence of complaint or one leader's sense of control
DominationInfluence or priority secured through threat, force, intimidation, submission or another power advantage, as observable conductCompetence, legitimacy, wisdom or collective safety
LeadershipBounded responsibility for direction, coordination, care, decision or representationGreater human worth, emotional health or unrestricted authority
ConfidenceExpressed certainty or willingness to actAccuracy, knowledge, concern or openness to correction
DetachmentReduced visible emotion or deliberate distance from immediate feelingNeutrality, rationality, resilience or absence of organising pressure

Domination can resemble safety from the position of the person whose uncertainty decreases when others submit. It can resemble strength to observers who see confidence and control but not the restricted option field or the transferred cost. F12 does not infer domination from style. It examines what happens to refusal, evidence, power, impact and revision.

How F12 connects to the whole, and its handoffs

F12 closes the first review cycle by handing questions back through the collection.

F1 supplies the biological background of affective, interoceptive, cognitive, relational and learning capacities, without one original social order or cultural destiny. F2 shows children learning emotion concepts, expression rules and self and other distinctions, which is not the entire origin of a cultural hierarchy. F3 shows people building explanations around what they can access; cognition is not the enemy. F4 shows institutions formalising which expressions, evidence and roles are acceptable; rules are not collective nervous systems. F5 lends the Filter of Worth as a decomposed recognition label. F6 shows categories and authority shaping credibility; literacy does not remove bias. F7 shows control rewarded as decisiveness while affected people carry cost; harm is established through behaviour, power and impact. F8 explains why reconnection matters without owning recovery or Return. F9 asks whose information looks legible; neurodivergence is not greater embodiment or lesser rationality. F10 traces continuity across generations without biological inheritance of disconnection. F11 keeps historical routes, levels and evidence types distinct. Model 1's coordinated conscious access is not a truth detector. No Gradient Position corresponds to a culture, period, political system or moral stage. Harm, Power and Repair keeps understanding, concern, behaviour, coercion, impact, feedback and repair apart.

To future historical research

Replace the single retrospective story with route-specific, cross-cultural and participatory inquiry whose evidence status remains visible.

The reflective loop runs: historical knowledge hierarchy; uneven language, permission and conscious access; protection routes that may reduce pressure or transfer cost; social reward, institutional selection and reproduced authority; normalised confusion among confidence, control, safety and leadership; literacy, evidence, viable refusal and institutional revision; and back into a new regulation pass through F1 to F12.

Grounding fields

Affective science; interoception, psychophysiology and neuroscience; cognitive science and judgement research; emotion-regulation science; interpersonal and social-regulation research; developmental psychology and education research; history of emotions, ideas and medicine; history and philosophy of science; historical sociology and sociology of knowledge; anthropology and cultural psychology; archaeology and bioarchaeology; archaeogenetics and population genetics; cultural-evolution research; political economy and labour history; organisational science and leadership research; sociology of status, stigma and inequality; political science, law and public administration; communication, persuasion and misinformation research; epistemology and philosophy of language; feminist epistemology and gender studies; Indigenous, decolonial and postcolonial scholarship; disability studies and neurodiversity research; clinical psychology, psychiatry and trauma research; behavioural economics and consumer research; human-computer interaction, AI ethics and technology studies; public health and implementation science; participatory, survivor-led and lived-experience research; measurement science and causal inference. No discipline can reconstruct the retrospective alone. Priority evidence is organised by route, time and level: historical claims need specified archives, concepts, populations and competing explanations; biological claims need defined processes and measures; institutional claims need selection criteria, incentives, decision access, behaviour, impact and response to feedback; educational claims need measured learning, transfer, duration, population and adverse or instrumental use.

Evidence status of the original source list

Dual-process theories do not validate ESS and CLS as two biological systems; their use here is analogy, not mechanism. Cultural evolution and gene and culture coevolution do not establish that culture outran a fixed brain. Archaeogenetic evidence reveals demographic patterns, not inner experience. The post-Neolithic Y-chromosome bottleneck is not evidence of emotional suppression or one beginning of patriarchy. Forager levelling research identifies important arrangements, but foragers vary and are not unchanged ancestral populations. Affective-primacy research does not show that feeling is always first or more truthful. Somatic-marker theory has a debated evidence base and cannot carry the claim that ESS gives accurate instructions which CLS overrides. Subcortical threat-processing research supports multiple routes; "low-road" simplifications should not divide emotion from thought. Interoception is not identical to emotion, self-knowledge or coordinated conscious access. Histories of emotion are interpretations of uneven archives. Leadership research cannot reveal one private regulation strategy or establish harm. Emotional-learning research does not show that literacy guarantees ethical behaviour or freedom from manipulation. F12 is a TEG-Blue historical and ethical synthesis built from narrower evidence families, and it should remain visibly revisable.
