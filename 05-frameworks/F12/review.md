## Decision

**Keep F12 as the reflective Foundational Framework, but replace a species-wide story of ESS–CLS disconnection with a testable, plural and forward-looking inquiry into emotional information, cultural authority, institutional selection and human possibility.**

Provisional title:

> **The Human Retrospective: Emotional Information, Power and Possibility**

Suggested explanatory line:

> How some cultures learned to mistrust emotional and bodily information, what became easier to sustain when those signals were poorly understood, and what may become possible when people and institutions learn to read them more accurately.

F12 stands slightly outside the F1–F11 sequence. It turns around and asks what the whole collection can help us notice about human history and present social organisation.

The original framework identifies a consequential pattern. Influential strands of Western philosophy, religion, science, law, administration and organisational life have often treated reason as detached, trustworthy and properly human while treating emotion as irrational, private, feminine, primitive, disruptive or in need of control. These traditions were neither universal nor internally consistent. They existed alongside philosophical, cultural, clinical, artistic, spiritual and Indigenous traditions that understood bodily and relational knowledge differently.

The biological processes involved in affect, interoception, motivation, attention and action did not disappear when a culture distrusted them. They continued participating in organism organisation. What could change was conscious access, language, interpretation, permission, response and whose account was accepted as knowledge.

F12 asks what followed—and what could now change.

## Collection-wide dependency: the regulation thread is not yet complete

> **Warning: F12 exposes a regulation question that belongs across all twelve Foundational Frameworks, Model 1 and the Nervous System Gradient. The present collection does not yet define or trace that thread with enough precision.**

Consumption, status, control, overwork, appeasement, avoidance, dissociation and domination can sometimes reduce pressure, preserve belonging, prevent contact with pain, restore a sense of certainty or secure an immediate outcome. They must not therefore be grouped casually as “ways humans regulate.” The word can hide several different processes:

- organism-level resource allocation in relation to demand;
- emotion regulation as influence on emotional experience or expression;
- interpersonal and social regulation involving another person;
- a learned behaviour that produces short-term relief;
- pressure reduction through compliance, control or avoidance;
- completion, updating and Return after a condition changes;
- imposed regulation in which another person carries the cost.

These are not interchangeable.

The later regulation pass should carry the following sequence through F1–F12:

```text
detected or predicted condition
        ↓
organism-level organisation and available information
        ↓
conscious access, interpretation and organising pressure
        ↓
possible action routes
        ↓
short-term pressure change or relief
        ↓
cost to self, other, relationship and system
        ↓
evidence of completion, persistence, updating or recurrence
```

F12 can establish why this pass is needed and preserve its boundaries. It should not pretend the missing architecture has already been completed.

## The question F12 owns

> How did some human societies learn to mistrust emotional and bodily information, what followed when those signals continued operating without being adequately understood, and what may become possible when people and institutions learn to read them more accurately?

F12 owns three movements:

1. **Look back:** How did particular cultures, institutions and knowledge traditions distinguish reason from emotion and assign them unequal authority?
2. **Look around:** What personal, relational and institutional patterns became easier to sustain when emotional and bodily information was difficult to access, name, test or act upon?
3. **Look forward:** What becomes possible when emotional information, reflective cognition, evidence, technology, responsibility and institutional protection can work together?

It owns questions concerning:

- where and when emotion became associated with irrationality, weakness, disorder or lesser authority;
- which kinds of embodied, relational, local, oral or experiential knowledge were subordinated or excluded;
- how institutions selected for detachment, certainty, productivity, obedience, dominance or control;
- how emotional and bodily processes continued when conscious access or social permission was constrained;
- which behaviours reduced pressure without resolving the underlying condition;
- how personal protection routes could be rewarded, normalised or scaled through roles and institutions;
- how strength, safety, dominance, confidence, competence, care and leadership became confused;
- how emotional literacy, nervous-system literacy and power literacy might change recognition and collective tolerance;
- what present evidence shows humans can learn, differentiate, revise and organise institutions differently;
- how technology can support education and inquiry while also increasing surveillance, classification and manipulation.

It does not own one history of humanity, a fall from an emotionally integrated past, proof that agriculture caused disconnection, a body–mind dualism, an account of every culture, a diagnosis of leaders, a claim that emotional awareness guarantees empathy or ethics, or a promise that education will eliminate manipulation or violence.

## The governing separation

F12 needs to preserve six distinctions:

```text
emotion participates in biological information
        ≠ emotion is an infallible verdict

limited conscious access
        ≠ the underlying processes stopped operating

short-term relief or pressure reduction
        ≠ completion, safety, repair or ethical acceptability

behaviour rewarded by an institution
        ≠ behaviour required for effective leadership

understanding another person's emotions
        ≠ caring about or protecting their welfare

greater emotional and nervous-system literacy
        ≠ immunity from manipulation
```

F12 should reconnect emotional information with reflective reasoning. It should not reverse the hierarchy and place feeling above evidence.

## Look back: how some cultures learned to distrust emotional and bodily information

### The history begins with a question, not a verdict

F12 should begin here:

> **How did some cultures learn to distrust or subordinate emotional and bodily information?**

The historical record does support traditions in which reason was elevated against passion or emotion. It also shows repeated disagreement over what emotion is, whether it helps or obstructs judgement, how it relates to the body and which emotions or expressions are acceptable for whom.

The category “emotion” itself has a history. Its present English-language psychological meaning should not be projected unchanged into Classical philosophy, medieval theology, non-Western languages or prehistoric societies. F12 therefore needs histories of concepts, practices and institutions rather than a search for one moment when humanity “stopped listening.”

Useful historical pathways may include:

- philosophical and theological distinctions among reason, passion, virtue, appetite and self-command;
- gendered, racialised, classed and colonial assignments of rationality and emotionality;
- writing, quantification, bureaucracy and law privileging information that can be recorded and standardised;
- medical and psychological classification of acceptable and disordered feeling;
- industrial requirements for time discipline, productivity and emotional presentation;
- scientific methods that protect inquiry from individual impression while sometimes treating situated or experiential knowledge as noise;
- colonisation and the suppression, appropriation or reclassification of Indigenous and local knowledge;
- organisational cultures that reward certainty, output and control while concealing dependence, uncertainty and impact.

These routes may interact. None should be treated as the history of all cultures.

### Agriculture is a possible condition, not the single cause

Settlement, storage, property, inheritance, population density, labour specialisation, warfare, administration and control of land or surplus may alter what people can leave, refuse or reorganise. Agriculture can participate in some of these changes without being necessary or sufficient for hierarchy.

The source map's example—when survival depends on staying, the signal to leave may become difficult to act upon—works as a possible human situation. It is not a historical law. People may stay because of care, season, land, dependence, coercion, identity, ecology, danger elsewhere or many conditions at once.

Research on foraging societies also shows variation. Mobile foragers should not be treated as living fossils, uniformly egalitarian, emotionally integrated or representative of the Pleistocene.

### The Y-chromosome bottleneck cannot carry the cultural argument

Post-Neolithic reductions in Y-chromosome lineage diversity indicate a demographic pattern requiring explanation. Models involving patrilineal kin groups and competition are one proposed route. The genetic pattern does not show:

- what individuals felt;
- that cultures distrusted embodied information;
- that men or women had one psychological organisation;
- that domination began at that point;
- that one social system existed across regions;
- that a change in lineage diversity caused the later authority of abstract reasoning.

It can remain one bounded clue about social organisation. It cannot become proof of F12.

## Look around: what continued when emotional information was poorly understood

### The emotional–somatic system kept operating

A culture can discourage emotional language without stopping affective, interoceptive, motivational or autonomic processes. Information may continue influencing:

- salience and attention;
- approach, avoidance and action readiness;
- memory and prediction;
- bodily state and perceived urgency;
- social orientation and belonging;
- judgement and decision-making;
- what becomes easier or harder to notice consciously.

TEG-Blue names part of this work **ESS**, the Emotional–Somatic System. ESS is an educational and functional model, not a standard neuroscientific system. **CLS**, the Cognitive–Logical System, is not disembodied reason. Reflective, symbolic and linguistic processing is also biological, emotionally influenced and recurrently connected with the rest of the organism.

The responsible claim is therefore:

> **Emotional and bodily information can continue shaping organisation and action even when the person or culture has limited language, permission or conscious access for understanding it.**

This is different from saying that the information “moved below awareness” as one historical event. Much organism-level processing is ordinarily outside awareness. What varies is which signals become accessible, differentiated, interpreted, trusted and usable by ME.

### Unread signals do not become one kind of behaviour

When pain, fear, shame, grief, fatigue, loneliness, anger or belonging threat is difficult to recognise or act upon, people may find other routes that change the immediate experience or surrounding conditions. Possible routes include consumption, work, achievement, status, explanation, avoidance, reassurance, appeasement, withdrawal, dissociation, surveillance, control or domination.

F12 should not infer the route from the feeling or the feeling from the route.

| Inquiry plane | Question |
| --- | --- |
| **Condition** | What internal, relational, material or institutional demand was present? |
| **Information and access** | What registered, and what could the person notice, name or interpret? |
| **Organising pressure** | What appeared urgent, necessary, threatened or unavailable? |
| **Behaviour** | What did the person actually do? |
| **Immediate consequence** | What pressure reduced, what access returned or what outcome was secured? |
| **Transferred cost** | Who carried the labour, fear, deprivation, restriction or recovery cost? |
| **Longer course** | Did the condition resolve, persist, recur, escalate or change form? |
| **Responsibility and repair** | What happened when impact, refusal or corrective information arrived? |

This is the regulation bridge F12 hands back to all earlier frameworks.

### Pressure reduction can preserve the cycle

A behaviour can produce real relief without resolving the condition that recruited it. Overwork may delay contact with grief while increasing exhaustion. Appeasement may end immediate conflict while leaving refusal unsafe. Control may reduce uncertainty for one person by narrowing another person's agency. Consumption may alter feeling briefly without addressing pain, deprivation or isolation.

Immediate relief is evidence of an outcome. It is not proof that the behaviour was adaptive overall, that the underlying signal was correctly interpreted or that nobody else paid the cost.

## The Filter of Worth as an institutional selection corridor

F5 retains **Filter of Worth** as a TEG-Blue recognition label. It names the confusion through which social access, recognition or protection is treated as evidence of inherent human value, while exclusion is treated as evidence of inadequacy.

F12 adds a retrospective question: can the filter also describe how institutions repeatedly select which presentations and behaviours look suitable for authority?

The working TEG-Blue hypothesis is:

```text
institutional criteria, incentives and existing power
        ↓
some presentations and behaviours receive reward
        ↓
reward increases status, credibility, resources and decision access
        ↓
people and practices that carry disconfirming information are filtered out or made costly
        ↓
those who advance gain more power to reproduce the criteria
```

Possible rewarded features may include confident self-presentation, competitive persistence, emotional restraint, availability for overwork, tolerance for imposing costs, loyalty to existing rules, strategic relationship-building or rapid decisive action. Their meaning depends on context. Emotional restraint may reflect skill, culture, fear, role discipline or concealment. Decisiveness may protect a group or close revision. Confidence may reflect competence or exceed evidence.

Research provides bounded support for parts of this question. Narcissistic characteristics show a more consistent relation with leadership emergence than leadership effectiveness. Psychopathic tendencies show, on average, only a weak positive association with leadership emergence and negative associations with effectiveness-related outcomes. Dominant leaders may become more attractive under some experiences of conflict or threat, while prestige, expertise, generosity and group commitment provide other routes to influence.

This evidence does not establish that emotionally aware people rarely reach the top or that leaders can be classified by empathy from their role. It does support examining selection separately from effectiveness and asking which institutional conditions reward one route over another.

F12 should observe:

- formal and informal advancement criteria;
- who nominates, sponsors and evaluates;
- which behaviours create visibility and status;
- whether care work and corrective work remain invisible;
- what happens to people who refuse harmful norms;
- whether confidence is checked against accuracy;
- whether leadership effectiveness includes effects on less powerful people;
- whether contradiction remains safe after authority is gained;
- whether the person revises when their impact becomes visible;
- how the institution responds when control produces results and harm at once.

The Filter of Worth is most useful here as a visible selection pattern, not a claim about the private emotional life of every person who advances.

## When society confuses strength with safety

Strength, safety, dominance and leadership answer different questions.

| Term | What it may describe | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Strength** | Capacity to tolerate demand, protect, persist, recover, tell the truth or use power with restraint | Invulnerability, certainty, emotional distance or entitlement to decide for others |
| **Safety** | Conditions in which threat, deprivation and coercive cost are sufficiently contained and viable agency remains | Quiet, obedience, absence of complaint or one leader's sense of control |
| **Dominance** | Influence or priority secured through threat, force, intimidation, submission or another power advantage | Competence, legitimacy, wisdom or collective safety |
| **Leadership** | Bounded responsibility for direction, coordination, care, decision or representation | Greater human worth, emotional health or unrestricted authority |
| **Confidence** | Expressed certainty or willingness to act | Accuracy, knowledge, concern or openness to correction |
| **Detachment** | Reduced visible emotion or deliberate distance from immediate feeling | Neutrality, rationality, resilience or absence of organising pressure |

Dominance can resemble safety from the position of the person whose uncertainty decreases when others submit. It can resemble strength to observers who see confidence and control but not the restricted option field or transferred cost.

F12 should not infer domination from style. It should examine what happens to refusal, evidence, power, impact and revision.

## Look forward: what becomes possible when the information is readable

### Emotional literacy is part of a wider literacy

F12 needs more than a vocabulary of feelings. The forward path joins several forms of learning:

| Literacy | What it makes more available | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Emotional literacy** | Naming, differentiating and locating affective experience and its possible meaning | A feeling is information, not proof of cause, fact or entitlement |
| **Bodily and interoceptive literacy** | Noticing changing internal signals, limits, activation, fatigue and need | Strong sensation does not provide one correct interpretation |
| **Nervous-system literacy** | Understanding how organisation may alter salience, cognition, action readiness and access | A Gradient Position does not diagnose character or excuse behaviour |
| **Relational literacy** | Distinguishing self and other, request and demand, connection and compliance, repair and restored access | Relationship language cannot create a duty to remain connected |
| **Power literacy** | Seeing asymmetry, viable refusal, coercive cost, credibility and transferred burden | Unequal power is not automatically abuse; its use must be examined |
| **Evidence literacy** | Separating feeling, interpretation, factual claim, uncertainty and revision | Data and procedure do not become neutral merely because they are formalised |
| **Technology literacy** | Understanding how tools measure, classify, persuade, connect, amplify and extract | A system's confidence score is not direct access to another person's inner state |

Together, these literacies can help people distinguish:

- hurt from harm;
- confidence from wisdom;
- authority from care;
- protection from control;
- quiet from safety;
- compliance from consent;
- explanation from exoneration;
- emotional resonance from accurate understanding;
- understanding from concern;
- immediate relief from completion;
- leadership from domination.

### A less manipulable society is a direction, not a guaranteed result

Learning the Two Biological Information Systems model and the Nervous System Gradient may help people notice when urgency, shame, fear, belonging threat, relief or certainty is influencing what seems true and what action seems available. Shared language may make some manipulative patterns easier to name before they become normal.

The evidence supports a bounded possibility. Emotion knowledge can improve some forms of emotional differentiation. Social and emotional learning can improve specified social, emotional and educational outcomes. Training that explains common misinformation techniques can improve some measures of discernment. Emotional intelligence alone, however, does not reliably create accurate deception detection, and understanding another person's feelings can also support instrumental influence.

The stronger pathway is collective:

```text
personal access and differentiation
        ↓
shared language for signals, behaviour, power and evidence
        ↓
safe contradiction, corroboration and viable refusal
        ↓
transparent decisions and accountable authority
        ↓
fewer rewards and smaller option fields for manipulation
```

Manipulation becomes harder to hide when people can compare accounts, retain access to one another, recognise coercive pressure, check evidence, refuse without punishment and rely on institutions that respond. Literacy helps. Collective conditions determine how usable that literacy becomes.

### Knowledge and technology expand possibility without guaranteeing direction

Humans now have methods for studying physiology, development, learning, relationships, institutions and history at forms of resolution and scale unavailable to earlier periods. Digital tools can distribute education, support repeated self-observation, connect dispersed knowledge and reveal patterns across large datasets.

The same tools can infer, target, rank, persuade, monitor and restrict. Emotion-recognition systems may assign internal states from faces, voices or physiological proxies beyond what the evidence supports. Platforms may learn which signals hold attention without helping people understand why. A language of nervous systems can be used for care or for more efficient behavioural control.

F12 should therefore ask of every new tool:

- What does it actually measure?
- What does it infer beyond the measurement?
- Who chose the categories?
- Whose language, body and culture shaped the training data?
- Who can inspect, correct or refuse the inference?
- Who benefits from the prediction?
- What happens when it is wrong?
- Does the tool expand agency or make behaviour easier to manage from outside?

Technology increases capacity. It does not choose the purpose.

### The evidence for “we can do better”

F12 should not promise that knowledge produces moral progress. It can show evidence that relevant human capacities are learnable and institutions are changeable.

People can acquire emotion concepts, improve some forms of differentiation, learn alternative responses, revise interpretations and develop practices for checking impact. Groups can change rules, distribute authority differently, protect reporting, make decision processes reviewable and measure outcomes that older systems ignored. Cultures already contain many examples of emotional, relational and ecological knowledge that survived despite being devalued by dominant institutions.

This is evidence of possibility, not inevitability.

The TEG-Blue proposition is that more coordinated access to emotional–somatic information and reflective cognition may widen conscious participation. Whether that access becomes care, manipulation, avoidance, protection or repair still depends on behaviour, power, choice, feedback and consequence.

## Evidence status of the original source list

- **Dual-process theories** distinguish different forms of reasoning or processing under specified models. They do not validate ESS and CLS as two biological systems. Their use in F12 is analogy, not mechanism.
- **Cultural evolution and gene–culture coevolution** support studying how learned practices, institutions and biology interact across time. They do not establish that culture simply outran a fixed human brain or moved in one direction.
- **Archaeogenetic evidence** can reveal demographic relatedness, movement and lineage patterns. Social organisation is inferred by combining it with archaeological and anthropological evidence; inner experience is not preserved in a genome.
- **The post-Neolithic Y-chromosome bottleneck** is compatible with models of patrilineal kin-group formation and competition. It is not evidence of emotional suppression, cultural CLS elevation or one beginning of patriarchy.
- **Forager egalitarianism and levelling research** identifies important social arrangements and anti-dominance practices. Foragers vary, present groups are not unchanged ancestral populations and equality can differ by resource, gender, age and domain.
- **Affective-primacy research** concerns specified timing and influence effects. It does not establish that feeling is always first, more truthful or separate from cognition.
- **Somatic-marker theory** is an influential account of bodily contribution to decision-making with a debated evidence base. It cannot carry the claim that ESS provides accurate instructions which CLS overrides.
- **Subcortical threat-processing research** supports multiple routes and timings in defensive processing. Older “low-road” simplifications should not be used to divide emotion from thought or claim that the body knows before the brain.
- **Interoception research** supports continuing sensing and integration of internal bodily information with conscious and non-conscious aspects. Interoception is not identical to emotion, self-knowledge or coordinated conscious access.
- **Histories of emotion and knowledge** can trace changing concepts, institutions and practices. They are interpretations of uneven archives, not experiments identifying one cause.
- **Leadership and organisational research** can test emergence, selection, influence, effectiveness, empathy, incentives and response to feedback. Role or trait scores cannot reveal one private regulation strategy or establish harm.
- **Emotional-learning research** supports specified and sometimes trainable capacities. It does not show that literacy guarantees ethical behaviour, accurate deception detection or population-level freedom from manipulation.

F12 is therefore a TEG-Blue historical and ethical synthesis built from narrower evidence families. The synthesis should remain visibly revisable.

## F12's handoffs

F12 closes the first architectural review cycle by handing questions back through the collection:

```text
historical knowledge hierarchy
        ↓
uneven language, permission and conscious access
        ↓
protection routes that may reduce pressure or transfer cost
        ↓
social reward, institutional selection and reproduced authority
        ↓
normalised confusion among confidence, control, safety and leadership
        ↓
literacy, evidence, viable refusal and institutional revision
        ↺
new regulation pass through F1–F12
```

Four handoffs matter most:

1. **To the regulation pass:** define organism regulation, emotion regulation, interpersonal regulation, pressure reduction, completion, imposed cost, updating and Return before describing their role in each framework.
2. **To F5, F7 and HPR:** develop the Filter of Worth selection corridor through observable advancement criteria, power use, feedback treatment and effects on people with less authority.
3. **To the public educational model:** explain ESS, CLS, nervous-system organisation and ME without dualism, diagnosis or promises of manipulation immunity.
4. **To future historical research:** replace the single retrospective story with route-specific, cross-cultural and participatory inquiry whose evidence status remains visible.

## Result of this pass

- **Framework status:** retain and substantially reframe.
- **Collection role:** reflective framework connecting histories of emotional authority with current regulation, institutional selection, power and future learning.
- **Title:** revise from “Retrospective ESS–CLS Disconnection and Cultural CLS Elevation” to “The Human Retrospective: Emotional Information, Power and Possibility.”
- **Opening movement:** begin by asking how some cultures learned to distrust or subordinate emotional and bodily information.
- **Present movement:** trace how biological information may continue with uneven conscious access and how some action routes reduce pressure, persist or transfer cost.
- **Filter of Worth:** extend it cautiously into a testable institutional selection corridor; do not infer emotional unawareness from status.
- **Leadership:** distinguish emergence, authority, effectiveness, care, confidence, dominance and safety.
- **Future movement:** connect emotional, nervous-system, relational, power, evidence and technology literacy.
- **Manipulation:** literacy may reduce some vulnerabilities, but collective protection also requires safe contradiction, corroboration, viable refusal and accountable institutions.
- **Technology:** treat it as increased capacity that may support understanding or intensify control.
- **Human possibility:** show evidence of learnable capacities and changeable institutions without promising inevitable progress.
- **Collection dependency:** mark a future regulation pass across F1–F12 as required work.
- **Evidence status:** architectural first pass; the historical synthesis remains a TEG-Blue proposition requiring route-, period-, culture- and institution-specific grounding.

## Grounding science and knowledge fields

F12 needs fields that can study biology, concepts, institutions, power and change without turning their different evidence into one story. No discipline can reconstruct the complete retrospective alone.

| Field | What it can ground in F12 | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Affective science and emotion research** | Emotion, affect, appraisal, salience, motivation, expression, differentiation and influence on judgement | One universal emotion architecture, historical cultural authority or ethical action |
| **Interoception, psychophysiology and neuroscience** | Internal sensing, conscious and non-conscious processing, autonomic organisation, attention and decision-related effects | ESS as a discrete system, one meaning of a bodily signal or the history of cultural suppression |
| **Cognitive science and judgement research** | Reasoning, attention, memory, confidence, dual-process models, evidence use and updating | Disembodied rationality, one superior information system or social legitimacy |
| **Emotion-regulation science** | Identification, selection, implementation and monitoring of strategies affecting emotion | Every pressure-reducing behaviour classified as emotion regulation or one universally adaptive strategy |
| **Interpersonal and social-regulation research** | How people seek, provide or impose changes in another person's emotional experience | Care, consent or ethical acceptability inferred from successful influence |
| **Developmental psychology and education research** | Emotion concepts, differentiation, socialisation, learning, self–other understanding and trainable skills | One childhood origin for a civilisation or literacy as immunity from harm |
| **History of emotions, ideas and medicine** | Changing meanings of passion, reason, body, self-command, disorder and emotional expression | Direct access to inner experience or one continuous Western view |
| **History and philosophy of science** | How methods, objectivity, measurement and disciplinary authority developed and changed | Rejection of empirical standards or proof that formal knowledge is inherently oppressive |
| **Historical sociology and sociology of knowledge** | Institutions, professions, authority, classification, bureaucracy and credibility | One nervous-system mechanism inside a culture or institution |
| **Anthropology and cultural psychology** | Variation in emotion concepts, selfhood, knowledge, hierarchy, care and expression | Cultures treated as uniform, timeless or psychologically diagnosable |

| **Archaeogenetics and population genetics** | Ancestry, migration, relatedness, demographic change and lineage patterns | Psychological organisation, gender system, emotional suppression or causation inferred from a bottleneck |
| **Cultural-evolution and gene–culture coevolution research** | Transmission, social learning, norms, institutions and biological–cultural interaction | Linear progress, one cultural trajectory or culture treated as faster software on fixed hardware |
| **Political economy and labour history** | Property, dependence, work discipline, production, inequality, extraction and material constraints | Individual motive or emotional access inferred from class position |
| **Organisational science and leadership research** | Selection, emergence, incentives, promotion, voice, silence, effectiveness, power and feedback | Diagnosis, empathy or moral character inferred from role or style |
| **Sociology of status, stigma and inequality** | Credibility, valuation, prestige, access, cumulative advantage and institutional reproduction | Filter of Worth as one hidden mechanism or equal responsibility across positions |
| **Political science, law and public administration** | Authority, governance, accountability, institutional checks, rights, evidence and policy change | Emotional literacy or biological organisation inferred from formal structure |
| **Communication, persuasion and misinformation research** | Framing, manipulation techniques, propaganda, inoculation, correction and information environments | Complete protection from influence or truth determined by communication style |
| **Epistemology and philosophy of language** | Evidence, reason, experience, testimony, uncertainty, interpretation and conceptual change | Which historical proposition is empirically correct without corresponding evidence |
| **Feminist epistemology and gender studies** | Situated knowledge, credibility, gendered reason–emotion hierarchies and excluded experience | Women, men or gender-diverse people assigned one way of knowing |

| **Clinical psychology, psychiatry and trauma research** | Emotional access, avoidance, dissociation, coping, intervention, deterioration and recovery | Diagnosis from a cultural pattern or trauma as a total theory of history and domination |
| **Behavioural economics and consumer research** | Decision environments, reward, scarcity, consumption, status and persuasive design | Consumption assigned one emotional function or consumer choice detached from material conditions |
| **Human–computer interaction, AI ethics and technology studies** | Digital education, affective computing, classification, persuasion, surveillance, bias and agency | Reliable access to inner emotion from behavioural proxies or technological progress as social progress |
| **Public health and implementation science** | Population conditions, prevention, education, institutional change, unintended effects and unequal access | Individual mechanisms or universal transfer across settings |
| **Participatory, survivor-led and lived-experience research** | Hidden regulation routes, manipulation, institutional harm, language needs and person-defined change | One account generalised to all people or subjective certainty treated as historical proof |
| **Measurement science and causal inference** | Construct validity, historical proxies, route testing, uncertainty, mediation and alternative explanation | Strong causal claims from converging correlation or one measure treated as the complete construct |

### Priority evidence

F12 should organise evidence by route, time and level. Historical claims need specified archives, concepts, populations and competing explanations. Biological claims need defined processes and measures. Institutional claims need selection criteria, incentives, decision access, behaviour, impact and response to feedback. Educational claims need measured learning, transfer, duration, population and adverse or instrumental use.

The strongest programme will combine history, archaeology, anthropology, psychology, organisational research, institutional comparison, participatory inquiry and careful causal methods without forcing them into one chronology. Evidence that several components exist does not establish that they form one universal history.

The future regulation pass should test each proposed route separately:

```text
route · intended or learned function · immediate outcome · duration
        · person-level cost · transferred cost · power used
        · response to feedback · recurrence · completion or Return
```

F12 does not need to prove that humanity will become better. It needs to show where change has already been possible, what conditions supported it and what remains available to learn.
