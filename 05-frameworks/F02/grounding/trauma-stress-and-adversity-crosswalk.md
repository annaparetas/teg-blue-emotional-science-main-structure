# F2 · Trauma, Stress and Adversity Crosswalk

Status: working claim-and-evidence routing document
Parent files: [F2 · Emotion Override](../diagram.html) · [Trauma, Stress and Adversity Research Map](trauma-stress-and-adversity-research-map.md)
Purpose: identify which trauma-, stress- and adversity research families can ground particular F2 claims without turning trauma into F2's single cause or explanation

## The central decision

F2 should not become a general trauma model. Its narrower scientific task is to examine how development and repeated relational outcomes may shape whether emotional information can be accessed, identified, expressed, trusted and used for action.

Trauma, stress and adversity research can strengthen that account in three different ways:

1. **Direct developmental grounding:** evidence about emotion socialisation, attachment, caregiving, neglect, learning, controllability and early development.
2. **Mechanism bridges:** evidence about conditioning, interoception, dissociation, stress physiology, defensive responding and updating.
3. **Downstream checks:** diagnostic and outcome research that may show consequences or correlates but cannot establish the F2 developmental route.

> **F2 describes a possible developmental learning process. Exposure categories, diagnoses and adult outcomes do not prove that process occurred in an individual.**

## F2's seven layers: evidence-routing questions

The present F2 page proposes seven places at which developmental learning may matter. Each needs a more exact research question.

### 1 · Signal generation

**Groundable claim:** emotionally relevant and defensive organisation can begin before deliberate reflection.

**Best families:** TSR-23, TSR-24, TSR-34 and TSR-35.

**Do not claim:** that development switches emotion off at source, that all bodily change becomes conscious feeling, or that a current signal reveals its historical cause.

### 2 · Bodily representation

**Groundable claim:** internal change may be represented while its precision, weighting and relation to context vary.

**Best families:** TSR-07, TSR-19, TSR-20 and TSR-22.

**Do not claim:** that one interoceptive profile defines trauma or that the body contains a literal, independently readable record of an event.

### 3 · Subjective access

**Groundable claim:** access to affective or bodily information may vary by context, attention, dissociation, learning and relational safety.

**Best families:** TSR-07, TSR-12, TSR-13, TSR-15, TSR-19 and TSR-20.

**Do not claim:** that lack of report means lack of signal, or that later access proves repression of a specific memory.

### 4 · Identification and differentiation

**Groundable claim:** emotion concepts, language, differentiation and contextual interpretation develop and can be supported or constrained.

**Best families:** TSR-14, TSR-15, TSR-19 and TSR-21.

**Do not claim:** that naming accuracy is the whole of emotional participation or that limited vocabulary establishes maltreatment.

### 5 · Expression

**Groundable claim:** expression is shaped by social learning and can be inhibited even when subjective experience remains.

**Best families:** TSR-14 through TSR-17 and TSR-24.

**Do not claim:** that visible calm means absence of activation, or that emotional restraint is necessarily pathological.

### 6 · Action

**Groundable claim:** a signal's usefulness depends partly on whether effective action is available, safe and capable of changing conditions.

**Best families:** TSR-26 through TSR-29, supported by TSR-11 through TSR-13.

**Do not claim:** that a person failed to act because they failed to feel, or that a defensive response determines responsibility for later harmful conduct.

### 7 · Emotion belief and learned relational expectation

**Groundable claim:** repeated outcomes may contribute to expectations about whether an emotion is legitimate, dangerous, useful, shameful or relationally costly.

**Best families:** TSR-14 through TSR-18, TSR-24 through TSR-28 and TSR-32.

**Do not claim:** that the proposed sequence is universal, that beliefs are purely verbal, or that one current expectation identifies one childhood history.

## Research families with the highest immediate value for F2

### Tier 1 · Build the developmental mechanism

- emotion socialisation and parental meta-emotion;
- emotional invalidation and childhood emotional maltreatment;
- attachment, social buffering and co-regulation;
- parentification and role reversal;
- preverbal learning and early pain;
- controllability, escape learning, entrapment and defensive immobility;
- emotion differentiation, alexithymia and interoception.

### Tier 2 · Test persistence and contextual variation

- conditioning, extinction, safety learning and renewal;
- threat generalisation and discrimination;
- stress sensitisation;
- harshness and unpredictability;
- dissociation;
- shame and humiliation.

### Tier 3 · Constrain the model and examine outcomes

- PTSD and CPTSD;
- ACE epidemiology;
- allostatic load;
- threat/deprivation dimensional models;
- social determinants and structural adversity;
- prenatal, intergenerational and epigenetic research.

Tier 3 is not less important. It is less direct for the particular mechanism F2 proposes.

## Concepts that can now improve materially

| Current or tempting phrase | More grounded direction |
| --- | --- |
| Emotion is overridden | Access, expression or signal-guided action may be inhibited, downweighted or made relationally costly. |
| The child learns not to feel | The child may learn not to attend to, identify, express, trust or act from some emotional information; these possibilities must be tested separately. |
| Trauma causes disconnection | Particular exposures and relational contingencies may be associated with different forms of access, dissociation, avoidance, inhibition or adaptation. |
| The nervous system chooses safety | Defensive and regulatory organisation may prioritise predicted protection or reduced cost without implying conscious choice or objective safety. |
| The body remembers | Non-declarative learning, physiological association, pain-related learning or sensorimotor patterning may persist; none independently verifies an autobiographical event. |
| Learned helplessness | Controllability, escape learning and expectations about action–outcome relations. |
| The emotion was suppressed | Specify whether visible expression, attention, identification, subjective access or action was reduced. |
| The pattern comes from childhood | Development is one possible route; present context, later learning, health and social conditions may also contribute. |

## What this changes in the F2 research programme

The next source review should be organised around claims, not around the umbrella word *trauma*:

1. Can relational responses prospectively change emotion knowledge, expression, regulation or help-seeking?
2. Which effects concern visible expression, subjective access, physiology, identification or action—and which measures distinguish them?
3. How do controllability and actual action availability alter learning?
4. When does a learned expectation generalise, remain relationship-specific or update?
5. Which developmental windows have evidence, and which are being inferred from adult retrospective reports?
6. What protective relationships, repair processes or later experiences change the route?
7. Which findings replicate across cultures, social conditions, emotions and caregiver relationships?

## Non-negotiable F2 boundaries

- F2 is not a diagnosis or trauma-screening instrument.
- It cannot infer childhood history from present organisation.
- It cannot equate low expression, emotional restraint or cultural display rules with pathology.
- It cannot treat caregiver influence as the only developmental route.
- It must distinguish present danger from overgeneralised expectation.
- It must preserve agency and responsibility when explaining harmful behaviour.
- It must represent buffering, repair, resilience, context specificity and updating—not only adversity and persistence.
- ESS, CLS and ME remain TEG-Blue constructs. Component research can ground relations represented in the model; it does not validate the constructs as established scientific entities.
