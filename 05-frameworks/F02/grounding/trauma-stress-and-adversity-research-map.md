# Trauma, Stress and Adversity Research Map

Status: working research-family register
Source: user-supplied 37-part research map; the supplied text ends partway through section 37
Purpose: organise trauma-, stress- and adversity-related knowledge without treating “trauma” as one exposure, mechanism, diagnosis or explanation

F2 companion: [F2 · Trauma, Stress and Adversity Crosswalk](trauma-stress-and-adversity-crosswalk.md)

## Why this register is needed

The word *trauma* is often asked to carry several different meanings:

- an event or exposure;
- an immediate response;
- a learned pattern;
- a psychiatric diagnosis;
- a developmental condition;
- a physiological mechanism;
- a clinical formulation;
- a social history;
- a lasting effect;
- a person’s lived description of what happened.

These meanings can connect. They are not interchangeable.

> **Before using trauma research, identify whether the claim concerns exposure, response, development, mechanism, diagnosis, outcome, recovery or practice.**

This register is an intake map. It identifies the role each research family may play and the inference it must not be asked to carry. Source-level grounding still requires individual studies, populations, measures, comparison groups, uncertainty and counterevidence.

## Seven knowledge roles

| Role | Main question | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Exposure** | What happened, how often, when and under what conditions? | Maltreatment, violence, neglect, pain, deprivation, instability |
| **Immediate response** | What occurred during or shortly after the event? | Acute stress, freezing, dissociation, pain modulation |
| **Learning and development** | What changed through timing, repetition, caregiving or controllability? | Threat generalisation, attachment, emotion socialisation |
| **Mechanism** | Which measured processes may contribute? | HPA activity, autonomic participation, conditioning, interoception |
| **Diagnosis or syndrome** | Does a defined symptom pattern meet diagnostic requirements? | PTSD, ICD-11 CPTSD |
| **Outcome and functioning** | What later health, behaviour, relationship or functional outcomes are associated? | ACE epidemiology, allostatic load, sleep disturbance |
| **Practice and recovery** | What helps, for whom, under what conditions and through which measured route? | Trauma-focused treatments, social buffering, relational repair |

## Core safeguards

- Exposure does not equal diagnosis.
- Diagnosis does not identify one mechanism.
- A mechanism found in a diagnostic group does not define every person in that group.
- Association does not reconstruct one person’s history.
- A physiological response does not reveal the meaning, truth or morality of an event.
- An adaptive response can still be costly.
- A costly response can still be responsive to real present danger.
- Absence of verbal or autobiographical memory does not mean absence of learning.
- Presence of bodily sensation does not prove a hidden narrative memory.
- Chronic does not mean permanent, continuously active or impossible to change.
- Trauma history does not establish harmful behaviour, and trauma explanation does not remove responsibility.
- Recovery, resilience, non-development of disorder and positive adaptation must remain visible alongside impairment.

## Research-family register

### TSR-01 · Psychological trauma

- **Role:** broad phenomenon and clinical-research tradition.
- **Studies:** responses following overwhelming, threatening or uncontrollable events, including intrusion, avoidance, arousal, dissociation, attention and meaning.
- **Useful for:** identifying questions that later mechanism, diagnosis and recovery research can test separately.
- **Do not infer:** that “psychological trauma” is one mechanism, one memory form or one nervous-system state.
- **Current connections:** IC-01 Conditions · IC-07 Situation-relevant information · NG-02 persistent/recurrent organisation · BE-03 Cost.
- **Lineage names supplied:** Pierre Janet · Judith Herman · Mardi Horowitz · Charles Figley · Bessel van der Kolk.

### TSR-02 · Post-traumatic stress disorder · PTSD

- **Role:** defined psychiatric diagnosis and major empirical research field.
- **Studies:** trauma exposure followed by specified patterns of re-experiencing, avoidance, arousal/reactivity, cognition/mood change, duration and impairment; exact requirements depend on the diagnostic system.
- **Useful for:** symptom trajectories, conditioning, extinction, generalisation, context processing, sleep, startle, avoidance and treatment research.
- **Do not infer:** that everyone exposed develops PTSD, that PTSD is one biological state or that PTSD categories map onto Gradient Positions.
- **Current connections:** NG-02 persistent/recurrent organisation · IC-10 Updating · IC-11 Return · NG-04 persistence and flexibility.
- **Names supplied:** Rachel Yehuda · Kerry Ressler · Joseph LeDoux · Roger Pitman · Scott Rauch · Mohammed Milad · Israel Liberzon · Ruth Lanius.

### TSR-03 · Complex PTSD · CPTSD

- **Role:** ICD-11 diagnosis, distinct from simply “more severe PTSD”.
- **Studies:** core PTSD requirements plus disturbances in self-organisation involving affect regulation, persistent negative self-concept and relationship difficulty.
- **Useful for:** examining symptom patterns spanning threat, self-experience and relationship functioning.
- **Do not infer:** that all complex trauma produces CPTSD, that CPTSD describes one developmental pathway or that its domains establish the Inner Compass architecture.
- **Current connections:** IC-08 ME · IC-09 Conscious Participation · NG-02 · RC-01 bodily information access and use · NG-06 reciprocal connection.
- **Names supplied:** Marylene Cloitre · Chris Brewin · Andreas Maercker · Julian Ford · Judith Herman.

### TSR-04 · Developmental trauma

- **Role:** developmental research umbrella; also includes contested diagnostic proposals.
- **Studies:** how repeated adversity during development relates to regulation, attention, learning, relationship, self-representation and functioning.
- **Useful for:** timing, sensitive periods, developmental opportunity, adaptation and heterogeneity.
- **Do not infer:** that Developmental Trauma Disorder is an established diagnosis, that adversity produces one profile or that every later difficulty originated in trauma.
- **Current connections:** IC-02 sensing and signalling · IC-04 embodied processing · IC-05 reflective/symbolic processing · NG-02 · RC-01 through RC-03.
- **Names supplied:** Dante Cicchetti · Sheree Toth · Michael De Bellis · Martin Teicher · Julian Ford · Christine Courtois · Bessel van der Kolk.

### TSR-05 · Childhood maltreatment

- **Role:** exposure family requiring type, timing, duration, relationship and severity detail.
- **Studies:** physical, sexual and emotional abuse; physical and emotional neglect; exposure to domestic violence; abandonment and instability, among other conditions.
- **Useful for:** separating exposure patterns and examining whether different conditions relate to different processes and outcomes.
- **Do not infer:** one maltreatment dose, mechanism or inevitable consequence; caregiver mental illness or substance use is not itself maltreatment without examining the child’s conditions.
- **Current connections:** IC-01 Conditions · IC-07 · NG-02 · HPR-01 power · HPR-05 restriction/deprivation · HPR-06 epistemic harm.

### TSR-06 · Adverse Childhood Experiences · ACEs

- **Role:** exposure measurement and epidemiology.
- **Studies:** associations between categories of childhood adversity and later health, behavioural and social outcomes, often cumulatively.
- **Useful for:** population-level burden, prevention and broad exposure–outcome gradients.
- **Do not infer:** mechanism, individual prognosis, equivalent severity across categories or a nervous-system score.
- **Current connections:** IC-01 Conditions · BE-03 Cost · TB-01 integrative grounding.
- **Names supplied:** Vincent Felitti · Robert Anda and colleagues.

### TSR-07 · Toxic stress

- **Role:** developmental stress formulation and prevention language.
- **Studies:** prolonged or repeated stress-system activation in childhood where sufficient buffering protection is absent.
- **Useful for:** distinguishing ordinary challenge from repeated activation without adequate support or recovery.
- **Do not infer:** a diagnosis, one biomarker or that every serious adversity produces “toxic” biological change.
- **Current connections:** NG-01 present-responsive organisation · NG-02 persistent/recurrent organisation · IC-11 Return · TSR-13 social buffering.

### TSR-08 · Allostasis and allostatic load

- **Role:** physiological regulation and cumulative-cost research family.
- **Studies:** anticipatory or demand-responsive adjustment across interacting physiological systems and multisystem cost across time.
- **Useful for:** resource allocation, repeated recruitment, recovery opportunity and cumulative biological burden.
- **Do not infer:** one load molecule, one universal index or a direct Gradient Position biomarker.
- **Current connections:** IC-03 whole-organism response organisation · NG-01 · NG-02 · BE-03 Cost · IC-11 Return.
- **Names supplied:** Peter Sterling · Joseph Eyer · Bruce McEwen.

### TSR-09 · Stress physiology

- **Role:** mechanism family.
- **Studies:** HPA, sympathetic–adrenal–medullary, autonomic, immune, metabolic and interacting mediator responses across acute stress, chronic demand and recovery.
- **Useful for:** timing, response-channel divergence, individual variation and recovery profiles.
- **Do not infer:** “high cortisol equals trauma”, one stress signature or a direct reading of subjective experience.
- **Current connections:** IC-03 · NG-08 protective mobilisation · NG-10 extreme defensive mobilisation · IC-11.
- **Names supplied:** Hans Selye · Bruce McEwen · Robert Sapolsky · George Chrousos · Sonia Lupien.

### TSR-10 · Early-life stress

- **Role:** broad exposure and developmental-mechanism family.
- **Studies:** varied early conditions including institutionalisation, deprivation, maltreatment, poverty, instability, separation, conflict and caregiver unpredictability.
- **Useful for:** timing and developmental calibration when exposure types are kept separate.
- **Do not infer:** that poverty, separation, neglect and violence are biologically interchangeable or that animal separation paradigms directly explain human subjective experience.
- **Current connections:** IC-01 · IC-02 · NG-02 · NG-04 · TSR-31 dimensional adversity.

### TSR-11 · Attachment research

- **Role:** developmental relationship and behavioural-system research.
- **Studies:** proximity seeking, protection, exploration, distress and expectations of caregiver availability within development and relationships.
- **Useful for:** relationship-specific regulation, secure-base behaviour, expectation and developmental support.
- **Do not infer:** fixed adult personality types, one attachment neurobiology or that attachment categories equal Gradient Positions.
- **Current connections:** NG-06 reciprocal connection · IC-07 · IC-11 · TSR-13 social buffering.
- **Names supplied:** John Bowlby · Mary Ainsworth · Mary Main · Alan Sroufe.

### TSR-12 · Disorganised attachment

- **Role:** specific attachment classification and research tradition.
- **Studies:** observed conflict, contradiction or lack of an organised attachment strategy under particular assessment conditions; later research examines multiple pathways and meanings.
- **Useful for:** the developmental problem created when proximity and alarm may be recruited around the same caregiver.
- **Do not infer:** abuse, a permanent trait, dissociation, one adult relationship style or a direct A↔B classification.
- **Current connections:** NG-07 orienting under uncertainty · NG-08 protection · NG-06 connection · NG-02 development.
- **Names supplied:** Mary Main · Judith Solomon.

### TSR-13 · Social buffering of stress

- **Role:** relational mechanism and developmental research family.
- **Studies:** how caregiver presence, attachment figures and social support alter measured stress responses under specified conditions.
- **Useful for:** relationship-dependent response modulation and developmental change.
- **Do not infer:** that all social presence regulates, that lower cortisol proves safety or that support and compliance are the same.
- **Current connections:** NG-06 · IC-11 · TSR-11 attachment · TSR-14 co-regulation.
- **Names supplied:** Megan Gunnar · Nim Tottenham · James Coan · Shelley Taylor.

### TSR-14 · Co-regulation and biobehavioural synchrony

- **Role:** interpersonal process family.
- **Studies:** behavioural, affective and physiological coordination between people and interpersonal emotion-regulation processes.
- **Useful for:** how regulation develops and is supported within interaction.
- **Do not infer:** that synchrony always helps, that correlated physiology identifies who regulates whom or that reduced expression proves successful regulation.
- **Current connections:** NG-06 · IC-11 · RC-02 affective sharing · HPR-09 pressure reduction boundary.
- **Name supplied:** Ruth Feldman.

### TSR-15 · Emotion socialisation

- **Role:** developmental relationship and learning family.
- **Studies:** how caregivers and cultures respond to, label, model, coach, dismiss, punish or shape emotional experience and expression.
- **Useful for:** development of emotion knowledge, expression, regulation, belief and use.
- **Do not infer:** that one parenting response has one universal effect or that expression directly reveals internal signal generation.
- **Current connections:** IC-04 · IC-05 · IC-06 · IC-07 · IC-09 · RC-01.
- **Names supplied:** Nancy Eisenberg · John Gottman · Susanne Denham · Judy Dunn.

### TSR-16 · Emotion-invalidating environments

- **Role:** developmental exposure and relational-process family.
- **Studies:** dismissing, punitive, minimising or contradictory responses to emotion and their associations with later access, expression and regulation.
- **Useful for:** testing separate pathways from signal generation through interpretation, expression and action.
- **Do not infer:** that invalidation erases physiological representation, causes one disorder or explains every difficulty identifying feelings.
- **Current connections:** IC-04 through IC-09 · HPR-06 epistemic harm · NG-02.

### TSR-17 · Linehan’s biosocial theory

- **Role:** clinical developmental theory associated with borderline personality disorder and DBT.
- **Studies/proposes:** interaction between emotional vulnerability and invalidating environments in emotion-regulation difficulty.
- **Useful for:** generating bounded questions about vulnerability × environment interactions and invalidation.
- **Do not infer:** that the complete theory is a general trauma mechanism, that invalidation causes BPD or that BPD should organise the Inner Compass.
- **Current connections:** TSR-16 · IC-09 · NG-02 · practice research.
- **Name supplied:** Marsha Linehan.

### TSR-18 · Childhood emotional neglect

- **Role:** specific exposure family.
- **Studies:** insufficient recognition, response, language, support or regulation around a child’s emotional states, with measurement definitions varying across studies.
- **Useful for:** absence of expected emotional input, emotion knowledge and later access questions.
- **Do not infer:** a hidden exposure from alexithymia, quiet family style or one adult symptom.
- **Current connections:** TSR-15 · TSR-16 · TSR-31 deprivation · IC-06 · RC-01.

### TSR-19 · Alexithymia

- **Role:** multidimensional psychological construct and measurement field.
- **Studies:** difficulty identifying feelings, difficulty describing feelings and externally oriented thinking, while instruments and factor models vary.
- **Useful for:** separating physiological processing, felt access, differentiation, language and reporting.
- **Do not infer:** absent emotion, absent interoception, trauma history or one biological deficit.
- **Current connections:** IC-04 · IC-05 · IC-06 · IC-08 · RC-01.
- **Lineage name supplied:** Peter Sifneos.

### TSR-20 · Interoception and trauma

- **Role:** bridge between trauma-related outcomes and interoception research.
- **Studies:** objective performance, subjective attention, sensibility, awareness, metacognitive correspondence, bodily prediction and central representation.
- **Useful for:** specifying what aspect of body-state processing or access differs, for whom and under which task.
- **Do not infer:** one interoceptive capacity, a trauma detector or that subjective bodily certainty is objective accuracy.
- **Current connections:** IC-02 · IC-06 body-state information · RC-01 bodily information access and use.
- **Names supplied:** A. D. Craig · Hugo Critchley · Sahib Khalsa · Ruth Lanius · Martin Paulus.

### TSR-21 · Dissociation

- **Role:** heterogeneous phenomenon and clinical-research family.
- **Studies:** depersonalisation, derealisation, altered awareness, emotional numbing, memory discontinuity and other disruptions of integration or consciousness.
- **Useful for:** distinguishing low access, altered consciousness, memory fragmentation and bodily disconnection.
- **Do not infer:** one mechanism, hidden trauma, complete physiological absence or equivalence among dissociation, freezing, fainting and shutdown.
- **Current connections:** NG-11 reduced-output/immobilisation/disconnection routes · IC-08 · RC-01.
- **Names supplied:** Pierre Janet · David Spiegel · Ruth Lanius · Onno van der Hart · Ellert Nijenhuis.

### TSR-22 · Peritraumatic responses

- **Role:** immediate-response research family.
- **Studies:** responses during and immediately around potentially traumatic events, including orienting, freezing, immobility, dissociation, narrowed attention, pain modulation and active defence.
- **Useful for:** refining extreme Gradient territories and timing.
- **Do not infer:** later PTSD, memory completeness, consent, moral meaning or one defence sequence from a single response.
- **Current connections:** NG-07 through NG-11 · IC-03 · IC-09 boundary.

### TSR-23 · Defensive survival circuits

- **Role:** comparative and affective-neuroscience research programme.
- **Studies:** neural systems involved in detecting and responding to threats, kept conceptually distinct from the conscious experience labelled fear.
- **Useful for:** preventing brain-region-to-emotion equations and separating response control from subjective feeling.
- **Do not infer:** that defensive circuits are an Inner Compass module, that amygdala activity equals fear or that animal defensive behaviour directly reports human meaning.
- **Current connections:** IC-02 · IC-03 · NG-08 · NG-10.
- **Name supplied:** Joseph LeDoux.

### TSR-24 · Predatory Imminence Continuum

- **Role:** comparative defensive-behaviour model.
- **Studies:** changes in defensive behaviour as threat moves through pre-encounter, post-encounter and circa-strike relations.
- **Useful for:** imminence as one dimension affecting defensive organisation.
- **Do not infer:** a universal human sequence, social-moral meaning or direct equivalence with the seven Gradient references.
- **Current connections:** NG-07 orienting · NG-08 defence · NG-10 extreme mobilisation · NG-11 low-output routes.
- **Names supplied:** Michael Fanselow · Lester.

### TSR-25 · Defence cascade

- **Role:** clinical/comparative synthesis across defensive responses.
- **Studies/proposes:** relationships among orienting, freezing, flight/fight, tonic immobility and collapsed immobility, with definitions and physiological findings varying.
- **Useful for:** insisting that low movement can occur at different moments for different functions.
- **Do not infer:** a fixed ladder, universal order or that freezing, tonic immobility, collapse and dissociation are one state.
- **Current connections:** NG-07 through NG-11; especially restructuring NG-11.
- **Names supplied:** Maggie Schauer · Thomas Elbert · Miranda Kozlowska and colleagues.

### TSR-26 · Controllability and escape learning

- **Role:** learning and stress-modulation research family; modern replacement for simplistic learned-helplessness language.
- **Studies:** whether actions change aversive outcomes and how prior control or lack of control alters later response.
- **Useful for:** “Can any available action change what is happening?” as a Gradient and developmental variable.
- **Do not infer:** that passivity proves learned helplessness, weakness, depression or a person’s history. Much mechanistic evidence comes from animal studies and requires careful translation.
- **Current connections:** IC-01 · IC-10 · NG-08 · NG-11 · HPR-02 viable refusal.
- **Names supplied:** Steven Maier · Martin Seligman.

### TSR-27 · Predictability, uncertainty and anticipatory stress

- **Role:** learning, attention and stress research family.
- **Studies:** responses under predictable, unpredictable, ambiguous, controllable and uncontrollable conditions.
- **Useful for:** continuous checking, sustained anticipation, household unpredictability and persistent demand.
- **Do not infer:** that uncertainty is pathology or that predictability always makes a harmful condition safe.
- **Current connections:** NG-07 orienting under uncertainty · NG-09 sustained demand management · NG-02 · IC-10.

### TSR-28 · Threat generalisation and safety discrimination

- **Role:** learning-mechanism research family.
- **Studies:** spread of learned responding beyond the original cue and ability to distinguish safety from danger across contexts.
- **Useful for:** testing similarity-based defensive recruitment while retaining present-context evaluation.
- **Do not infer:** that every strong response is generalised, that the present setting is safe or that a person consciously believes two contexts are identical.
- **Current connections:** IC-07 · IC-10 · NG-02 · NG-04.

### TSR-29 · Conditioning, extinction, return and reconsolidation

- **Role:** multiple learning and memory research programmes.
- **Studies:** acquisition of associations; new inhibitory or safety learning; renewal, reinstatement and spontaneous recovery; conditions under which retrieved memories may change.
- **Useful for:** replacing “the body remembers” with claim-specific learning processes.
- **Do not infer:** that extinction erases original learning, that reconsolidation occurs whenever memory is discussed or that exposure alone guarantees safety learning.
- **Current connections:** IC-10 Updating · IC-11 Return · NG-02 · NG-04.

### TSR-30 · Childhood abuse and neglect by exposure type

- **Role:** explicit subtyping within maltreatment research.
- **Studies:** physical abuse, sexual abuse, emotional abuse and neglect with partly different conditions, measures and possible pathways.
- **Useful for:** preventing one undifferentiated childhood-trauma mechanism.
- **Do not infer:** equal exposure meaning, equivalent developmental route or one expected symptom profile.
- **Current connections:** TSR-05 · TSR-31 · IC-01 · NG-02 · HPR claims where power and agency are involved.

### TSR-31 · Dimensional models of childhood adversity · DMAP

- **Role:** dimensional developmental model and empirical research programme.
- **Studies:** threat—harm or threat of harm—and deprivation—absence of expected cognitive or social input—as partly separable dimensions with proposed pathways.
- **Useful for:** improving on cumulative counts and generating pathway-specific hypotheses.
- **Do not infer:** that threat and deprivation capture every adversity, are always separable in lived environments or have one settled mechanism each.
- **Current connections:** IC-01 Conditions · IC-02 · IC-05 · NG-02 · TSR-05 · TSR-10.
- **Name supplied:** Katie McLaughlin and colleagues.

### TSR-32 · Harshness and unpredictability

- **Role:** developmental and life-history-related environmental dimensions.
- **Studies:** environmental danger/resource scarcity and variation or inconsistency across time, with definitions differing across disciplines.
- **Useful for:** anticipatory monitoring, resource allocation and difficulty relying on stable patterns.
- **Do not infer:** that harshness and unpredictability equal threat and deprivation, that poverty is inherently unpredictable or that human behaviour follows a simple evolutionary optimisation model.
- **Current connections:** IC-01 · NG-07 · NG-09 · NG-02 · TSR-27.

### TSR-33 · Prenatal stress and developmental origins

- **Role:** prenatal exposure, placental biology and Developmental Origins of Health and Disease research.
- **Studies:** maternal stress and health, endocrine and immune pathways, placental processes, fetal development and later outcomes through probabilistic associations.
- **Useful for:** well-specified prenatal biological pathways and developmental timing.
- **Do not infer:** “intrauterine trauma”, psychologically structured fetal memory, maternal blame or deterministic programming.
- **Current connections:** IC-01 · IC-02 · NG-02 development · TSR-36 intergenerational routes.
- **Lineage name supplied:** David Barker and the wider DOHaD field.

### TSR-34 · Preverbal and infant stress learning

- **Role:** developmental learning and infant research family.
- **Studies:** pain, associative learning, sensory learning, attachment disruption, stress responses and fear learning before mature autobiographical memory and language.
- **Useful for:** distinguishing nonverbal learning from later narrative memory.
- **Do not infer:** literal hidden narratives stored in the body, adult recall of infancy or a unique preverbal-trauma syndrome.
- **Current connections:** IC-02 · IC-04 · IC-10 · NG-02 · TSR-35 infant pain.

### TSR-35 · Infant and neonatal pain

- **Role:** developmental nociception, pain and clinical-outcome research.
- **Studies:** behavioural and physiological pain responses, repeated procedures, analgesia, sensory development and later associations.
- **Useful for:** establishing that pain and stress learning can occur before verbal report.
- **Do not infer:** adult-like conscious interpretation, autobiographical memory or one later trauma outcome from neonatal response.
- **Current connections:** IC-02 · IC-06 body-state information · NG-02 development.

### TSR-36 · Intergenerational and transgenerational pathways

- **Role:** multi-route transmission question, not one mechanism.
- **Studies:** parenting, attachment, modelling, family narratives, violence exposure, socioeconomic conditions, prenatal biology and possible germline pathways.
- **Useful for:** separating social, relational, material, prenatal and biological continuity.
- **Do not infer:** epigenetic inheritance, transmission of specific memories or a direct route merely because similar patterns occur across generations.
- **Current connections:** IC-01 · NG-02 · TB-01 · HPR-12 recurrence · TSR-33 prenatal routes · TSR-37 epigenetics.
- **Name supplied:** Rachel Yehuda.

### TSR-37 · Epigenetics of trauma and stress

- **Role:** molecular and developmental mechanism research family.
- **Supplied text:** begins a list including DNA methylation and glucocorticoid-related processes, then ends mid-sentence. The missing material has not been reconstructed.
- **Useful for:** carefully specified associations or experimental mechanisms involving gene regulation under defined exposures, tissues, developmental periods and generations.
- **Do not infer:** inherited traumatic memory, permanent molecular damage, causal human germline transmission or one whole-person trauma signature.
- **Current connections:** TSR-33 prenatal stress · TSR-36 intergenerational routes · NG-02 development · TB-01 boundaries.

## Highest-impact terminology decisions

| Avoid using as shorthand | Prefer |
| --- | --- |
| Trauma as explanation | Name the exposure, response, mechanism, diagnosis or outcome |
| The body remembers | Conditioning, habit, sensitisation, memory, bodily prediction, procedural learning or another specified process |
| Intrauterine trauma | Prenatal stress/adversity plus the measured maternal, placental or fetal pathway |
| Preverbal trauma memory | Preverbal stress, pain or associative learning unless memory type is directly measured |
| Learned helplessness | Controllability, escape learning and later passivity, with species and design stated |
| Amygdala fear centre | Threat-related learning and response processes involving distributed systems |
| Trauma stored in the body | Specify persistent bodily response, pain, interoceptive change, conditioning, memory or motor pattern |
| Dysregulated nervous system | Name the costly, persistent, context-insensitive or impaired process |
| Intergenerational trauma biology | Separate social, relational, material, prenatal and possible germline pathways |
| Fight, flight or freeze | Name the observed behaviour or measured defensive process and its timing |

## Missing research families to add

The supplied map is strong in developmental and defensive science. The following families would make it more complete.

### Candidate A · Acute stress reactions, adjustment and prolonged grief

These should remain distinct from PTSD and from ordinary early reactions after danger or loss. Diagnostic systems separate acute stress reaction, adjustment disorder and prolonged grief because exposure, symptom pattern, duration and impairment differ.

### Candidate B · Moral injury

Research examines lasting distress related to perpetrating, failing to prevent, witnessing or being betrayed around acts that violate deeply held moral expectations. Moral injury is not identical to PTSD and is not uniformly a diagnosis.

### Candidate C · Institutional betrayal and institutional courage

This examines how institutions may worsen harm by failing to prevent it, responding inadequately, retaliating or protecting themselves—and how transparent, accountable responses can alter outcomes. It connects directly to HPR-06 epistemic harm and HPR-08 feedback.

### Candidate D · Discrimination, racism and identity-targeted traumatic stress

Interpersonal violence, discrimination, structural exposure, vigilance and collective or historical conditions should not be reduced to individual appraisal. Measures, populations and political context require careful handling.

### Candidate E · War, displacement, torture, disaster and mass violence

These literatures keep actual danger, loss, community disruption, displacement, material conditions and collective recovery visible. They also prevent clinical symptoms from becoming the whole account.

### Candidate F · Medical trauma and traumatic brain injury boundary

Medical events, intensive care, invasive treatment, birth, serious illness and injury can involve threat, pain, helplessness and altered memory. Traumatic brain injury is a physical neurological injury and must not be conflated with psychological trauma even when both occur.

### Candidate G · Secondary traumatic stress, vicarious trauma and burnout

These overlapping terms require separation by exposure, occupation, symptom pattern and outcome. Burnout is not simply secondary trauma, and indirect exposure does not produce one expected response.

### Candidate H · Sleep, circadian disruption and nightmares

Sleep is both an outcome and a possible maintenance or recovery process. Trauma-related nightmares, insomnia, sleep fragmentation and circadian disruption warrant their own evidence family.

### Candidate I · Neuroimmune and inflammatory research

This may help examine long-term physiological associations, but findings are heterogeneous and easily overgeneralised. Tissue, timing, medication, health, sleep and socioeconomic conditions matter.

### Candidate J · Pain, somatic symptoms and functional impairment

Trauma exposure can be associated with pain and bodily symptoms through several pathways. This area requires protection against both psychologising physical illness and assuming one biological trauma signature.

### Candidate K · Resilience, recovery and non-development of disorder

Most exposed people do not follow one pathological trajectory. Recovery research should examine timing, support, resources, meaning, safety, treatment, spontaneous change and functioning without turning resilience into a moral trait.

### Candidate L · Treatment mechanisms and comparative outcomes

Trauma-focused cognitive-behavioural therapies, exposure-based approaches, EMDR and other interventions should be assessed through trials, comparative effectiveness, mechanisms, harms, dropout, accessibility and population fit. A treatment helping does not by itself validate every theory used to explain it.

### Candidate M · Post-traumatic growth and meaning reconstruction

Reported positive change can coexist with distress and loss. Research must distinguish perceived growth, measured behavioural change, cultural expectations and pressure to find benefit in suffering.

### Candidate N · Substance use, self-harm and comorbidity

These outcomes may function as coping, avoidance, regulation, habit or independent disorder and require their own evidence. Trauma association does not establish causation in an individual case.

## Initial authoritative anchors

- [WHO · ICD-11 Clinical Descriptions and Diagnostic Requirements](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/375767/9789240077263-eng.pdf?sequence=1) — keeps PTSD, CPTSD and other stress-associated diagnoses distinct.
- [NIMH · Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/post-traumatic-stress-disorder-ptsd) — diagnostic overview, course, impairment and the observation that exposure does not mean everyone develops PTSD.
- [CDC · Adverse Childhood Experiences](https://www.cdc.gov/aces/about/index.html) — exposure definitions, public-health context and prevention.
- [Center on the Developing Child · toxic stress framework](https://developingchild.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Policy_Framework.pdf) — prolonged stress activation in the absence of buffering support.
- [McLaughlin and colleagues · dimensional adversity](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5070918/) — threat and deprivation as an alternative to cumulative-risk-only approaches.
- [Maier & Seligman · Learned Helplessness at Fifty](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4920136/) — major revision of the original learned-helplessness account around control.
- [Fanselow · Predatory Imminence and RDoC threat constructs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9788377/) — connects imminence with changing defensive behaviour while preserving model boundaries.
- [LeDoux & Pine · two-system framework](https://www.cns.nyu.edu/labs/ledouxlab/pdf/LeDoux%20Pine%20Two%20Systsem.pdf) — separates defensive survival circuitry from conscious fear.

## Source-level review order

1. **Clarify the central words:** trauma, adversity, stress, persistent organisation and recovery.
2. **Build exposure records:** maltreatment subtypes, threat, deprivation, unpredictability, prenatal conditions and actual ongoing danger.
3. **Build learning records:** controllability, generalisation, extinction, safety discrimination and context renewal.
4. **Build response records:** stress physiology, defensive repertoires, pain, dissociation, sleep and recovery.
5. **Build developmental relationship records:** attachment, buffering, co-regulation, emotion socialisation, invalidation and neglect.
6. **Build access records:** interoception, alexithymia, dissociation and conscious participation.
7. **Build diagnosis records:** PTSD and CPTSD without using either as the explanation for all trauma-related change.
8. **Build intergenerational records:** social, relational, material, prenatal and molecular routes separately.
9. **Build recovery and treatment records:** non-development of disorder, resilience, treatment, harm, access and fit.
10. **Connect only reviewed claims** into active Inner Compass, Gradient and HPR pages.

The value of this map is not the number of trauma theories it contains. It is the ability to say which part of the human process each body of research can help us examine—and where its explanation stops.
