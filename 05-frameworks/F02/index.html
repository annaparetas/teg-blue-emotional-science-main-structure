The question F2 owns

What is available at birth, how does it widen, and what does experience teach?

What capacities are available at birth, how do they widen across development, and how may repeated relational outcomes shape whether emotional information can be noticed, identified, expressed, trusted, used for action and included in the child's developing understanding of who they are?

Seven separable layers

Condition detection and signal formation; bodily representation; subjective access; identification and differentiation; expression; action; beliefs, self-explanations and learned expectations about emotion, relationship and worth. These can change differently. A child may feel but hide, notice but not identify, understand but be unable to act, or retain access in one relationship while suppressing expression in another. "Emotion override" must name what was overridden; it never implies the emotion ceased.

Calibration, narrowly

"Developmental Calibration" implied blank settings configured once. The word stays only when its target is named: calibration of a prediction, a recruitment threshold, an attentional priority, a danger–safety discrimination, or an expectation that action will change an outcome. Never the person's whole organisation.

The core insight

The child is affected, learns and acts before the child can explain.

The developmental asymmetry

A child can be affected, learn relational consequences and organise action before they can fully explain what is happening, compare accounts, leave the relationship, or understand the adult as a separate person with their own history, limits and motives.

Repeated outcomes therefore become part of what the child learns about a signal. Expression may bring support, dismissal, punishment, withdrawal, role reversal or no effective change. Over time those outcomes shape what receives attention, what feels relationally costly, which actions appear viable and what later explanations become available. A possible route, not a universal account and not a verdict on any caregiver, culture or adult.

Capacities widen

Development

Embodied detection and response; learning, memory and prediction; communication and language; emotion differentiation; self–other representation; perspective and reflection; deliberate comparison, choice and revision.

At the same time, experience teaches

Relational learning

What can be noticed; what can be shown; whose account carries authority; which actions change anything; what preserves connection; what kind of self appears acceptable. Development and social learning happen together, inside relationships, cultures and material conditions.

What the child begins with

A newborn is not a blank organism waiting for settings. From birth there are active capacities for internal and external detection, bodily coordination, arousal and distress, orienting, approach and withdrawal, communication, learning, memory and expectation. What is not yet present are adult forms of language, explanation, perspective-taking and deliberate reflective choice. Dependence matters: connection to adults affects food, shelter, protection, comfort, orientation and access to the world, and the child need not reason about that for repeated outcomes to become biologically and behaviourally important.

Dependent regulation

The infant regulates through its own organism, within conditions others provide.

Borrowing regulation, translated

The infant does not literally borrow another person's nervous system. The infant regulates through their own developing sensory, autonomic, endocrine, metabolic, motor and neural processes within conditions another person often has to provide or change: warmth, nourishment, contact, movement, sensory demand, protection, rest.

When connection is also protection

Early dependence couples the two problem-spaces F1 keeps distinct. If showing a feeling appears to threaten the relationship through which care and protection remain available, the child faces two competing demands: keep the organism's information visible, or preserve access to the person on whom they depend. Repeatedly protecting connection can make concealment, appeasement, monitoring, role performance or an acceptable explanation easier to recruit. Not a conscious choice of relationship over self; expression, action, consequence and access to care are learnt together while independence and reflective comparison are still developing. Later, relational restoration, organism-level Return and Return without relational restoration remain three different outcomes.

A broad developmental timeline

Windows that overlap, not deadlines.

Capacities becoming more available, not maturity scores. Culture, health, disability, neurodivergence, language, relationships, stress and opportunity change timing and form. The full table is in the review.

ESS is active from birth and develops in precision, differentiation, weighting and relation to action. CLS has early foundations in learning, memory and prediction; language-based comparison, explanation and metacognition widen gradually. ME may become more able to notice, name, compare, question, choose and revisit. No system waits for another to finish; none is a maturational stage, and F2 does not date the beginning of consciousness.

When emotion is treated as noise, error or wrongdoing

Emotion invalidation.

Working description

Repeated interactions in which a child's emotional experience or report is treated as meaningless, false, excessive, shameful, dangerous, inconvenient or evidence that the child is the problem, without helping them examine the signal, the situation, the interpretation and the action separately.

Caregivers are one source. Peers, schools, health and care systems, religious and community settings, media and cultural rules also teach which emotions are credible, permitted, shameful or ignored. Invalidation is not every disagreement, correction or limit. An adult can recognise fear while checking for danger, acknowledge anger while preventing harm, or limit expression while helping the child keep access to what the feeling communicates.

The distinction that must stay available

The experience can be real, the first interpretation can be incomplete, and the behaviour still requires its own evaluation.

The acceptable character

A practised organisation, not a false person.

Working definition

The acceptable character is a practised organisation of expression, behaviour, attention and self-explanation built around the version of the child that appears most likely to preserve care, approval, safety or belonging.

Not necessarily deliberate. It can grow from thousands of ordinary comparisons between what the child feels and what adults say is happening, what the child needs and what the relationship tolerates, what brings warmth and what brings withdrawal. The one who is calm, cheerful, grateful, mature, useful, strong, agreeable or never in need; the one who is entertaining, invisible, competent, endlessly understanding or responsible for everyone's feelings. Relationship-preserving roles, not personality types.

  1. A signal formsEmotional or bodily.
  2. ConsequenceExpression, need or action meets a repeated relational outcome.
  3. DetectionThe child detects what appears to preserve connection or reduce danger.
  4. PracticePermitted behaviour and explanations receive practice; some signals stay private, mistrusted or redirected.
  5. Identity"This is what I must be" becomes easier to represent than "this is what I learned to show here".

There is no hidden real self intact behind a mask. The developing person is real throughout. What can diverge are signal, felt access, public expression, available action, relational role and self-explanation. Nor does F2 conclude that the adults did not love the child: adults can feel love while making access to approval, calm or closeness conditional in practice. Both systems participate: ESS keeps registering need, hurt, alarm, anger, longing and relief; CLS learns the names, rules, ideals and predictions about what a lovable person is; ME participates from what is available, sometimes performing the role and sometimes experiencing it as who I am. A solution first organised around a relationship can become self-maintaining without becoming the person's essence.

One possible handoff into Chronic participation

The signal remains; what changes is around it.

Fusion

Feeling and anticipated rejection arrive together.

Reduced access

The feeling becomes faint, delayed, numb or hard to differentiate.

Performed emotion

An acceptable display replaces or conceals what is privately available.

Intensification

Quieter communication fails repeatedly; mobilisation becomes easier to recruit or slower to settle.

Borrowed certainty

Another person's account becomes easier to use than one's own incomplete or costly information.

Restricted action

Help, protest, boundaries, grief or repair appear unavailable, unsafe or ineffective.

A developmental possibility, not a causal chain. Similar patterns arise through continuing danger, pain, illness, sensory or communication mismatch, discrimination, material instability, repeated loss or interrupted recovery. A present Chronic pattern cannot be used to reconstruct a childhood. Two opposite risks, connected developmentally: feeling-fact collapse and emotional-state disconnection. Emotionally literate development refuses both, keeping the signal as information, testing its interpretation, and keeping behaviour and impact on their own plane.

The regulation warning

Silence is not co-regulation.

Invalidation may reduce visible conflict by producing silence, compliance, concealment or an acceptable display. F2 does not call that co-regulation or successful settling without further evidence. It may be imposed stability: the interaction became easier for the adult while the child carried the uncertainty, inhibition or recovery cost. The acceptable character can be a regulation route with real relief; relief does not establish that the condition changed, the emotion became understandable, a viable action existed or recovery completed.

Six questions, not one

Did the child remain able to recognise the experience? Could the condition and interpretation be examined? Was a safe and effective action available? Did the interaction provide credible information, support or repair? Could recruitment reduce, recovery continue and wider participation return? Did the child need to become a particular version of themselves for connection to remain available?

Handoff

F2 ends with shaped possibilities; F3 begins with the adult now.

  1. Inherited repertoireF1.
  2. Developmental conditions and repeated outcomesF2.
  3. Access, expression, action availability, expectationWhat F2 hands on.
  4. Present information and organisationModels 1 and 2.
  5. Adult maintenance, revision and route selectionF3.

F2 can explain how a route became available or costly. It cannot reconstruct a person's history from the route they use now. Development also happens inside cultural display rules, gender, race, class, disability and neurodivergence expectations, institutions, material safety, unequal power, peers and teachers, and in interactions where children also shape the socialisation. A limit on expression is not automatically invalidation; the question is what remains readable, expressible and actionable, and whether support and repair remain possible.

Boundaries and provenance

Retired wording, and the grounding route.

Narrowed or retired

"A child is born with the architecture but not the settings." Relationships "calibrate what the nervous system treats as safety or threat" as one setting. One caregiver transition as decisive. Signals "designed to complete through another person". Uncompleted signals "simply accumulate". "What the caregiver cannot hold becomes a structural gap." The young child automatically attributing activation to the self. State-to-state transmission. "Experience sets thresholds" without naming which. Allostatic load as "the carried cost" of override.
