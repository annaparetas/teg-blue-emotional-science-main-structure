F9 · Neurodivergent Variation, Disability and Environmental Fit

How differences in sensing, attention, communication, movement, learning and recovery interact with environments to shape access, participation, disability and cost. The same person may communicate, work, learn, relate or recover very differently when noise, pace, ambiguity, interruption, movement, format, support and social expectation change. Performance cannot be read as a fixed measure of the person without examining the conditions in which it appeared.

The decision

Keep F9; reframe it around the interaction among variation, health, task, environment, support and power.

"Neurodivergent Configuration and Adaptive Fit" began with a useful observation: the same person performs differently as conditions change, so performance cannot be read as a fixed measure of the person. The observation does not require a choice between difference and disability. A variation may be neutral or valuable in one setting, disabling in another and intrinsically painful or limiting in a third. An inaccessible environment can create or intensify disability; a health condition or impairment can also matter even in a supportive environment. Both may be true at once.

F9 therefore resists two reductions: locating every difficulty inside the person, and locating every difficulty outside them. It asks what changes when the person, task, environment, support or relationship changes, and whether the outcome improves in a way that matters to the person. "Configuration" is retired as the name of a neurodivergent person-type and reserved for explicitly defined TEG-Blue access or organisation variables.

The question F9 owns

How do variation, health, task, environment, interpretation, support and power interact?

How do neurocognitive variation, bodily and mental health, task demands, environmental design, social interpretation, support and power interact to shape access, participation, cost and disability?

F9 owns questions about

What the person is trying or required to do; which sensory, attentional, cognitive, communicative, motor, interoceptive or recovery demands the task creates; where capacities are available, variable, effortful, context-bound or supported; which physical, social, informational, institutional and attitudinal conditions help or hinder; whether the environment assumes one pace, channel, posture, communication style or form of social performance; what access need, accommodation or redesign might change the outcome; how masking, camouflaging, compensation, suppression and ordinary adaptation differ; whether outward performance conceals unsustainable effort, distress or delayed cost; whether fatigue or loss of access requires assessment; how diagnosis, self-identification, culture, language, class, race, gender and service access affect recognition; and what the person identifies as a meaningful outcome.

F9 does not own

A diagnosis, a biological essence, a complete description of a person, a hierarchy of nervous systems, a universal neurodivergent profile, a character judgement, a prediction of empathy or behaviour, a treatment plan, a legal determination of disability, or the assumption that every difficulty disappears through design. The unit of inquiry is the interaction. The person remains more than the interaction.

The governing interaction

F9 does not begin with "What is wrong with this person?"

Nor does it assume that the person has no impairment or health need. It begins with five factors in interaction and reads the outcome from them together.

  1. Person and current organisationDiagnoses, identities, health, strengths, difficulties and lived description.
  2. Task and expected outcomeWhat is required, at what pace, through which channel, by whose standard.
  3. Physical, sensory and informational environmentWhat the setting assumes and what it makes available.
  4. Social interpretation, rules and powerWhat counts as normal, competent or credible here.
  5. Available support, accommodation and recoveryWhat can change, and whether recovery time exists.
  6. OutcomeAccess, performance, participation, cost, wellbeing and disability.

Changing one part of the interaction may change the outcome. The result then says that the changed factor contributed under those conditions. It does not, by itself, reveal one underlying configuration or prove that every remaining difficulty has the same source.

Working vocabulary

Terms with their boundaries attached.

TermWorking meaningBoundary
NeurodiversityVariation in human neurocognitive development and functioning; also a political and scholarly paradigm that challenges one idealised norm.Not one diagnosis, one neurological mechanism or a claim that every variation is neutral in every context.
Neurodivergent · NeurotypicalA community and identity term for people whose functioning diverges from socially dominant expectations; a relational term for people treated as fitting them.Neither is a formal diagnosis or a validated brain type. ImpairmentA difference or difficulty in body function or structure that may affect activity or wellbeing.Does not determine worth, full capacity, participation or disability without context.
DisabilityRestriction or disadvantage arising through the interaction among bodily or cognitive conditions, activities, participation and environmental or social factors.Neither only an internal defect nor only an external barrier in every case.
Environmental fitThe degree to which current conditions allow a person to access, participate, communicate, recover and meet meaningful demands without unnecessary cost.Task- and person-specific. Comfort, compliance or invisibility does not establish it.
Masking or camouflagingStrategies through which a person hides, suppresses, compensates for or manages the visibility of stigmatised or socially penalised characteristics.Definitions and measures vary. Not every learned social skill, privacy choice or context-sensitive adaptation is masking.
Autistic burnoutAn emerging lived-experience and research construct: severe exhaustion, reduced tolerance and increased difficulty or loss of access reported by some autistic people.Not yet one established biological state, biomarker or automatic explanation for fatigue, regression or functional change.

Also in use, each with its own boundary: access need, which need not be justified by appearing unable in every setting; accommodation or reasonable adjustment, which is not an advantage, a cure or a substitute for wider accessibility; universal design, which serves no one completely and does not remove the need for specific accommodation; support need, which varies across settings and removes no agency in unrelated domains; compensation, which may be helpful, costly or both and should not be removed in the name of authenticity; participation, defined with the person rather than by normative performance; and agency, for which speech, speed, independence and conventional presentation are not prerequisites.

The eight F9 construct families

A way to locate a barrier precisely enough to change it.

FamilyGoverning questionBoundary
F9-A · Person, profile and self-descriptionWhat diagnoses, identities, health conditions, strengths, difficulties, preferences and lived descriptions are relevant to this situation?No label describes the whole person, and group-level findings do not settle an individual's profile.
F9-B · Task, demand and outcomeWhat is the person being asked to do, at what pace, for how long, through which channel and according to whose standard of success?Difficulty cannot be interpreted before the task and its hidden demands are named.
F9-C · Sensory, attentional, cognitive and motor accessWhich aspects of detection, filtering, switching, memory, planning, initiation, movement, language, interoception or recovery are helping or constraining participation?Distinct construct families; "sensory processing" and "executive function" should not become catch-all explanations.
F9-D · Communication and mutual interpretationWhich communication forms are available, what does each person expect and where does meaning become lost between people or systems?Misunderstanding may be mutual without power, responsibility or burden becoming equal.
F9-E · Environment, barriers and facilitatorsWhat in the physical, sensory, social, informational, temporal, digital or institutional environment changes access and cost?An environmental contribution does not prove that all difficulty is external or that one adjustment suits everyone.
F9-F · Adaptation, masking and cumulative costWhat strategies maintain outward performance, why are they used, what agency do they provide and what immediate or delayed cost follows?Effort and distress should be measured rather than assumed; unmasking should not be prescribed where it would reduce safety or choice.
F9-G · Overload, fatigue, health and recoveryWhat changed in energy, skills, tolerance, sleep, mood, pain, daily functioning and support needs, and what explanations require assessment?Burnout language must not delay evaluation of depression, catatonia, sleep disorder, medication effects, pain, infection or other medical and psychological conditions.
F9-H · Accessibility, support and outcome testingWhich redesign, accommodation, technology, communication support, assistance or demand change improves meaningful participation with acceptable cost?Visible performance alone is insufficient; benefit, autonomy, sustainability, safety and person-defined outcome all matter.

These families do not form a profile score or diagnostic instrument. They locate a barrier precisely enough to change it and then observe what follows.

Four separate questions

Difference, disability, distress and mismatch must remain separate.

The old map moved quickly from variation to the claim that much of what is called disorder is an environmental gap. That direction corrects a long history of treating normative difference as personal failure. It still needs a more careful structure.

Difference

Is this a variation from an expected or statistically common pattern?

Impairment or distress

Does the person experience pain, loss of function, reduced access or another difficulty that matters even in supportive conditions?

Disability

Does the interaction with barriers restrict activity, participation, rights or opportunity?

Mismatch

Would a different task, environment, communication partner, pace or support change the outcome?

The answers combine in different ways. A person may value an aspect of their neurodivergence and still need substantial support. They may experience intrinsic difficulty and disabling exclusion. They may be distressed mainly because other people punish a harmless difference. No one answer cancels the others.

Environmental change

An intervention and a source of evidence.

The old map proposed a clear test: change the environment and observe what changes. F9 preserves the method and narrows the conclusion. Reduce unpredictable noise while keeping the task the same. Offer written information as well as speech. Allow more processing time without reducing intellectual demand. Make timing, sequence and expectations explicit. Reduce unnecessary switching and interruption. Permit movement, sensory tools or a quieter location. Use augmentative and alternative communication. Separate social performance from the competence being assessed. Provide a predictable recovery period after sustained demand. Change the interaction partner's communication as well as asking the neurodivergent person to adapt.

Observe more than task completion: accuracy, comprehension, autonomy, distress, energy cost, delayed recovery, communication, retention, participation and whether the person wants to repeat the arrangement. If performance improves, the environment or support contributed. If it does not, the adjustment may have been wrong, insufficient or aimed at the wrong barrier. Neither result should become a judgement about motivation.

The double empathy problem

Mutual difference does not erase unequal burden.

The double empathy problem corrects one-sided deficit accounts of autistic communication. Some studies have found stronger rapport or information transfer within same-neurotype interactions than in mixed autistic and non-autistic interactions, which suggests that communication can depend on the pairing and context, not only on an autistic person's isolated skill. The correction must not become a universal symmetry claim.

People within the same diagnostic group remain highly varied. Familiarity, language, relationship, culture, task and power also shape understanding. A professional, employer, teacher or caregiver usually has greater responsibility to make communication accessible, and the person with less institutional credibility may carry the greater cost of mutual misunderstanding. Some misunderstandings are ordinary; others involve neglect, coercion, prejudice or refusal to use available communication. Mutual difference does not make impact, authority or responsibility equal. F9 relocates the question from "Does this person have empathy?" to "Who understood what, through which channel, under what conditions, and what changed when each side adjusted?"

Masking and camouflaging

Route-specific language, not one act of override.

The old map defined masking as sustained override with a running physiological cost. Research and lived accounts support serious concern about social pressure, concealment, effort and poorer wellbeing. The construct remains broader and less settled than that sentence suggests. Camouflaging research includes suppressing or hiding visible characteristics; imitating expected expressions, gestures or conversational timing; rehearsing scripts or rules; deliberate compensatory strategies; enduring sensory or social demand without communicating its cost; managing disclosure, privacy and stigma; and adapting in ways that provide access, employment, safety or connection. These routes may be partly conscious or automatic, wanted or compelled, helpful or harmful. Some are not unique to autistic people. Measurement often relies on self-report or a discrepancy between internal report and outward observation, and samples have not represented the full autistic population evenly.

What F9 asks about a strategy

What is being hidden, changed or compensated for? Why is the strategy being used? What choice and benefit does it provide? What immediate and delayed cost is observed? What would happen if it stopped in this setting? Could the environment change without removing the person's chosen strategy?

The aim is not compulsory unmasking. People may have good reasons to manage disclosure or presentation. The aim is to reduce unnecessary coercion and cost while preserving safety, privacy, skill and choice.

Autistic burnout

Autistic burnout must not become a Gradient Position.

Participatory and qualitative research has made autistic burnout visible as severe exhaustion, reduced tolerance to stimulus and loss or reduction of previously available skills. Later reviews support its importance while identifying limited epidemiology, unsettled measurement and samples weighted towards White, female, late-diagnosed adults with relatively strong verbal access. The old phrase "reorganisation of the resting state" is not yet established, and connecting burnout directly to allostatic overload risks turning a broad stress concept into a specific mechanism without matching evidence.

Levels F9 keeps distinct

The person's account of exhaustion, reduced tolerance and increased support need; observed changes in daily functioning and access; sustained demands, masking, exclusion, illness and recovery opportunity; clinical differential assessment where change is severe or sudden; physiological mechanisms, which remain an open research question; accommodations and support that may help; and the Gradient organisation present at a particular moment.

Autistic burnout is not X, Z, Chronic organisation or one fixed baseline shift. A person in burnout may move across Gradient Positions while remaining exhausted and disabled. Rest, solitude, sensory relief, demand reduction, care and community support may help some people; no single route should be promised or withheld.

Connections and handoffs

An iterative access question, and four handoffs.

From F1, F2 and F3

Human populations contain substantial variation, and development may alter skills, thresholds, strategies and access over time. Evolution does not sort people into superior, defective or specialised nervous-system types; neurodivergence is not simply maladaptive calibration. F9 supplies F3 with alternative explanations: a repeated response is not false coherence when the environment keeps recreating the same barrier.

From F4 to F7

Rules decide which pace, posture, communication and work style count as competent; ableism, stigma and unequal credibility shape recognition and cost; diagnostic, gendered, racialised and communication biases shape whose report is believed; forced normalisation, restraint, seclusion and withdrawal of support restrict agency. Neurodivergence or disability never establishes dangerousness, innocence or acceptable conduct.

Model 1 and Relational Capacity Configurations

Sensory thresholds, attention, language, interoception and communication may affect what information becomes consciously usable. Different access is not lesser humanity, failed intuition or evidence of moral character. A TEG-Blue access configuration is not an autistic, ADHD or personality profile.

The Gradient and Chronic organisation

The same organism-level lenses apply while allowing person-specific thresholds, demands and supports. No diagnosis, identity or sensory profile maps directly to X, A, A↔B, B, C, D or Z. Ongoing mismatch, repeated overload and interrupted recovery may contribute to recurrence; autism, ADHD, dyslexia or another neurodivergence is not Chronic organisation.

F8 receives environmental change, accommodation and reduced cost as possible recovery routes; unmasking is not completed recovery, and being accepted is not the only route through which capacity may return. F10 receives the observation that children and caregivers may have different access, sensory and communication needs; difference must not be read as failed attachment, and family support must not become normalisation.

The iterative access question that replaces accommodation-as-afterthought

Name the person's goal and the actual task. Identify sensory, cognitive, communication, motor, social and recovery demands. Locate barriers, supports, health needs, norms and power. Change one or more conditions with the person's participation. Observe access, outcome, autonomy, effort, delayed cost and repeatability. Retain, revise or replace the support.

To health and diagnostic assessment

When the person wants assessment, when a diagnosis could open useful support, or when pain, sudden change, severe exhaustion, skill loss, catatonia, sleep disturbance, mood change or another health concern requires investigation.

To accessibility, education, employment and service design

When avoidable barriers can be removed for many people at once while individual adjustments remain available.

To Model 1, Model 2 and F8

When the question concerns changing sensory access, organisation, recovery, participation or cost within the person under specified conditions.

To HPR and safeguarding

When difference or dependency is being used to discredit consent, justify coercion, remove agency, punish harmless behaviour or conceal neglect and abuse.

Boundaries and provenance

Narrowed wording, and the grounding route.

Narrowed, revised or retired

"The regulatory architecture is universal." Inputs vary while one architecture stays the same. Much of disorder is an environmental gap. Environmental change tests the claim in a way personality claims cannot. One "configuration" performed over another. Masking as a running physiological cost. Burnout as a reorganisation of the resting state. Rest alone does not reverse burnout. Recovery requires being received. Environments simply "built for variation". Each is replaced by a narrower statement: name the specific process, examine the whole person, task and environment interaction, separate difference from impairment, disability, distress and mismatch, treat environmental manipulation as one source of within-person evidence, measure subjective, behavioural, health and recovery outcomes separately, keep mechanisms and differential assessment open, promise no single recovery route, and pair broad accessibility with routes for individual adjustment.

Grounding fields

Neurodiversity studies and critical autism studies; disability studies and crip theory; autistic, ADHD and other neurodivergent scholarship and activism; developmental psychology and psychopathology; clinical psychology, psychiatry, neurology and paediatrics; neuropsychology and cognitive science; sensory science and perception research; affective science and interoception; communication sciences, speech and language therapy and AAC research; occupational therapy and rehabilitation; human factors and work design; education and Universal Design for Learning research; accessibility, inclusive design and human-computer interaction; public health; sociology and anthropology; organisational psychology; social work and independent-living research; disability, education and employment law; human-rights scholarship and ethics; measurement science; implementation research; economics and social policy; participatory and qualitative research. Neurodivergent and disabled people must participate in deciding which questions and outcomes matter. Priority evidence: within-person environmental and task manipulations, longitudinal and naturalistic studies, co-designed intervention research, communication-partner studies, accessibility and implementation outcomes, participatory qualitative work, and measures of effort and delayed recovery alongside visible performance. Sampling must improve, and proxy evidence must not silently replace the person's own communication.

Old map title: Neurodivergent Configuration and Adaptive Fit. Evidence status of the original source list: the old alignment combined a political paradigm, disability models, interaction research, self-report and qualitative studies, stress physiology and an educational design framework, each contributing at a different level. The neurodiversity paradigm shapes authorship, language and interpretation but validates no mechanism; disability models differ and do not deny impairment; the WHO ICF is a classification vocabulary, not a causal model; the double empathy problem rests on bounded studies; camouflaging and autistic burnout research report heterogeneous definitions, measures and samples; allostatic load cannot identify burnout or prove a changed baseline; Universal Design for Learning is education-specific; reasonable accommodation and universal design also carry legal meanings that depend on jurisdiction. WHO, NICE and UN guidance support F9's interactional direction; they do not establish one TEG-Blue mechanism.
