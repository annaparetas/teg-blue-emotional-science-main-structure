## Decision

**Keep F9 as a Foundational Framework, but reframe it around the interaction among neurocognitive variation, health, task demands, environment, support and power.**

Provisional title:

> **Neurodivergent Variation, Disability and Environmental Fit**

Suggested explanatory line:

> How differences in sensing, attention, communication, movement, learning and recovery interact with environments to shape access, participation, disability and cost.

The original framework begins with a useful observation. The same person may communicate, work, learn, relate or recover very differently when noise, pace, ambiguity, interruption, movement, format, support and social expectation change. This means performance cannot be read as a fixed measure of the person without examining the conditions in which it appeared.

The observation does not require a choice between *difference* and *disability*. A variation may be neutral or valuable in one setting, disabling in another and intrinsically painful or limiting in a third. An inaccessible environment can create or intensify disability. A health condition or impairment can also matter even in a supportive environment. Both may be true at once.

F9 should therefore resist two reductions: locating every difficulty inside the person, and locating every difficulty outside them. It asks what changes when the person, task, environment, support or relationship changes—and whether the outcome improves in a way that matters to the person.

## The question F9 owns

> How do neurocognitive variation, bodily and mental health, task demands, environmental design, social interpretation, support and power interact to shape access, participation, cost and disability?

F9 owns questions concerning:

- what the person is trying or required to do;
- which sensory, attentional, cognitive, communicative, motor, interoceptive or recovery demands the task creates;
- where capacities are available, variable, effortful, context-bound or supported;
- which physical, social, informational, institutional and attitudinal conditions help or hinder participation;
- whether the environment assumes one pace, channel, posture, communication style or form of social performance;
- what access need, accommodation, assistive support or redesign might change the outcome;
- how masking, camouflaging, compensation, suppression and ordinary adaptation differ;
- whether outward performance conceals unsustainable effort, distress or delayed cost;
- whether fatigue, loss of access or increased support needs require medical, psychological, practical or environmental assessment;
- how diagnosis, self-identification, culture, language, class, race, gender and service access affect recognition and support;
- what the person identifies as a meaningful outcome.

It does not own a diagnosis, biological essence, complete description of a person, hierarchy of nervous systems, universal neurodivergent profile, character judgement, prediction of empathy or behaviour, treatment plan, legal determination of disability or assumption that every difficulty disappears through design.

## The governing interaction

F9 should not begin with the question *What is wrong with this person?* It should also not assume that the person has no impairment or health need.

It begins here:

```text
person and current organisation
        ×
task and expected outcome
        ×
physical, sensory and informational environment
        ×
social interpretation, rules and power
        ×
available support, accommodation and recovery
        ↓
access · performance · participation · cost · wellbeing · disability
```

Changing one part of the interaction may change the outcome. The result then tells us that the changed factor contributed under those conditions. It does not, by itself, reveal one underlying configuration or prove that every remaining difficulty has the same source.

The unit of inquiry is the interaction. The person remains more than the interaction.

## Working vocabulary

| Term | Working meaning | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Neurodiversity** | Variation in human neurocognitive development and functioning; also used within a political and scholarly paradigm that challenges one idealised norm | It is not one diagnosis, one neurological mechanism or a claim that every variation is neutral in every context |
| **Neurodivergent** | A community and identity term for people whose neurocognitive functioning diverges from socially dominant expectations | It is not a formal diagnosis, homogeneous biological class or complete account of support needs |
| **Neurotypical** | A relational term for people treated as fitting dominant neurocognitive expectations | It should not be treated as one scientifically validated brain type; *non-autistic* is more precise when autism status is the actual comparison |
| **Configuration** | In TEG-Blue, a stated arrangement of access or organisation variables | F9 should not use the word as a synonym for diagnosis, identity, fixed trait profile or hidden nervous-system type |
| **Impairment** | A difference or difficulty in body function or structure that may affect activity or wellbeing | It does not determine the person's worth, full capacity, participation or disability without context |
| **Disability** | Restriction or disadvantage arising through the interaction among bodily or cognitive conditions, activities, participation and environmental or social factors | It is neither only an internal defect nor only an external barrier in every case |
| **Environmental fit** | The degree to which current conditions allow a person to access, participate, communicate, recover and meet meaningful demands without unnecessary cost | Fit is task- and person-specific; comfort, compliance or invisibility does not establish it |
| **Access need** | A condition, format, support or adjustment required for meaningful entry, understanding, participation or communication | It need not be justified through appearing unable in every setting |
| **Accommodation or reasonable adjustment** | An individual change intended to remove a barrier or make participation possible on an equal basis | It is not an unfair advantage, a cure or a substitute for wider accessibility |
| **Universal design** | Designing environments, products, information or learning for broad use from the outset, while preserving routes for individual adjustment | No universal design serves every person or removes the need for specific accommodation |
| **Support need** | Assistance required for safety, communication, daily life, participation, decision-making, health or recovery | It may vary across settings and time and does not remove agency or competence in unrelated domains |
| **Masking or camouflaging** | Strategies through which a person hides, suppresses, compensates for or manages the visibility of stigmatised or socially penalised characteristics | Definitions and measures vary; not every learned social skill, privacy choice or context-sensitive adaptation is masking |
| **Compensation** | Use of another strategy, tool or route to reach an outcome when a usual or expected route is difficult or unavailable | It may be helpful, costly or both and should not automatically be removed in the name of authenticity |
| **Autistic burnout** | An emerging lived-experience and research construct describing severe exhaustion, reduced tolerance and increased difficulty or loss of access reported by some autistic people | It is not yet one established biological state, biomarker or automatic explanation for fatigue, regression or functional change |
| **Participation** | Involvement in meaningful life situations, roles, activities and relationships, defined with the person rather than only by normative performance | Presence, productivity or compliance does not necessarily mean effective or wanted participation |
| **Agency** | The person's practical and supported ability to communicate, choose, refuse, direct support and influence what happens | Speech, speed, independence and conventional social presentation are not prerequisites for agency |

## Eight F9 construct families

| Construct family | Governing question | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **F9-A · Person, profile and self-description** | What diagnoses, identities, health conditions, strengths, difficulties, preferences and lived descriptions are relevant to this situation? | No label describes the whole person, and group-level findings do not settle an individual's profile |
| **F9-B · Task, demand and outcome** | What is the person being asked to do, at what pace, for how long, through which channel and according to whose standard of success? | Difficulty cannot be interpreted before the task and its hidden demands are named |
| **F9-C · Sensory, attentional, cognitive and motor access** | Which aspects of detection, filtering, switching, memory, planning, initiation, movement, language, interoception or recovery are helping or constraining participation? | These are distinct construct families; “sensory processing” and “executive function” should not become catch-all explanations |
| **F9-D · Communication and mutual interpretation** | Which communication forms are available, what does each person expect and where does meaning become lost between people or systems? | Misunderstanding may be mutual without power, responsibility or burden becoming equal |
| **F9-E · Environment, barriers and facilitators** | What in the physical, sensory, social, informational, temporal, digital or institutional environment changes access and cost? | An environmental contribution does not prove that all difficulty is external or that one adjustment suits everyone |
| **F9-F · Adaptation, masking and cumulative cost** | What strategies maintain outward performance, why are they used, what agency do they provide and what immediate or delayed cost follows? | Effort and distress should be measured rather than assumed; unmasking should not be prescribed where it would reduce safety or choice |
| **F9-G · Overload, fatigue, health and recovery** | What changed in energy, skills, tolerance, sleep, mood, pain, daily functioning and support needs, and what explanations require assessment? | Burnout language must not delay evaluation of depression, catatonia, sleep disorder, medication effects, pain, infection or other medical and psychological conditions |
| **F9-H · Accessibility, support and outcome testing** | Which redesign, accommodation, technology, communication support, assistance or demand change improves meaningful participation with acceptable cost? | Visible performance alone is insufficient; benefit, autonomy, sustainability, safety and person-defined outcome all matter |

These families do not form a profile score or diagnostic instrument. They provide a way to locate a barrier precisely enough to change it and then observe what follows.

## Difference, disability, distress and mismatch must remain separate

The source map moves quickly from variation to the claim that much of what is called disorder is an environmental gap. That direction corrects a long history of treating normative difference as personal failure. It still needs a more careful structure.

Four questions should be asked separately:

1. **Difference:** Is this a variation from an expected or statistically common pattern?
2. **Impairment or distress:** Does the person experience pain, loss of function, reduced access or another difficulty that matters even in supportive conditions?
3. **Disability:** Does the interaction with barriers restrict activity, participation, rights or opportunity?
4. **Mismatch:** Would a different task, environment, communication partner, pace or support change the outcome?

The answers can combine in different ways. A person may value an aspect of their neurodivergence and still need substantial support. They may experience intrinsic difficulty and disabling exclusion. They may also be distressed mainly because other people punish a harmless difference.

No one answer cancels the others.

## Environmental change is an intervention and a source of evidence

The source proposes a clear test: change the environment and observe what changes. F9 should preserve this as a method while narrowing the conclusion.

Examples include:

- reduce unpredictable noise while keeping the task the same;
- offer written information as well as speech;
- allow more processing time without reducing intellectual demand;
- make timing, sequence and expectations explicit;
- reduce unnecessary switching and interruption;
- permit movement, sensory tools or a quieter location;
- use augmentative and alternative communication;
- separate social performance from the actual competence being assessed;
- provide a predictable recovery period after sustained demand;
- change the interaction partner's communication as well as asking the neurodivergent person to adapt.

Observe more than task completion. Relevant outcomes include accuracy, comprehension, autonomy, distress, energy cost, delayed recovery, communication, retention, participation and whether the person wants to repeat the arrangement.

If performance improves, the environment or support contributed. If it does not, the adjustment may have been wrong, insufficient or aimed at the wrong barrier. Neither result should become a judgement about motivation.

## Mutual difference does not erase unequal burden

The double empathy problem offers an important correction to one-sided deficit accounts of autistic communication. Some studies have found stronger rapport or information transfer within same-neurotype interactions than in mixed autistic–non-autistic interactions. This suggests that communication can depend on the pairing and context, not only on an autistic person's isolated skill.

The correction must not become a universal symmetry claim.

- People within the same diagnostic group remain highly varied.
- Familiarity, language, relationship, culture, task and power also shape understanding.
- A professional, employer, teacher or caregiver usually has greater responsibility to make communication accessible.
- The person with less institutional credibility may carry the greater cost of mutual misunderstanding.
- Some misunderstandings are ordinary; others involve neglect, coercion, prejudice or refusal to use available communication.
- Mutual difference does not make impact, authority or responsibility equal.

F9 can therefore relocate the communication question from *Does this person have empathy?* to *Who understood what, through which channel, under what conditions, and what changed when each side adjusted?*

## Masking and camouflaging require route-specific language

The original map defines masking as sustained override with a running physiological cost. Research and lived accounts support serious concern about social pressure, concealment, effort and poorer wellbeing. The construct remains broader and less settled than the sentence suggests.

Camouflaging research includes at least:

- suppressing or hiding visible characteristics;
- imitating expected expressions, gestures or conversational timing;
- rehearsing scripts or rules;
- using deliberate compensatory strategies;
- enduring sensory or social demand without communicating its cost;
- managing disclosure, privacy and stigma;
- adapting in ways that provide access, employment, safety or connection.

These routes may be partly conscious or automatic, wanted or compelled, helpful or harmful. Some are not unique to autistic people. Current measurement often relies on self-report or a discrepancy between internal report and outward observation, and samples have not represented the full autistic population evenly.

F9 should ask:

```text
What is being hidden, changed or compensated for?
Why is the strategy being used?
What choice and benefit does it provide?
What immediate and delayed cost is observed?
What would happen if it stopped in this setting?
Could the environment change without removing the person's chosen strategy?
```

The aim is not compulsory unmasking. People may have good reasons to manage disclosure or presentation. The aim is to reduce unnecessary coercion and cost while preserving safety, privacy, skill and choice.

## Autistic burnout must not become a Gradient Position

Participatory and qualitative research has made autistic burnout visible as an experience of severe exhaustion, reduced tolerance to stimulus and loss or reduction of previously available skills. Later reviews support its importance while also identifying limited epidemiology, unsettled measurement and samples weighted towards White, female, late-diagnosed adults with relatively strong verbal access.

The source phrase *reorganisation of the resting state* is not yet established. Connecting autistic burnout directly to allostatic overload also risks turning a broad stress concept into a specific mechanism without matching evidence.

F9 should keep several levels distinct:

- the person's account of exhaustion, reduced tolerance and increased support need;
- observed changes in daily functioning and access;
- sustained demands, masking, exclusion, illness and recovery opportunity;
- clinical differential assessment where change is severe or sudden;
- physiological mechanisms, which remain an open research question;
- accommodations and support that may help;
- the Gradient organisation present at a particular moment.

Autistic burnout is not X, Z, Chronic organisation or one fixed baseline shift. A person in burnout may move across Gradient Positions while remaining exhausted and disabled. Rest, solitude, sensory relief, demand reduction, care and community support may help some people; no single route should be promised or withheld.

## Reading the original F9 language against the current system

### Preserve, with precise translation

| Original direction | Current reading |
| --- | --- |
| Different nervous systems need different conditions | People differ in sensory, attentional, cognitive, communicative, motor and recovery needs; useful conditions must be identified with the person and task |
| The environment may create the difficulty | Physical, social, informational, temporal and institutional barriers can restrict participation or increase cost |
| Change the environment and observe | Treat redesign and accommodation as testable interventions and measure meaningful, delayed and person-defined outcomes |
| Mutual misreading can occur | Communication difficulty may arise within the interaction rather than residing entirely in one person |
| Masking may carry cost | Examine pressure, strategy, agency, benefit, distress and delayed cost without assuming one mechanism |
| Design for variation from the beginning | Accessible and universal design can reduce predictable barriers while individual accommodations remain available |
| People whose needs fit the default may not see the fit | Norms and built environments can appear neutral to the people they already serve |

### Narrow, revise or retire

| Map wording or implication | Why it needs care | Safer direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “The regulatory architecture is universal” | Shared biological functions do not establish one universal architecture, threshold or route | State the specific process thought to be widely shared and preserve within- and between-person variation |
| Inputs vary while one architecture remains the same | Variation may involve detection, integration, attention, learning, movement, communication, health and development—not only incoming input | Examine the whole person–task–environment interaction without locating difference at one stage |
| Much of disorder is an environmental gap | It can erase intrinsic distress, impairment, health needs and the value some people find in diagnosis | Separate difference, impairment, disability, distress and mismatch, then assess how they interact |
| Environmental change tests the claim in a way personality claims cannot | Improvement shows a contextual contribution but does not identify a complete cause or invalidate stable traits | Use environmental manipulation as one source of within-person evidence |
| One “configuration” is performed over another | It assumes two fixed inner types and makes complex strategies one act of override | Describe the particular characteristic, expectation and strategy involved |
| Masking has a running physiological cost | Distress and fatigue associations matter, but the physiological mechanism and individual cost cannot be assumed | Measure subjective, behavioural, health and recovery outcomes separately |
| Burnout is a reorganisation of the resting state | It presents an emerging lived-experience construct as an established biological mechanism | Describe reported exhaustion, reduced tolerance and skill access; keep mechanisms and differential assessment open |
| Rest alone does not reverse burnout | Recovery experiences vary and current evidence does not establish one necessary package | Assess demand, rest, solitude, sensory relief, health, support and time without promising one route |
| Recovery requires being received | Acceptance may be important, but the phrase makes social reception a universal prerequisite | Examine which relational, material, sensory, clinical and solitary conditions help this person recover |
| Environments can simply be “built for variation” | Every design still makes choices and no environment fits every person | Use broad accessibility plus routes for individual adjustment, feedback and revision |

## Evidence status of the original source list

The source alignment combines a political paradigm, disability models, interaction research, self-report and qualitative studies, stress physiology and an educational design framework. Each contributes at a different level.

- **The neurodiversity paradigm** is a political, ethical and scholarly framework with strong roots in autistic community and disability-rights work. It should shape authorship, language, research questions and interpretation. It is not an empirical mechanism that validates every claim made in its name, and its history should not be assigned to one person alone.
- **Social and interactional models of disability** relocate attention towards barriers, institutions and participation. They do not require denial of impairment, pain, illness or individual support need. Different disability models make different claims and should not be treated as one theory.
- **The WHO ICF** provides a useful interactional vocabulary across body functions, activity, participation and environmental factors. It is a classification framework, not a complete causal model or a substitute for disability-led critique.
- **The double empathy problem** is a theoretical correction supported by several bounded studies of rapport and information transfer. Findings should remain tied to the sampled people, task and interaction; they do not establish that every same-neurotype exchange succeeds or every cross-neurotype difficulty is symmetrical.
- **Autistic camouflaging research** identifies varied strategies and associations with poorer mental health. Reviews also report heterogeneous definitions, measures and samples. Most findings do not establish one-way causation or one physiological override mechanism.
- **Autistic burnout research** has developed through lived experience, participatory qualitative work and emerging reviews. The construct is important and the reported disability can be severe; biological mechanisms, prevalence, differential diagnosis and generalisation across autistic populations remain underdeveloped.
- **Allostatic load and overload** concern cumulative physiological wear and stress-related processes. They cannot identify autistic burnout, prove a changed baseline or establish that masking caused a particular health outcome.
- **Universal Design for Learning** offers education-specific design principles across engagement, representation and action or expression. Accessibility outcomes need evaluation, and UDL should not be presented as biological evidence or a complete design framework for workplaces, healthcare and public space.
- **Reasonable accommodation and universal design** also have legal and human-rights meanings. Their duties and thresholds depend on jurisdiction; scientific fit and legal entitlement answer related but different questions.

Authoritative guidance supports the interactional direction. The WHO describes functioning and disability in context and includes environmental factors rather than inferring performance from impairment alone. NICE recommends communication and environmental adjustments in services for autistic adults. The UN disability-rights framework treats denial of reasonable accommodation as discrimination and defines universal design while preserving assistive routes for particular groups. These sources support F9's boundaries; they do not establish one TEG-Blue mechanism.

## F9's handoffs

F9 replaces accommodation-as-afterthought with an iterative access question:

```text
Name the person's goal and the actual task
        ↓
identify sensory, cognitive, communication, motor, social and recovery demands
        ↓
locate barriers, supports, health needs, norms and power
        ↓
change one or more conditions with the person's participation
        ↓
observe access, outcome, autonomy, effort, delayed cost and repeatability
        ↓
retain, revise or replace the support
```

Four handoffs matter most:

1. **To appropriate health and diagnostic assessment:** when the person wants assessment, when a diagnosis could open useful support or when pain, sudden change, severe exhaustion, skill loss, catatonia, sleep disturbance, mood change or another health concern requires investigation.
2. **To accessibility, education, employment and service design:** when avoidable barriers can be removed for many people at once while individual adjustments remain available.
3. **To Model 1, Model 2 and F8:** when the question concerns changing sensory access, organisation, recovery, participation or cost within the person under specified conditions.
4. **To HPR and safeguarding:** when difference or dependency is being used to discredit consent, justify coercion, remove agency, punish harmless behaviour or conceal neglect and abuse.

## Result of this pass

- **Framework status:** retain and substantially reframe.
- **Collection role:** interaction and access framework locating outcomes across person, task, environment, support and power.
- **Title:** revise from “Neurodivergent Configuration and Adaptive Fit” to “Neurodivergent Variation, Disability and Environmental Fit.”
- **Configuration:** retire as the name of a neurodivergent person-type; reserve it for explicitly defined TEG-Blue access or organisation variables.
- **Core architecture:** use eight construct families—person, task, access profile, communication, environment, masking and cost, health and recovery, and support testing.
- **Main distinction:** neurodiversity, impairment, disability, distress and mismatch may overlap but are not interchangeable.
- **Environmental method:** change conditions and observe several outcomes, including delayed cost; improvement supports a contextual contribution rather than one complete causal story.
- **Double empathy:** retain as an interactional hypothesis and evidence route, not a universal symmetry claim or removal of professional responsibility.
- **Masking:** distinguish concealment, suppression, compensation, privacy and ordinary adaptation; preserve agency and do not prescribe unmasking.
- **Autistic burnout:** recognise the reported experience and emerging evidence without turning it into a Gradient Position, allostatic mechanism or settled baseline shift.
- **Design:** combine broad accessibility, individual accommodation, assistive support, feedback and revision.
- **Evidence status:** architectural first pass; terminology, intervention, diagnostic, physiological and legal claims still require source-, population- and jurisdiction-specific grounding.

## Grounding science and knowledge fields

F9 needs disciplines that can examine biological and cognitive variation, disability, communication, daily participation, design, rights and lived experience. Neurodivergent and disabled people must participate in deciding which questions and outcomes matter.

| Field | What it can ground in F9 | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Neurodiversity studies and critical autism studies** | Paradigm, identity, language, epistemic justice, norm critique and community priorities | One biological theory, a uniform community position or the support needs of every individual |
| **Disability studies and crip theory** | Social, relational, political and cultural accounts of disability, access, dependency and normality | Individual diagnosis, physiology or the claim that impairment never matters |
| **Autistic, ADHD and other neurodivergent scholarship and activism** | Lived concepts, research critique, hidden cost, priorities, service harm and meaningful support | One spokesperson or subgroup generalised to every neurodivergent person |
| **Developmental psychology and developmental psychopathology** | Developmental trajectories, heterogeneity, learning, adaptation and changing support needs | One linear course, deficit ranking or adult profile reconstructed from a present difficulty |
| **Clinical psychology, psychiatry, neurology and paediatrics** | Diagnosis where useful, co-occurring conditions, risk, treatment, health monitoring and differential assessment | Identity, worth, empathy, behaviour or environmental fit inferred from diagnosis |
| **Neuropsychology and cognitive science** | Attention, memory, language, executive functions, learning, processing speed and task performance | A complete person profile, everyday capacity or moral interpretation from a test score |
| **Sensory science, psychophysics and perception research** | Detection, discrimination, responsivity, adaptation, multisensory processes and context effects | Subjective distress, preference or one sensory-processing type from threshold data alone |
| **Affective science and interoception research** | Bodily access, emotion differentiation, arousal, appraisal and regulation under specified conditions | That low report means no processing or that high awareness guarantees accuracy or wellbeing |
| **Communication sciences, speech and language therapy and AAC research** | Receptive and expressive communication, language, interaction, augmentative communication and partner support | Intelligence, agency or comprehension inferred from speech production |
| **Occupational therapy and rehabilitation sciences** | Activity analysis, sensory and motor demands, pacing, adaptation, assistive support and daily participation | Normal appearance as the goal or one intervention suitable for every person |
| **Human factors, ergonomics and work design** | Person–task–system interaction, workload, error, fatigue, usability, scheduling and sustainable performance | Diagnosis or inner mechanism inferred from workplace output |
| **Education, learning sciences and Universal Design for Learning research** | Instruction, representation, engagement, assessment, scaffolding and multiple routes for expression | UDL as a clinical treatment, universal solution or evidence for nervous-system architecture |
| **Accessibility, inclusive design and human–computer interaction** | Digital, informational, physical and communication barriers; co-design and assistive technology | That one compliant design is accessible to everyone or that technical access equals social inclusion |
| **Public health and social epidemiology** | Population health, service gaps, inequity, stigma, exclusion and social determinants | An individual's cause, diagnosis or support plan from a group association |
| **Sociology and anthropology** | Norms, institutions, culture, identity, stigma, work, family and meanings of competence | Culture or social structure used to erase individual pain, preference or biology |
| **Organisational and occupational psychology** | Job demands, burnout, disclosure, climate, belonging, management and adjustment | Autistic burnout reduced to occupational burnout or diagnosis inferred from job strain |
| **Social work, community psychology and independent-living research** | Person-in-environment assessment, community support, supported decision-making, services and practical access | Independence defined as doing everything without assistance |
| **Disability law, education law and employment law** | Equality duties, accessibility, discrimination, accommodation, consent and procedural remedy | Clinical mechanism or identical legal entitlement across jurisdictions |
| **Human-rights scholarship and ethics** | Dignity, autonomy, participation, non-discrimination, bodily integrity and freedom from coercion | Empirical effectiveness or individual capacity without corresponding evidence |
| **Measurement science and psychometrics** | Construct validity, reliability, measurement invariance, proxy limits and change detection | A score treated as the whole construct or group norm treated as an ideal |
| **Implementation and services research** | Whether accommodations and supports are available, acceptable, sustained and effective in ordinary settings | Efficacy in a study assumed to produce access in every institution |
| **Economics and social-policy research** | Resource distribution, service eligibility, employment, care, poverty and the costs of exclusion | Human value reduced to productivity or support justified only through economic return |
| **Participatory, community-based and qualitative research** | Lived experience, concept formation, hidden mechanisms, priorities, acceptability and contextual meaning | Anecdote treated as prevalence or one sample assumed to represent people excluded from the research |

### Priority evidence

F9 should favour research that names the people, task, environment, support and outcome rather than comparing broad diagnostic groups without context. Particularly useful evidence includes within-person environmental and task manipulations, longitudinal studies, naturalistic observation, co-designed intervention research, communication-partner studies, accessibility and implementation outcomes, participatory qualitative work, and measures of effort and delayed recovery alongside visible performance.

Sampling must improve. Research should include people with higher support needs, intellectual disability, minimal or no speech, varied communication routes, children and older adults, and people across race, culture, gender, class, geography and diagnostic access. Self-report, observation, task performance, physiological measures and proxy report should remain distinct. Proxy evidence may be necessary and valuable, but it should not silently replace the person's communication or perspective.

Every proposed adjustment should record the goal, person-defined acceptability, autonomy, immediate benefit, delayed cost, adverse effects, sustainability and who gains or loses access. Group averages should never become an individual prescription.
