F6 · Bias, Categorisation and Revisable Judgement

How prior learning, categories, affect, identity, incentives and institutional practice may shape what is noticed, weighted, inferred, decided and corrected. People do not begin every judgement with complete information and a neutral point of view. Attention is selective, categories carry learned associations, existing beliefs affect interpretation, and social position influences which accounts are available and credible. None of that makes bias one process, and none of it makes physiological relief its master cause.

Does not establish
A universal source of irrationality, the truth of every contested account, a diagnosis of a biased person, the biological cause of prejudice, deliberate discrimination inferred from an implicit measure, or the assessment of coercion and domination. Nor does confidence, disagreement or error reveal a nervous-system state.

The decision

Keep F6; replace "bias as regulation" with a decomposed account.

"Bias Regulates" protected an important observation and then explained it with one untested master mechanism. "Bias" can name a statistical error, a pattern of attention, a cognitive shortcut, an association, a social stereotype, a discriminatory decision, a distorted measure or an institutional outcome. F6 has to identify which one is being examined before explaining why it occurs.

Regulation may participate in some judgements. Uncertainty, affect, identity relevance or anticipated consequence may change information use. This does not make physiological relief the master cause of bias, and it does not allow confidence, disagreement or error to reveal a nervous-system state. F6 is retained as a Foundational Framework, reframed as information selection, categorisation, judgement, decision and revision, across individual and institutional levels.

The question F6 owns

What shapes a judgement, how does it differ from the criterion, and what allows correction?

What information, categories, prior expectations and conditions shape a judgement or decision, how does the result differ from the relevant criterion, and what allows correction when new evidence becomes available?

F6 owns questions about

What kind of bias is being proposed; the target, comparator and criterion against which it is identified; what information was available, selected or absent; how categories and associations shaped interpretation; how evidence was weighted; how affect, goals, identity, incentives or group membership participated; how confidence related to accuracy; whether a judgement changed behaviour, access or another person's options; how feedback was received, tested or dismissed; what changed across belief, confidence, decision and later conduct; and how measures, procedures, datasets and institutions may produce patterned error.

F6 does not own

A universal source of irrationality, the truth of every contested account, a diagnosis of a biased person, the biological cause of prejudice, deliberate discrimination inferred from an implicit measure, or the assessment of coercion and domination. Conduct, power and imposed cost belong to F7 and Harm, Power and Repair; F6 makes the process measurable before they examine it.

The core insight

What feels like direct observation may already include selection, categorisation and interpretation.

Two people can look at the same event and notice different things. The difference may come from prior experience, role, expectation, available evidence, category, goal or the practical consequences of reaching one conclusion rather than another. Those processes can remain open to investigation and revision.

Distinctions, not verdicts

Perception is not a complete copy of reality. Interpretation is not automatically distortion. A shortcut is not always an error. Confidence is not accuracy, and uncertainty is not accuracy either. An implicit association is not a complete attitude, intention or behaviour. A stereotype is not identical to prejudice; prejudice is not identical to discriminatory action. Unequal outcome is not sufficient evidence of one cognitive bias. Neutral wording is not proof of an unbiased measure or procedure. Disagreement is not proof of defensiveness. Correction is not the same as forced agreement. "Everyone has bias" does not mean bias has equal effects under unequal power.

The seven F6 construct families

Seven questions replace one mechanism.

FamilyGoverning questionBoundary
F6-A · Bias target and criterionWhat result is considered biased, relative to which empirical, statistical, procedural or ethical criterion?A contested criterion must be made visible rather than presented as neutral fact.
F6-B · Information environment and attentionWhat information was available, encountered, selected, ignored or made difficult to access?Absence of attention does not reveal motive, and repeated exposure does not guarantee belief.
F6-C · Categorisation and associationWhich categories organised similarity, difference, expectation or memory?Categorisation is necessary for thought and is not automatically stereotyping or prejudice.
F6-D · Weighting, inference and confidenceHow were evidence, prior expectation, affect and uncertainty combined into a judgement?A confident or emotionally intense judgement may be accurate, inaccurate or partly accurate.
F6-E · Motivation, identity and consequenceWhat goals, identity positions, incentives, belonging concerns or anticipated costs may shape information use?Motivated cognition is one family of processes, not the explanation for every error or disagreement.
F6-F · Decision, behaviour and system implementationHow did the judgement affect action, access, allocation, policy or another person's options?Internal bias cannot be inferred from outcome alone; behaviour and systems require their own evidence.
F6-G · Feedback, correction and redesignWhat evidence, contact, accountability, procedure or environmental change supports more accurate and equitable decisions?Change on one measure does not establish changed behaviour, durable revision or institutional repair.

These families are a provisional TEG-Blue integration. They are not stages, types or one validated model of bias. Each research programme should ground a specific construct family rather than validate F6 as a whole.

Working vocabulary

F6 does not use "bias" without naming its level.

TermWorking meaningBoundary
Statistical biasSystematic difference between an estimate and the quantity it is intended to estimate.Concerns a defined estimator and target, not a person's moral orientation.
Measurement biasA measure functions differently across conditions or groups in ways not explained by the intended construct.Group difference is not automatically measurement bias; the measure must be tested.
Perceptual or attentional biasSelective orientation, detection or weighting of particular information under specified conditions.Task- and context-dependent; does not establish belief, prejudice or intent.
Cognitive biasA recurring judgement pattern that differs from a stated normative or empirical criterion.The criterion must be named; some heuristics are efficient or ecologically useful.
Implicit associationAn automatically measured association under a particular task and scoring model.Not a hidden confession, fixed trait or direct measure of discriminatory behaviour.
StereotypeA generalised belief or association concerning a social category.May be descriptive, prescriptive, positive, negative, endorsed or merely recognised.
PrejudiceEvaluation or affect directed toward a person or group through category membership.Does not by itself establish one behaviour, policy or institution.
DiscriminationDifferential treatment based on category or position within a defined context.Behavioural or institutional; not inferred solely from an attitude measure.
Institutional biasProcedures, criteria or arrangements that systematically produce unequal or distorted treatment.May persist without one shared individual belief; requires system-level evidence.
Algorithmic or data biasPatterned error or unequal performance arising through data, labels, objectives, features, modelling or deployment.Technical performance alone does not settle fairness; the social use and affected population matter.

These distinctions describe different research objects. Connections among them must be demonstrated rather than assumed.

Bias is not a shared Chronic state

No group occupies one Gradient Position.

The original account said that when enough people share a Chronic state, they share a filter. That merges individual organisation, social learning and institutional persistence. People may arrive at similar judgements through different routes: shared information sources, categories, incentives, group identities, professional routines, laws, technologies or material interests. A procedure may reproduce an unequal result even when participants report different beliefs. An institution may change while some personal attitudes remain stable, or attitudes may change while the institution keeps producing the same outcome.

Individual

Attention, association and judgement within a person.

Interactional

Interaction, influence and group norms.

Organisational

Procedures and incentives.

Institutional

Institutional, cultural and material conditions.

The levels stay distinct. Chronic may describe recurrence or difficult revision within a person; social and institutional persistence require their own variables and evidence. Institutional bias and social persistence are not Chronic Positions.

Reading the old language

Six directions preserved, with precise translation.

Bias is not only a reasoning error

Bias may involve attention, association, affect, motivation, identity, procedure and material interest as well as deliberate reasoning.

Judgement begins before explanation is complete

Selection, categorisation and prior expectation can participate before or alongside reflective reasoning.

Felt observation includes interpretation

What appears self-evident may contain learned categories, weighting and assumptions that can be examined.

Contradiction can be difficult to use

Feedback may carry identity, status, belonging, uncertainty or practical costs; response varies by context and power.

Group processes can stabilise a shared account

Norms, information environments, authority, incentives and institutions may support similar conclusions through different routes.

Bias interventions require more than information

Outcomes depend on the kind of bias, setting, intervention, measure, duration and structural conditions involved.

Where the old map said certainty tracks physiological relief, F6 measures confidence, evidence, affect and accuracy separately. Where it said contradiction "argues with the body", F6 examines identity relevance, uncertainty, affect, evidence use, power and revision behaviour. Where it said revision follows safety, F6 treats safety as one possible condition and distinguishes physical safety, relational safety, team psychological safety and freedom from retaliation.

From F5

F5 hands forward categories and valuations that may influence attention, credibility, judgement and allocation. F6 must not psychologise inequality or explain a social hierarchy only through individual bias.

From Model 1 and Model 2

Present organisation may affect attention, salience, uncertainty and available reconsideration. Activation does not establish prejudice, inaccuracy or moral failure, and calm does not establish unbiased judgement.

To F7 and Harm, Power and Repair

When judgement becomes behaviour or policy that uses power to restrict another person's agency, access, safety or options. F6 hands forward observed decisions and justifications, not an inevitable path from perception to enforcement; behaviour, authority and imposed cost must be observed.

To F8

When correction requires accountability, changed procedures, restitution or relational and institutional repair rather than awareness alone. Awareness or attitude change does not by itself repair a discriminatory decision or institution.

Back to F4 and F5

When the information environment, rule or valuation system must change for individual revision to become usable and durable. Shared wording or compliance does not prove one shared belief or association.

To the Chronic Gradient

When a judgement route becomes recurrent, generalised, context-insensitive or difficult to revise within a person. Institutional bias and social persistence are not Chronic Positions.

F1 contributes broad biological and evolutionary foundations for attention, categorisation, learning and decision; evolution does not justify a modern stereotype or make one bias inevitable. F2 contributes language, socialisation, exposure, consequences and institutions that may shape categories; an adult judgement cannot reconstruct developmental history, and social learning is not one caregiver pathway. F3 contributes present meaning, certainty, identity relevance, expected consequence and feedback response; F3 cannot decide that a belief is biased before F6 names the criterion and evidence. F6 does not require every bias to become discrimination or domination.

Boundaries and provenance

Retired wording, and the grounding route.

Narrowed or retired

Bias "is regulation". Beliefs persist because they reduce activation. Bias occurs "below awareness, before reasoning starts". Perception has already delivered a conclusion. Certainty tracks physiological relief rather than accuracy. Contradiction "argues with the body". Enough people in a Chronic state share one filter. Education and moral argument fail because state maintains the filter. Revision follows safety. "Cost: accuracy". The safer direction in each case is to name the bias, target, criterion, process, context, measure, timescale and the person or group carrying each cost, and to test immediate affect, longer-term consequence and alternative routes rather than infer relief.

Grounding fields

The grounding field must match the type of bias being claimed. Cognitive psychology and cognitive science; perception science and psychophysics; judgement and decision science; social psychology and intergroup relations; political psychology and political science; cultural psychology and psychological anthropology; sociology and sociology of knowledge; communication, media and information science; organisational science and industrial and organisational psychology; education research; statistics, psychometrics and measurement science; computer science, data science and algorithmic fairness; science and technology studies; public health, medicine and health-services research; criminology and criminal-justice research; forensic and legal psychology; affective science and neuroscience; critical race, feminist, disability, queer, decolonial and Indigenous scholarship; philosophy, epistemology and ethics; participatory and community-based research. Priority evidence: controlled experiments combined with longitudinal and within-person research, field and audit studies, behavioural and administrative outcomes, qualitative observation, psychometric testing, algorithmic audits and policy evaluation, each record naming bias type, target, comparator, criterion, population, context, measure and timescale, and intervention research reporting implicit measures, explicit attitudes, decisions, behaviour and institutional outcomes separately.

Old map title: Bias Regulates. Evidence status of the original source list: motivated cognition, affect-as-information and the affect heuristic, predictive processing, attentional narrowing and threat-related attention, social identity and identity-protective cognition, implicit-bias intervention research, prejudice-reduction research, intergroup contact and psychological safety identify relevant research territories, but they do not combine into one bias-as-regulation theory. Predictive processing appears in the source map at analogy level only. Prejudice-reduction reviews do not support the claim that education fails; they show uneven evidence and outcome dependence. Psychological safety was developed to study voice and learning in teams and is not established as a general mechanism for correcting prejudice.
