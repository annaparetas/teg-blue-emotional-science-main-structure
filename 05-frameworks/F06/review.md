# F6 · Bias, Categorisation and Revisable Judgement

Status: first-pass architectural review · 21 August 2026

Current foundations: [Original Frameworks Claim Reassessment](../../evidence/reviews/original-frameworks-claim-reassessment.md) · [Scientific Lineage and Integrative Grounding](../../evidence/reviews/scientific-lineage-and-integrative-grounding.md) · [Project Terminology Ledger](../../GLOSSARY.md) · [Harm, Power and Repair Grounding](../../07-reference/grounding/harm-power-and-repair.md)

## Decision

**Keep F6 as a Foundational Framework, but replace “bias as regulation” with a decomposed account of information selection, categorisation, judgement, decision and revision.**

Provisional title:

> **Bias, Categorisation and Revisable Judgement**

Suggested explanatory line:

> How prior learning, categories, affect, identity, incentives and institutional practice may shape what is noticed, weighted, inferred, decided and corrected.

The original framework protects an important observation: people do not begin every judgement with complete information and a neutral point of view. Attention is selective. Categories carry learned associations. Existing beliefs affect interpretation. Social position influences which accounts are available and credible.

“Bias,” however, is not one process. It can name a statistical error, a pattern of attention, a cognitive shortcut, an association, a social stereotype, a discriminatory decision, a distorted measure or an institutional outcome. F6 needs to identify which one is being examined before explaining why it occurs.

Regulation may participate in some judgements. Uncertainty, affect, identity relevance or anticipated consequence may change information use. This does not make physiological relief the master cause of bias, and it does not allow confidence, disagreement or error to reveal a nervous-system state.

## The question F6 owns

> What information, categories, prior expectations and conditions shape a judgement or decision, how does the result differ from the relevant criterion, and what allows correction when new evidence becomes available?

F6 owns questions concerning:

- what kind of bias is being proposed;
- the target, comparator and criterion against which it is identified;
- what information was available, selected or absent;
- how categories and associations shaped interpretation;
- how evidence was weighted;
- how affect, goals, identity, incentives or group membership participated;
- how confidence related to accuracy;
- whether a judgement changed behaviour, access or another person's options;
- how feedback was received, tested or dismissed;
- what changed across belief, confidence, decision and later conduct;
- how measures, procedures, datasets and institutions may produce patterned error.

It does not own a universal source of irrationality, the truth of every contested account, a diagnosis of a biased person, the biological cause of prejudice, deliberate discrimination inferred from an implicit measure, or the assessment of coercion and domination.

## The core insight worth preserving

Two people can look at the same event and notice different things. The difference may come from prior experience, role, expectation, available evidence, category, goal or the practical consequences of reaching one conclusion rather than another.

The strongest original insight is therefore:

> What feels like direct observation may already include selection, categorisation and interpretation, and those processes can remain open to investigation and revision.

Several distinctions follow:

- perception is not a complete copy of reality;
- interpretation is not automatically distortion;
- a shortcut is not always an error;
- confidence is not accuracy;
- uncertainty is not accuracy either;
- an implicit association is not a complete attitude, intention or behaviour;
- a stereotype is not identical to prejudice;
- prejudice is not identical to discriminatory action;
- unequal outcome is not sufficient evidence of one cognitive bias;
- neutral wording is not proof of an unbiased measure or procedure;
- disagreement is not proof of defensiveness;
- correction is not the same as forced agreement;
- “everyone has bias” does not mean bias has equal effects under unequal power.

## Working vocabulary

F6 should not use “bias” without naming its level.

| Term | Working meaning | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Statistical bias** | Systematic difference between an estimate and the quantity it is intended to estimate | It concerns a defined estimator and target, not a person's moral orientation |
| **Measurement bias** | A measure functions differently across conditions or groups in ways not explained by the intended construct | Group difference is not automatically measurement bias; the measure must be tested |
| **Perceptual or attentional bias** | Selective orientation, detection or weighting of particular information under specified conditions | It is task- and context-dependent and does not establish belief, prejudice or intent |
| **Cognitive bias** | A recurring judgement pattern that differs from a stated normative or empirical criterion | The criterion must be named; some heuristics are efficient or ecologically useful |
| **Implicit association** | An automatically measured association under a particular task and scoring model | It is not a hidden confession, fixed trait or direct measure of discriminatory behaviour |
| **Stereotype** | A generalised belief or association concerning a social category | It may be descriptive, prescriptive, positive, negative, endorsed or merely recognised |
| **Prejudice** | Evaluation or affect directed toward a person or group through category membership | It does not by itself establish one behaviour, policy or institution |
| **Discrimination** | Differential treatment based on category or position within a defined context | It is behavioural or institutional and should not be inferred solely from an attitude measure |
| **Institutional bias** | Procedures, criteria or arrangements that systematically produce unequal or distorted treatment | It may persist without one shared individual belief and requires system-level evidence |
| **Algorithmic or data bias** | Patterned error or unequal performance arising through data, labels, objectives, features, modelling or deployment | Technical performance alone does not settle fairness; the social use and affected population matter |

These distinctions describe different research objects. Connections among them must be demonstrated rather than assumed.

## Seven F6 construct families

| Construct family | Governing question | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **F6-A · Bias target and criterion** | What result is considered biased, relative to which empirical, statistical, procedural or ethical criterion? | A contested criterion must be made visible rather than presented as neutral fact |
| **F6-B · Information environment and attention** | What information was available, encountered, selected, ignored or made difficult to access? | Absence of attention does not reveal motive, and repeated exposure does not guarantee belief |
| **F6-C · Categorisation and association** | Which categories organised similarity, difference, expectation or memory? | Categorisation is necessary for thought and is not automatically stereotyping or prejudice |
| **F6-D · Weighting, inference and confidence** | How were evidence, prior expectation, affect and uncertainty combined into a judgement? | A confident or emotionally intense judgement may be accurate, inaccurate or partly accurate |
| **F6-E · Motivation, identity and consequence** | What goals, identity positions, incentives, belonging concerns or anticipated costs may shape information use? | Motivated cognition is one family of processes, not the explanation for every error or disagreement |
| **F6-F · Decision, behaviour and system implementation** | How did the judgement affect action, access, allocation, policy or another person's options? | Internal bias cannot be inferred from outcome alone; behaviour and systems require their own evidence |
| **F6-G · Feedback, correction and redesign** | What evidence, contact, accountability, procedure or environmental change supports more accurate and equitable decisions? | Change on one measure does not establish changed behaviour, durable revision or institutional repair |

These families are a provisional TEG-Blue integration. They are not stages, types or one validated model of bias.

## Bias is not a shared Chronic state

The original account says that when enough people share a Chronic state, they share a filter. This merges individual organisation, social learning and institutional persistence.

People may arrive at similar judgements through different routes. They may share information sources, categories, incentives, group identities, professional routines, laws, technologies or material interests. A procedure may reproduce an unequal result even when participants report different beliefs. An institution may change while some personal attitudes remain stable, or individual attitudes may change while the institution continues to produce the same outcome.

The levels should remain distinct:

```text
individual attention, association and judgement
        ↕
interaction, influence and group norms
        ↕
organisational procedures and incentives
        ↕
institutional, cultural and material conditions
```

No group occupies one Gradient Position. Chronic may describe recurrence or difficult revision within a person; social and institutional persistence require their own variables and evidence.

| Current layer | What F6 receives or contributes | Boundary of the connection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **F1 · evolutionary foundations** | Selective attention, categorisation, learning and decision have broad biological and evolutionary foundations | Evolution does not justify a modern stereotype or make one bias inevitable and adaptive |
| **F2 · developmental shaping** | Language, socialisation, repeated exposure, consequences and institutions may shape categories, associations and information use | An adult judgement cannot reconstruct developmental history, and social learning is not one caregiver pathway |
| **F3 · adult maintenance and revision** | F3 contributes present meaning, certainty, identity relevance, expected consequence and feedback response | F3 cannot decide that a belief is biased before F6 names the criterion and evidence |
| **F4 · shared rules** | Rules and norms can shape what information is available, speakable, credible or rewarded | Shared wording or compliance does not prove one shared belief or association |
| **F5 · social valuation** | F5 hands forward categories and valuations that may influence attention, credibility, judgement and allocation | F6 must not psychologise inequality or explain a social hierarchy only through individual bias |
| **Model 1 and Model 2** | Present organisation may affect attention, salience, uncertainty and available reconsideration | Activation does not establish prejudice, inaccuracy or moral failure, and calm does not establish unbiased judgement |

## Reading the original F6 language against the current system

### Preserve, with precise translation

| Original direction | Current reading |
| --- | --- |
| Bias is not only a reasoning error | Bias may involve attention, association, affect, motivation, identity, procedure and material interest as well as deliberate reasoning |
| Judgement begins before conscious explanation is complete | Selection, categorisation and prior expectation can participate before or alongside reflective reasoning |
| Felt observation includes interpretation | What appears self-evident may contain learned categories, weighting and assumptions that can be examined |
| Contradiction can be difficult to use | Feedback may carry identity, status, belonging, uncertainty or practical costs; response varies by context and power |
| Group processes can stabilise a shared account | Norms, information environments, authority, incentives and institutions may support similar conclusions through different routes |
| Bias interventions require more than information | Outcomes depend on the kind of bias, setting, intervention, measure, duration and structural conditions involved |

### Narrow, revise or retire

| Map wording or implication | Why it needs care | Safer direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bias “is regulation” | It replaces a broad descriptive term with one untested master mechanism | Name the bias, target, criterion, process, context and consequence before proposing regulation as one contributor |
| Beliefs persist because they reduce activation | Some judgements may reduce uncertainty or protect identity; others persist through habit, evidence environment, incentives, authority or accurate experience | Test immediate affect, longer-term consequence and alternative routes rather than infer relief |
| Bias occurs “below awareness, before reasoning starts” | Automatic and reflective processes interact; some biases are explicit, strategic, procedural or institutional | State what was measured and whether awareness, endorsement or deliberation was assessed |
| Perception has already delivered a conclusion | Perception, attention, memory, inference and language are distinguishable but interacting | Ask which stage or measure shows the difference and what alternative information was available |
| Certainty tracks physiological relief rather than accuracy | Confidence and accuracy can diverge, but relief is not the universal source of certainty | Measure confidence, evidence, affect and accuracy separately |
| Contradiction “argues with the body” | The metaphor can make disagreement sound biologically closed and self-validating | Examine identity relevance, uncertainty, affect, evidence use, power and revision behaviour |
| Enough people in a Chronic state share one filter | Groups do not share one nervous-system state, and institutional patterns may persist without common private psychology | Distinguish individual judgement, influence, norms, procedures, incentives and institutional history |
| Education and moral argument fail because state maintains the filter | Intervention effects vary, and knowledge, norms, contact, incentives, procedures and policy target different outcomes | Specify the intervention, comparison, outcome, timescale and context |
| Revision follows safety | Interpersonal safety may support voice and learning in some settings; prejudice and discrimination also require evidence, contact conditions, accountability and structural change | Treat safety as one possible condition and distinguish physical safety, relational safety, team psychological safety and freedom from retaliation |
| “Cost: accuracy” | Some biases concern fairness, representation, access or validity rather than factual accuracy; some heuristics can be useful | Name the criterion and the person or group carrying each cost |

## Evidence status of the original source list

The original sources identify several relevant research territories, but they do not combine into one bias-as-regulation theory.

- **Motivated cognition** concerns ways goals or desired conclusions may shape reasoning. It should not become the explanation for every judgement that resists revision.
- **Affect-as-information and the affect heuristic** concern distinct questions about how feeling may inform judgement. They do not show that confidence is physiological relief or that affect makes a conclusion inaccurate.
- **Predictive processing** appears in the source map at analogy level. Prediction-error language should not be used as direct evidence for social bias without a tested model and matching measures.
- **Attentional narrowing and threat-related attention research** can ground specified changes in selection under defined conditions. They cannot directly explain prejudice, ideology or institutional discrimination.
- **Social identity and identity-protective cognition** may help examine group-relevant information use. The construct, population, domain and outcome need separate evidence.
- **Implicit-bias intervention research** must distinguish change in implicit measures from change in explicit attitude, decision and behaviour. Durability and real-world transfer remain separate questions.
- **Prejudice-reduction research** does not support the general claim that education fails. Reviews instead show uneven evidence, outcome dependence and the need for stronger field research and structural approaches.
- **Intergroup contact** often corresponds with reduced prejudice across studied settings, but contact quality, status, power, target group, selection and context remain important.
- **Psychological safety** was developed mainly to study interpersonal risk-taking, voice and learning in teams. It is not established as a general mechanism for correcting prejudice.

No dedicated F6 claim decomposition or source-level review has yet been completed. Each research programme should ground a specific construct family rather than validate F6 as a whole.

## F6's handoffs

F6 follows the movement from available information to consequence without assuming one hidden process:

```text
information environment + prior expectation
        ↓
attention + categorisation
        ↓
weighting + inference + confidence
        ↓
decision, behaviour or procedure
        ↓
feedback, correction, persistence or redesign
```

Three handoffs matter most:

1. **To F7 and HPR:** when judgement becomes behaviour or policy that uses power to restrict another person's agency, access, safety or options.
2. **To F8:** when correction requires accountability, changed procedures, restitution or relational and institutional repair rather than awareness alone.
3. **Back to F4 and F5:** when the information environment, rule or valuation system must change for individual revision to become usable and durable.

F6 does not require every bias to become discrimination or domination. It makes the relevant process measurable before later frameworks examine conduct and impact.

## Result of this pass

- **Framework status:** retain and substantially reframe.
- **Collection role:** framework for separating selection, categorisation, judgement, decision and correction across individual and institutional levels.
- **Title:** revise from “Bias Regulates” to “Bias, Categorisation and Revisable Judgement.”
- **Core architecture:** use seven construct families—criterion, information and attention, categorisation, weighting and confidence, motivation and identity, implementation, and correction or redesign.
- **Main protection:** do not make bias one nervous-system mechanism, infer prejudice from activation, collapse implicit association into behaviour or treat institutional persistence as a shared Chronic state.
- **F5 connection:** receives categories and social valuations, but does not reduce unequal conditions to individual psychology.
- **F7 connection:** hands forward observed decisions and justifications, not an inevitable path from perception to enforcement.
- **Evidence status:** architectural first pass only; the source list requires construct-specific evidence review and outcome-specific intervention records.

## Grounding science and knowledge fields

F6 crosses individual, interactional and institutional levels. The grounding field must match the type of bias being claimed.

| Field | What it can ground in F6 | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cognitive psychology and cognitive science** | Attention, memory, categorisation, inference, confidence, metacognition and belief revision | Social structure, discriminatory intent or moral character from one task effect |
| **Perception science and psychophysics** | Detection, discrimination, signal uncertainty and perceptual decision under controlled conditions | A social stereotype, prejudice or institutional outcome without additional evidence |
| **Judgement and decision science** | Heuristics, probability judgement, risk, choice architecture and decision under uncertainty | That one laboratory norm is the correct criterion for every real-world judgement |
| **Social psychology and intergroup-relations research** | Stereotypes, prejudice, social identity, influence, contact, attribution and group-relevant judgement | Institutional inequality explained by individual attitudes alone |
| **Political psychology and political science** | Identity-protective information use, ideology, elite cues, polarisation, institutions and public decision | A universal psychological explanation of political disagreement |
| **Cultural psychology and psychological anthropology** | Variation in categories, selfhood, attention, evidence and socially available interpretations | A culture as one mind or one cognitive standard as universal |
| **Sociology and sociology of knowledge** | Classification, expertise, credibility, institutions, professional judgement and socially organised knowledge | A nervous-system mechanism or individual motive inferred from institutional position |
| **Communication, media and information science** | Information exposure, framing, agenda-setting, networks, misinformation and source credibility | Belief or behaviour inferred from exposure alone |
| **Organisational science and industrial–organisational psychology** | Hiring, evaluation, teams, routines, voice, accountability and decision procedure | Direct transfer from one organisation to families, courts or society as a whole |
| **Education research** | Learning, curricula, classroom expectation, assessment, disciplinary decision and intervention | Ability, prejudice or family history inferred from a single educational outcome |
| **Statistics, psychometrics and measurement science** | Sampling, estimation, construct validity, reliability, measurement invariance and differential item functioning | Ethical fairness or lived impact settled by technical performance alone |
| **Computer science, data science and algorithmic-fairness research** | Dataset composition, labels, model performance, error distribution, objectives and deployment | That a mathematical fairness criterion resolves the social or ethical question |
| **Science and technology studies** | How categories, tools, expertise and infrastructures shape knowledge and institutional decisions | That all measured differences are merely social constructions or empirically empty |
| **Public health, medicine and health-services research** | Diagnostic, treatment, triage and access decisions; disparities and intervention outcomes | A clinician's motive or a patient's condition inferred from group disparity alone |
| **Criminology and criminal-justice research** | Police, court, sentencing, correctional and victim-recognition decisions and system effects | Guilt, dangerousness or criminal intent inferred from category or assessment score alone |
| **Forensic and legal psychology** | Eyewitness memory, interviewing, juror judgement, testimony, risk communication and psycholegal assessment | A lie detector, universal credibility test or general inference from confidence to accuracy |
| **Affective science and neuroscience** | Relations among affect, arousal, salience, attention, learning and decision under specified tasks | Prejudice, ideology or accuracy read directly from brain or physiological activity |
| **Critical race, feminist, disability, queer, decolonial and Indigenous scholarship** | Missing categories, standpoint, institutional power, lived consequences and assumptions built into dominant criteria | One generic theory or decorative confirmation of a model developed elsewhere |
| **Philosophy, epistemology and ethics** | Normative criteria, evidence, testimony, rationality, fairness and the distinction between error and injustice | Empirical prevalence, causal mechanism or intervention effect without corresponding research |
| **Participatory and community-based research** | How affected people identify hidden assumptions, meaningful outcomes and implementation effects | One uniform community account or automatic causal proof |

### Priority evidence

F6 should combine controlled experiments with longitudinal and intensive within-person research, field and audit studies, behavioural and administrative outcomes, qualitative observation, psychometric testing, algorithmic audits and policy evaluation. Each record should identify the bias type, target, comparator, criterion, population, context, measure and timescale. Intervention research should report implicit measures, explicit attitudes, decisions, behaviour and institutional outcomes separately rather than treating movement in one as change in all.
