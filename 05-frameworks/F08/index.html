F8 · Recovery, Reconnection and Repair as Distinct Routes

How conditions, access, capacity, updating and recovery may change within a person, and what additional work is required when behaviour has affected another person's safety, agency or trust. Change is not completed by explanation alone, adulthood does not close the possibility of change, and uneven progress is not proof of failure. Neither is one name enough for several different changes.

The decision

Keep F8; replace one self-reconnection and repair pathway with a map of several routes.

The original framework recognises something important: change is not completed by explanation alone, adulthood does not close the possibility of change, and uneven progress is not proof of failure. It also reaches for the difference between recovering access to something familiar and developing a capacity that has had little opportunity to form. Its difficulty is that it gives several different changes one name. A person may recognise an inner state, gain a skill, revise an expectation, begin Return, recover physiologically, stop a harmful behaviour, make reparation or rebuild a relationship. These events may support one another. None establishes all the others.

F8 therefore organises a field of routes, not a staircase. The practical question is not whether the person is moving through the correct rhythm. It is what is changing, what remains constrained, what conditions are current, whose outcome is being measured and what further work belongs on a different evidence plane. The title moves from "Self-Reconnection and Repair Pathway" to "Recovery, Reconnection and Repair as Distinct Routes".

The question F8 owns

After restriction, persistence or harm, what is changing, and what must not be inferred from it?

After restriction, persistence or harm, what conditions, access, capacities, learning and recovery are changing, and which additional behavioural, material or relational processes must not be inferred from internal change?

F8 owns questions about

Whether danger, deprivation, coercion, overload or another constraining condition is still present; which bodily, emotional, reflective, relational and action-related information is available; which capacities are available, difficult to use, context-bound, less practised or still developing; what support, accommodation, protection, rest, treatment, practice or material change may widen participation; which expectation, interpretation, discrimination, response weighting or action route is changing; whether learning transfers beyond the supportive setting; how Return, recovery and everyday functioning change across channels and timescales; whether harmful conduct has stopped and changed under later comparable conditions; what accountability, restitution or restoration of agency remains necessary; whether trust, access, forgiveness or reconciliation is wanted by the affected person.

F8 does not own

A universal healing sequence, diagnosis, treatment prescription, one physiological recovery endpoint, a requirement to disclose or reconnect, proof that danger has ended, forgiveness, restored trust or entitlement to relationship. F8 is a route-selection lens that keeps internal, behavioural, material and relational change connected without collapsing them.

The governing separation

The word "repair" has been carrying too much.

  1. Self-reconnectionIncreased access to bodily, emotional, reflective and relational information.
  2. Skill or capacity developmentWidening what the person can notice, tolerate, communicate, choose, do or recover.
  3. UpdatingChange in expectation, interpretation, response weighting or future action.
  4. Return or recoveryMovement towards Baseline X; rebuilding across domains and timescales.
  5. Behaviour changeChanged observable conduct under later comparable conditions.
  6. ReparationActor-side work after harm to another person.
  7. Trust, renewed access or reconciliationThe affected person's voluntary decisions.

These processes can interact without becoming interchangeable. Increased bodily awareness may support more conscious participation but does not guarantee a different act. Insight may alter meaning without changing a learned response. A person who caused harm may become calmer or more self-aware without addressing its impact. A person who was harmed may recover without forgiving, reopening access or rebuilding the relationship. Each time F8 uses the language of reconnection, recovery or repair, it must identify the route and the person whose outcome is being described.

Working vocabulary

Several kinds of safety, several kinds of change, and outcomes that are never owed.

Kinds of safety

Present safety is current protection from credible danger, coercion, deprivation or serious imposed cost, with viable routes to help and refusal; it cannot be established from calm appearance, reassurance or the actor's intention. Felt safety is the subjective experience that danger is sufficiently absent; it may lag behind improved conditions or coexist with unrecognised danger, and it is information, not a complete risk assessment. Relational safety is behaviourally supported: limits, disagreement, refusal and distance do not bring retaliation or manipulation; warmth, apology and reported love do not establish it. Psychological safety is an organisational construct about interpersonal risk-taking in a team, not a synonym for bodily calm or freedom from abuse. Stabilisation may be necessary in some circumstances but is not a universal stage or an indefinite barrier to other care.

Change within the person

Self-reconnection is increased access to, differentiation of or participation with bodily, emotional, reflective and relational information; not discovery of one untouched original self, compulsory inward attention or proof of accuracy. Capacity or skill development widens ability under relevant conditions; performance in one setting does not establish general capacity, and difficulty does not reveal whether a pathway was never formed. Updating is not one mechanism, simple belief correction or proof that an earlier response was mistaken. Return is TEG-Blue's term for movement towards Baseline X; not forced calm, suppression, forgiveness, completed recovery or a requirement to reconnect. Recovery is person- and domain-specific across timescales; symptom reduction or visible calm is not the whole of it.

Change that concerns another person

Behaviour change is a meaningful change in observable conduct, especially under later conditions resembling those in which the behaviour occurred; intention, insight, shame, apology or temporary absence of opportunity does not establish it. Reparation is actor-side work: stopping harm, naming specific impact, accepting limits and consequences, restoring resources or agency where possible and sustaining changed conduct. It cannot be completed by the affected person's forgiveness, silence or renewed participation.

Relational outcomes

Trust is willingness to rely on another within a particular domain, built from evidence, consequence and time; specific, revisable and never owed. Access is permission or practical opportunity to enter another person's time, attention, space, body, information, resources or relationship; previous intimacy, apology, need or self-improvement creates no entitlement to it. Forgiveness is a personal process whose meaning varies; not excusing, forgetting, trusting, withdrawing protection or reconciling. Reconciliation is a voluntary rebuilding of relationship or cooperation; not the required endpoint of recovery or reparation, and it may be unsafe or unwanted.

The eight F8 construct families

Eight assessment routes replace one pathway.

FamilyGoverning questionBoundary
F8-A · Conditions and protectionWhat danger, demand, deprivation, dependency, exclusion or material pressure remains, and what protection or accommodation is available?Internal work cannot make an unsafe condition safe; subjective and externally assessed safety must both be considered without substituting one for the other.
F8-B · Access and self-reconnectionWhich bodily, emotional, reflective, relational and contextual information is becoming more noticeable, distinguishable or usable?More awareness is not always more accuracy, and inward attention may be difficult, unwanted or clinically inappropriate in some conditions.
F8-C · Capacity, skill and supportWhat can the person do now, under which conditions, with what assistance, cost and reliability?A capacity may be uneven, context-bound, newly developing, temporarily constrained or supported externally; the route cannot be inferred from appearance.
F8-D · Updating and generalisationWhat expectation, meaning, discrimination, action route or consequence is changing, and does that change travel across contexts?Insight, one corrective experience or improvement in a protected setting does not establish broad or permanent revision.
F8-E · Return and recovery profileWhich physiological, experiential, relational and practical channels are widening or rebuilding, at what rate and with what remaining cost?No single biomarker, mood, behaviour or Gradient Position establishes completed Return or recovery.
F8-F · Conduct, accountability and reparationIf another person was affected, has the behaviour stopped, has impact been specifically owned, have resources or options been restored and does change hold under later pressure?The actor's recovery is not reparation, and understanding why harm occurred does not remove responsibility.
F8-G · Trust, access and possible reconciliationWhat does the affected person want, what boundaries remain, and what evidence would be needed for any form of renewed reliance or contact?Forgiveness, trust, access and reconciliation are distinct, voluntary and never owed.
F8-H · Maintenance, recurrence and changing needsWhat supports later participation, detects recurrence, responds to setbacks and adapts when health, context, power or resources change?A setback is information for reassessment, not proof of moral failure or evidence that all previous change was false.

These families are assessment routes, not stages. More than one may operate at once, some may be irrelevant, and progress in one may occur while another remains unchanged.

Conditions before conclusions

Not one kind of safety before all capacity.

The original phrase "safety before capacity" contains a protective intention but cannot serve as a universal law. When credible danger, coercion or deprivation is current, practical protection matters: distance, housing, money, healthcare, legal support, communication access or assistive technology may be needed before an invitation to reinterpret a response is appropriate. No amount of internal calm makes continuing coercion safe. At the same time, the body and mind are not two independent judges. Cognitive appraisal may accurately identify danger or safety; bodily responses may carry important information; both may be influenced by learning, illness, medication, pain, disability, sleep and context. Some capacities are also needed to create safety: noticing a limit, planning, seeking support, evaluating evidence or leaving.

The safer organising principle

Establish the current conditions, protect viable agency and identify the particular capacity being asked of the person before deciding what kind of support or learning is needed. This keeps actual safety, felt safety, relational reliability, material security and team psychological safety as different constructs.

Replace two hidden routes with several observable possibilities

A blocked pathway and a pathway never built look identical from outside.

The source proposes two origins that cannot be told apart by observation. It is a useful reminder that similar difficulty may need different support; it is not a distinction that can usually be inferred cleanly. A capacity may be available in one relationship or condition but not another; familiar but difficult to access under demand; less practised because opportunities were limited; developing through education, therapy, rehabilitation or ordinary life; altered by injury, illness, pain, medication, fatigue or sensory conditions; supported through another person, technology, routine or environmental design; present but too costly to use repeatedly; unnecessary in one setting but expected in another; or obscured by a measurement that rewards speech, speed, eye contact or normative social performance.

F8 does not decide which of these is the person's hidden essence. It asks what is observable now, where the capacity appears, what makes access easier or harder, what support is acceptable and whether the change matters in everyday life.

Nonlinearity without a prescribed oscillation

Variation is information, not a master mechanism.

Change may involve approach and retreat. It may also be steady, delayed, abrupt, domain-specific, cyclical, interrupted by current conditions or invisible to an outside observer. Calling every fluctuation the nervous system's test of whether territory is survivable turns a possible interpretation into a master mechanism. F8 keeps the humane point without fixing one rhythm: variation does not automatically mean failure; the meaning of a setback depends on the conditions and outcome being measured; pressure, new danger, grief, health, sleep, discrimination, resources and treatment effects may change what is available; some learning stays context-specific before it generalises; some routes require repetition while others require removal of the demand; persistence may indicate ongoing correspondence rather than failed internal updating; and adverse effects, overwhelm, disengagement and deterioration must not be relabelled as necessary healing.

Information, experience and sustained conditions interact

The original map assigned insight and experience to two systems updated through different channels. That protects against expecting understanding to change every response at once, but it recreates the ESS and CLS separation the current architecture avoids. Insight is itself an experience and may change attention, meaning, action and relationship; practice can change what becomes available without a conscious explanation; reliable conditions may change expectation gradually; a changed account may coexist with unchanged physiological recruitment, and a behavioural change may occur before the person can explain it. F8 tracks which outcome actually changed, across access, expectation, behaviour and recovery, rather than assigning information and sustained conditions to two systems. Verbal insight is not a substitute for observing later participation, conduct and recovery; nor is it irrelevant because another channel changes more slowly. The route is adjusted in response to evidence, preference and cost, not defended because difficulty was expected.

Connections and handoffs

A route-selection question replaces the map's fixed reversal chain.

  1. What is current?Conditions and danger · access · capacity · learning · recovery · conduct · relationship.
  2. Which outcome matters, to whom?And what evidence would show change.
  3. Select a routeSupport, protection, treatment, practice, accommodation or accountability.
  4. ObserveBenefit, cost, transfer, recurrence and affected-person agency.
  5. Revise the routeWithout turning one outcome into proof of another.

To immediate protection and appropriate services

When danger, coercion, self-harm risk, violence, severe impairment or urgent health need is present. A conceptual framework must not replace clinical, safeguarding, legal or emergency assessment.

To Model 1 access and Model 2 Return work

When the question concerns changing access, participation, organisation, updating or recovery within the person. Greater access and differentiation may give ME more information with which to participate; bodily access, affective sharing and mentalizing do not establish accuracy, care or acceptable behaviour. Return belongs to Model 2 and is not a treatment, moral achievement, completed recovery or relational repair.

To Harm, Power and Repair

When behaviour has affected another person's safety, agency, credibility, resources or future options. Cessation, accountability and reparation remain on their own evidence plane; F8 must not turn an actor's internal progress into evidence that repair is complete.

To F9

When access and participation depend on sensory, cognitive, communicative, environmental or social fit rather than correction towards a normative form. Difference from a normative route is not failed healing.

Across the collection: F1 makes change possible through broad capacities for learning, recovery, care and cooperation, without establishing an original healthy state to return to. F2 helps explain why access, expectation, skill and support needs differ, though present difficulty cannot reveal whether a pathway was blocked, absent or formed differently. F3 identifies expectations, meanings, response routes and revision conditions that F8 can follow over time; a coherent new account is not by itself repair. F4 rules can support voice and accountability but do not establish safe conditions or equal power. F5 valuation may expand or restrict recovery routes; recovery cannot be reduced to personal resilience while unequal conditions continue. F6 judgement can improve assessment, and categorising a person as healed, resistant or unrecoverable can itself distort the route. F7 identifies what must stop; self-reconnection cannot substitute for protection, cessation or restored agency. The Chronic plane examines persistence, reconstruction and recovery opportunity; Chronic does not mean permanent, damaged identity or mismatch while adverse conditions continue. F10 notes that no person's healing guarantees a particular outcome in the next generation.

Boundaries and provenance

Narrowed or retired wording, and the grounding route.

Narrowed or retired

F3 to F7 as substitutes for something original that was missing. "Building the original is what repair is." Blocked pathway versus pathway never built. Felt safety must come before capacity. The body registers safety while the mind merely concludes it. Oscillation as the nervous system checking survivability. Experience changes one system and insight another. "Awareness rebuilds through safety." Repair as returning to contact with oneself, which leaves impact and accountability invisible when another person was harmed. Relationship as the endpoint, when some recovery occurs through distance, solitude, community, treatment, peer support, environmental fit or material security.

Grounding fields

Recovery science and psychiatric rehabilitation; clinical psychology and psychotherapy process and outcome research; psychiatry and clinical medicine; trauma, stress and adversity research; learning and behavioural sciences; cognitive science and neuropsychology; affective science and interoception; neuroscience, psychophysiology and stress physiology; developmental science and attachment research; relationship, family and couple research; social work and community psychology; public health and social epidemiology; occupational therapy and rehabilitation sciences; pain, sleep and behavioural medicine; disability studies, neurodiversity scholarship and accessibility research; sociology and anthropology; organisational and occupational psychology; education and skill-development research; criminology, violence research and forensic psychology; restorative and transformative justice; law, safeguarding, ethics and human rights; implementation and services research; lived-experience, survivor-led and participatory research. No single discipline can decide what recovery means for a person. Priority evidence: longitudinal and multi-method designs that name the person, condition, route and outcome; every intervention claim records benefit, adverse effects, dropout, accessibility, preference, exclusions and generalisation; every repair claim separately records cessation, impact, accountability, restored agency, later conduct and the affected person's freedom over trust and access.

Evidence status of the original source list

The original alignment draws useful vocabularies from different units of analysis that cannot be combined into one established pathway. Neuroception and Polyvagal Theory are theory-specific and their core physiological claims remain disputed. Window of tolerance is a clinical heuristic, not a standardised physiological window. Pendulation and titration belong to particular somatic traditions. Stages of change and stages of trauma recovery are organising concerns, not a fixed or biological order. Earned security is a narrow attachment-research construct. Emotional granularity and affect labelling support bounded questions; naming is not always beneficial, sufficient or necessary. Alliance rupture and repair, Gottman's repair attempts and Tronick's still-face work each concern a particular relationship type and cannot ground repair in coercive relationships, families, organisations or society. Psychological safety stays within its organisational scope. Current guidance from SAMHSA and NICE supports plural, specified routes rather than validating F8 as a whole.
