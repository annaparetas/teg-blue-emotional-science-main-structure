# F8 · Recovery, Reconnection and Repair as Distinct Routes

Status: first-pass architectural review · 21 August 2026

Current terminology companions: [Current Construct and Claim Decomposition](../../evidence/claims/current-construct-decomposition.md) · [Regulation and Return · Settled Concepts](../../03-model-2-gradient/notes/regulation-and-return-concepts.md) · [Return to Baseline](../../03-model-2-gradient/return.html)

## Decision

**Keep F8 as a Foundational Framework, but replace one self-reconnection and repair pathway with a map of several related routes whose outcomes must be assessed separately.**

Provisional title:

> **Recovery, Reconnection and Repair as Distinct Routes**

Suggested explanatory line:

> How conditions, access, capacity, updating and recovery may change within a person, and what additional work is required when behaviour has affected another person's safety, agency or trust.

The original framework recognises something important: change is not completed by explanation alone, adulthood does not close the possibility of change and uneven progress is not proof of failure. It also reaches for the difference between recovering access to something familiar and developing a capacity that has had little opportunity to form.

Its difficulty is that it gives several different changes one name. A person may recognise an inner state, gain a skill, revise an expectation, begin Return, recover physiologically, stop a harmful behaviour, make reparation or rebuild a relationship. These events may support one another. None establishes all the others.

F8 should therefore organise a field of routes, not prescribe one staircase. The practical question is not whether the person is moving through the correct rhythm. It is what is changing, what remains constrained, what conditions are current, whose outcome is being measured and what further work belongs on a different evidence plane.

## The question F8 owns

> After restriction, persistence or harm, what conditions, access, capacities, learning and recovery are changing—and which additional behavioural, material or relational processes must not be inferred from internal change?

F8 owns questions concerning:

- whether danger, deprivation, coercion, overload or another constraining condition is still present;
- which bodily, emotional, reflective, relational and action-related information is available to the person;
- which capacities are available, difficult to use, context-bound, less practised or still developing;
- what support, accommodation, protection, rest, treatment, practice or material change may widen participation;
- which expectation, interpretation, discrimination, response weighting or action route is changing;
- whether learning transfers beyond the supportive setting in which it first appeared;
- how Return, recovery and everyday functioning change across different channels and timescales;
- whether harmful conduct has stopped and changed under later comparable conditions;
- what accountability, restitution or restoration of agency remains necessary;
- whether trust, access, forgiveness or reconciliation is wanted by the affected person.

It does not own a universal healing sequence, diagnosis, treatment prescription, one physiological recovery endpoint, a requirement to disclose or reconnect, proof that danger has ended, forgiveness, restored trust or entitlement to relationship.

## The governing separation

The word *repair* has been carrying too much.

```text
self-reconnection
        ≠
skill or capacity development
        ≠
updating
        ≠
Return or recovery
        ≠
behaviour change
        ≠
reparation
        ≠
trust, renewed access or reconciliation
```

These processes can interact without becoming interchangeable. Increased bodily awareness may support more conscious participation but does not guarantee a different act. Insight may alter meaning without changing a learned response. A person who caused harm may become calmer or more self-aware without addressing its impact. A person who was harmed may recover without forgiving, reopening access or rebuilding the relationship.

The framework must identify the route and the person whose outcome is being described each time it uses the language of reconnection, recovery or repair.

## Working vocabulary

| Term | Working meaning | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Present safety** | Current protection from credible danger, coercion, deprivation or serious imposed cost, together with viable routes to help and refusal | It cannot be established from calm appearance, reassurance or the actor's intention |
| **Felt safety** | A person's subjective experience that danger or imposed cost is sufficiently absent | It may lag behind improved conditions or coexist with unrecognised danger; it is information, not a complete risk assessment |
| **Relational safety** | Behaviourally supported conditions in which limits, disagreement, refusal and distance do not bring retaliation or manipulation | Warmth, attachment, apology and reported love do not establish it |
| **Psychological safety** | In organisational research, a shared belief that interpersonal risk-taking in a team is sufficiently safe | It is not a synonym for bodily calm, trauma recovery, freedom from abuse or general wellbeing |
| **Stabilisation** | Support that helps manage immediate distress, danger, functioning or treatment participation | It may be necessary in some circumstances but should not become a universal stage or indefinite barrier to other care |
| **Self-reconnection** | Increased access to, differentiation of or participation with bodily, emotional, reflective and relational information | It is not discovery of one untouched original self, compulsory inward attention or proof of accuracy |
| **Capacity or skill development** | Widening the ability to notice, tolerate, communicate, choose, act, recover or seek support under relevant conditions | Performance in one setting does not establish general capacity, and difficulty does not reveal whether a pathway was never formed |
| **Updating** | Change in expectation, interpretation, response weighting or future action as new information and consequences become usable | It is not one mechanism, simple belief correction or proof that an earlier response was mistaken |
| **Return** | TEG-Blue's term for movement towards Baseline X, where a wider range of capacities and restoration becomes available | It is not forced calm, suppression, forgiveness, completed recovery or a requirement to reconnect |
| **Recovery** | Rebuilding, restoration or improved participation across physiological, psychological, practical and social domains over different timescales | It is person- and domain-specific; symptom reduction or visible calm is not the whole of it |
| **Behaviour change** | A meaningful change in observable conduct, especially under later conditions resembling those in which the behaviour occurred | Intention, insight, shame, apology or temporary absence of opportunity does not establish it |
| **Reparation** | Actor-side work of stopping harm, naming specific impact, accepting limits and consequences, restoring resources or agency where possible and sustaining changed conduct | It cannot be completed by the affected person's forgiveness, silence or renewed participation |
| **Trust** | Willingness to rely on another within a particular domain, built from evidence, consequence and time | Trust is specific, revisable and never owed |
| **Access** | Permission or practical opportunity to enter another person's time, attention, space, body, information, resources or relationship | Previous intimacy, apology, need or self-improvement creates no entitlement to renewed access |
| **Forgiveness** | A personal moral, emotional, spiritual or relational process whose meaning varies | It is not the same as excusing, forgetting, trusting, withdrawing protection or reconciling |
| **Reconciliation** | A voluntary rebuilding of relationship or cooperation by the people involved | It is not the required endpoint of recovery or reparation and may be unsafe or unwanted |

## Eight F8 construct families

| Construct family | Governing question | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **F8-A · Conditions and protection** | What danger, demand, deprivation, dependency, exclusion or material pressure remains, and what protection or accommodation is available? | Internal work cannot make an unsafe condition safe; subjective and externally assessed safety must both be considered without substituting one for the other |
| **F8-B · Access and self-reconnection** | Which bodily, emotional, reflective, relational and contextual information is becoming more noticeable, distinguishable or usable? | More awareness is not always more accuracy, and inward attention may be difficult, unwanted or clinically inappropriate in some conditions |
| **F8-C · Capacity, skill and support** | What can the person do now, under which conditions, with what assistance, cost and reliability? | A capacity may be uneven, context-bound, newly developing, temporarily constrained or supported externally; the route cannot be inferred from appearance |
| **F8-D · Updating and generalisation** | What expectation, meaning, discrimination, action route or consequence is changing, and does that change travel across contexts? | Insight, one corrective experience or improvement in a protected setting does not establish broad or permanent revision |
| **F8-E · Return and recovery profile** | Which physiological, experiential, relational and practical channels are widening or rebuilding, at what rate and with what remaining cost? | No single biomarker, mood, behaviour or Gradient Position establishes completed Return or recovery |
| **F8-F · Conduct, accountability and reparation** | If another person was affected, has the behaviour stopped, has impact been specifically owned, have resources or options been restored and does change hold under later pressure? | The actor's recovery is not reparation, and understanding why harm occurred does not remove responsibility |
| **F8-G · Trust, access and possible reconciliation** | What does the affected person want, what boundaries remain, and what evidence would be needed for any form of renewed reliance or contact? | Forgiveness, trust, access and reconciliation are distinct, voluntary and never owed |
| **F8-H · Maintenance, recurrence and changing needs** | What supports later participation, detects recurrence, responds to setbacks and adapts when health, context, power or resources change? | A setback is information for reassessment, not proof of moral failure or evidence that all previous change was false |

These families are assessment routes, not stages. More than one may operate at once, some may be irrelevant and progress in one may occur while another remains unchanged.

## Conditions before conclusions, not one kind of safety before all capacity

The original phrase *safety before capacity* contains a protective intention but cannot serve as a universal law.

When credible danger, coercion or deprivation is current, practical protection matters. The person may need distance, housing, money, healthcare, legal support, communication access, assistive technology or another material route before an invitation to reinterpret their response is appropriate. No amount of internal calm makes continuing coercion safe.

At the same time, the body and mind are not two independent judges. Cognitive appraisal may accurately identify danger or safety; bodily responses may carry important information; both may be influenced by learning, illness, medication, pain, disability, sleep and context. Some capacities are also needed to create safety: noticing a limit, planning, seeking support, using communication, evaluating evidence or leaving.

The safer organising principle is:

> **Establish the current conditions, protect viable agency and identify the particular capacity being asked of the person before deciding what kind of support or learning is needed.**

This principle allows actual safety, felt safety, relational reliability, material security and team psychological safety to remain different constructs.

## Replace two hidden routes with several observable possibilities

The source proposes two origins that look identical from outside: a pathway that exists but is blocked and one that was never built. This is a useful reminder that similar difficulty may need different support. It is not a distinction that can usually be inferred cleanly.

A capacity may be:

- available in one relationship or condition but not another;
- familiar but difficult to access under demand;
- less practised because opportunities were limited;
- developing through education, therapy, rehabilitation or ordinary life;
- altered by injury, illness, pain, medication, fatigue or sensory conditions;
- supported through another person, technology, routine or environmental design;
- present but too costly to use repeatedly;
- unnecessary in one culture or setting but expected in another;
- obscured by a measurement that rewards speech, speed, eye contact or normative social performance.

F8 should not decide which of these is the person's hidden essence. It should ask what is observable now, where the capacity appears, what makes access easier or harder, what support is acceptable and whether the change matters in everyday life.

## Nonlinearity without a prescribed oscillation

Change may involve approach and retreat. It may also be steady, delayed, abrupt, domain-specific, cyclical, interrupted by current conditions or invisible to an outside observer. Calling every fluctuation the nervous system's test of whether territory is survivable turns a possible interpretation into a master mechanism.

F8 can preserve the humane point without fixing one rhythm:

- variation does not automatically mean failure;
- the meaning of a setback depends on the conditions and outcome being measured;
- pressure, new danger, grief, health, sleep, discrimination, resources and treatment effects may change what is available;
- some learning remains context-specific before it generalises;
- some routes require repetition, while others require removal of the demand;
- persistence may indicate ongoing correspondence rather than failed internal updating;
- adverse effects, overwhelm, disengagement and deterioration must not be relabelled as necessary healing.

The route should be adjusted in response to evidence, preference and cost rather than defended because difficulty was expected.

## Information, experience and sustained conditions interact

The original map says insight and experience update two different systems through different channels. This protects against the assumption that understanding should immediately change every response. The proposed split, however, recreates the ESS–CLS separation that the current architecture is trying to avoid.

Insight is itself an experience and may change attention, meaning, action and relationship. Practice can change what becomes available without producing a conscious explanation. Reliable conditions may change expectation gradually. A changed account may coexist with unchanged physiological recruitment, and a behavioural change may occur before the person can explain it.

F8 should therefore track several outcomes rather than assign information and sustained conditions to two systems:

```text
present conditions + bodily and sensory information + meaning + relationship
        + practice + action + consequence + recovery opportunity
                              ↓
        possible change in access, expectation, behaviour or recovery
                              ↓
              check which outcome actually changed
```

The important correction remains: verbal insight is not a substitute for observing later participation, conduct and recovery. It is also not irrelevant simply because another channel changes more slowly.

## Reading the original F8 language against the current system

### Preserve, with precise translation

| Original direction | Current reading |
| --- | --- |
| Change remains possible in adulthood | Access, expectation, skill, conduct and relationship can change, with route and outcome assessed separately |
| Similar outward difficulty may need different work | Assess context, history, current performance, support and cost without claiming to know whether a pathway was blocked or never built |
| Insight may not immediately change the whole response | Meaning, action, physiology, relationship and recovery may change at different rates |
| Progress may be uneven | Variation should prompt contextual reassessment rather than shame or a predetermined interpretation |
| Precise naming may help | Differentiating experience can support communication and choice for some people; naming is one possible tool, not the goal or proof of repair |
| Relationship can support change | Reliable, non-coercive relationships can offer feedback, practice, resources and support; they are not mandatory and do not replace material conditions |
| Group conditions affect participation | Teams and institutions can make speaking, questioning, reporting and learning more or less costly |

### Narrow, revise or retire

| Map wording or implication | Why it needs care | Safer direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| F3–F7 are substitutes for something original that was missing | It makes diverse social, cognitive and harmful processes one deficit chain and assumes an original natural arrangement | Ask what process is occurring now and what conditions, capacities, rules or power maintain it |
| “Building the original is what repair is” | Skills may be new, adapted or externally supported; repair after harm has actor- and affected-person requirements | Separate self-reconnection, capacity development, behaviour change and reparation |
| Blocked pathway versus pathway never built | The binary is rarely observable and excludes uneven, contextual, altered and differently supported capacity | Describe where the capacity is available, constrained, developing or costly and test support needs |
| Felt safety must come before capacity | It merges several kinds of safety and creates a universal order | Assess actual conditions, subjective experience, viable agency and the specific capacity together |
| The body registers safety while the mind merely concludes it | It divides one organism and discounts accurate reasoning, evidence and conscious appraisal | Treat bodily, sensory, cognitive, relational and contextual information as interacting sources |
| Oscillation is the nervous system checking survivability | It assigns one mechanism to every fluctuation and can normalise deterioration | Observe the pattern, context, cost and outcome before interpreting why change varied |
| Experience changes one system and insight another | It reinstates a hard ESS–CLS split and treats insight as disembodied | Track how information, experience, action, conditions and consequence affect each outcome |
| “Awareness rebuilds through safety” | Awareness is not the sole outcome, safety is not one variable and rebuilding may require material, clinical, social or accessibility changes | Map several routes and identify the particular outcome being sought |
| Repair means returning to contact with oneself | This leaves impact and accountability invisible when another person was harmed | Reserve self-reconnection for internal access; use reparation and relational repair for the separate HPR route |
| Relationship is the endpoint | Some recovery occurs through distance, solitude, community, treatment, peer support, environmental fit or material security | Let the person define meaningful participation and whether any relationship is rebuilt |

## Evidence status of the original source list

The proposed alignment contains useful clinical and research vocabularies, but the terms come from different units of analysis and cannot be combined into one established pathway.

- **Neuroception and Polyvagal Theory** are theory-specific. Social and environmental cues can affect autonomic and subjective responses, but *neuroception* should not be treated as an independently established detector or a universal safety hierarchy. The theory's core physiological claims remain actively disputed in the literature.
- **Window of tolerance** is a widely used clinical heuristic for discussing workable arousal and overwhelm. It is not a standardised physiological window or a universal prerequisite for learning.
- **Pendulation and titration** belong to particular somatic practice traditions. They may describe how some clinicians and participants pace experience, but they do not establish the rhythm or mechanism of all recovery.
- **Stages of change** can support questions about readiness, action and maintenance. They should not become a fixed sequence through which every person must move.
- **Earned security** is a narrower attachment-research construct, often operationalised through Adult Attachment Interview classifications. It should not name general adult nervous-system repair or imply a known developmental route from a present classification.
- **Emotional granularity and affect labelling** support bounded questions about differentiating and naming experience. Findings do not establish that naming is always beneficial, sufficient for regulation or necessary for recovery.
- **Stages of trauma recovery** are an influential clinical synthesis. Safety, remembrance and reconnection may be valuable organising concerns, but they should not be presented as a universal biological order or used to delay appropriate evidence-based care indefinitely.
- **Psychotherapy alliance rupture and repair** has its own empirical literature; a meta-analysis found a moderate association between rupture resolution and treatment outcome. This concerns a therapeutic relationship and cannot by itself ground repair in families, organisations, coercive relationships or society.
- **Gottman's repair attempts** concern interaction within couple relationships. They cannot establish accountability or safe joint work where coercion, violence or credible retaliation is present.
- **Tronick's still-face and mutual-regulation work** informs early dyadic coordination and mismatch. It does not directly establish one adult trauma-recovery sequence or make relational reunion necessary.
- **Psychological safety** in Edmondson's research is an organisational construct concerning interpersonal risk-taking, voice, teamwork and learning. It should be used within that scope rather than as a synonym for felt bodily safety or freedom from harm.

Current authoritative guidance also argues against one route. SAMHSA describes recovery as person-driven, holistic and occurring through many pathways. NICE recommends several structured PTSD interventions, with attention to preference, risk, symptoms, trained delivery and ongoing review. These sources concern particular service and diagnostic contexts; they support plural, specified routes rather than validating F8 as a whole.

## F8's handoffs

F8 should replace the map's fixed reversal chain with a route-selection question:

```text
What is current?
        ↓
conditions and danger · access · capacity · learning · recovery · conduct · relationship
        ↓
Which outcome matters, to whom, and what evidence would show change?
        ↓
select support, protection, treatment, practice, accommodation or accountability route
        ↓
observe benefit, cost, transfer, recurrence and affected-person agency
        ↓
revise the route without turning one outcome into proof of another
```

Four handoffs matter most:

1. **To immediate protection and appropriate services:** when danger, coercion, self-harm risk, violence, severe impairment or urgent health need is present. A conceptual framework must not replace clinical, safeguarding, legal or emergency assessment.
2. **To Model 1 access and Model 2 Return work:** when the question concerns changing access, participation, organisation, updating or recovery within the person.
3. **To HPR:** when behaviour has affected another person's safety, agency, credibility, resources or future options. Cessation, accountability and reparation remain on their own evidence plane.
4. **To F9:** when access and participation depend on sensory, cognitive, communicative, environmental or social fit rather than correction towards a normative form.

## Result of this pass

- **Framework status:** retain and substantially reframe.
- **Collection role:** route-selection framework that keeps internal, behavioural, material and relational change connected without collapsing them.
- **Title:** revise from “Self-Reconnection and Repair Pathway” to “Recovery, Reconnection and Repair as Distinct Routes.”
- **Core architecture:** use eight construct families—conditions, access, capacity, updating, Return and recovery, conduct and reparation, trust and access, and maintenance.
- **Safety:** replace one felt-safety hierarchy with a specified assessment of actual danger, subjective safety, relational reliability, material conditions and viable agency.
- **Development:** retire the observable-looking binary between blocked and never-built pathways; describe current availability, context, support, cost and change.
- **Nonlinearity:** preserve permission for uneven progress without treating every oscillation as one biological mechanism or every deterioration as necessary healing.
- **ESS–CLS boundary:** information, meaning, experience, action and sustained conditions interact; change may occur unevenly without belonging to two separate systems.
- **Repair boundary:** self-reconnection, Return and actor recovery do not complete protection, accountability, reparation, trust or relational repair.
- **Agency:** forgiveness, renewed access and reconciliation remain voluntary and are never owed.
- **Evidence status:** architectural first pass; every intervention, diagnostic, safety and mechanism claim still requires population-, context- and outcome-specific grounding.

## Grounding science and knowledge fields

F8 needs fields that can examine recovery across body, experience, everyday participation, material conditions, behaviour and relationship. No single discipline can decide what recovery means for a person or establish all forms of repair.

| Field | What it can ground in F8 | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Recovery science and psychiatric rehabilitation** | Person-defined recovery, participation, hope, functioning, support, citizenship and multiple pathways | One universal endpoint, stage sequence or requirement to eliminate every symptom |
| **Clinical psychology and psychotherapy process and outcome research** | Treatment outcomes, therapeutic alliance, rupture, mechanisms, skills, preference, dropout and adverse effects | A general account of social repair or the safety of a specific relationship without direct assessment |
| **Psychiatry and clinical medicine** | Diagnosis where relevant, comorbidity, medication, risk, health conditions and treatment planning | Identity, responsibility, credibility or relational repair from diagnosis |
| **Trauma, stress and adversity research** | Post-traumatic responses, persistence, recovery heterogeneity, risk and protective factors and intervention outcomes | One response to trauma, one required treatment or a nervous-system story inferred from exposure alone |
| **Learning and behavioural sciences** | Extinction, inhibitory learning, discrimination, reinforcement, expectancy, controllability, habit and generalisation | A complete recovery pathway or proof that current danger is only a learned prediction |
| **Cognitive science and neuropsychology** | Attention, memory, appraisal, metacognition, executive function, language and the effects of injury or illness | A body–mind split, moral judgement or universal inference from test performance to everyday capacity |
| **Affective science and interoception research** | Emotion differentiation, affect labelling, bodily sensing, appraisal and regulation strategies | That more bodily attention or more precise naming is always accurate, beneficial or sufficient |
| **Neuroscience, psychophysiology and stress physiology** | Bounded changes in autonomic, endocrine, neural, sleep and recovery processes | Felt safety, completed recovery, repair, trust, responsibility or a whole-person state from one measure |
| **Developmental science and attachment research** | Change and continuity across development, caregiving, relational expectations and attachment constructs | Adult outcome reconstructed from childhood, or attachment classification used as a general healing endpoint |
| **Relationship, family and couple research** | Communication, support, conflict, trust, rupture, caregiving and relationship change | That joint work, reunion or reconciliation is appropriate in coercive or unsafe conditions |
| **Social work and community psychology** | Person-in-environment assessment, safeguarding, practical support, community resources, mutual aid and service systems | Recovery reduced to individual coping while material or coercive conditions remain |
| **Public health and social epidemiology** | Population patterns, social determinants, prevention, access, inequality and health outcomes | An individual pathway or cause inferred from group-level association |
| **Occupational therapy and rehabilitation sciences** | Everyday activity, pacing, adaptation, participation, environment, assistive support and functional goals | A normative life pattern or internal mechanism inferred from task performance |
| **Pain, sleep and behavioural medicine** | Bidirectional relations among health, fatigue, pain, sleep, stress and participation | Psychological meaning or relational safety inferred from a bodily symptom |
| **Disability studies, neurodiversity scholarship and accessibility research** | Environmental fit, access barriers, supported capacity, masking, accommodation and non-normative participation | Difference defined as deficit or recovery defined as appearing typical |
| **Sociology and anthropology** | Cultural meanings of self, healing, relationship, family, institution, community and social role | Culture treated as uniform, static or an excuse for harm |
| **Organisational and occupational psychology** | Psychological safety, voice, learning, role, burnout, work design and institutional support | Trauma recovery or general felt safety from a team-climate construct |
| **Education and skill-development research** | Scaffolding, practice, feedback, transfer, adult learning and accessible instruction | That performance reflects fixed capacity or that teaching one skill repairs a relationship |
| **Criminology, violence research and forensic psychology** | Cessation, recurrence, risk communication, accountability, behaviour-change interventions and system response | Coercion, guilt, credibility or future harm inferred from diagnosis or inner-state language |
| **Restorative and transformative justice research and practice** | Voluntary accountability, restitution, community participation, affected-person voice and institutional repair | Guaranteed safety, compelled forgiveness or reconciliation without consent |
| **Law, safeguarding, ethics and human rights** | Rights, duties, protection, consent, capacity, least-restrictive practice, remedy and procedural fairness | Clinical mechanism, individual treatment plan or a universal legal threshold across jurisdictions |
| **Implementation and services research** | Whether supports can be delivered accessibly, safely, consistently and at useful scale | Efficacy in principle treated as benefit in every real setting |
| **Lived-experience, survivor-led and participatory research** | Meaningful outcomes, hidden costs, service harm, priorities, acceptable support and contextual knowledge | One participant's account generalised to every person or used without the methodological care applied to other evidence |

### Priority evidence

F8 should favour longitudinal and multi-method evidence that names the person, condition, route and outcome being studied. Depending on the claim, this may include controlled treatment studies, comparative effectiveness research, mechanism experiments, everyday functioning and participation measures, physiological recovery across multiple channels, later behaviour under comparable conditions, affected-person reports, service and implementation data, qualitative research and participatory work.

Every intervention claim should record benefit, adverse effects, dropout, accessibility, preference, contraindications, who was excluded from the evidence and whether change generalised beyond the research or treatment setting. Every repair claim should separately record cessation, impact, accountability, restored agency or resources, later conduct and the affected person's freedom over trust and access. No single symptom score, biomarker, narrative of insight or relationship outcome should stand for the whole route.
