## Decision

**Keep F11 as a Foundational Framework, but replace paradox as a universal solution with a disciplined method for distinguishing ambivalence, contradiction, complexity, uncertainty and competing demands.**

Provisional title:

> **Ambivalence, Complexity and Responsible Integration**

Suggested explanatory line:

> How more than one feeling, need, perspective or level may matter at once—and how evidence, choice, power and responsibility remain visible when they do.

The original framework protects a human capacity that several earlier frameworks need. A person can love someone and need distance. They can understand why a behaviour developed and still reject its impact. They can grieve what a relationship offered and what it never became. More than one experience may be real without one cancelling the other.

The difficulty begins when every conflict is called a paradox and every side is called a truth. Feelings can coexist. Needs can compete. Perspectives can each contain partial information. Factual claims still require evidence, actions still produce unequal impact and power does not become symmetrical because a situation is complex.

F11 should therefore become the framework that identifies what kind of conflict is present before deciding whether it should be tolerated, investigated, integrated, chosen between, repaired or refused.

## The question F11 owns

> When experiences, needs, values, claims, actions or systems appear to conflict, what kind of conflict is present, what can coexist, what requires evidence or choice, and how can action remain revisable and accountable?

F11 owns questions concerning:

- which statements, feelings, needs, goals, values, relationships or levels appear to conflict;
- whether the conflict is logical, factual, motivational, emotional, relational, ethical, practical or institutional;
- whether the terms refer to the same person, event, time, scale and meaning;
- what evidence supports each factual claim;
- whether two perspectives are incompatible, partial, differently situated or simply about different things;
- which needs can coexist and which actions cannot occur together;
- how uncertainty and incomplete information should be represented;
- how current organisation, stress, health, skill, context and power affect access to alternatives;
- what choice, sequence, boundary, compromise or experiment may be required;
- whether complexity is being used to widen understanding or avoid decision and responsibility;
- what changed after action, feedback and consequence.

It does not own a diagnosis, truth detector, theory that every contradiction is meaningful, requirement to integrate incompatible accounts, permission to suspend protection, proof of healing from emotional discomfort or narrative messiness, or conclusion that all sides hold equal truth, power or responsibility.

## The governing separation

F11 needs one boundary above all others:

```text
two experiences can coexist
        ≠
two factual claims are equally supported
        ≠
two actions are equally acceptable
        ≠
two people have equal power or responsibility
```

Someone may sincerely feel abandoned when another person sets a necessary boundary. That feeling matters. It does not establish that abandonment occurred, that the boundary was wrong or that the person setting it must restore access.

Someone may have been harmed and later harmed another person. Both facts can be true. Their coexistence explains why history and responsibility must be held together; it does not make the harms equivalent or cancel either one.

Responsible integration keeps each claim on its own evidence plane.

## Working vocabulary

| Term | Working meaning | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Complexity** | The presence of several interacting factors, levels, timescales or possible explanations | More detail is not automatically greater accuracy, fairness or usefulness |
| **Paradox** | A statement or situation that appears self-contradictory or counterintuitive and may reveal an unresolved assumption, level or meaning | It is not a synonym for every conflict, mixed feeling or difficult decision |
| **Logical contradiction** | Two propositions that cannot both be true in the same sense, at the same time and within the same scope | Changing time, level or meaning may dissolve an apparent contradiction; if it does not, logic does not “hold” both as true |
| **Inconsistency** | Lack of correspondence among statements, evidence, values, decisions or behaviour | It may reflect change, error, context, memory, ambivalence, deception or imprecise language; the cause must not be assumed |
| **Ambivalence** | Simultaneous or alternating positive and negative evaluations, feelings or motivations concerning the same object or choice | It is not indecision in every case and does not mean the available options are equally safe or valuable |
| **Mixed emotion** | Co-occurrence or close succession of more than one emotional experience | Coexistence of love and anger does not settle what happened or what action should follow |
| **Competing need or goal** | Two or more valued outcomes that cannot all be fully pursued under present constraints | Calling a goal a need does not make every means of securing it acceptable |
| **Value conflict** | Tension among principles or commitments that point towards different choices | Values still require interpretation, prioritisation and attention to rights and consequences |
| **Perspective** | A situated view shaped by access, role, history, knowledge and position | Every perspective is not equally informed, accurate or free from power and incentive |
| **Uncertainty** | A recognised limit in knowledge, prediction or confidence | Uncertainty is not evidence that all explanations are equally likely or that action must wait when protection is needed |
| **Dialectic** | A method that examines tension among positions and may seek a more adequate formulation without erasing difference | It is not automatic synthesis, compromise or proof that the middle position is correct |
| **Dialectical thinking** | Capacity or tendency to recognise change, context, tension and possible coexistence of opposites | It varies culturally and by task and is not a universal measure of maturity or mental health |
| **Cognitive flexibility** | Ability to shift task sets, strategies, representations or responses as demands and evidence change | It is one cognitive construct, not the same as moral openness, emotional tolerance or integrative complexity |
| **Integrative complexity** | A research construct concerning differentiation among perspectives and integration of their relations in expressed thought | High scores do not guarantee truth, ethical conduct, good judgement or action |
| **Double bind** | A proposed communication pattern involving incompatible demands within an important relationship where response, escape or metacommunication is constrained | It is not ordinary mixed messaging, ambivalence or an accepted explanation of schizophrenia |
| **Narrative coherence** | Organisation of an account so its sequence, relations and meanings can be followed within a specified coding system or context | Coherence is not factual accuracy, emotional integration, healthy embodiment or acceptable conduct |
| **Responsible integration** | Holding relevant experience and context together while testing claims, making necessary choices and preserving impact, agency and responsibility | Integration does not require reconciliation, agreement or one smooth story |

## Eight F11 construct families

| Construct family | Governing question | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **F11-A · Conflict identification and scope** | What exactly conflicts, for whom, at what time, level and meaning? | Apparent contradiction should not be interpreted before its terms and scope are fixed |
| **F11-B · Coexisting affect and ambivalence** | Which feelings, evaluations or motivations coexist, alternate or remain uncertain? | Emotional coexistence does not establish factual equivalence or prescribe action |
| **F11-C · Needs, values, goals and choice** | Which valued outcomes compete, what constraints make them incompatible and what choice or sequence is required? | A valid need does not validate coercive, deceptive or harmful means |
| **F11-D · Claims, evidence and uncertainty** | Which statements concern observable events, interpretation, prediction or value, and how well is each supported? | “Both truths” must not replace evidence, probability, correction or acknowledgement that one claim may be wrong |
| **F11-E · Access, flexibility and current conditions** | What attention, memory, language, inhibition, perspective-taking and action alternatives are available under present conditions? | Stress may constrain some capacities without determining belief, character or behaviour |
| **F11-F · Self, identity and narrative organisation** | How are conflicting memories, self-positions, roles and meanings represented over time? | A smooth, fragmented, emotional or detached account cannot diagnose integration, truth or harm by itself |
| **F11-G · Relationship, power and institutional contradiction** | Who may speak, refuse, define the problem or impose a resolution, and which rules or incentives sustain the conflict? | Mutual complexity does not make power, danger, impact or responsibility mutual |
| **F11-H · Integration, action and revision** | What boundary, decision, experiment, repair or provisional action follows, and what does later evidence change? | Reflection without choice or changed conduct may preserve the conflict rather than integrate it |

These construct families are an inquiry method. They are not stages, a personality profile or a claim that greater complexity is always better.

## “Both are true” can mean several different things

The phrase can clarify or conceal. F11 should translate it before accepting it.

| Use of “both are true” | What it may responsibly mean | What still needs to happen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Two feelings** | “I love this person and I am angry with them” | Decide what the feelings concern and what action is safe and wanted |
| **Two needs** | “I need belonging and protection” | Examine whether both can be met, sequenced or supported in different places |
| **Two perspectives** | Each person noticed different parts of an event or gave them different meaning | Compare access, evidence, memory, role, incentive and power |
| **Two times** | A relationship was supportive in one period and harmful in another | Preserve the timeline rather than making one period erase the other |
| **Two levels** | An individual intended care while an institutional rule produced exclusion | Assess intention, rule, outcome and responsibility at their respective levels |
| **Explanation and responsibility** | Context contributed to a behaviour and the person remains responsible for its impact | Use context to improve prevention without turning it into exoneration |
| **Incompatible event claims** | The accounts cannot both describe the same event accurately in the same way | Investigate; do not declare equivalence because each account is sincerely held |
| **Ethical conflict** | Two values point towards different actions | Name rights, duties, consequences, power and the decision that must be made |

The phrase earns its place only when it makes the conflict more precise.

## Multiple needs do not make every behaviour valid

The original framework says behaviour usually serves several valid needs. Behaviour may indeed relate to connection, protection, autonomy, belonging, relief, recognition, certainty, status, care or coherence. Some proposed functions will be mistaken, and the means selected still require separate assessment.

```text
need, goal or organising pressure
        ≠
chosen or learned response route
        ≠
observable behaviour
        ≠
impact on another person
        ≠
ethical or legal acceptability
```

A person may want connection and use surveillance to prevent separation. The wish for connection does not make surveillance a relational need or acceptable strategy. Another person may want protection and leave without explaining in the moment. The behaviour and context need their own assessment.

F11 can hold the need and the impact together by refusing to merge them.

## State may shape complexity without owning it

The source claims that a settled nervous system can hold two truths while a threatened one simplifies to binaries because binaries are cheaper. Research supports a narrower connection. Meta-analytic work suggests that acute stress can impair working memory and cognitive flexibility on average, while effects on different forms of inhibition are mixed and moderated. Stress can also prioritise salient or immediately useful information.

This does not create a Gradient rule.

- High activation may coexist with strategic, complex and accurate thinking.
- Low outward activation may coexist with rigid, deceptive or confused thinking.
- Expertise and rehearsed action may preserve useful discrimination under pressure.
- Sleep, pain, medication, illness, disability, language, time and task design affect cognitive access.
- Identity, ideology, incentives, authority and material interest can maintain binary judgement without acute threat.
- A clear binary may be accurate: consent was present or absent; a payment occurred or did not; a boundary was respected or violated.

The safer formulation is:

> **Some forms of flexible and integrative processing may become harder to access under some kinds of pressure. The effect must be assessed by task, person, condition and outcome.**

No Gradient Position establishes open-mindedness, truth, maturity or moral character.

## Feeling worse is a signal to assess, not a marker of depth

Recovery can bring grief, anger, fatigue, uncertainty or clearer awareness of pain that was previously difficult to access. A person may feel worse on one measure while functioning or choosing more freely on another. This possibility deserves language.

It should not become the claim that feeling worse means getting better.

Worsening may reflect:

- contact with previously avoided or unnamed experience;
- an expected short-term cost of a chosen intervention;
- loss of a protective strategy before another route is available;
- current danger, deprivation or relational harm;
- treatment mismatch, excessive pace or adverse effect;
- deterioration in mental or physical health;
- grief that needs support rather than correction;
- ordinary variation unrelated to the intervention.

Psychotherapy research does not monitor adverse events consistently, but deterioration and other unwanted effects do occur. F11 should therefore route worsening into assessment: what changed, how much, for how long, in which domain, with what risk, and what adjustment or protection is needed?

Discomfort can accompany meaningful change. It cannot prove it.

## A smooth or messy story is not a diagnostic shortcut

The source offers a compelling reversal: a smooth story may be coherence without the body, while a messy story may show the channel opening. Either interpretation may fit one person at one time. Neither can be inferred from narrative form alone.

A smooth account may reflect:

- accurate memory and thoughtful organisation;
- a rehearsed or socially expected story;
- distance from affect;
- constrained disclosure;
- deliberate deception;
- language skill or familiarity with the setting.

A fragmented or changing account may reflect:

- active reconsideration;
- emotion, stress or fatigue;
- ordinary memory limits;
- trauma-related difficulty;
- neurodivergent communication;
- language difference;
- uncertainty, contradiction or misinformation;
- an event that is genuinely complex.

The Adult Attachment Interview codes attachment-related discourse through a specific method. Its coherence and classification findings cannot be turned into a general test of whether a story is embodied, whether someone is healing or whether an event occurred. Coherence, accuracy, felt access and later conduct remain separate.

## Complexity must not become a refuge from power and evidence

Some situations are difficult because several valid concerns interact. Others are made to look complicated by ambiguity, secrecy, strategic language or the distribution of responsibility across many people.

F11 should ask:

- Who benefits if the situation remains unresolved?
- Who has the authority to define the contradiction?
- Whose account requires more proof, and why?
- Can each person refuse, leave, report or retain a separate account?
- Is “both sides” language being used where power and impact are unequal?
- Does institutional complexity prevent any person from becoming accountable?
- Which fact or decision is already clear enough for protection?

Context can explain behaviour without removing responsibility. Complexity can improve the explanation without weakening that boundary.

## Reading the original F11 language against the current system

### Preserve, with precise translation

| Original direction | Current reading |
| --- | --- |
| More than one feeling can be present | Mixed and ambivalent experience can be differentiated without requiring one feeling to cancel another |
| Several needs may pull in different directions | Name the need, goal, constraint and action route, then decide what can coexist or must be chosen |
| A person may have been harmed and caused harm | History can explain context while behaviour, impact and responsibility remain directly assessable |
| Love and anger can coexist | Emotional coexistence is ordinary and does not decide contact, trust or behaviour |
| Current state may affect available complexity | Stress can constrain some executive functions in some conditions; assess the specific task and alternative explanations |
| Recovery may involve difficult emotion | Track distress, functioning, agency, risk and benefit separately rather than treating comfort as the only outcome |
| Institutions can reproduce unwanted outcomes | Compare stated purpose, rules, incentives, implementation, impact and response to evidence across levels |

### Narrow, revise or retire

| Map wording or implication | Why it needs care | Safer direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Paradox holds what logic cannot” | Logic distinguishes contradiction, scope and validity; paradox does not make incompatible propositions jointly true | Identify whether the conflict concerns feeling, value, evidence, time, level or meaning |
| People contradict themselves because behaviour serves several valid needs | Inconsistency has many routes, and a goal does not validate the behaviour used to secure it | Separate need, route, act, impact and acceptability |
| Contradiction is the signature of a multi-need system | It turns ordinary inconsistency into one master mechanism | Treat competing motivation as one hypothesis and test alternatives |
| Complexity depends on state, not character | State can affect access, but skill, development, health, ideology, incentive, power and stable differences also contribute | Use graded, task-specific language and avoid trait verdicts |
| Settled systems can hold both truths | Low demand does not guarantee accuracy, openness or ethical conduct | Assess differentiation, evidence use, revision and behaviour directly |
| Threatened systems simplify because binaries are cheaper | The metabolic metaphor is unsupported and stress effects vary across cognitive functions | State which function, stressor, timing and outcome were measured |
| Feeling worse while improving marks depth | It can romanticise adverse effects or current harm | Monitor benefit, cost, deterioration, risk and person-defined outcome separately |
| Grieving what was never there is a paradox | Grief can concern absence, unrealised possibility or ambiguous loss without violating logic | Name the object and conditions of grief directly |
| A smooth story may be false coherence; a messy one may show opening | Narrative form has many causes and cannot diagnose embodiment or truth | Assess coherence, accuracy, access, context and later conduct separately |
| Paradox operates identically across person, family and institution | Similar language across levels does not establish one mechanism | Name the unit, actors, rules, incentives and outcomes at each level |

## Evidence status of the original source list

The original alignment combines a historical communication theory, psychotherapy traditions, motivational research, a clinical arousal heuristic, attachment coding, developmental systems theory and social theory. Their terms should remain at their own levels.

- **Double-bind theory** was developed as a family-communication explanation of schizophrenia. That aetiological claim did not become an accepted model and risked blaming families. A carefully specified incompatible-demand pattern may remain useful descriptively when power, constrained response and blocked metacommunication are observed.
- **Dialectical behaviour therapy** combines acceptance and change within a structured clinical treatment. Its efficacy for specified populations and outcomes does not establish that paradox is a nervous-system capacity or that dialectical synthesis is always the right response.
- **Internal Family Systems** is a clinical model that may help some people represent conflicting experiences, protective strategies or self-positions. Parts should not be presented as literal neural modules or a universal multi-need architecture without corresponding evidence.
- **Motivational interviewing** offers a collaborative method for exploring ambivalence around a specified behaviour change. Reviews support some applications while evidence about its active mechanisms remains limited. Ambivalence is not one process across every moral, relational and clinical decision.
- **Window of tolerance** is a clinical heuristic rather than a standardised physiological range. It cannot establish how much complexity a person can hold or prescribe the order in which conflict should be addressed.
- **Acute-stress and executive-function research** supports bounded effects on working memory and cognitive flexibility, with heterogeneous and moderated findings across functions. It does not establish binary thinking as a universal defensive state.
- **Adult Attachment Interview coherence** is a method-specific feature of attachment-related discourse. Current research also questions simple categorical structure and supports dimensional variation. It is not a diagnostic of embodied truth, healing or narrative accuracy.
- **Bronfenbrenner's ecological systems theory** helps locate development within nested relationships, institutions and historical time. It does not establish that contradictions across levels are paradoxes or share one mechanism.
- **Foucault's historical and philosophical work** can support inquiry into knowledge, discipline and power. It is interpretive lineage, not experimental evidence of nervous-system or institutional paradox.
- **Institutional isomorphism** concerns how organisations become similar within a field. It does not by itself show that institutions reproduce the specific problem they claim to solve.

F11's strongest scientific base therefore comes from several narrower literatures: mixed emotion and ambivalence, executive function and stress, cognitive and integrative complexity, judgement and decision-making, narrative and memory, conflict and communication, organisational behaviour, and psychotherapy process and harm monitoring. Their convergence supports disciplined differentiation, not one paradox mechanism.

## F11's handoffs

F11 should use a conflict-differentiation sequence:

```text
Name what appears to conflict
        ↓
fix person · time · level · meaning · scope
        ↓
classify feeling · need · value · fact · prediction · action · rule
        ↓
test evidence, uncertainty, power, consequence and viable alternatives
        ↓
coexist · sequence · choose · protect · investigate · repair · refuse
        ↓
observe outcome and revise without rewriting responsibility
```

Four handoffs matter most:

1. **To immediate decision or protection:** when delay creates danger or restricted agency. Complexity should inform proportionate action, not prevent it.
2. **To F3 and F6:** when the conflict concerns self-explanation, evidence, bias, judgement, feedback and revision.
3. **To clinical or therapeutic support:** when ambivalence, grief, narrative difficulty, worsening or cognitive change creates significant distress or impairment. F11 is not a treatment protocol.
4. **To F12:** when the inquiry moves from individual or relational conflict to competing historical explanations, cultural hierarchy and species-level claims.

## Result of this pass

- **Framework status:** retain and substantially reframe.
- **Collection role:** cross-level conflict-differentiation method connecting experience, evidence, choice, power and revision.
- **Title:** revise from “Paradox Holds What Logic Cannot” to “Ambivalence, Complexity and Responsible Integration.”
- **Paradox:** reserve for genuinely apparent contradiction; do not use as an umbrella for mixed emotion, competing needs, uncertainty or disagreement.
- **Core architecture:** use eight construct families—scope, ambivalence, needs and values, evidence, access, narrative, power, and action and revision.
- **Main protection:** coexisting experience does not make factual claims, actions, power or responsibility equivalent.
- **State connection:** some forms of flexibility may become harder under pressure; no Gradient Position establishes complexity, truth or character.
- **Recovery:** difficult emotion can accompany change, but worsening requires assessment and never proves depth.
- **Narrative:** smoothness and messiness are not diagnostic of embodiment, accuracy or healing.
- **Responsibility:** context can remain fully explanatory without becoming exoneration.
- **Evidence status:** architectural first pass; every clinical, cognitive, narrative and institutional claim needs construct-, task-, population- and level-specific grounding.

## Grounding science and knowledge fields

F11 needs disciplines that can distinguish subjective coexistence from factual contradiction, individual cognition from social power and useful complexity from avoidable ambiguity. No one field can decide all three.

| Field | What it can ground in F11 | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Formal logic and philosophy of language** | Contradiction, consistency, scope, reference, ambiguity, inference and validity | Which premise is empirically true, how a person feels or what action is ethical |
| **Epistemology and philosophy of science** | Evidence, uncertainty, explanation, causal inference, underdetermination and revision | A clinical diagnosis, factual case finding or one final account of a contested event |
| **Affective science and mixed-emotion research** | Co-occurring affect, valence, arousal, differentiation and emotional dynamics | Factual truth, ethical choice or one meaning inferred from an emotion label |
| **Motivational science and attitude research** | Ambivalence, competing goals, approach–avoidance, intention, value and behaviour relations | That all inconsistency reflects ambivalence or that two options are equally safe |
| **Cognitive psychology and executive-function research** | Working memory, inhibition, shifting, attention, cognitive flexibility and task effects | Moral openness, maturity, truth or behaviour inferred from laboratory performance |
| **Judgement and decision science** | Evidence weighting, uncertainty, confidence, trade-offs, framing, choice and feedback | A universal rational solution or removal of social power and values from decisions |
| **Social and political psychology** | Integrative complexity, identity, threat, group conflict, ideology and perspective | Individual motive or historical truth inferred from a group score |
| **Developmental psychology** | Development of cognitive flexibility, perspective-taking, mixed emotion, self-concept and moral judgement | One universal maturity ladder or adult capacity inferred from age |
| **Cultural psychology and anthropology** | Cultural variation in dialectical thinking, contradiction, selfhood, emotion and decision | One culture treated as uniformly complex, collectivist or tolerant of contradiction |
| **Clinical psychology and psychotherapy process research** | Ambivalence, dialectical methods, narrative change, treatment response, deterioration and adverse effects | A universal therapy sequence or worsening interpreted as necessary progress |
| **Psychiatry and neuropsychology** | Health conditions, medication, cognition, language, memory and differential assessment | Credibility, responsibility or truth determined by diagnosis or test score |
| **Stress, psychophysiology and neuroscience** | Bounded effects of stress and arousal on attention, memory, inhibition and flexibility | Binary thinking, paradox tolerance, moral behaviour or one “settled” brain state |
| **Narrative psychology, autobiographical memory and discourse research** | How accounts are organised, remembered, revised and adapted to audience or context | Event accuracy, embodiment or healing inferred from coherence alone |
| **Communication science and conflict research** | Mixed messages, framing, metacommunication, negotiation, misunderstanding and escalation | Equal power, good faith or truth inferred from interaction form |
| **Sociology and organisational science** | Role conflict, institutional contradiction, rules, incentives, professional fields and implementation gaps | One organisation's private psychology or an individual nervous-system mechanism |
| **Political science, public administration and policy research** | Competing mandates, governance, institutional trade-offs, accountability and policy outcomes | Individual motive or ethical legitimacy from formal policy consistency |
| **Ethics and moral psychology** | Value conflict, duties, consequences, moral judgement, responsibility and repair | Facts of an event or legal threshold without corresponding evidence |
| **Law, evidence and jurisprudence** | Burdens of proof, competing accounts, rights, duties, procedural fairness and reasoned decision | Psychological mechanism or identical answer across jurisdictions |
| **Power, violence and victimology research** | How ambiguity, coercion, credibility, dependence and “mutual conflict” language affect safety and agency | Diagnosis, guilt or complete motive from a risk or relationship pattern |
| **Systems science and complexity research** | Feedback, nonlinearity, emergence, interacting levels and unintended effects | Permission to use complexity metaphor without measurable variables or causal evidence |
| **Disability studies, neurodiversity and accessibility research** | Communication difference, cognitive access, normative fluency, supported decision-making and epistemic exclusion | Narrative style, speech or processing speed treated as competence or integration |
| **Participatory, qualitative and lived-experience research** | Ambivalence, grief, hidden conflict, meaning, service harm and outcomes missed by fixed measures | One account generalised to everyone or subjective certainty treated as factual proof |
| **Measurement science and psychometrics** | Construct validity, task effects, reliability, dimensionality and change measurement | A score treated as the whole person or proxy for truth and ethical conduct |

### Priority evidence

F11 should favour studies that define the kind of conflict and keep subjective experience, cognitive performance, expressed reasoning, factual accuracy, decision, behaviour and impact as separate outcomes. Useful evidence includes experimental task research, ecological and longitudinal studies, repeated within-person observation, communication and conflict studies, intervention trials with adverse-effect monitoring, organisational case comparison, cross-cultural measurement and qualitative work that shows how people describe coexistence and choice.

Every state-related claim should identify the stressor, timing, cognitive function, task, population and moderator rather than using arousal as a general explanation. Every narrative claim should identify the coding system, audience, language, memory conditions and criterion. Every relational or institutional claim should record power, viable refusal, material consequence, behaviour, impact and response to feedback.

F11 should not reward complexity for its own sake. The useful outcome is a more accurate and actionable account: one that preserves relevant feeling and context, identifies what remains uncertain, makes necessary choices and changes when later evidence requires it.
