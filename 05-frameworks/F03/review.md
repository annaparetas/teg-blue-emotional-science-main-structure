## Decision

**Keep F3 as a Foundational Framework, but retire “Adult Cognition and False Coherence” as its governing title and False Coherence as its master mechanism.**

Provisional title:

> **Adult Pattern Maintenance and Revision**

Suggested explanatory line:

> How adults anticipate consequences, assign meaning, protect valued self-positions, select relational response routes and use feedback in ways that may contribute to repetition or revision.

The original title makes cognition sound like the sole actor and makes inaccuracy the framework's conclusion before the relevant account has been tested. The current evidence work reveals a wider and more neutral question. Adults act with bodily, affective, relational, remembered and contextual information of varying availability and quality. They anticipate consequences, assign meaning, protect valued positions, choose or recruit responses, experience immediate effects and sometimes revise when feedback arrives.

The framework should examine that process without assuming that the resulting account is false, that stability is its purpose, or that one mechanism explains why it continues.

F3 is an explanatory layer, not a fourth causal model or a shortened version of ESC. It contributes questions about **how an adult participates within an event**. ESC owns the event's movement through entry conditions, organisation, action, feedback, updating, Return, recovery, learning and the next entry.

## The question F3 owns

> Given present conditions and the information currently available, how do prediction, meaning, valued self-position and available response routes shape adult participation—and how is feedback represented and used?

F3 owns adult maintenance-and-revision questions concerning:

- anticipated relational and practical consequences;
- meaning assignment and felt certainty;
- valued roles, status, belonging and identity continuity;
- the response route selected from available options;
- experienced immediate relief, reward, conflict reduction or restored predictability;
- response to contradiction, changed conditions and another person's feedback;
- differences among changed language, changed confidence, changed action and changed later conduct as candidate dimensions of revision.

F3 uses **maintenance** to name relations that may help keep a route available, not to establish temporal persistence from one event. It uses **revision** to distinguish changes in account, confidence, represented options and chosen action, not to declare that the whole event updated.

It does not own developmental origin, present biological organisation, the full event sequence, completion, Return, recovery, carryover, learning across cycles, persistence across time, truth, diagnosis, harm, coercion or completed repair.

## The core insight worth preserving

The strongest original insight is the separation between an explanation feeling coherent and an explanation being sufficiently accurate, revisable or behaviourally consequential:

> A person can form a coherent account from the information presently available, and that account can organise action or reduce uncertainty without necessarily incorporating all relevant bodily, contextual or interpersonal information.

Several further distinctions follow:

- coherence is not accuracy;
- felt certainty is not evidence;
- immediate relief is not durable updating;
- changed language is not necessarily changed access, expectation, action or conduct;
- disagreement is not proof of defensiveness;
- bodily intensity is not proof of an external interpretation;
- reduced felt access is not objectivity;
- explanation is not exoneration;
- internal revision is not relational repair.

These are inquiry rules, not a declaration that every stable account is defensive or false.

## The six F3 construct families

The current governing synthesis replaces one master mechanism with six questions.

| Construct family | Governing question | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **F3-A · Relational prediction and expected consequence** | What is expected to happen after disclosure, disagreement, dependence, withdrawal, error, status loss or another person's independent action? | The prediction may be accurate, condition-matched or newly formed; F3 cannot assume pathology or childhood origin |
| **F3-B · Meaning assignment and felt certainty** | What is a feeling, contradiction, delay, boundary, error or response taken to mean, and how certain does that interpretation become? | F3 cannot infer hidden bodily truth, deception or the correctness of another person's account |
| **F3-C · Self-position and identity-contingent maintenance** | Which valued role, status, belonging claim or identity continuity is being protected, and what would revision appear to cost? | A protected self-position does not establish diagnosis, fragility, a False Self or harmful behaviour |
| **F3-D · Relational response-route selection** | Which route is recruited: pause, support seeking, reassurance, accommodation, appeasement, negotiation, structure, withdrawal, concealment, imposition, retaliation or repair? | The visible route does not identify one mechanism, biological state, personality or inevitable sequence |
| **F3-E · Relief, reinforcement and replacement** | What immediate effect was experienced, what did the route support or replace, and is there evidence that it became more available later? | Relief does not establish reinforcement, recurrence, completion or updating; ESC compares immediate and longer outcomes, and Chronic owns demonstrated recurrence and rigidity |
| **F3-F · Feedback, revision and repair readiness** | How are contradiction, impact, failed prediction or changed conditions represented and used, and what changes across account, confidence, options and action? | F3 does not establish whole-event updating, Return, recovery or learning; agreement does not prove insight, and internal revision does not complete accountability, restitution or relational repair |

These families are provisional TEG-Blue integrations supported by component literatures. They are not validated types, stages or one established scientific model.

ESC sharpens two terms in the provisional family names. **Reinforcement** should be claimed only when later learning or increased route availability is evidenced; relief alone is insufficient. **Repair readiness** can describe a question about participation in feedback, but it cannot stand in for accountability, restitution, restored agency or completed repair.

## How F3 uses ESC without becoming ESC

The twelve Foundational Frameworks are explanatory layers, not extra stages inside the Emotional Systems Cycle. F3 can be applied at several moments in an ESC pass, but it does not own the pass itself.

| ESC moment | F3 contribution | Ownership boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Entry and conditions** | Ask which learned expectations, valued roles and anticipated relational consequences are presently relevant | F3 does not infer developmental origin or establish that a prior outcome was carried into this event; F2 owns developmental claims and ESC owns carryover |
| **Signal + organisation** | Ask what bodily, affective, represented-other and contextual information is currently participating in prediction and meaning | Model 1 owns information processing and coordinated conscious access; Model 2 owns present whole-organism organisation |
| **Access + meaning** | Apply F3-A through F3-C to prediction, interpretation, felt certainty and valued self-position | A feeling is not proof of the external conclusion, and reduced felt access is not proof that nothing is happening |
| **Action + response** | Apply F3-D to the response route considered, selected, inhibited or changed | The route must be described behaviourally; F3 does not derive behaviour from an account, and Harm–Power–Repair governs any restriction of another person's options |
| **Immediate and longer outcome** | Apply F3-E to the experienced value of the route and to candidate support, substitution or avoidance functions | ESC's route audit establishes what actually changed first, what changed later, who carried the cost and whether the condition remained open |
| **Feedback + updating** | Apply F3-F to whether feedback can remain representable and alter confidence, options, explanation or later choice | ESC owns whether the signal, prediction, route or organisation updated; changed language alone does not establish updating |
| **Return, recovery, learning + next entry** | Identify any revised prediction, self-position or response option that may be available for later use | The Gradient owns Return, ESC owns recovery, carryover and what enters later cycles, Chronic owns demonstrated persistence, and repair remains separate |

ESC therefore supplies the minimum route audit whenever F3 uses the words **maintenance**, **relief**, **revision** or **reinforcement**:

1. What condition was present?
2. What information was available?
3. What action or inhibition actually occurred?
4. What changed immediately?
5. What changed later across updating, recruitment, recovery, Return and recurrence?
6. Who carried the cost, what power was involved and did viable refusal remain?

The division of labour is concise:

> **F3 asks why a particular interpretation or route was plausible, valuable or revisable for the adult participant. ESC asks what actually happened through time and what the next event begins inside.**

## Reading the original F3 language against the current system

### Preserve, with precise translation

| Original direction | Current reading |
| --- | --- |
| Adult thinking does not occur in a neutral body | Interpretation occurs with bodily, affective, developmental, relational and present-condition influences; none alone settles the account |
| Coherent stories can organise identity and action | Narrative coherence, felt certainty, accuracy, behavioural effect and revisability should be measured separately |
| Insight may fail to change a recurring route | Changed language can diverge from changed access, prediction, action and feedback use; ESC then tracks updating, recovery, later conduct and what enters the next event separately |
| Challenge can make revision costly | Contradiction may carry identity, belonging, status, uncertainty or practical consequences; the exact cost and present conditions must be named |
| Immediate relief can help maintain a response | Reassurance, withdrawal, justification, control, compliance or restored closeness may reduce pressure; ESC must compare immediate and longer outcomes before reinforcement or persistence is claimed |
| Internal discomfort can affect relational interpretation | Bodily and affective information may influence appraisal and confidence; attribution must be investigated rather than assumed |

### Narrow, revise or retire

| Map wording or implication | Why it needs care | Safer direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “The lock” | It implies one closed mechanism and underplays revision, context and branching responses | Treat maintenance and revision as a sequence of questions with multiple possible routes |
| “The cost: truth” | F3 cannot decide truth in advance, and cost must identify recipient, form and timescale | Track accuracy, flexibility, access, action, relational impact and who carries each cost without assuming all are present |
| Development has already “configured” the adult system | It makes development fixed and gives F2 causal priority over present conditions and later learning | Treat developmental history as one possible contributor alongside current danger, culture, health, disability, goals, power and later experience |
| Unreadable signals cause cognition to build an account “from its own output” | People use partial bodily, social, remembered, linguistic and contextual information; reduced access is not total informational closure | Ask what information was available, how it was weighted, what alternatives were representable and how confidence changed |
| False Coherence is a complete story whose job is stability rather than accuracy | Purpose and falsity are inferred; coherence may be useful, accurate, provisional or socially required | Separate coherence, felt certainty, evidential correspondence, immediate function, revision and conduct |
| Relief is “precisely why” the story persists | Immediate relief does not by itself establish reinforcement or persistence | Use the ESC route audit to separate immediate from longer outcomes, then let Chronic research test recurrence, generalisation, rigidity and updating difficulty |
| Insight fails because it is produced by the same replacing system | This is circular and does not specify what changed | Compare narrative, bodily access, expected consequence, action repertoire, physiological recovery and later behaviour separately |
| Questioning the narrative registers as threat | Disagreement, caution or refusal may be accurate, protective or principled | Test affective intensity, identity relevance, uncertainty, social consequence, evidence use and capacity for revision |
| Skills can grow while “the body has learned nothing new” | The claim is not operationalised and creates a body–mind split | State which expectation, response, action or measure changed, then use ESC to distinguish updating, recovery, carryover and later learning |
| Internal discomfort is attributed outward and another person's boundary becomes attack | This is one possible attribution pattern and cannot be read from disagreement or distress alone | Assess the condition, attribution, confidence, alternative accounts, response route, behaviour and feedback |
| Others are recruited to regulate through closeness, distance, management or control | Support seeking, chosen distance, structure and coercive control are not one continuum | Name the behaviour, consent, reciprocity, authority, imposed cost and effect on another person's options |

## The status of False Coherence

**False Coherence should remain as a historical or early recognition label, not the framework title, a person-type, a score or a causal mechanism.**

The useful observation can be preserved by decomposing it:

```text
available information
        ↓
prediction + interpretation + felt certainty + valued self-position
        ↓
available response routes
        ↓
selected action or inhibition + experienced immediate effect
        ↓
feedback use + possible revision of account, confidence, options or action
```

This is an **F3 analytic slice**, not a replacement cycle. When the event is followed through time, these variables must be placed inside ESC:

```text
ESC entry + conditions
        ↓
signal + organisation + access
        ↓
F3 prediction · meaning · self-position
        ↓
action + response, including the F3 route selected
        ↓
ESC immediate outcome · longer outcome · feedback + updating
        ↓
Return · recovery · learning · carryover into the next entry
```

An account should not be called false merely because it is coherent, protective, emotionally intense, resistant to challenge or different from another person's account. The relevant inquiry is whether it remains connected to available evidence, current conditions, alternative explanations, another person's separateness and later consequences—and whether it can revise when those inputs change.

## F3 is a branching field, not a pathway to harm

The same prediction or self-position pressure can be followed by very different routes:

- pausing and gathering information;
- seeking support or reassurance;
- accommodating or appeasing;
- negotiating or setting structure;
- choosing distance or a boundary;
- concealing or avoiding;
- imposing, retaliating or coercing;
- accepting feedback and repairing.

Internal pressure does not select the branch by itself. Goals, power, available options, concern, habits, culture, consequences and deliberate participation may all matter. Harm becomes assessable only when behaviour, authority, imposed cost, option-field restriction, impact, recurrence and response to accountability are examined directly.

ESC adds a second protection: the surface route does not reveal what it accomplished. Pausing, reassurance, withdrawal, structure, confrontation or connection may change the relevant condition, support later capacity, reduce pressure without updating or produce a mixed outcome. The route audit—not the route's appearance—determines what can responsibly be said.

ESC's relational-escalation sequence must not be imported into F3 as a cognitive or biological pathway. It is an observational set of questions about demands, power, compliance, feedback, recurrence and protection. F3 may contribute hypotheses about prediction, meaning and self-position, but those hypotheses neither establish nor excuse escalation.

## F3's handoffs

F2 can contribute developmentally shaped possibilities and expectations to F3's adult questions. This is an explanatory handoff, not a claim that a person moves through F1, F2 and F3 as temporal stages. A live event remains organised through ESC:

```text
inherited capacities + developmental possibilities + present conditions
        ↓
current information and whole-organism organisation
        ↓
F3 prediction + meaning + valued self-position
        ↓
F3 response-route selection within ESC action + response
        ↓
ESC immediate and longer outcomes
        ↓
feedback + updating + Return / recovery / carryover / learning
```

Four connections then remain visible:

1. **Across ESC:** F3 variables are located inside the event, while ESC records actual action, immediate and longer outcomes, updating and what enters the next cycle.
2. **To Chronic:** when recurrence, generalisation, rigidity, cumulative cost or updating difficulty becomes the question.
3. **To Harm, Power and Repair:** whenever a response affects another person's agency, safety or options.
4. **To F4:** when a personally held expectation becomes part of a shared norm, rule or institution—but only after collective processes and power are investigated independently.

F3 does not convert one event into a maintained pattern, private coherence into collective rules, internal revision into whole-event updating, or insight into repair.

## Result of this pass

- **Framework status:** retain and substantially reframe.
- **Collection role:** third Foundational Framework; adult maintenance-and-revision layer between developmental shaping and collective rule formation.
- **Title:** revise from “Adult Cognition and False Coherence” to “Adult Pattern Maintenance and Revision.”
- **Scientific-facing subtitle:** Adult prediction, meaning, self-position, response-route selection and revision across present relational events.
- **False Coherence:** retain only as a historical or recognition label after decomposition.
- **Core architecture:** use the six construct families—prediction, meaning, self-position, response route, immediate effect and feedback/revision.
- **ESC relationship:** treat F3 as an adult-participation cross-section inside ESC, not a competing temporal model; use ESC to test actual action, immediate and longer outcomes, updating, recovery, carryover and the next entry.
- **Route-audit rule:** do not infer reinforcement, persistence, completion or successful revision from relief, coherence, agreement, quiet behaviour or changed language.
- **Main protection:** do not infer falsity, developmental history, biological state, diagnosis, harm or coercion from coherence, discomfort, disagreement, relief or one visible response.
- **Evidence status:** Pilots 05–10 support the architecture and boundaries; the six F3 construct families still require formal claim decomposition and a dedicated source-level review.

## Grounding science and knowledge fields

F3 needs evidence about adult interpretation, action and revision without reducing those processes to cognition alone. The same outward response may arise through different combinations of memory, affect, prediction, role, present conditions and available choice.

| Field | What it can ground in F3 | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cognitive psychology and cognitive science** | Attention, memory, reasoning, attribution, prediction, confidence, metacognition and updating | The truth of a relational account or a person's motive from one task or bias label |
| **Social psychology** | Identity, influence, self-relevance, dissonance, attribution, motivated cognition and response to feedback | A universal mechanism or a direct explanation of institutional structure |
| **Personality and individual-differences research** | Relatively stable variation and context-dependent differences in adult functioning | A fixed person type, diagnosis, moral character or inevitable conduct |
| **Affective science and interoception research** | Relations among bodily information, feeling, appraisal, awareness and action readiness | A hidden truth that overrides context or one bodily signature of accuracy |
| **Judgement, decision and behavioural science** | Choice under uncertainty, confidence, incentives, trade-offs, defaults and feedback | That laboratory choice captures a relationship, culture or ethical responsibility in full |
| **Learning, reinforcement and habit research** | How immediate consequences, repetition and context can make a response more available | That relief proves falsity or that one reinforced response is Chronic |
| **Clinical psychology and psychotherapy-process research** | Experiential avoidance, coping, narrative change, therapeutic process and changes across time | Diagnosis from a TEG-Blue pattern or evidence that one therapy theory is a biological mechanism |
| **Relationship, family and interpersonal research** | Dependence, conflict, accommodation, support, boundary response, mutual influence and repair | One person's internal mechanism considered without the other people and conditions involved |
| **Communication science, discourse research and conversation analysis** | How accounts are built, negotiated, resisted and revised in interaction | Whether a statement is true, deceptive or unconsciously motivated without additional evidence |
| **Sociology and microsociology** | Roles, identity positions, interaction order, status expectations and socially available accounts | A nervous-system state or personal developmental origin inferred from a social role |
| **Cultural psychology and psychological anthropology** | Cultural variation in selfhood, emotion, explanation, obligation and acceptable response | A culture as one mind or an individual as a simple expression of group membership |
| **Organisational behaviour and occupational psychology** | Role pressure, authority, incentives, voice, silence, feedback and decision-making at work | Direct transfer from a workplace finding to family, clinical or legal settings |
| **Moral psychology and ethics** | Moral judgement, responsibility, justification and distinctions among explanation, blame and repair | The correct ethical outcome from a psychological mechanism alone |
| **Legal psychology and forensic psychology** | Psycholegal questions involving memory, testimony, decision-making, capacity, risk communication and legal or administrative judgement | A general test of honesty, dangerousness or responsibility from coherence, confidence or diagnosis |
| **Criminology and violence research** | Behaviour patterns, context, opportunity, recurrence, prevention and system response where offending or violence is actually in scope | A crime or harmful act explained by one inner state, emotion or developmental story |
| **Philosophy of mind, epistemology and phenomenology** | Clarification of experience, interpretation, self-knowledge, evidence and the limits of inference | Empirical validation of F3 without corresponding observation and research |

Forensic psychology and criminology enter F3 conditionally. They matter when the framework is used in legal, violence, offending, testimony or administrative contexts. They should not make ordinary disagreement, defensiveness or narrative coherence sound forensic.

### Priority evidence

F3 should combine experiments with ecologically valid interaction research, longitudinal and intensive within-person designs, behavioural observation, multi-informant evidence and studies that separately measure explanation, confidence, action and later conduct. ESC integration also requires temporally separated measurement of entry conditions, prediction, action, immediate outcome, later updating and what is available in a subsequent event; retrospective coherence should not substitute for that sequence. Research on harm or coercion must also measure power, option restriction, impact and response to feedback rather than infer them from an internal account.
