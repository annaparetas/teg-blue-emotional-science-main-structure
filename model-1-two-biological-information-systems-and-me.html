Two distinguishable forms of processing inside one organism.

Model 1 core definition

ESS and CLS are two distinguishable but overlapping forms of biological information processing within one organism.

They are not isolated anatomical machines and do not operate in a fixed sequence. Both can work outside conscious access, become partly accessible and continuously influence one another. ME is the person who may participate with accessible information—not a third system or internal controller.

Model 1 does not establish

Two sealed brain modules, an unconscious ESS opposed to a conscious CLS, an ESS → CLS conveyor belt, a hierarchy of wiser and less-wise systems, or a person-level moral classification.

Approved definition

ESS · Emotional–Somatic System

Functional territory

ESS names distributed biological information processing concerned with bodily information, emotional significance, biological weighting, felt qualities and action-readiness.

Emotional–somatic does not mean conscious emotion or “the body without the brain.” ESS participation may occur whether or not its information becomes consciously available to ME.

Approved definition

CLS · Cognitive–Logical System

Functional territory

CLS names biological information processing through which information may be represented, compared, sequenced, contextualised, imagined, expressed through language or other symbolic forms, organised into explanations, used for planning and reconsidered.

Cognitive–logical does not mean all cognition, pure logic, objective truth, consciousness itself or a command centre. CLS can construct a coherent explanation from incomplete, distorted or differently accessible information. Its processing is embodied, may remain partly outside present conscious access and continuously interacts with ESS.

Approved access relationship

Coordinated conscious access

Coordinated conscious access names the relationship through which some participating information may become experienceable or inspectable by ME. It is not another biological system.

Processing

Information may participate

ESS and CLS processing can influence the organism without becoming consciously available.

Availability

Information may become accessible

Accessibility does not guarantee recognition, accurate identification, interpretation or deliberate use.

Layer 5

Conscious availability and felt access

Layer 5 is the first explicit conscious-availability question in the analytic lens—not a biological threshold where consciousness begins.

Participation

ME may participate

Possible participation remains different from deliberate action, behaviour and later updating.

Approved participant boundary

ME is the living person.

ME

ME names the person who lives, responds and may turn toward, examine, communicate, inhibit or act with what becomes available.

ME is not a homunculus above biology, an executive outside the organism or a guarantee that information will be used accurately or ethically.

Approved Model 1 dimensions

Three capacities can vary differently.

Emotional–somatic information access and use

How much bodily and emotional information ME can notice, differentiate, interpret and use.

Affective-sharing access

How much another person’s emotional state becomes consciously readable while self and other remain distinguishable. This does not guarantee accuracy, concern or agreement.

Mentalizing access and use

How much ME can imagine, represent, question and revise an understanding of another person’s perspective, feelings or intentions. This does not guarantee truth or ethical use.

The capacities may be uneven, partial, delayed and context-dependent. They do not define a Gradient Position, personality, diagnosis, morality or overall level of awareness.

Open decision

Variables will be added to the variables.

The eight configurations remain under development

The three capacities and the existing eight structural combinations are not being removed or replaced by a simple graded model. Anna will determine which additional variables are required to explain the quality, organisation and use of each capacity. No final configuration architecture has been approved.
