# Emotional Access And Reality Contact

Status: foundation concept draft, added 2026-07-09

This file develops a core TEG-Blue idea:

> When people lose contact with emotion and body state as information, they
> lose contact with part of reality.

This does not mean they lose all reality testing, become irrational, or cannot
think clearly. It means cognition may be modelling the situation from
incomplete data.

The safer TEG-Blue formulation:

> Reality contact depends on holding body state, emotion, evidence, context,
> impact, and another person's reality together.

This file depends on:

- `foundation/12-emotions-as-information.md`
- `foundation/10-ess-cls-educational-model.md`
- `foundation/06-state-shaped-perception.md`

## Core Idea

The brain/body system does not model reality from facts alone.

It models reality through:

- body state
- emotion
- sensation
- memory
- prediction
- attention
- cognition
- evidence
- context
- relationship
- impact
- available response

Emotion and nervous-system state are part of the information the system uses
to know where it is, what matters, what is safe, what is threatening, what
needs protection, and what needs repair.

When a person loses access to that information, cognition can keep working. It
can explain, plan, narrate, justify, compare, argue, and appear coherent. But
it may be working without enough contact with the body state, emotional
signal, relational impact, or repair need that is also part of the situation.

This is why TEG-Blue should not equate cognitive coherence with reality
contact.

> A person can make sense and still be missing important reality.

## Not Feeling Is Not Neutrality

Not feeling something does not mean the person is objective.

Numbness, detachment, calmness, analysis, certainty, or lack of visible impact
may be state-shaped too.

Sometimes the system disconnects from emotion because emotion is too much to
hold. Sometimes it disconnects because the person learned that feeling was
unsafe, useless, shameful, weak, punishable, or impossible to act on. Sometimes
disconnection is a survival adaptation.

That adaptation may protect the person.

It can also narrow reality.

Examples:

- "I do not feel affected, therefore it does not matter."
- "I can explain it, therefore I have understood it."
- "I am calm, therefore I am accurate."
- "I do not feel empathy right now, therefore there was no real harm."
- "I do not feel my boundary, therefore I do not have one."
- "I do not feel danger, therefore this is safe."
- "I do not feel grief, therefore nothing was lost."
- "I do not feel anger, therefore nothing was violated."
- "I have a logical reason, therefore impact is irrelevant."
- "If I cannot feel it, it is not real."

These are not signs that cognition is bad. They show cognition operating with
missing emotional or body-state information.

The question is not:

> Am I being emotional or rational?

The question is:

> What is my body feeling or reporting?
>
> What part of that information is my cognition using?
>
> What part might cognition be leaving out, overriding, or turning into a story?
>
> Am I interpreting reality through a protective lens?
>
> What facts, context, impact, or another person's experience still need to be
> included?

## Two Reality Distortions

TEG-Blue needs to hold two distortions at once.

### 1. Feeling-Fact Collapse

This is the focus of `foundation/06-state-shaped-perception.md`.

The person has strong access to a feeling, but the feeling fuses with fact,
evidence, interpretation, entitlement, or control.

Pattern:

> I feel it, therefore it is true.

### 2. Emotional-State Disconnection

This file names the second distortion.

The person loses access to emotion or body state as information, and cognition
models reality without enough contact with what the organism is carrying.

Pattern:

> I do not feel it, therefore it is not real.

or:

> I can explain it, therefore I am in contact with the whole situation.

Both distortions can harm reality contact.

The answer is not to obey emotion or reject cognition.

The answer is integration:

> emotion, body state, cognition, evidence, context, impact, and repair all
> becoming available enough to inform response.

## Reality Contact Layers

In TEG-Blue, reality contact is layered.

It includes:

1. Internal reality: what the body and emotion are carrying.
2. External reality: what happened, what was said or done, what can be
   verified, and what remains uncertain.
3. Relational reality: what another person experienced, what impact occurred,
   and what the relationship now needs.
4. Contextual reality: history, power, timing, role, resources, risk, and
   constraints.
5. State reality: whether the system is in safety, threat, control, shutdown,
   regulation, or repair capacity.
6. Repair reality: what would actually change the pattern, restore shared
   reality, protect boundaries, or reduce harm.

No single layer is the whole truth.

Emotion without evidence can collapse into certainty.

Evidence without emotional and relational contact can become detached,
defensive, or incomplete.

Cognition without body-state contact can become false objectivity.

Body information without context can become old prediction.

Repair requires enough access to more than one layer.

## Why This Matters

If a person cannot feel or recognise their emotional and nervous-system state,
they may lose access to:

- their limits
- their needs
- their fear
- their anger
- their grief
- their desire
- their care
- their impact on others
- another person's distress
- the cost of staying in a pattern
- the need for repair

This can make harmful or unsafe conditions feel normal.

It can also make controlling, avoidant, detached, or over-rational responses
feel like accuracy.

The person may believe they are dealing with facts, when they are actually
dealing with a model built under limited access.

This is one reason TEG-Blue should treat emotional access as part of reality
contact, not as the opposite of reality.

## Self-Awareness

Self-awareness is not only noticing what one feels.

It is also noticing when feeling is unavailable.

Useful questions:

- What am I feeling?
- What am I not able to feel right now?
- What is my body doing?
- Am I calm, or am I disconnected?
- Am I clear, or am I certain because complexity is unavailable?
- What body information is my cognition using?
- What body information might my explanation be leaving out or overriding?
- Am I interpreting reality through a protective lens?
- What facts, context, impact, or another person's experience still need to be
  included?
- What impact might I not be registering?
- What would become visible if I could include emotion, body state, evidence,
  and another person's experience at the same time?

Self-awareness does not mean believing every feeling.

It means including emotional and body-state information in the reality model
without making that information the whole truth.

## Guardrails

Do not use this concept as a clinical claim about psychosis, delusion, or
formal loss of reality testing.

Use `reality contact` here as TEG-Blue framework language: contact with
internal state, external facts, relational impact, context, and repair needs.

Do not use this concept to say that emotion is always accurate.

Do not use it to rank feeling above evidence or body above cognition.

This concept depends on the learning frame in
`foundation/10-ess-cls-educational-model.md`: TEG-Blue distinguishes body-side
condition-reading from thinking-side modelling so people can notice how both
information streams cooperate, miss each other, or distort. This is an
educational model, not a literal split inside the person.

Do not use it to shame people who are numb, detached, shut down, or unable to
feel. Disconnection may be a survival adaptation.

Do not force emotional access when the system is overwhelmed. Sometimes
contact with emotion has to return slowly, with enough safety, support,
distance, or repair.

Do not use emotional access to excuse harm. A person may have reduced access
to emotion or body state and still be responsible for impact, repair, and
changed behaviour.
