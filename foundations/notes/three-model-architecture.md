## Decision status

This note records the accepted orientation and the open decisions that remain beneath it. The public orientation is now active, but it does not make every term or implementation choice settled.

The proposal is:

1. **Emotions carry information** becomes a foundational premise across TEG-Blue.
2. **The Emotional Signal Map** becomes a reference map rather than a fourth causal model.
3. **The Two Biological Information Systems and ME**, **The Nervous System Organisation Gradient** and **The Emotional-Somatic Cycle** remain three differentiated models.
4. The twelve Foundational Frameworks explain how the architecture may be shaped, maintained, constrained and changed across different levels and timescales. They are not additional stages inside any model.

## Why this separation is needed

The current system already contains four distinct questions:

- What information does an emotion carry?
- How is emotional–somatic and reflective–symbolic information processed and made available to the person?
- How is the organism organised now, and how easily can that organisation change?
- What happens to the response through action, feedback, updating, recovery and later learning?

These questions affect one another. They are not interchangeable.

When they sit inside one large map without a visible architecture, several collapses become easy:

- a feeling starts to look like a complete reading of external reality;
- interpretation starts to look like the signal itself;
- nervous-system organisation starts to define the emotion or behaviour;
- immediate relief starts to look like completion;
- Return starts to look like recovery, repair or reconciliation;
- the twelve Foundational Frameworks start to look like twelve more mechanisms inside one organism.

The architecture begins by giving each question a home.

## Foundational premise · Emotions carry information

### Working statement

> **Emotions carry organism-relative information about bodily and situational relevance. Feelings are how some of that information becomes consciously perceptible.**

Emotional information may concern viability, bodily need, connection, loss, danger, obstruction, possibility, boundaries, action or recovery. It can influence attention, physiology and readiness without becoming a clear feeling or a named explanation.

The premise rejects two opposing errors:

~~~
emotion is irrational noise
        ≠
emotion is automatic truth
~~~

The presence of a feeling establishes that an experience is occurring and that something has acquired relevance within the organism. It does not, by itself, establish:

- the complete external condition;
- the cause of the feeling;
- the accuracy of the first interpretation;
- another person's intention or internal state;
- the action that should follow;
- the safety, fit or ethical direction of that action.

### Public validity boundary

The accessible phrase **feelings are valid signals** may remain when its meaning is stated directly:

> **Valid does not mean infallible. The experience is real and worth reading rather than dismissing; its source, meaning and implications may still need clarification.**

### Why information is preferred to data

**Emotion is data** is memorable provenance. As a formal scientific-facing phrase, however, **data** can suggest a raw, objective or already interpreted measurement of reality.

**Emotions carry information** better preserves selection, construction, learning, context and uncertainty. It also keeps emotional information available for examination without demoting it beneath articulate reasoning or placing it beyond correction.

## The Emotional Signal Map

Accessible working name: **The Emotional Signal Map**
Public orientation line: **Emotions as Information**
Scientific-facing subtitle: **Body and Situation Signals across function, felt experience, conditions, interpretation and updating**

### What kind of structure it is

The Emotional Signal Map is a functional classification and inquiry map. It is not proposed as a separate organ, anatomical system, Gradient state or fixed sequence.

It owns one central question:

> **What information is this signal organised to carry?**

### Two signal groups

| Signal group | Starting point | Core question | Important boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Body Signals** | Changes in internal physiological conditions | What does the body itself require or need restored? | The felt signal does not identify every cause, and no single biomarker is the feeling |
| **Situation Signals** | What current, remembered, imagined or anticipated conditions may mean for the organism | What has become relevant for connection, safety, action, loss, possibility or survival? | Emotional significance is not a complete external fact or instruction |

Both groups are embodied. A Situation Signal can change breathing, temperature, pain, hunger and energy. A Body Signal can reveal danger or change how a situation is interpreted.

### Five reading questions

The present Fluid page uses four questions: function, body, conditions and completion. The proposed map keeps those observations while separating updating from completion and making felt access visible.

| Reading | Question | What it prevents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Function** | What does the signal interrupt, protect, drive, slow or make available? | Treating the emotion name as a behaviour or moral quality |
| **Felt experience** | How may the signal become perceptible, and does it become differentiated feeling? | Treating signal formation and conscious feeling as the same event |
| **Conditions** | What bodily or situational condition may the signal be organised to report? | Treating the first explanation as the condition itself |
| **Updating** | What evidence or changed condition could revise its weighting, meaning or readiness? | Treating relief, explanation or suppression as evidence of revision |
| **Completion** | What must change sufficiently for the particular demand or recruited task to change course? | Treating completion as one universal discharge or whole-body endpoint |

### Terms that must remain separate

| Term | Working meaning in this architecture |
| --- | --- |
| **Emotional information** | Organism-relative information about bodily or situational relevance |
| **Emotional signal** | Coordinated biological signalling through which that relevance participates in attention, physiology and readiness |
| **Emotion** | The larger coordinated event or signal configuration |
| **Feeling** | The consciously perceptible aspect of some emotional information |
| **Interpretation** | What the available information is understood or explained to mean |
| **Action** | What occurs next; influenced but not authored or justified by the signal alone |

~~~
signal formation
        ≠
conscious feeling
        ≠
interpretation
        ≠
external fact
        ≠
action
~~~

## Model 1 · The Two Biological Information Systems and ME

Accessible working name: **The Two Biological Information Systems and ME**
Scientific-facing subtitle: **Emotional–somatic and reflective–symbolic information processing, conscious access and human participation**

### Governing question

> **What forms of information processing may be occurring, what becomes available to the lived person, and how can the person participate with it?**

### This model owns

- ESS and CLS as interacting educational groupings;
- ME as the lived conscious participant rather than a third biological system;
- coordinated conscious access as an access relationship rather than the source of the signal or a separate biological system;
- presence, access, differentiation, interpretation and use as separate questions;
- recurrent influence between emotional–somatic and reflective–symbolic processing;
- participation, reflection, choice and responsibility within constrained conditions.

### Relationship to the Emotional Signal Map

The Signal Map identifies the information being generated. This model asks how that information forms, becomes accessible, is interpreted and may participate in conscious action.

ESS does not own truth, and CLS does not own regulation or rationality. Both may contribute to clarification, persistence, updating, care or harmful control.

### This model does not own

- Gradient Positions;
- Fluid or Chronic depth;
- a complete account of action;
- the temporal course of an emotion episode;
- Return, recovery or repair as outcomes belonging to one system.

## Model 2 · The Nervous System Organisation Gradient

Accessible working name: **The Nervous System Organisation Gradient**
Scientific-facing subtitle: **Present organism-level resource and capacity organisation across Fluid and Chronic reference positions**

### Governing question

> **How is the organism currently organised, and how available are flexibility, updating, protection, connection and restoration?**

### This model owns

- nervous-system organisation as a field of changing availability;
- Positions X, A, A↔B, B, C, D and Z;
- Fluid and far Chronic as reference planes rather than two kinds of person;
- intermediate and uneven depths across processes, relationships and contexts;
- resource-allocation emphasis, perception, feeling, connection and action availability;
- Return as movement towards wider flexibility, less defensive dominance and renewed restorative availability.

### Relationship to the Emotional Signal Map

The signal remains emotional information across the Gradient. Organisation changes which signals receive priority, whether they become differentiated feeling, how easily they can be interpreted and whether new conditions can revise them.

The Gradient does not turn a strong feeling into proof, and Chronic organisation does not make a feeling false. Real danger, loss, injustice, deprivation or absent repair can keep an accurate signal active.

### This model does not own

- the identity or moral quality of a person;
- one autonomic branch or chemical fingerprint;
- the external cause of an emotion;
- behaviour, motive or ethical direction;
- the whole temporal cycle through action, feedback and learning.

## Model 3 · The Emotional-Somatic Cycle

Accessible working name: **The Emotional-Somatic Cycle · ESC**
Scientific-facing subtitle: **Time-indexed emotional–somatic and reflective–symbolic processing across conditions, organism organisation, conscious participation, action, feedback, recovery and learning**

**Naming decision · 23 August 2026:** **The Emotional-Somatic Cycle** is the canonical accessible name. It keeps the emotional–somatic information stream visible while the scientific-facing subtitle explicitly includes reflective–symbolic processing, organism organisation and ME participation. The earlier public label **The Emotional Systems Cycle** remains searchable provenance; it is no longer the canonical page title.

### Governing question

> **What changes, what remains active, what becomes learnable, and what does the next event begin inside?**

### This model owns

- entry conditions, capacity, prior learning and carryover;
- conditions becoming relevant;
- signal and organisation changing together;
- access, interpretation and ME participation;
- action, deliberate inhibition and environmental or relational response;
- feedback and updating;
- completion as a condition- or task-specific question;
- Return availability, recovery and learning as separable outcomes;
- recurrence, escalation and what becomes the next cycle's input.

### Relationship to the Emotional Signal Map

The Signal Map identifies what the emotional information may concern. ESC follows what happens to that information through conditions, action and feedback.

The same signal may:

- update when its condition changes;
- remain active because the condition remains;
- become less consciously available while still organising behaviour;
- reduce in immediate intensity without relevant updating;
- enter a later cycle as changed expectation, readiness, depletion or accumulated cost.

### This model does not own

- a fixed biological sequence or universal clock;
- one required pathway for every emotion;
- a claim that expression or discharge completes an event;
- an inference from relief to reinforcement, recovery or Return;
- a diagnosis of a person from one recurrent route.

## Shared vocabulary and model ownership

These terms connect the architecture but should not become synonyms.

| Term | Working meaning | Primary home | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Regulation** | Continuing biological or level-specific activity through which something changes, is maintained or restored | Cross-model; level must be named | Does not mean calm, health, kindness or successful completion |
| **Completion** | Sufficient change in a particular condition, demand or task for recruitment to change course | Emotional Signal Map and ESC | Not one universal physiological discharge or whole-person endpoint |
| **Updating** | Changed weighting, expectation, interpretation, readiness or future recruitment following new conditions or evidence | ESC, with participation described by Model 1 | A changed explanation does not establish changed physiology, behaviour or repair |
| **Return** | Renewed availability of more flexible, less defensively dominated and more restorative organisation | Nervous System Gradient | Not calm, compliance, forgetting, completed recovery or restored trust |
| **Baseline re-access** | Renewed access to Baseline X where the active Gradient architecture supports that description | Nervous System Gradient | Should not be used as a universal physiological set point |
| **Recovery** | Rebuilding after demand across physiological, cognitive, experiential, sleep, pain, metabolic and other channels | ESC outcome and Gradient support layer | Different channels can change at different rates |
| **Restoration** | Resource rebuilding or renewed availability of restorative processes | Gradient and physiology support pages | Not identical to Return or limited to one observable state |
| **Repair** | Action concerning impact, responsibility, restitution, protection and possible relational rebuilding | Harm, Power and Repair; F8 handoff | Another person's Return does not repair harm |
| **Carryover** | Residual activation, changed weighting, expectation, depleted capacity, learned route or accumulated cost entering later conditions | ESC | A decomposable description, not an emotion stored intact in tissue |
| **Learning** | Change carried beyond the immediate event in weighting, prediction, skill, expectation, readiness, concept, narrative or action tendency | Cross-model; ESC follows what enters later cycles, Model 1 distinguishes participating processes, and the Gradient locates changed recruitment | Learning is not necessarily accurate, beneficial, conscious, generalised or permanent; harmful and protective routes can also be learned |
| **Integration** | A process through which relevant emotional–somatic, reflective–symbolic, contextual, relational and impact information can remain differentiated while becoming mutually usable in later understanding, choice and action | Cross-model, with responsible integration examined in F11 | Not fusion, complete agreement, one smooth narrative, calm, recovery, reconciliation, ethical conduct or a score by which people can be ranked |

## Completion profile

Completion should not become a single yes-or-no verdict. ESC can record a profile through separate questions:

1. **Condition:** Did the relevant bodily, material or relational condition change?
2. **Information:** Did credible evidence alter the signal or interpretation?
3. **Recruitment:** Could the previous protective or problem-solving task reduce?
4. **Recovery:** Could spent resources begin rebuilding across the required channels?
5. **Return:** Did wider organisational flexibility become available?
6. **Carryover:** What remains active or changed as the next cycle begins?

The answers may differ. A condition can end while physiology is still recovering. Pressure can fall while the condition remains. Return can begin before recovery is complete. Repair may still be required after all six questions have been answered.

## Learning and integration across the architecture

Learning and integration describe longer change, not additional gates that must occur before one event can finish.

**Learning is broader than immediate updating.** Updating asks whether information, weighting or readiness changed within or following an event. Learning asks what part of that change persists, transfers, generalises or becomes easier to recruit in later conditions.

**Integration is not the fusion of ESS and CLS or the production of one final account.** It asks whether relevant signals, interpretations, evidence, context, impact and possible action can remain distinguishable while becoming available to inform one another.

| Architecture layer | Learning contribution | Integration contribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Emotional Signal Map** | Identifies what changed signal recognition, differentiation and expectation across later encounters | Keeps signal, feeling, interpretation, external fact and action distinct while allowing their relationships to be examined |
| **Two Biological Information Systems and ME** | Distinguishes changed emotional–somatic weighting and readiness from revised concepts, narratives, plans and deliberate skills | Examines whether emotional–somatic and reflective–symbolic information become sufficiently accessible, differentiated and revisable for ME to use together |
| **Nervous System Gradient** | Locates possible changes in recruitment threshold, persistence, flexibility, recovery and what becomes available under later conditions | Examines whether a wider range of capacities can become available together without treating one Position as an integration score |
| **Emotional-Somatic Cycle** | Follows what feedback and consequence carry into later entry conditions, expectations, routes and action | Examines whether what happened becomes usable across later understanding and action rather than remaining only relief, explanation or isolated insight |

Neither term supplies an ethical conclusion. A person can learn a more effective harmful route, integrate information instrumentally or construct a coherent account that excludes another person's impact. Behaviour, power, viable refusal, responsibility and repair remain separate evidence planes.

## Role of the Fluid and Chronic emotion pages

The two active pages become companion views of the Emotional Signal Map rather than separate emotion taxonomies.

### Emotions and Feelings · Fluid Gradient

Primary questions:

- What does each signal help the organism register?
- How may it become consciously readable?
- How does its priority change with present organisation?
- What evidence, condition or action could allow updating or completion?

The Fluid page remains the clearest home for the Body and Situation Signal groups, the four Situation Signal families and individual signal definitions.

### Emotions and Feelings · Chronic Survival

Primary questions:

- What emotional information remains active?
- How has its weighting, accessibility or felt texture changed?
- What reaches ME as differentiated feeling?
- What interpretation does CLS construct from available information?
- What keeps the original requirement open or difficult to revise?

The Chronic page should retain two opposing interpretive risks:

- **Feeling–Fact Collapse:** a feeling becomes the main evidence for an external conclusion.
- **Emotional-State Disconnection:** faint or absent felt access becomes evidence that nothing is happening.

Both risks concern how information is accessed and interpreted. Neither makes the person a type, proves distortion or removes the possibility of real danger.

## Provisional closing

An emotion can show that something matters without explaining the whole situation. Reflection can clarify that information without standing outside the organism or becoming neutral merely because it is articulate. The Signal Map identifies what may be present; the three models show how it is processed, organised and changed through time.
