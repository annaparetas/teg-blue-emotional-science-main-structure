# Ethics And Responsibility

Status: active foundation and cross-ecosystem constraint

This file consolidates the ethical guardrails that should inform both public sites, the TEG-Blue Engine, and applied builds.

## Core Guardrail

TEG-Blue can explain the mechanism behind a pattern without excusing its impact or removing responsibility.

The gradient explains organisation, not innocence.

Understanding why a pattern emerged does not make the pattern harmless. A nervous-system explanation can clarify what happened, but it cannot decide that harm does not matter.

This rule governs the public framework, both Engine resolutions, `.com` tools, and every applied build.

## Ethical Information Model

TEG-Blue treats emotions as structured biological and relational information, not automatic fact.

That creates two ethical duties at once:

- do not dismiss a feeling, body signal, or distressed response merely because it needs interpretation
- do not turn feeling, calmness, certainty, explanation, or state language into proof of what happened, what another person intended, or who a person is

Reality contact requires more than either feeling or explanation alone. It may include body state, emotion, observable events, evidence, context, power, impact, another person's experience, and repair needs.

Use:

> Emotion is real information, but not automatic fact.

Use:

> Disconnection from emotion is not objectivity, and cognitive coherence is not the same as complete reality contact.

Boundary:

These are educational framework claims, not diagnoses of psychosis, delusion, empathy capacity, or formal reality testing.

## Mechanism And Impact

Use:

> This pattern may have a protective origin, and it still caused harm.

Use:

> Mechanism explains the pattern; it does not erase impact.

Use:

> The goal is to understand the pattern clearly enough to interrupt harm and support repair.

Avoid:

- `They could not help it.`
- `It was just their nervous system.`
- `Because there is a mechanism, there is no responsibility.`
- `Understanding the pattern is the same as repairing it.`

## Calm And Safety

Calm is not the same as safety.

Distress is not the same as danger.

Control may look calm, coherent, reasonable, socially legible, or even regulated after causing harm. The person harmed by control may be the one who looks distressed, angry, confused, dissociated, or unstable because their nervous system is responding to distortion, pressure, threat, or unrepaired harm.

In chronic control, cognition can remain active while empathy, attunement, and repair capacity are reduced or offline. Someone may be able to explain, justify, plan, and appear coherent while still not registering the other person's experience or the impact of the pattern.

Use:

> Control often makes its impact visible in the person being controlled first.

Avoid:

- `The calm person is the regulated one.`
- `The distressed person is the unsafe one.`
- `If someone looks composed, they are not causing harm.`
- `If someone can explain their reasons clearly, they are acting with empathy.`

## Protection, Control, And Manipulation

The detailed acute/chronic and formation logic lives in `foundation/08-acute-chronic-formations.md`.

Short version:

> Some protective states are acute movements. Others become chronic patterns. A pattern may have a protective origin and still cause harm when it becomes rigid, repeated, or controlling.

`Nervous-system protection` names a defensive pattern organized around threat, survival, control, shutdown, or self-protection.

It may be reactive, state-driven, narrowed, frightened, dissociated, or learned through repeated conditions. It can still cause harm, and when it does, the harm must be named.

`Intentional manipulation` names a strategic pattern of using another person's perception, vulnerability, dependence, fear, hope, or confusion to control their choices or extract benefit.

The two can overlap, but they are not the same.

A survival pattern can become harmful. A manipulative pattern can be supported by nervous-system states. TEG-Blue should not collapse deliberate control into protection, and should not collapse protection into moral condemnation.

## Responsibility

Responsibility increases with:

- awareness
- repetition
- power
- available choice
- refusal to repair
- impact on others

TEG-Blue should keep harm attached to behaviours, patterns, mechanisms, and conditions rather than turning people into fixed moral categories.

Reduced access to emotion, empathy, self-awareness, or repair may help explain why a pattern persists. It does not transfer the cost or responsibility for change to the person affected by the pattern.

## Acute, Chronic, And Identity

Acute and chronic describe organisation, not moral worth.

- acute means state movement remains responsive and return is available; it does not mean harmless
- chronic means a protective organisation has become rigid, repeated, or chronically online; it does not mean fake, chosen, permanent, or morally fixed
- a Formation is a coordinate in the map, not a person type, diagnosis, or whole identity
- a practical mode is an orientation category, not proof of another person's internal state or intention

Use acute/chronic language to clarify flexibility, repetition, impact, access, and repair—not to classify people from the outside.

## Repair

Repair is not only apology.

Repair means a pattern becomes clear enough that shared reality, responsibility, care, boundary, and changed behaviour can return.

Repair depends on perception becoming flexible again. It often requires separating:

- what happened
- what was felt
- what was assumed
- what evidence is available
- what harm or rupture occurred
- what needs responsibility, care, boundary, or change

## Public Use

Public examples should stay close to observable pattern and impact.

Use examples to clarify:

- feeling vs fact
- protection vs control
- calm vs safety
- distress vs danger
- apology vs repair
- explanation vs responsibility

Do not use public examples to diagnose, expose, shame, or assign motive to a person or group.

## Reader Contracts

The same mechanism must be translated differently depending on the reader's position.

### Whole-System / Science-Facing: `teg-blue.org`

The reader's task is to understand the architecture and its grounding.

Ethical requirements:

- explain Formation as a coordinate, not an identity
- keep source support distinct from TEG-Blue integration
- distinguish observation, state hypothesis, interpretation, impact, and diagnosis
- keep both acute impact and chronic rigidity visible
- do not make a composed presentation evidence of safety or a distressed presentation evidence of danger

### Self-Facing: `teg-blue.com`

The reader's task is to examine their own pattern, response, impact, responsibility, regulation, and repair.

Ethical requirements:

- use `I` / `my` framing where the tool's purpose is self-reflection
- do not label emotions for the user or prescribe the correct action
- do not convert self-reflection into diagnosis of another person
- help separate feeling, interpretation, evidence, impact, and response
- keep support, professional care, legal help, and crisis resources outside the tool's authority

### Harm-Facing: El Gradiente de la Violencia And Comparable Builds

The reader's task is to recognise what is happening to them, recover trust in what they are registering, understand how agency and available routes may be narrowing, and orient toward options, support, and safety.

Ethical requirements:

- preserve the asymmetry of power, cost, agency, and route deprivation
- describe observable patterns and effects before explaining the acting person's mechanism
- use mechanism only when it strengthens recognition or reduces confusion
- do not ask the person receiving harm to empathise with, regulate, confront, wait for, or repair the person causing harm
- do not imply that insight, a boundary, leaving, reporting, or any single action is always safe or available
- protect privacy, device safety, discoverability, storage, and exit routes as product-level ethical requirements

Core harm-facing line:

> Explanation must strengthen recognition. It must never relocate responsibility for the harm onto the person living with it.

## Surface Translation

On `teg-blue.org`, this belongs near methodology, ethics, foundations, and research posture.

On `teg-blue.com`, this should appear as concise tool guardrails:

- pattern read, not diagnosis
- impact still matters
- no tool can prove intent
- use clarity alongside support, context, and judgment
- repair requires changed pattern, not just explanation

In the TEG-Blue Engine, this should remain a constraint on labels, tool logic, and generated interpretations.

Engine implementation constraints:

- keep the four practical modes distinct from the 14 Formation-state positions
- preserve source wording separately from public/tool wording
- label grounded science, TEG-Blue integration, and working hypotheses distinctly
- never turn a visible behaviour into certainty about state, intent, diagnosis, or identity
- never let a practical compression erase power, impact, chronicity, or repair limits
- route every applied build through its reader contract and risk context
