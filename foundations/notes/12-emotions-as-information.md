# Emotions As Information

Status: foundation concept draft, added 2026-07-09; recovery pass 2026-08-01

This file names a core TEG-Blue premise:

> Emotions are structured biological and relational information.

They are not vague feelings, moral labels, irrational noise, decorative
intensity, or automatic facts.

This file should inform `foundation/00-working-definition.md`,
`foundation/06-state-shaped-perception.md`,
`foundation/10-ess-cls-educational-model.md`, `.org`, `.com`, and the
TEG-Blue Engine.

## The Premise This Answers

Most people arrive already holding a position on this, and it is usually the
opposite one:

> Emotion is the opposite of reason. It is noise, bias, distraction.

That is a false binary. Name it directly rather than writing around it.

Emotion is not irrational. It is **relational**. It reports on what is safe,
what is dangerous, what matters, and what is happening between people.

> When we ignore emotion, we do not become more rational. We become less
> informed.

TEG-Blue is not trying to displace logic. It is restoring emotion to its place
as a form of intelligence, alongside logic rather than beneath it.
`foundation/13-emotional-access-and-reality-contact.md` follows what happens
when that access is gone.

## Core Idea

Emotion is information moving through the whole organism.

An emotion can carry patterned data about:

- safety
- threat
- need
- boundary
- memory
- connection
- separation
- loss
- attraction
- pressure
- shame
- anger
- longing
- urgency
- impact
- repair

This information may arrive as body sensation, action readiness, attention,
impulse, relational orientation, image, memory, meaning, prediction, or a felt
sense that something matters.

Emotion is not separate from the body. It is not separate from cognition
either. It is part of a whole-organism information system in which body state,
attention, memory, prediction, meaning, relationship, and response are moving
together.

TEG-Blue reads emotional patterns as structured biological and relational
information because emotions show how the organism is organising around the
conditions it perceives.

## Real Information, Not Automatic Fact

Calling emotion information does not mean every emotion is objectively
accurate.

It means the feeling is carrying a signal.

That signal may point toward:

- a real present danger
- a real boundary
- a real need
- a real rupture
- a real memory
- a learned prediction
- an old threat pattern
- missing context
- a state of exhaustion, pressure, or overwhelm
- an interpretation that needs checking
- a repair need

The signal matters. It should not be dismissed.

But the signal may still need context, evidence, relationship, and time before
it can be understood accurately.

The central distinction:

> Emotion is real information, but it is not automatically complete evidence.

Or:

> A feeling can be true as an inner signal without being the whole truth about
> the outer situation.

## Survival Before Accuracy

The brain/body system is not designed first to produce neutral objectivity.

Its first task is survival: staying alive, protected, connected enough,
oriented enough, coherent enough, and ready enough to respond.

When a system is under threat, it may prioritise:

- speed over precision
- protection over curiosity
- certainty over uncertainty
- coherence over complexity
- old learning over new evidence
- prediction over present-moment checking
- action readiness over reflection

This does not make the emotion fake.

It means the emotion may be shaped by what the system is trying to protect,
predict, prevent, preserve, or repair.

## Signal, Not Moral Failing

Emotions are not good or bad. They are the system responding to conditions —
trying to protect, alert, orient, or connect.

> Fear is not weakness. Anger is not cruelty. Sadness is not failure.

Treating an emotion as a character verdict does two things at once: it makes the
signal harder to read, and it gives the person a reason not to look at it.

This does not become permission, because:

> What matters is what we do with the signal, not whether we had the feeling.

The feeling is not the accountable event. The response is. Taking moral weight
off the emotion does not take responsibility off what follows it — the same
mechanism-versus-responsibility line held in
`foundation/04-ethics-and-responsibility.md`.

An emotional behaviour usually has more than one source at once: a
nervous-system response, an unmet need, and a learned strategy that once
worked. TEG-Blue maps the mix rather than assigning the behaviour to a single
cause. `Unmet need` is Nonviolent Communication's concept and should be named as
such where the source matters; it is borrowed, not native.

This is also why TEG-Blue prioritises **clarity over correctness**. The task is
to see what the pattern is doing, not to rule on whether someone was right to
feel it.

## Emotion Must Be Perceived As Emotion

Self-awareness begins when emotion can be recognised as emotion.

That means a person can notice:

> Something is happening inside me.

Then:

> This is a feeling, a body signal, a memory, a prediction, or a need.

Then:

> I need to understand what kind of information it is carrying.

If a person has not learned to listen to emotions and body states as internal
information, or if a state makes that access unavailable, the emotion may not
be experienced as a signal.

It may be experienced as reality itself.

That is where state-shaped perception begins to matter:

> Real inside me can become treated as true outside me.

This is why `foundation/06-state-shaped-perception.md` should build on this
file rather than trying to define emotion from inside the perception concept.

Related foundation:

`foundation/13-emotional-access-and-reality-contact.md` develops the other
side of this problem: when emotion and body state are unavailable, cognition
may still sound coherent while modelling reality from incomplete data.

## Cognition's Role

Cognition can help emotion become usable.

It can:

- name what is being felt
- compare the feeling with evidence
- add context
- remember relevant history
- notice assumptions
- slow down urgency
- imagine options
- include another person's reality
- choose a response
- support repair

Cognition can also distort emotion.

It can:

- build a story too quickly
- protect an old prediction
- justify a defensive response
- override body information
- turn partial information into certainty
- miss impact
- preserve coherence at the expense of repair

The goal is not for cognition to defeat emotion.

The goal is cooperation:

> emotion, body information, context, evidence, impact, and response becoming
> possible to hold together.

## Emotion And Logic Work Together

TEG-Blue should not ask people to choose between emotion and intelligence.

Emotion tracks meaning, safety, connection, need, boundary, memory, impact, and
repair.

Logic helps make sense of what is happening. It can check evidence, add
context, compare possibilities, notice assumptions, include consequences, and
choose a response.

Together, emotion and logic can create emotional clarity: the ability to
recognize patterns, spot harm, support repair, and make decisions aligned with
care, boundary, clarification, action, or support.

The goal is not to fix, flatten, or defeat emotion. The goal is to see its
logic: how it forms, escalates, spreads, protects, warns, confuses, or points
toward repair.

## What TEG-Blue Should Not Say

Do not say:

> Emotion is irrational.

Do not say:

> Emotion is only intensity.

Do not say:

> Emotion is just a story the mind creates.

Do not say:

> The body always knows the truth.

Do not say:

> If I feel it, it is fact.

Do not say:

> If someone feels hurt, harm definitely happened.

Do not say:

> If someone feels unsafe, danger is definitely present.

Do not say:

> Logic is better than emotion.

Do not say:

> Thinking is just a defence.

Do not say:

> The goal is to be less emotional.

## Guardrails

Do not use this concept to dismiss distress.

The fact that emotion needs interpretation does not make it meaningless.

Do not use this concept to deny actual danger, abuse, injustice, coercion, or
structural harm. Sometimes the body is detecting a real threat.

Do not use this concept to make people distrust themselves. The aim is more
accurate self-trust: learning what a feeling is carrying without forcing it to
be the whole truth.

Do not use this concept to rank body over thought or thought over body. Both
can help. Both can distort. Both depend on state, access, evidence, context,
power, impact, and repair capacity.

Do not force immediate analysis when someone is overwhelmed. Sometimes the
system needs safety, grounding, space, or support before emotion can be
understood.

## Surface Translation

### `teg-blue.org`

On `.org`, this belongs near the core framework, methodology, and foundations.

Suggested public line:

> TEG-Blue treats emotions as structured biological and relational
> information: signals about body state, safety, threat, need, boundary,
> connection, impact, and repair.

It should be paired with the guardrail:

> Emotion is real information, but not automatic fact.

### `teg-blue.com`

On `.com`, this should become practical tool language.

Possible prompt sequence:

- What am I feeling?
- Where do I feel it in my body?
- What does this feeling seem to be protecting, predicting, needing, or naming?
- What story is my mind building from it?
- What do I actually know?
- What context or evidence might be missing?
- What needs care, boundary, action, clarification, or repair?

Tone:

> Your feeling is information. It may be important. It may also need context
> before it can tell you what happened, what is happening now, or what response
> is needed.

### TEG-Blue Engine

In the Engine, this concept should sit beneath:

- emotional-pattern legibility
- ESS / CLS translation
- state-shaped perception
- feeling-fact differentiation
- repair capacity
- acute/chronic state movement
- protection/control distinctions

Engine question:

> What kind of information is this emotion carrying, and how does the current
> state affect the system's capacity to interpret, test, communicate, or repair
> from that information?

## Data Or Information

The recovered material leads with `Emotions are data`. This file uses
`information`, and that stays the definitional term.

The two are not interchangeable. `Data` implies something recordable and
measurable, which pulls toward exactly the claim TEG-Blue does not make: that a
person's internal state can be measured or read from outside. `Information`
keeps the meaning — a signal that carries something — without the instrument.

`Emotions are data` remains usable as a **public entry line**, where its whole
job is to borrow the authority of the thing it is being contrasted with. It
should not travel into methodology, Engine, research or tool language, and it
should never appear near a claim about tracking, scoring, or detecting a state.

## Did Not Come Back

**`Like radar for the inner world`.** Radar detects objects at a distance and
returns their position. The metaphor implies detection and measurement, and it
places the observer outside the thing observed. It fails for the same reason
`thermometer for emotional states` fails — see
`foundation/16-where-teg-blue-sits.md` § What Did Not Come
Back.
