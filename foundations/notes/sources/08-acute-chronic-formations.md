# Acute, Chronic, And Formations

Status: foundation source for `.org` public Formation science and Engine alignment

This file defines the working distinction between `acute`, `chronic`, and `formations` in TEG-Blue.

It supports the ethics, methodology, Engine, and `.org` Formation pages. The
public doorway remains TEG-Blue / The Emotional Gradient Blueprint, while
`.org` may now explain the full acute/chronic Formation architecture after a
clear visual orientation.

## Why This Exists

Safety and threat are basic survival configurations of the body.

Across animals, survival is not only one reaction. Mammals can organize around rest, connection, proximity, protection, pursuit, escape, ambush, stealth, exhaustion, dominance, submission, collapse, and energy conservation.

Humans added unusually developed cognitive capacity: planning, abstraction, logic, social interpretation, self-story, strategy, and symbolic meaning.

Over time, human cultures often treated explicit logic as the only valid way to understand reality. Body information was often treated as noise, weakness, instinct, irrationality, or something to suppress.

TEG-Blue starts from a different premise:

> The body is an intelligent survival system.

The body does not only feel. It organizes.

It organizes energy, attention, perception, movement, inhibition, connection, protection, control, shutdown, regulation, and repair.

That design is what TEG-Blue currently calls `acute`.

## Working Definitions

Acute:

> Acute names the organism's designed survival configurations when they are available, responsive, and able to move.

Acute does not mean harmless. Acute protection, control, domination, or shutdown can still have impact. But acute states are not stuck as the whole system's default mode.

Chronic:

> Chronic names what happens when protective mechanisms become stuck, overlearned, or chronically online.

Chronic does not mean fake, chosen, or morally fixed. It means an originally protective organization has become rigid, repeated, or unavailable to easy return.

In chronic states, cognition may remain active while empathy, attunement, and repair capacity are reduced or offline.

This matters because a chronic state can still think, plan, explain, justify, strategize, and appear coherent. The issue is not the absence of cognition. The issue is cognition operating without enough relational empathy, body-level flexibility, or repair access.

The central distinction:

> Acute is state movement. Chronic is state rigidity.

Or:

> Acute is what a protective mechanism is designed for. Chronic is what happens when that mechanism becomes the system's repeated home.

## Self-Awareness And Empathy

Acute formations assume that some self-awareness has been learned and remains available enough for the organism to notice, adjust, return, and repair.

In acute movement, empathy is usually available when the system is in safety, connection, or flexible regulation. Empathy may narrow or go offline when threat requires immediate organism-protection, but it can return when the threat passes and the system has enough capacity to regulate.

Chronic Formations are different.

In chronic Formations, self-awareness may never have been learned, may be underdeveloped, or may not be reliably accessible under stress. The system may confuse feelings with facts, body predictions with external truth, and interpretation with reality.

Empathy may also be underdeveloped, unavailable, or state-dependent. In some chronic patterns, a person may learn the social performance of empathy without having stable felt access to empathy, attunement, or repair capacity.

This matters ethically because chronic states can preserve cognition, language, explanation, planning, and social performance while self-awareness and empathy are reduced or offline.

Public boundary:

> TEG-Blue can name that self-awareness and empathy may be unavailable in chronic states. It should not use that absence to diagnose a person, excuse harm, or claim certainty about what someone truly feels inside.

## Survival Problems

TEG-Blue currently distinguishes two survival problems that run through the map:

1. Connection-preservation: staying connected enough to survive inside the group.
2. Organism-protection: protecting the organism from threat, harm, exhaustion, or overwhelm.

These problems are not opposites. They often compete.

A person may need connection and protection at the same time. A state may be trying to preserve belonging while also defending the organism. This is one reason emotional and relational patterns can become confusing from the outside.

## Acute Formations

The acute formations describe the organism's designed state capacities.

| Formation | Working Name | Survival Configuration | What It Is Designed For |
|---|---|---|---|
| X | Acute Baseline | safety configuration | Resting without active defense; conserving energy when no threat is detected. |
| A | Acute Connection | safety configuration | Connection, belonging, co-regulation, and staying part of community. |
| A<->B | Acute Transition | connection-preservation configuration | Monitoring the edge between connection and threat; staying safe inside the group. |
| B | Acute Protection | organism-protection configuration | Defensive mobilization against danger, boundary threat, rupture, or harm. |
| C | Acute Control / Management | organism-protection configuration | Cognitive control, planning, organization, strategy, and management when danger persists. |
| D | Acute Domination | organism-protection configuration | Maximum survival activation: heightened energy, senses, attention, force, and action in life-threatening conditions. |
| Z | Acute Shutdown | organism-protection configuration | Collapse, inhibition, and energy conservation when activation cannot be sustained. |

## Acute By Formation Group

Formations `X` and `A` are safety-detected patterns.

`A<->B` is the transition zone: the connection-preservation edge where the system is still oriented to belonging, but threat may be emerging.

Formations `B`, `C`, and `D` are threat patterns.

Formation `Z` is shutdown: the organism has no more sustainable activation and collapses or inhibits to conserve what energy remains.

## Chronic Formations

The chronic formations describe what happens when these protective organizations become repeated, overlearned, or chronically online.

| Formation | Working Name | Chronic Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| X | Chronic Baseline | What happens when safety has never been learned or is not available as a reliable body state. |
| A | Chronic Connection | What happens when safety has not been learned, but connection is still required for survival. |
| A<->B | Chronic Transition | What happens when connection-preservation is chronically active and the system stays on the edge of safety and threat. |
| B | Chronic Protection | What happens when organism-protection becomes the repeated default. |
| C | Chronic Control / Management | What happens when persistent danger trains the mind to stay organized around control, strategy, monitoring, and management. |
| D | Chronic Domination | What happens when extreme survival activation, force, threat response, or dominance becomes chronically available or overused. |
| Z | Chronic Shutdown | What happens when collapse, inhibition, disconnection, or energy conservation becomes the repeated home of the system. |

## Acute Control And Chronic Control

Acute control is not automatically manipulation.

Acute control can be management: the mind organizing around a persistent danger, making plans, sequencing action, reducing uncertainty, and trying to overcome threat.

Chronic control is different.

Chronic control appears when the system repeatedly organizes around monitoring, managing, directing, constraining, or controlling conditions as its default way to survive.

In chronic control, cognition can become highly active while empathy is narrowed or offline. The mind may plan, explain, justify, predict, organize, and manage, but without enough felt access to the other person's experience or to the repair needs created by its impact.

This distinction matters ethically:

- control may have a protective origin
- control can still cause harm
- chronic control can become relationally distorting
- cognition can remain coherent while empathy and repair capacity are reduced
- manipulation should not be excused as mere protection
- protection should not be collapsed into moral condemnation

## F1-F3 Regulation Thread

F1, F2, and F3 should be held together by a regulation thread, not led by specialized physiology language.

Use the top-level language to explain what the system is doing:

- `F1`: how activation, regulation, return, and repair are designed to move when the protective system is responsive.
- `F2`: how repeated conditions train the system into recognizable routes for protection, management, shutdown, connection-preservation, or repair.
- `F3`: how those routes can become rigid, costly, and chronically online when return and repair are no longer easily available.

Homeostasis, allostasis, stress physiology, and allostatic load may help underwrite parts of this sequence, but they should sit below the primary explanation as source-trace or research-note language.

Do not make F1, F2, or F3 top-of-page explanations about homeostasis or allostatic load. The public-facing doorway, if these layers are ever translated, should stay functional and legible:

> How activation rises, how regulation tries to meet it, how return becomes possible, and what happens when the pattern gets stuck.

## Public Boundary

`.org` may now show and explain the seven acute plus seven chronic
Formation-state positions. It should introduce Formation as a map coordinate,
not a person type, diagnosis or fixed identity. The home page may show the dual
gradient visually; detailed Formation science belongs on dedicated pages.

`.com` and applied tools should still use the simplest language that fits their
reader task. They do not need to expose all 14 positions unless the tool's job
requires that depth.

Public general pages should still not lead with:

- F1-F6
- L0-L6 or raw ATLAS row architecture
- chronic-state typologies presented as identities
- diagnostic labels

Useful plain-language translations remain:

> Some patterns are temporary state movements. Others become repeated or chronic.

or:

> TEG-Blue distinguishes between protective states that move and protective patterns that get stuck.

or:

> A pattern may have a protective origin and still cause harm when it becomes rigid, repeated, or controlling.

## Ethics Link

This distinction supports the ethics rule:

> Mechanism explains the pattern; it does not erase impact.

Acute and chronic language should never be used to excuse harm, diagnose a person, or claim certainty about another person's internal state.

It should help clarify:

- whether a pattern is temporary or repeated
- whether a state can move or is rigid
- whether self-awareness has been learned and is accessible
- whether empathy is felt, available, performed, narrowed, or offline
- whether cognition is operating with empathy and repair capacity, or without enough access to them
- whether protection has become control
- whether control has become manipulation
- whether repair capacity is available
- whether responsibility, boundary, care, or interruption is needed
