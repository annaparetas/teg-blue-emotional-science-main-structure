# State-Shaped Perception

Status: foundation concept draft, rebuilt 2026-07-09

This file names a core TEG-Blue idea that should inform `.org`, `.com`,
and the TEG-Blue Engine:

> Body and nervous-system states do not only change what people feel. They
> change what feels true.

This file depends on `foundation/12-emotions-as-information.md`.

Sibling concept:

`foundation/13-emotional-access-and-reality-contact.md` develops the other
reality-contact distortion: when emotion and body state are unavailable,
cognition may sound coherent while modelling from incomplete data.

The core sequence is:

1. Emotion is information moving through the organism.
2. The brain/body system uses that information to model what is happening.
3. Under threat, the system prioritises survival, protection, prediction,
   coherence, and action more than neutral fact-analysis.
4. If emotion is not recognised as internal information, it can be mistaken
   for the whole reality of the situation.

The central distinction:

> A feeling can be completely real inside a person without being complete
> evidence about what is happening outside them.

This concept is not about dismissing emotion. It is about protecting the
boundary between feeling, fact, interpretation, impact, and repair.

## Core Idea

Perception is state-shaped.

The brain does not passively receive reality as a neutral stream of facts. It
models what is happening through body state, emotion, memory, prediction,
context, and available evidence.

In TEG-Blue, emotion is not a decorative layer added to perception. Emotion is
structured biological and relational information. It can carry real data about
safety, threat, need, boundary, memory, connection, loss, attraction, pressure,
impact, and repair.

Cognition then tries to make a usable model from the information available.
It may name, explain, compare, predict, narrate, justify, contextualise, or
distort what the body and emotion are carrying.

The brain/body system's first task is survival. Accuracy matters, but under
threat the system may choose speed, protection, certainty, and coherence over
fuller reality-testing.

This is why self-awareness matters.

Emotion has to become recognisable as emotion:

> This is a real signal inside me.

When a person has not learned to listen to body state and emotion as internal
information, or when a state makes that access unavailable, that emotional
information can be mistaken for the whole reality of the situation.

The collapse happens when:

> This is real inside me.

becomes:

> This is therefore true outside me.

That is the feeling-fact collapse.

In fluid states, a person may have enough capacity to notice the difference
between:

- what happened
- what they felt
- what their body predicted
- what they assumed
- what evidence is actually available
- what remains uncertain
- what the other person may have experienced
- what impact occurred
- what still needs care, boundary, repair, or clarification

In chronic states, this separation can collapse. A feeling can become a fact.
An interpretation can become evidence. A body prediction can feel like
external truth. A current state can overwrite context, uncertainty, and another
person's reality.

The feeling is real as an experience. It may carry important information about
state, history, needs, limits, or danger. But internal intensity is not the
same as external accuracy.

## Why This Matters

This explains why chronic nervous-system patterns can make reality feel fixed,
even when the evidence is incomplete, mixed, or contradictory.

The person may not be trying to lie. They may be trying to survive inside the
model their state has built.

Examples of feeling-fact collapse:

- "I feel unsafe, therefore danger is present."
- "I feel hurt, therefore harm was done."
- "I feel rejected, therefore I am being abandoned."
- "I feel ashamed, therefore I am wrong or worthless."
- "I feel accused, therefore I am being attacked."
- "I feel controlled, therefore every boundary is control."
- "I feel pressure, therefore this must happen now."
- "I feel angry, therefore the other person intended harm."
- "I do not feel affected, therefore it does not matter."
- "I feel certain, therefore no further evidence is needed."

These are not signs that emotion is false or unimportant. They show that
emotion is information carried through a state-shaped system.

The question is not:

> Is this feeling real?

The question is:

> What kind of information is this feeling carrying, and is my current state
> able to separate internal signal, interpretation, evidence, impact, and
> response?

## The State-Reality Boundary

The State-Reality Boundary is the capacity to recognise the difference between
inner reality and shared reality.

Inner reality includes:

- body signals
- emotion
- memory
- prediction
- fear
- shame
- longing
- urgency
- protective meaning

Shared reality includes:

- what happened
- what was said or done
- what can be verified
- what remains uncertain
- what impact occurred
- what each person experienced
- what responsibility, boundary, repair, or change is needed

When this boundary is available, a person can feel strongly without making the
feeling the whole truth.

They can say:

> This feels true in my body. I need to check what is actually true in the
> situation.

When this boundary collapses, the person may not experience their perception
as perception. They may experience it as obvious reality.

That is when feeling can become proof, certainty, accusation, entitlement, or
control.

## From Feeling To Control

This concept must be strong enough to prevent harm from being excused.

A nervous-system state can explain why something felt real. It does not
automatically make the behaviour fair, accurate, or harmless.

Dysregulation does not equal coercive control. A person can be overwhelmed,
afraid, angry, ashamed, or hurt without trying to control someone else.

The coercive risk begins when a person treats their felt reality as something
another person must accept, obey, mirror, or organise around.

Private feeling becomes relational control when someone:

- uses fear as proof that the other person is dangerous, without checking evidence
- uses hurt as proof that the other person owes compliance
- uses shame as proof that the other person is attacking or humiliating them
- uses discomfort as proof that a boundary is unfair
- uses anger as permission to punish, intimidate, or dominate
- uses calmness, detachment, or lack of visible impact as proof that no harm occurred
- demands agreement with their interpretation as the price of connection or safety
- pressures others to deny what happened because it threatens the story their state is using to stay regulated

This is how a state-shaped perception can become a reality demand:

> You must treat what I feel as what is true.

Or:

> You must stop naming what my state cannot tolerate.

In this pattern, the person is no longer only having a feeling. They are
trying to make another person live inside the reality created by that feeling.

This is one of the places where confusion, emotional pressure, gaslighting,
coercive control, and reality distortion can begin.

## Self-Awareness

Self-awareness is not detachment from feeling. It is the learned capacity to
recognise emotion and body state as real internal information before treating
them as external fact.

A more regulated system has more room between sensation and certainty. It can
ask:

- What am I feeling?
- What is my body predicting?
- What survival problem might my system be trying to solve?
- What story did my mind build around this feeling?
- What do I actually know?
- What am I assuming?
- What evidence is missing?
- What might the other person have experienced?
- Am I asking someone to treat my feeling as fact?
- Is my response protecting a boundary, or controlling another person?
- What impact might my response have?

This should not be framed as a moral hierarchy between "self-aware" and
"unself-aware" people.

It is a capacity question:

> How much access does the person have, in this state, to body signals,
> emotion, context, evidence, self-reflection, another person's reality, and
> repair?

The aim is not to make people distrust themselves. The aim is to help them
trust themselves more accurately.

Self-trust becomes stronger when feeling, fact, interpretation, and response
are not collapsed into one thing.

## Accountability

Accountability depends on the ability to separate state from impact.

A person can say:

> This is what felt true to me.

without using it to avoid:

> This is what happened.
>
> This is how my behaviour affected you.
>
> This is what needs repair or change.

State explains the route by which perception narrowed. It does not erase the
consequences of acting from that narrowed perception.

A nervous-system state can reduce capacity. It can increase urgency,
defensiveness, fear, shame, or certainty. But it does not remove
responsibility for repair, changed behaviour, or the impact on others.

When a person refuses the State-Reality Boundary, accountability collapses
because every challenge to the behaviour is experienced as a challenge to the
reality their state is defending.

This is why repair requires more than apology. It requires enough flexibility
to include facts, impact, and another person's reality.

## Repair

Repair depends on perception becoming flexible again.

Repair is not only apology. It is the return to shared reality after
state-shaped perception has narrowed the field.

Repair becomes more possible when the system can separate:

- what happened
- what it meant to each person
- what was felt
- what was assumed
- what evidence is available
- what evidence is missing
- what harm or rupture occurred
- what needs responsibility, care, boundary, or change

When chronic states make feeling indistinguishable from fact, repair becomes
harder because the person is not only defending a position. They may be
defending the reality their body is using to stay regulated.

Repair begins when the person can return from:

> This is true because I feel it.

into:

> This is what I felt. Now I need to understand what happened, what I assumed,
> what you experienced, and what needs care or repair.

## Guardrails

Open: these guardrails may be in the wrong place. The argument that they belong
inside the definition rather than at the end of it — because the concept is
unfalsifiable when applied to someone else, and therefore points at whoever is
easier to discredit — is worked through in `questions/open-questions.md`
§ Reading Someone Else's Perception. It waits on the engine-2 AEC and
consciousness work. Do not restructure this file before then.

Do not use this concept to dismiss distress.

A feeling may not be the whole fact, but it is still real data about the
person's state, history, needs, limits, or environment.

Do not use this concept to tell someone:

> Your perception is not real.

That sentence can itself become a tool of dismissal, gaslighting, or control.

More accurate language is:

> Your feeling is real. It may be carrying important information. We still
> need to separate what is felt, what is known, what is assumed, what happened,
> and what impact occurred.

Do not use this concept to deny actual danger, abuse, injustice, coercion, or
structural harm. Sometimes the body is detecting a real threat. Sometimes calm
belongs to control, and distress belongs to the person being harmed.

Do not use this concept to privilege calm over distress. Calm does not always
mean accuracy. Distress does not always mean distortion.

Do not use this concept to excuse impact. A nervous-system state can reduce
capacity, but it does not remove responsibility for repair, changed behaviour,
or the impact on others.

Do not force immediate fact-checking when someone is overwhelmed. First, the
system may need safety, grounding, space, or support. Differentiation becomes
possible when there is enough capacity to think, feel, and perceive at the
same time.

## Surface Translation

### `teg-blue.org`

On `.org`, this belongs as framework language:

> The Nervous System Gradient describes not only emotional states, but
> perceptual conditions. Each state changes what the system notices, ignores,
> predicts, protects, and treats as true.

`.org` should explain the distinction between feeling, interpretation,
evidence, impact, and shared reality.

Suggested architecture terms:

- State-Shaped Perception
- State-Reality Boundary
- Feeling-Fact Differentiation
- Feeling-Fact Collapse
- Perceptual Accountability
- Reality Demand
- Repair Capacity

### `teg-blue.com`

On `.com`, this should become practical and user-facing.

Possible tool language:

> Feeling / Fact / Impact / Repair

Prompt sequence:

- What am I feeling?
- What does this feeling make me believe is true?
- What is my system trying to protect, predict, or solve?
- What do I actually know?
- What am I assuming?
- What evidence is missing?
- What might the other person have experienced?
- Am I asking someone to treat my feeling as fact?
- What needs care, boundary, clarification, accountability, or repair?

The tone should be compassionate and non-corrective:

> Your feeling is information. It may be telling you something important about
> your state, your history, your needs, your limits, or the situation. It may
> not be giving you the full facts of what happened.

### TEG-Blue Engine

In the Engine, this connects to existing concepts such as locked perception,
false coherence, self-awareness, coercive reality, and repair capacity.

Possible Engine labels:

- State-Reality Boundary
- Feeling-Fact Differentiation
- Feeling-Fact Collapse
- Reality Demand
- Perceptual Accountability
- Perception-Repair Capacity
- Coercive Reality Export

The Engine question:

> In this state, how much can the system distinguish internal signal, narrative
> meaning, external evidence, another person's reality, impact, and the repair
> route?
